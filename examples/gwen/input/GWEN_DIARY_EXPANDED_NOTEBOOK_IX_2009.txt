[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook IX: 2009]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 415 — 2009-01-04 — Age 23 — The quiet house

Mother has taken to putting a second mug on the table whenever I visit, without asking whether I want tea. We talked about the neighbor's dog, a synagogue fundraiser, and the leaky gutter. We did not discuss Daniel, and I was grateful. Before I went home she told me I didn't have to prove I was fine. I said I knew. Then I went back inside because I had left my gloves on the radiator. I am twenty-three soon enough, and still capable of forgetting the objects attached to my own body.

------------------------------

ENTRY 416 — 2009-01-16 — Age 23 — A sensible notebook

I have started a separate notebook for observations during field work: weather, location, method, uncertainty, and what I actually saw. I have been ruthless about keeping speculation in the margin. It is a satisfying discipline. A friend asked whether I was doing the same thing with people. I said people become irritated if you label them by date and sampling location. She said I hadn't answered. I don't think I ever do when the question is close to the bone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 417 — 2009-01-29 — Age 23 — A talk about money

A person at a gathering asked if my family would sponsor a project whose budget I had not seen. I said I would gladly read a proposal with specific objectives and independent accounting. He looked disappointed, as if generosity should involve no questions. I'm willing to support work that matters. I'm not willing to call laziness a charitable virtue. Daddy would have approved of the arithmetic. Mother would have asked whether the work served actual people. I think both questions belong together.

------------------------------

ENTRY 418 — 2009-02-12 — Age 23 — The gull and the sandwich

A gull stole almost half my sandwich while I was helping a friend fix a slipping backpack strap. I knew better than to leave food in reach. I nevertheless stood there offended, watching the bird carry away its prize. My friend laughed for ten minutes. The gull did not offer restitution. I wrote a note to myself about urban wildlife and learned precisely nothing, since I shall almost certainly be robbed again next summer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 419 — 2009-02-24 — Age 23 — Sitting through music

I heard an older pianist take his time through a standard Daddy loved. It wasn't a particularly showy performance, and I suspect that was the point. I remembered Daddy explaining how to make room in the music for the notes you don't play. Afterward I talked with the pianist about his left hand and the recording he had learned from. He didn't know my father. For once I liked that. I could simply listen and remember without making the whole room responsible for my history.

------------------------------

ENTRY 420 — 2009-03-09 — Age 23 — A close friend tells the truth

My friend said she had worried about me last year but had hesitated to say so because I seemed determined to defend Daniel. I told her she should have said something. She said she had, twice. I had forgotten—or perhaps I had chosen not to hear. That embarrassed me. I thanked her instead of arguing. We went to a little place with terrible chairs and wonderful soup and spent the remainder of lunch complaining about a lecture. I need friends who will risk my displeasure occasionally.

------------------------------

ENTRY 421 — 2009-03-22 — Age 23 — The ferry notebook

I missed the ferry because I was watching a seal and lost track of boarding. This is the least heroic reason ever recorded for missing transportation. I had a long wait and used it to finish a chapter of a book about estuaries. The next ferry arrived on time; the seal did not return. I wrote both facts down. If the diary exists partly to rescue my days from oblivion, it ought to rescue the foolish parts too.

------------------------------

ENTRY 422 — 2009-04-08 — Age 23 — Preparing for Pesach

Mother and I were sorting dishes and discussing her Brooklyn childhood when she found a small spoon she had thought was lost. She held it as though it had returned from a voyage. She told me her mother had used one like it and launched into a story about crowded kitchens and cousins who arrived without warning. I wanted to write it all down and settled for her telling the story again while I made notes. She said I had inherited my father's appetite for documentation and her appetite for asking questions.

------------------------------

ENTRY 423 — 2009-04-24 — Age 23 — Wet soil

A project supervisor corrected me sharply in front of everyone after I used the wrong comparison set. I was angry for ten minutes and then admitted the correction was necessary. The tone was not kind; the data were not mine to defend with pride. We repaired the table that afternoon. I like field science partly because something outside my wishes gets a vote. A wet hillside will not care that I thought my first interpretation was elegant.

------------------------------

ENTRY 424 — 2009-05-12 — Age 23 — A letter not sent

I wrote Daniel a letter and didn't send it. It wasn't a plea or an accusation; it was a list of things I wished we had told each other honestly before making promises. By the end I realized I was writing for the person I had imagined, not necessarily the person I knew. I put the pages in the back of the drawer. They may belong there for years. I am done negotiating with a ghost for an explanation I already have.

------------------------------

ENTRY 425 — 2009-05-28 — Age 23 — A different summer plan

I have been invited to assist with a habitat survey and I said yes before discussing dates with anyone. Mother was pleased but asked whether I had actually read the safety requirements. I said yes. She asked whether I had brought proper boots last time I claimed to have read instructions. Fair enough. I will buy new socks and make sure the kit contains everything listed. My enthusiasm does not protect other people from the consequences of a forgotten item.

------------------------------

ENTRY 426 — 2009-06-15 — Age 23 — Mosquitoes

We spent the morning identifying plants and the afternoon explaining why our sample method might miss important things. I was covered in mosquito bites and delighted by an obscure little flower nobody else seemed especially interested in. A volunteer asked why I bothered with work that paid so little when I could live off investments. I said the plant hadn't checked my bank balance before blooming and neither should the research. He laughed, then asked a serious question, and we talked until it grew cold.

------------------------------

ENTRY 427 — 2009-06-30 — Age 23 — A small lie

Someone I was getting to know said he loved hiking but turned out to mean that he once walked from a parking lot to an overlook. I was about to tease him when I realized he was trying to impress me. We went on a gentle trail and had a perfectly good afternoon. Not every exaggeration is a plot. Still, I wish people would start with the true thing. It is usually more interesting than what they invent.

------------------------------

ENTRY 428 — 2009-07-18 — Age 23 — The mountain afternoon

The weather changed while we were walking, and a bank of cloud erased the view. We turned around before it became unsafe and arrived back damp, hungry, and satisfied. Someone complained we hadn't reached the destination. I heard my own graduation speech in my head and nearly laughed. A journey can be worthwhile without the summit. I didn't deliver a sermon about it. I ate the last apple and offered half to the disappointed man. He accepted and seemed happier.

------------------------------

ENTRY 429 — 2009-08-02 — Age 23 — A Jewish summer

I visited family acquaintances and came away with recipes, arguments about observance, and an invitation to another dinner I may not be able to attend. People have more than one way of being serious about their faith. I was raised with certain habits and I keep most of them because they belong to me, not because I can win arguments by practicing them. I wrote down a melody I heard sung before the meal and called Mother to ask whether she remembered it. She hummed the next phrase immediately.

------------------------------

ENTRY 430 — 2009-08-21 — Age 23 — The house repairs

A tradesman explained what needed doing on the house, and I asked so many questions that he finally handed me a pencil and drew a little diagram. Mother said she had married a man who treated contracts as a form of conversation and produced a daughter who treated gutters as a laboratory. I made coffee for everyone and managed to put the lid on the sugar bowl crooked. A small sticky disaster followed. Nobody suggested I supervise the next stage of the repairs.

------------------------------

ENTRY 431 — 2009-09-07 — Age 23 — A new term

I have plans for the autumn, but they are written in pencil. I learned something about the cost of confusing intention with control. I want the year to contain serious work and good friends and a little romance, if it arrives honestly. That last qualification makes me sound stern. I am not stern. I still fall in love too quickly with interesting voices and kind eyes. The point is not to stop hoping. It is to listen when the facts become inconvenient.

------------------------------

ENTRY 432 — 2009-09-20 — Age 23 — The new year

At Rosh Hashanah services a child near us mispronounced a word and then looked mortified. The grown-ups around her did nothing dramatic. Someone quietly helped her find her place. I was grateful for the absence of performance. I thought about all the times I have made too much of being right. When we came home Mother asked me to help with dinner, and I peeled apples while she told me which guests were coming. The guest list was a minor diplomatic achievement.

------------------------------

ENTRY 433 — 2009-10-11 — Age 23 — An argument over a river

I attended a meeting where a developer spoke about jobs and a conservation group spoke about a threatened stream. Both presented real information and both omitted things that complicated their positions. I asked whether we could see the underlying survey. People shifted uncomfortably, as though I had taken sides. I wasn't trying to. The stream will carry water through whatever policy we write. If we're wrong about its condition, earnest speeches will not correct the measurements.

------------------------------

ENTRY 434 — 2009-10-28 — Age 23 — The little donation

I paid for veterinary care for an animal brought to the shelter and asked not to have my name attached to the gift. The director called anyway to explain the procedure and thank me. I appreciated the detail. It is easy to spend money and feel useful. Harder to learn whether the spending actually helped. The animal is recovering. I have a photograph now, and its expression suggests it is unimpressed by every human involved, including me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 435 — 2009-11-15 — Age 23 — A walk with Mother

Mother and I walked as far as she wanted, then turned back. She has begun to tire more quickly, though she objects to the subject. I didn't pretend not to notice and didn't make a fuss. We stopped to look at a mushroom growing from an old stump. She said my father would have poked it with his cane and tried to identify it without a guide. I said he would probably have been wrong and refused to admit it. We laughed and missed him together.

------------------------------

ENTRY 436 — 2009-11-26 — Age 23 — Kitchen politics

We hosted friends, and two guests managed to turn a conversation about roads into a quarrel about nearly everything. Mother intervened by assigning them opposite ends of a mountain of dishes. It worked. I don't know if they agreed on any issue when they were finished, but they did agree the kitchen towels were useless. I grew up with people who disagreed at dinner and kept eating. I wish more adults remembered that argument does not always require an enemy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 437 — 2009-12-12 — Age 23 — A book in the bag

I left a novel in a borrowed car and spent an absurd portion of the day worrying that the owner would think its subject matter reflected a secret confession. The book is perfectly ordinary. I retrieved it with an apology, and the owner said he hadn't noticed it. I have the peculiar gift of constructing social disasters in situations where no one else has supplied any material. The actual disaster today was knocking a jar of jam onto the floor. Mother says I should write that down too. So I have.

------------------------------

ENTRY 438 — 2009-12-31 — Age 23 — A year without a headline

I look back and see a year of work, music, conversations, walks, religious holidays, irritating mistakes, and kind people. There was no grand event. I'm glad. I still sometimes feel the bruise of last year, particularly when someone asks an innocent question about marriage. But I have not become a different species of person. I am twenty-three, and I still think people are more interesting than the explanations we make for them. The diary is getting thick. I shall buy another notebook tomorrow.
