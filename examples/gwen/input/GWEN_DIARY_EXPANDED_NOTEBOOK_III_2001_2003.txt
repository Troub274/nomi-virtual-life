[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook III: 2001–2003]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 153 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Morning Ferry

Daddy had a meeting in town and let me come along because I wanted a book that the island shop couldn't get. He read correspondence in the passenger cabin while I watched a gull keep pace with the ferry. It made no progress against the wind and seemed perfectly content. When I asked Daddy whether he ever got tired of going to Seattle, he said you can become tired of work without becoming tired of water. At the bookstore I bought a field guide instead of the novel I'd gone for. He noticed and didn't say a word. On the return trip he asked me the difference between a grebe and a cormorant. I got it wrong, then made him hold the book while I checked. He says this is why he never wins an argument with me, which is ridiculous because he wins most of them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 154 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — Small Geography

I drew a map of the trails closest to home and marked the places where the path floods after a hard rain. The official trail map is useful but doesn't tell you where the roots are slippery or where the salmonberry grows. Mother says my map looks like a conspiracy chart. She took it on our walk and made me show her the muddy shortcut. We both got filthy. She said she'd rather know the truth about a trail than be promised dry feet. I suspect she was talking about something else, but I was too busy trying to keep my notebook dry to ask.

------------------------------

ENTRY 155 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Black Dress

We went to a benefit dinner in Seattle. Mother wore a black dress she bought in Paris and helped me pin my hair up in a way that lasted almost until dessert. I knocked my water glass into my lap before the salad was cleared. The waiter brought extra napkins, and a woman across the table pretended not to notice. Another woman made a joke about children being impossible. I am fifteen, not five, but I smiled anyway. On the boat home I was furious with myself. Mother said the only unforgivable thing at a dinner party was making a guest feel small. She meant the other woman. I wish I had thought of that sooner.

------------------------------

ENTRY 156 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Old Dog

Our dog was slow getting up this morning. He's been slowing down for months, but I keep acting surprised. I carried his water bowl closer to the place where he sleeps and sat with him while he drank. Mother says there's a difference between helping an animal and insisting it perform health for your comfort. We went to the veterinarian after lunch. Nothing sudden, just old joints and another instruction sheet for us to follow. I wrote down his medicine schedule twice, once for the kitchen and once for my diary. He still wanted to sniff every tree on the way from the car to the front door.

------------------------------

ENTRY 157 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — Shabbat With Guests

A couple from the congregation came over for Friday dinner, and their son spent half the evening trying to persuade me that all environmentalists hate people. I asked where he had learned this and he said the radio. Daddy nearly laughed into his soup. Mother made me wait until after the blessing before answering. We argued about old-growth forests and housing, which was a real argument once we stopped announcing what everyone supposedly believed. At the end he asked if he could borrow a book I mentioned. I lent it to him. I don't think that means I won anything. It was a good evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 158 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — Vail Is Too Warm

There wasn't as much snow as we expected. The slopes were crowded where the snowmaking worked and thin where it didn't. Daddy explained how to choose a line without cutting off another skier. Mother sat with a book and told us to come back with all our bones in their customary locations. Later a boy in the lodge announced that climate change was made up by people who didn't ski. I wanted to explain how foolish that sounded, but he wasn't speaking to me and I was tired. I wrote the temperature in the margin of my lift-ticket envelope. It might mean something or it might just mean a warm week.

------------------------------

ENTRY 159 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Piano Bench

Daddy and I played four hands very badly this evening. I can read the notes, but he can hear where a phrase wants to go before I understand what the notes are doing. He stopped in the middle of the piece to show me how a bass line can carry the melody without advertising itself. Then he played a Coltrane record and pointed out the same kind of movement in a completely different setting. I liked the record. I did not like being told I was tapping my foot on the wrong beat. Daddy said there was no wrong beat if I could defend it. I couldn't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 160 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Question About Money

One of the girls at school asked whether our family really owned a yacht. I said Daddy owns a boat and that a person can own a boat without it becoming an article of government. She wanted to know how much it cost. I didn't know, and I wouldn't have told her if I did. When I told Mother, she said the best thing about money was being able to forget about it sometimes. That's easy for us to say. I know that much. I also know I don't want every conversation to turn into a guessing game about what we own.

------------------------------

ENTRY 161 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Rainy Tuesday

I did poorly on a chemistry quiz because I copied a number from the wrong line. I understood the problem. I still got it wrong. After school I spent forty minutes looking for my left glove, which was in the pocket of the coat I was wearing. There are days when I wonder whether intelligence is a practical skill. Daddy was at the piano when I came home. Mother was sorting bills and hummed a song she refused to identify. Nothing important happened after dinner. I want this day recorded precisely because nothing important happened.

------------------------------

ENTRY 162 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Tide Pool

We went out at low tide and found anemones in a rocky pool. A younger child nearby wanted to touch every one. I told her to use one finger very gently and to leave the animals where they were. Her father thanked me, which made me blush. I talked for too long about how they feed. Mother finally asked whether I intended to marry an anemone. I said an anemone would be a reliable listener. She said that wasn't the same as a good husband. The sun came out as we walked back, and suddenly everything smelled warm and salty.

------------------------------

ENTRY 163 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Call From Brooklyn

Grandmother called just before dinner to ask whether I was studying Hebrew properly. I told her yes, which is true if you don't ask how well. We talked about a relative I'd forgotten and then about challah, and then she asked why Mother lets me wear trousers to services. Mother took the phone and changed the subject with such expertise that I should have taken notes. I love Grandmother. I also enjoy the fact that she can't reach through a telephone and rearrange my clothes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 164 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Swiss Footpath

In Switzerland a narrow path climbed above a meadow and passed through a gate that had to be latched behind us. There were cows higher up, and the bells made a sound like someone practicing a slow musical exercise. I liked that we were expected to be responsible enough to close a gate without a warning sign telling us fifty times. Daddy stopped several times, not because he couldn't walk, he insisted, but because the view deserved inspection. I think perhaps he was tired. I slowed down without telling him that I had.

------------------------------

ENTRY 165 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — New Shoes

Mother took me shopping in Paris and bought me shoes I could barely walk in. They are beautiful and entirely unsuited to moving across the earth. I wanted them anyway. I tripped on a seam in the pavement and nearly took Mother down with me. She laughed until she had tears in her eyes. Later, in our hotel room, I tried them on again because I am apparently incapable of learning from experience. I packed my walking shoes for tomorrow. It's possible to want lovely things and still prefer being comfortable.

------------------------------

ENTRY 166 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — High Holy Days

During services I was distracted by the light moving across the wall and then embarrassed because everyone else seemed to be attending better than I was. I asked Mother afterward whether she ever loses her place in a prayer. She said constantly, and that was not a reason to stop. Daddy remembered a passage from memory and then couldn't find the synagogue parking ticket. He looked for it in three pockets before discovering it was in the prayer book. I am definitely his daughter.

------------------------------

ENTRY 167 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Unkind Story

Someone repeated an old story about my parents at a party. She didn't know I was standing just outside the doorway. I knew the facts already. What bothered me was how pleased she sounded to have something to tell. I didn't confront her. I went outside where it was cool and stayed until Mother found me. She asked if I wanted to leave, and I said yes. In the car she didn't tell me to forgive anyone or pretend it hadn't happened. We listened to the radio. That was enough.

------------------------------

ENTRY 168 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Real Storm

The ferry crossing was rougher than usual. A woman nearby was frightened and kept saying she couldn't swim. Daddy spoke to her quietly about the boat and its crew, not making promises about anything he couldn't know. He has a different voice when he's discussing a vessel. I held the arm of the seat and watched spray come over the windows. Later he said fear was useful if it helped you pay attention. It was less useful if it convinced you that shouting could change the weather.

------------------------------

ENTRY 169 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Archive Box

I found a box of Mother's college photographs. There were young people with long hair, protest signs, cups of coffee, and the sort of clothes I would pay money to find now. Mother pointed out the people she remembered and was frank about the ones whose names she couldn't recall. In one picture she looked stubborn and terribly pleased with herself. I asked whether she missed being young. She said she missed having knees that didn't complain on stairs. Then she turned up the music and danced with me in the kitchen.

------------------------------

ENTRY 170 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Clean Walk

I brought a bag on my walk and collected wrappers and fishing line along the shore. The bag filled before I reached the end of the beach. A man thanked me, and another asked why I bothered when people would only litter again. I couldn't think of an elegant answer, so I said because the rubbish was there. Later I untangled a loop of line from driftwood. It took a long time and left my fingers aching. I hope no bird gets caught in it now.

------------------------------

ENTRY 171 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — The Boat's Galley

Daddy let me plan lunch aboard the boat. I chose something too elaborate, forgot one ingredient, and discovered that chopping vegetables while underway requires more care than chopping them at home. He didn't rescue me. He asked where the knife belonged when I wasn't using it. I knew the answer and felt stupid anyway. We ate late and everyone said the food was good. I wrote down what I would do differently next time. Mother says I am the only person she knows who writes a postmortem report about sandwiches.

------------------------------

ENTRY 172 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — A Better Apology

I snapped at a friend who was late meeting me. I was sure she'd forgotten. She'd been helping her little brother who was sick. I apologized, but the first version included a long explanation of why I was annoyed, which isn't much of an apology. I tried again without the explanation. She accepted it. On the way home I remembered something Daddy once said about cross-examination: if you keep talking after the useful question, you'll usually make things worse. He was right about this, too.

------------------------------

ENTRY 173 — 2001-01-01 — Age 15 — December List

I wrote down every bird I can identify by sight without reaching for a book. The list is longer than last year. I still confuse two kinds of gull, and Mother says most adults simply call them seagulls and get on with their lives. Daddy asked what I wanted for the coming year. I said a field notebook that doesn't dissolve in rain and more time outside. He asked if I also wanted a better chemistry grade. I said that would be welcome but less exciting.

------------------------------

ENTRY 174 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The Permit

I studied the driver's handbook carefully and then forgot a simple question during the practice test. Mother says my brain sometimes runs ahead of my body. Daddy says driving is a good activity for bringing them back together. The instructor told me to look farther down the road and not at the front bumper. It turns out this is much harder to remember than it sounds. I'm excited and worried about driving, particularly because everybody seems to have an opinion about how I should do it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 175 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The BMW

There is a BMW in the driveway, and apparently it is mine to drive. I stood looking at it for so long that Daddy asked whether I expected it to introduce itself. It's beautiful, and I love it, and I know exactly what the girls at school will say. Mother insisted we go over insurance, maintenance, and what happens if I take a corner too fast. Daddy wanted me to understand the brakes. I wanted to understand the stereo. We all got what we wanted eventually. I am trying not to look too pleased with myself, but it's difficult.

------------------------------

ENTRY 176 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — First Solo Errand

Mother sent me to get bread and a few groceries. It took three attempts to park neatly, and I left the shopping list on the kitchen counter. I bought everything I remembered, which was not everything. I returned for the eggs. I parked better the second time. The car is lovely, but I find that it does not make me more organized. Daddy says this is a feature of cars generally.

------------------------------

ENTRY 177 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The Lunch Table

A girl who hasn't spoken to me in months asked whether she could come with us on the boat. I said I'd have to ask Daddy and then hated that I'd said even that much. My closest friend gave me a look. After school she told me I don't have to say yes just because someone asks nicely. I know this in theory. In practice I dislike disappointing people. I told the girl the trip was family only. She seemed annoyed and found another table at lunch. That made the situation clear, though not pleasant.

------------------------------

ENTRY 178 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Purim

I helped with the congregation's Purim preparations and got glitter on nearly everything I wore. Someone suggested a costume that would have been funny for approximately three seconds and unkind for much longer. We made a different one. After the reading, the younger children had enough energy to run through walls. I admired them and then went home exhausted. Daddy had played piano at a small gathering earlier and was still humming when he made tea.

------------------------------

ENTRY 179 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — A Fallen Tree

A storm brought down part of a tree near one of our paths. The trunk lay across the trail and smelled wonderful where the wood had split. I wanted to count its rings, but the cut wasn't clean. An older neighbor showed me where the trunk had been hollow, and we talked about fungi. I looked for the same species of mushroom the next afternoon. Nature isn't always kind, but it does nothing without becoming part of something else. I wrote that down and then crossed it out because it sounded like a speech. I still like the thought.

------------------------------

ENTRY 180 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The History Teacher

We talked about the Cold War, and I repeated something Daddy says about Kennedy. My teacher asked where I'd got the idea. When I told him, he invited me to look at several competing accounts. I did. I still don't agree with every interpretation in the textbook, but I can at least distinguish Daddy's opinion from the historical record. At dinner Daddy asked whether I'd gone over to the enemy. Mother laughed so hard she had to put down her fork. Daddy laughed too, eventually.

------------------------------

ENTRY 181 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Two Songs

Mother played an old protest song and Daddy responded by sitting at the piano and playing a complicated jazz arrangement of a tune I thought I knew. They weren't arguing. They were trying to make the other one listen closely. I sat on the stairs with a book in my lap and listened to them. Sometimes I think our house is loud even when nobody raises a voice.

------------------------------

ENTRY 182 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Biology Lab

We dissected a specimen today. I didn't faint or cry, despite predictions from a boy who thinks caring about animals means you can't study them. I paid attention and drew what I saw, then came home and read about the animal's habitat. Mother said the point of learning biology isn't to prove you have a hard heart. I agree. I wish the boy had looked more closely before he started making jokes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 183 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — A First Dance

At a school dance I said yes when a boy asked me to dance, partly because I wanted to and partly because I didn't want to hurt his feelings. The first part turned out to be important. He was funny, and I enjoyed myself. I stepped on his shoe twice. He pretended he hadn't noticed, which was kind and impossible to believe. When he asked if I wanted to see him again, I said perhaps we should actually talk when the music wasn't so loud. He smiled. I have been replaying that smile all evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 184 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The Five Cousins

We saw my cousins at a family gathering. There are five of them and only one of me, which makes every conversation feel like a committee meeting. One asked pointed questions about the trust. I told him I don't administer it and don't intend to debate it over dessert. He rolled his eyes. Later we ended up laughing about a boat mishap from years ago. Families can be irritating and funny within the same twenty minutes. I wish there were less accounting at the table.

------------------------------

ENTRY 185 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Passover at Home

I helped set the table for the Seder and dropped a spoon that made a remarkable noise on the tile. Everything stops sounding dignified when you do that. A younger guest asked whether the story was true in every detail or whether some parts could be read as teaching. Nobody agreed on one answer. I loved that. Daddy said important questions were worth returning to. Mother said we should return to them after everyone had eaten something.

------------------------------

ENTRY 186 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Summer Switzerland

We walked in the hills almost every day. Daddy went shorter distances this year and took more photographs than usual. I asked whether he was tired. He said he was sixty-seven kinds of tired and none of them should concern me. That is not a numerical answer. We sat at a little place above the valley and watched clouds move through the peaks. He told me about sailing in rough water when he was young. I've heard the story before. I asked him to tell it again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 187 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — A Small Quarrel

I disagreed with Mother about a skirt, which is an embarrassingly ordinary reason to spend an afternoon miserable. She thought it was too short for the occasion. I thought she was being old-fashioned. We both got louder than the subject required. Later she asked whether I really liked it or just liked being able to argue about it. I hated how good a question that was. I wore something else and kept the skirt for a different day.

------------------------------

ENTRY 188 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Lake Washington

I was allowed to take the helm for a long stretch while Daddy stood nearby. He corrected me twice for watching the wake instead of where we were going. The boat moved more slowly than I felt it should, and I wanted to hurry every maneuver. He said the lake would still be there if I took another thirty seconds. When we tied up, I made a clumsy first throw with the line and a better second one. Daddy said that's why we practice. He didn't say I was brilliant, and I was grateful.

------------------------------

ENTRY 189 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — A Letter From Jerusalem

An acquaintance sent a photograph from Jerusalem and a note about the street outside the place they were staying. I recognized the stone, the sunlight, and the crowded feeling in the alley even before I read the caption. I spent an hour sorting our photographs from earlier trips and found myself remembering people whose names I never knew: the woman with the oranges, the man repairing a chair, children playing near a wall. Places remain in your body, I think, long after an itinerary is thrown away.

------------------------------

ENTRY 190 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — The Unfair Joke

A boy joked about a girl whose family couldn't afford an expensive school trip. Everyone around him laughed awkwardly. I asked what was funny. He said I couldn't take a joke. I said perhaps not. I didn't shout. I wasn't trying to make a speech. I just couldn't bear the look on the girl's face. We sat together afterward, and she didn't want to talk about what happened, so we talked about a movie instead.

------------------------------

ENTRY 191 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — A Broken Cup

I broke one of Mother's cups while unloading the dishwasher. It was part of a set she'd had longer than I've been alive. I apologized before she saw it. She examined the pieces and said it had survived long enough to be useful. Then she told me about buying the set with money from her first years of work. I hadn't known that. We wrapped the pieces in newspaper. I kept the little painted fragment that had fallen under the table.

------------------------------

ENTRY 192 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — Autumn Wind

Leaves collected in the garden and the first real rain made the road shine. I went walking with a friend and we talked about whether we'd stay near Seattle after graduation. She wants somewhere hot. I can't imagine wanting to live without the smell of wet fir trees, though perhaps I'd be happier if I had fewer socks that smelled like wet fir trees too. Daddy made soup when I came home and asked what we'd decided about our futures. I said we were postponing the vote.

------------------------------

ENTRY 193 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — My Own Opinions

Daddy and I argued about whether people should be allowed to build houses close to certain streams. He made a serious case about property rights, and I tried to make a serious case about the water and the fish. We went back and forth until Mother told us to stop using dinner as a courtroom. Later Daddy slid an article under my bedroom door with a note: You might disagree, but read it. I did. I left him another article in the morning.

------------------------------

ENTRY 194 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — November Appointment

Daddy had an appointment that made Mother anxious. They didn't explain much, and I told myself not to invent frightening possibilities. He came home, sat at the piano, and played for a while. I listened from the doorway. At dinner he teased me about the chemistry quiz. I got the grade right this time. I hate how easily I can make myself worried when adults are quiet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 195 — 2002-01-01 — Age 16 — End of a Long Year

I can drive, I can handle certain lines on the boat without being told twice, and I can identify more birds than any member of my family wants to hear about. I have also lost two scarves, failed to remember a birthday until late in the day, and spilled tea on my notes. This seems like a representative year. Next year I want to spend more time in the field and less time worrying whether I look foolish. I suspect that second goal will take longer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 196 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — Junior Year, Somehow

I am seventeen, and people have begun asking me what university I intend to attend as though the answer will reveal the whole future. UW seems obvious in some ways and frightening in others. Daddy speaks fondly about Harvard, not because he insists I go there but because the place mattered to him. Mother says college is where I should find interesting questions, not a socially acceptable answer to the question of what I'll become. I took that advice seriously until someone asked me the same thing again at lunch.

------------------------------

ENTRY 197 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Wet Notebook

I took my field notebook onto the beach and neglected to close the bag properly. Half the pages got damp. Ink ran across a drawing of a shorebird until the bird looked like a ghost. I was angry for ten minutes and then tried to rescue what I could. I recopied the observations but saved the ruined drawing too. Sometimes the accident is part of the record. Mother says I was born to make an archive of disasters that aren't disasters.

------------------------------

ENTRY 198 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Tour of the Office

Daddy took me to his office for part of an afternoon. There were fewer people than I remembered from childhood, and some of the older names weren't on the doors anymore. The receptionist remembered me, which was nice. A young attorney talked earnestly about a deal she was working on, and I realized there were parts of this profession I could genuinely enjoy. I also realized I did not want to live in that building. Daddy didn't ask me to make a choice. We had lunch and talked about music instead.

------------------------------

ENTRY 199 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Volunteer Morning

Our group spent Saturday removing invasive plants from a damp area beside a trail. At first it felt as though we were making no progress. By noon there was a visible cleared patch and an enormous pile of what we'd pulled. A little boy who came with his mother asked whether the plants were bad. I said plants aren't wicked, but some spread where they don't belong and make life difficult for others. He thought about that and asked whether people were the same. I told him that was a harder question.

------------------------------

ENTRY 200 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — Someone Likes Me

A boy waited for me after class and asked whether I wanted to get coffee over the weekend. I said yes and immediately began wondering whether my answer sounded too eager. We talked for an hour about school, his younger sister, and music. He asked what kind of car I drove. I told him. It might have been an ordinary question. I wish I could let ordinary questions remain ordinary for five minutes before I start taking them apart.

------------------------------

ENTRY 201 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Practice Room

I stayed late to practice piano and spent half an hour on a passage that should not have defeated me. Daddy listened when I played it at home. He didn't correct the notes. He asked what I was trying to say with the phrase. I told him I was trying not to make a mistake. He said that wasn't music. I was annoyed because he was right. We tried it again, slower. It sounded like something this time.

------------------------------

ENTRY 202 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — An Invitation

We went to a party in Seattle where everyone seemed to know what everyone else's parents did for a living. I have learned to smile when people say they knew Daddy in the old days. Sometimes they mean it kindly and sometimes they're fishing for a story. One woman asked about his former firm in a tone that made me feel she was testing an old bruise. I answered with the weather. Mother caught my eye from across the room and gave the smallest approving nod.

------------------------------

ENTRY 203 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Map of Streams

I traced a watershed using a map and then tried to follow part of it on foot. It's one thing to draw lines on paper and another to see how a ditch, a culvert, and a steep bank can change where water goes. I went home with muddy shoes and a list of questions about runoff. Daddy said I was beginning to sound like an expert witness. I told him I was beginning to sound like a girl who needed cleaner socks.

------------------------------

ENTRY 204 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Long Seder

We had more guests than usual, and the evening stretched on until the youngest child had fallen asleep against his mother's shoulder. I read part of the Haggadah and stumbled on a word I should have known. Grandmother would have corrected me immediately. Mother waited until afterward and showed me. I don't mind correction nearly as much when it isn't delivered as a public performance. Daddy enjoyed the discussion so much that he forgot the time. He was still arguing gently with a guest while we cleared the plates.

------------------------------

ENTRY 205 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Doctor's Orders

Daddy came home with instructions about what he should eat and how much he should exert himself. He complained about the language on the sheet, as though grammar were the medical emergency. Mother placed it on the refrigerator. I asked whether he was frightened. He said growing older was not a surprise, though some of its details were objectionable. I wanted him to say he would be fine. He didn't make a promise he couldn't keep. We went for a short walk together afterward.

------------------------------

ENTRY 206 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Interview

I had a conversation with someone about university applications and discovered I was much more comfortable talking about the work I did outdoors than listing achievements. When asked why law interested me, I talked about Daddy and then about how laws affect land and water. The interviewer said those might not be such separate interests. I hadn't thought of them that way. On the ferry back I wrote three pages of questions, then nearly left the notebook on the seat.

------------------------------

ENTRY 207 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — Summer Among Mountains

We returned to Switzerland. Daddy preferred places where he could sit and listen to the world go by. Mother and I took a longer walk and returned with photographs, one damp sock, and a story about a goat that tried to eat the corner of my map. Daddy asked whether the goat had expressed an opinion about the route. I told him it wanted us to go wherever the best paper was. He laughed until he coughed. I didn't like the cough but didn't mention it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 208 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Language of Boats

Daddy went over navigation with me again, although I've heard some of it since I was small. He wanted me to know why a procedure exists, not merely remember the rule. We discussed weather and charts and how easy it is to be overconfident when the water looks friendly. I asked him if he was this fussy with his crews in the Navy. He said he was worse when it mattered. He did not tell me another war story. Sometimes he chooses silence, and I am learning to respect it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 209 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — An Argument and Tea

Mother and I argued over whether I should go to a party where I barely knew the host. She thought the situation sounded unwise. I thought she was treating me like a child. We went in circles. Finally she asked what I would tell a friend in the same circumstances. I said I'd ask who else was going and how she'd get home. Mother raised an eyebrow. I asked those questions and decided not to go. I dislike when my mother wins by making me answer myself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 210 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Bird in the Window

A bird struck the glass and fell onto the porch. For one awful moment I thought it was dead. I kept the dog away and watched from inside so I wouldn't frighten it further. After a while it moved, stood uncertainly, and then flew to the nearest branch. I was so relieved that I cried, which embarrassed me because nobody else was in the room. Mother came out and found me wiping my eyes. She hugged me and didn't make a joke.

------------------------------

ENTRY 211 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The First Rosh Hashanah Alone in the Choir

I helped with a small music contribution at the synagogue and was nervous enough to drop my folder before the service. A woman beside me gathered the pages and passed them back without comment. The music went well, or at least I remember being able to breathe by the end. Daddy said afterward that I listened to the other singers instead of pushing my own voice forward. That was the nicest compliment I could have received.

------------------------------

ENTRY 212 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A Question for Daddy

I asked Daddy whether he would be disappointed if I studied environmental science instead of law. He said he would be disappointed if I made an expensive decision out of politeness. Then he asked what I wanted to learn. We talked about ecosystems and environmental regulation until Mother shouted that dinner was getting cold. It wasn't a final decision, but it was a relief to have the conversation. I suspect I'll change my mind six more times before university.

------------------------------

ENTRY 213 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — The Wrong Impression

A girl told me I'd been unfriendly at a party. I hadn't meant to be. I'd been tired and distracted and spent most of the evening talking with someone about a rescue dog. I apologized for seeming dismissive. She said she'd assumed I thought I was better than everyone. That stung. I know how it looks when I arrive in a car someone else can't afford. I can't undo the fact of the car. I can try harder to notice the people in front of me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 214 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — A November Walk

Daddy and I walked a short path near home. He stopped to rest twice. The second time we sat on a damp bench until the light began to fade. He told me about working piano bars when he was a student, how he could tell a room was listening before anyone applauded. I asked him whether he missed playing for strangers. He said he still played for strangers now and then, and that I was a particularly demanding audience. I leaned against his shoulder. We stayed until Mother called to ask where we'd disappeared.

------------------------------

ENTRY 215 — 2003-01-01 — Age 17 — Applications on the Table

University forms have taken over the dining table. I am filling them out with the thoroughness of someone who fears discovering one overlooked box after the deadline. Mother brought tea and told me that being admitted to a university is not a verdict on my worth. I told her she was interrupting my panic with excellent advice. Daddy looked over an essay and crossed out three unnecessary sentences. He said the rest sounded like me. I'm sending it.
