Type: Journal

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Fictional first-person diary. Entries are imagined from established canon; new interpersonal details remain provisional.

ENTRY 526 — 2012-01-04 — Age 26 — The ink froze in my pocket

I went to the shoreline before sunrise to check the stakes from our winter survey. The water had the peculiar pewter color it gets when the clouds seem to be sitting directly on the Sound. My pen refused to write until I held it under my arm. I had remembered spare batteries, measuring tape, sample bags, and a laminated checklist, but not that ink dislikes freezing. A heron lifted out of the reeds before I got within fifty yards. It was the best thing about the morning. I came home, hung my damp coat over a chair Mother says isn't a coat rack, and copied the measurements while I could still read them. The little mistakes are worth recording because they are the ones I repeat.

ENTRY 527 — 2012-01-11 — Age 26 — A perfectly ordinary Tuesday

Mother wanted to go into Seattle for an appointment and insisted she could manage the ferry by herself. She probably could. We went together anyway, then stopped for soup and spent forty minutes arguing about whether the restaurant had changed its recipe or whether we had changed our tastes. She remembers taking the ferry when the passenger waiting areas were quite different. I remember the particular bench where Dad taught me to recognize approaching weather. Neither memory is particularly useful, but both kept us talking until we reached home. I made a list of things to buy, promptly left it in the kitchen, and had to reconstruct it at the store. We were out of dish soap, not olive oil. Naturally I bought olive oil.

ENTRY 528 — 2012-01-20 — Age 26 — What he remembers

The man I've been seeing remembered that I dislike receiving flowers cut from a garden in the middle of winter. He brought a little potted fern instead. I wasn't sure what to do with the expression on my face. A fern is not a pledge of eternal love. It is a fern. But I know it required attention, which can feel rarer than extravagance. We ate dinner without discussing money, a refreshing experience for both of us. Afterward he asked whether I would ever consider living somewhere besides Bainbridge. I said of course, but I would miss knowing which birds came back every spring. He said he thought he could learn them. I wanted to believe him, and perhaps that is all right for tonight.

ENTRY 529 — 2012-01-29 — Age 26 — The piano tuner

The tuner arrived late because of a ferry delay and apologized three times. I told him he wasn't responsible for the sea. He said that was the nicest absolution he'd received all week. He spent an hour bringing the old piano back into something resembling obedience. When he finished, I tried a passage from one of Dad's handwritten arrangements and discovered a note I'd been blaming on the instrument was simply me. The tuner gave me a useful exercise and refused to be impressed by my father's handwriting until I'd played the chord cleanly. I liked him immensely for that. Mother asked me to play after supper. She sat in her chair with her eyes closed, but she didn't look sad.

ENTRY 530 — 2012-02-07 — Age 26 — Four different names for a creek

Our restoration volunteers use a place-name that doesn't match the county records, the map, or the name on a historical photograph. We spent the morning trying to untangle it and discovered that every person was describing the same short watercourse. I made a cross-reference in the field binder. I wish every misunderstanding were so easily resolved. At lunch someone asked why I insisted on preserving old names when they were inconvenient. Because inconvenient records are still records, I said. We ought to know when we're rewriting something. I had brought two gloves for the same hand. Nobody mentioned it until we started packing up.

ENTRY 531 — 2012-02-15 — Age 26 — A discussion about permission

A new volunteer wanted to photograph nesting sites for an online page. He meant well, and his photographs are lovely, but advertising a sensitive location can put the animals at risk. I explained and he was embarrassed. I was careful not to make him feel foolish. If people are punished for asking questions, they stop asking. Later I wondered whether I'd been too gentle. Perhaps people mistake a gentle tone for uncertainty. Then he sent me a message with revised captions and the locations removed. That seemed answer enough. On the ferry home I saw two children pressing their noses against the window, delighted by gulls I've stopped noticing. I tried to notice them again.

ENTRY 532 — 2012-02-23 — Age 26 — The wrong ferry lane

I was thinking about an argument in a book and followed the car ahead of me into the wrong queue. The attendant waved me back with the weary patience of a man who has been rescuing distracted drivers since breakfast. I thanked him and wished fervently to evaporate. Nothing terrible happened. I still arrived in time for the meeting. During introductions I managed to call one person by the name of someone I hadn't seen in four years. I had charts and data and perfectly usable ideas, but apparently no command of my own mouth. I returned home with muddy boots and a headache from laughing at myself. Mother said it was good to hear me laugh rather than apologize for existing.

ENTRY 533 — 2012-03-03 — Age 26 — The hinge on the greenhouse

The little greenhouse at the education plot has a door that drags along its frame. Several of us kept adjusting the latch, which wasn't the problem. An older volunteer took one look, fetched a wrench, and showed us how the upper hinge had shifted. Ten minutes later it swung freely. I wrote down what he'd done because it was something I ought to have seen. He told me his granddaughter studies engineering and asked whether I knew anyone who could help her find summer fieldwork. I do, as it happens. We exchanged numbers. I went to bed grateful for people who can fix things without giving a lecture about how obvious the solution was.

ENTRY 534 — 2012-03-12 — Age 26 — A conversation I'd been postponing

He asked what would happen if we became serious. I asked what he meant by serious, which was either a reasonable question or an evasion. He talked about sharing a home, perhaps someday children, and being part of each other's decisions. I told him that legal paperwork would be part of any marriage I entered. He looked surprised and then said he could understand that. I was relieved. It would have been so easy to turn his surprise into a verdict, but surprise isn't guilt. We went for a walk afterward, and I stumbled on a root I'd warned him about not thirty seconds earlier. He took my elbow and did not tease me until I was steady.

ENTRY 535 — 2012-03-26 — Age 26 — Pesach preparations

Mother supervised the kitchen as though it were the opening night of a complicated theatrical production. I have inherited some of her insistence on order but not her ability to put three instructions into the air at once and have everyone understand them. We checked the dishes, the shopping, the seating, and which cousin could come on which evening. She wanted the old Haggadot brought down rather than the newer copies. A page in Dad's book has a wine stain and a penciled comment that made Mother laugh before she would explain it. I wonder how many private jokes survive in the margins of ordinary objects. I hope I can learn them while she is here to tell me.

ENTRY 536 — 2012-04-07 — Age 26 — The questions at the table

A little boy asked why adults tell the same story every year when everyone already knows the ending. His father started explaining ritual, but I said I wondered if the people at the table might be different each year even when the story remains the same. I don't know whether that helped. He said the ending still seemed repetitive and asked for more parsley. Sensible child. Mother was in splendid form, telling a story about her own grandmother in Brooklyn, and a cousin gently corrected a date. Mother corrected his correction. They both laughed. After everyone left we stood in the quiet kitchen, looking at the chairs. I washed and she dried until she announced that we were tired enough to stop.

ENTRY 537 — 2012-04-19 — Age 26 — A small kindness without a receipt

Someone left a bag of used field guides outside our office with a note that said they hoped they'd be useful. No name, no institution, no request for a plaque on a wall. I spent the afternoon stamping the organization's name inside each book and distributing them among the volunteers. One was a little water-damaged, and the pages stuck together near the frogs. I took it home to repair. It made me think of how much energy wealthy people can spend arranging recognition for gifts. I give money myself; I know the machinery. There is something restful about a useful object arriving with no ceremony whatsoever.

ENTRY 538 — 2012-04-30 — Age 26 — A salmon is not an abstraction

We visited a stream where restoration work has changed the pattern of shade and bank erosion. The measurements were encouraging, but I don't want to confuse a favorable chart with a finished job. A resident told us where he used to see fish when he was young. His recollection wasn't laboratory evidence, but it was a lead worth investigating. I wrote down his words and asked permission to call him again. Later I stood beside the water and listened until everyone else had moved up the path. I can explain what shade does to water temperature; I still feel wonder when something living flicks through the current.

------------------------------

ENTRY 539 — 2012-05-08 — Age 26 — The matter of a name

At a planning meeting I was introduced as a major donor before I was introduced as a field volunteer. It changes the temperature of a room. People become careful about disagreeing with me, which is exactly when disagreement is most useful. I asked the coordinator to use my role on the project next time. He apologized. I told him it wasn't an offense; it was a request. Later one woman challenged a number on my slide and turned out to be right. I thanked her publicly. I hope she believed the thanks. When people have learned to expect money to settle arguments, ordinary corrections start to feel strangely brave.

------------------------------

ENTRY 540 — 2012-05-19 — Age 26 — The new binoculars

I bought binoculars after trying a friend's pair and discovering that mine were making everything look vaguely seasick. Mother said I was overdue to replace them. They aren't glamorous, but I spent a ludicrously happy hour using them from the porch. A flicker worked a tree near the garden fence. I could see the pattern of its feathers and the way it braced its body before striking the bark. It reminded me of being small and staring out these same windows with Dad explaining things I didn't yet understand. I called Mother over to look. She said she saw the bird just fine without the binoculars, then took them and refused to give them back for twenty minutes.

ENTRY 541 — 2012-05-30 — Age 26 — The two versions of a meeting

Someone later described our meeting as 'unanimous,' though I had specifically asked for a reservation to be recorded. It wasn't a battle. I simply wrote a courteous email attaching the notes and asking that the correction be entered. The reply suggested it was a minor technicality. I sent the exact language again, without decoration. The correction appeared the next morning. I don't know why factual carelessness makes me colder than open disagreement, but it does. I can disagree pleasantly for hours. Pretending no disagreement occurred feels like stealing a small piece of someone's voice. I went walking afterward to let the sharpness leave my body.

ENTRY 542 — 2012-06-10 — Age 26 — A harbor full of memories

We took the boat out in decent weather. I worked the lines and checked the chart as automatically as I once checked homework. Dad taught me to look at what the water was actually doing instead of what I expected it to do. A guest asked whether I planned to keep the boat forever. I said I didn't know. I want to make decisions about possessions as possessions, and about memories as memories, but they get tangled. At lunch someone played a bit of jazz from a phone speaker, and I found myself explaining why Dad had loved Ella's phrasing. I was alarmed by how much I sounded like him and then pleased by it.

ENTRY 543 — 2012-06-21 — Age 26 — Solstice, not a ceremony

I walked after supper while the sky remained stubbornly bright. A neighbor was trying to persuade a dog to come home, and the dog was engaged in a serious study of a shrub. I helped corner the animal with kindness and a biscuit. The neighbor thanked me and said I must be good with dogs. I am good with some dogs and some people; the difficulty is that neither group is reliably consistent. I returned to find a voicemail from him asking whether I wanted to spend a weekend away later in the summer. I called back and said yes. We didn't have to invent a grand occasion. An ordinary weekend sounded just right.

ENTRY 544 — 2012-07-03 — Age 26 — The cabin and the wrong socks

We spent two nights in a little rented cabin near a trailhead. It smelled of cedar and old heat. On the first morning I pulled on socks from two different pairs; one was much thicker, and I discovered my error three miles into a walk. My heel complained the rest of the day. He carried an extra bandage and didn't make a story of rescuing me. We saw a small waterfall whose name isn't printed on the tourist maps. I sat on a rock and ate an apple. He asked whether this was what I would do with every holiday if left to myself. Almost certainly, I said. He seemed happy with the answer.

ENTRY 545 — 2012-07-15 — Age 26 — An uneven conversation

At a dinner with friends he talked about how difficult it must be to administer my trust and whether the assets could be 'put to work' more productively. The questions weren't inappropriate in themselves. What troubled me was how confidently he assumed he should be involved. I said the trustee and advisers were competent, and that I preferred to keep our relationship separate from investment decisions. He laughed a little too quickly and changed the subject. I have written the exchange down without deciding what it means. I know how easy it is for me to become watchful. I also know I have regretted ignoring small things because I wanted to believe the larger story.

ENTRY 546 — 2012-07-27 — Age 26 — A July inventory

I have been collecting notes about our habitat work in three different books. This afternoon I reconciled them and made a proper index. There is a certain pleasure in knowing where everything belongs, though I suspect the universe doesn't care for my filing system. I also sorted photographs from our travels and found Mother smiling into the wind on a boat years ago. She was wearing an absurd scarf and looked wonderfully alive. I printed the picture and put it on the refrigerator. She asked why I'd chosen that one rather than a proper portrait. Because it looks like you, I told her. She called me sentimental, but left it there.

------------------------------

ENTRY 547 — 2012-08-08 — Age 26 — Switzerland in August

We arrived in Switzerland after a tiresome day of planes and a very pleasant train ride. Mother knew precisely what she wanted for supper and claimed she wasn't tired until she fell asleep in her chair with a book open. I walked outside and listened to cowbells across the valley. I have heard the sound since childhood, but this evening I counted three separate rhythms and wondered which animals wore which bells. I caught myself looking for a pen before remembering I was on holiday. Of course I still wrote it down. I haven't lost my capacity for delight; I've merely developed an inconvenient need to annotate it.

ENTRY 548 — 2012-08-17 — Age 26 — Rain on the mountain

We turned back from a higher trail when the weather changed. The clouds lowered astonishingly fast, the rock became slick, and the useful choice was to descend. A couple following us seemed disappointed. I was disappointed too, but I don't regard turning around as a moral defeat. Mother once told me Dad could be stubborn about business and wonderfully conservative about weather at sea. Perhaps she meant that competence includes knowing when not to prove anything. We returned damp, drank hot chocolate, and spent an hour identifying flowers from photographs. I will remember the walk, even though we didn't reach the point someone had circled on a map.

ENTRY 549 — 2012-08-29 — Age 26 — What Mother doesn't need

I suggested that we hire someone to help Mother with more of the housework. She said we already did and asked whether I intended to employ an audience for her breakfast. I laughed, and then we talked seriously. She wants assistance with heavy things and transport on days she doesn't feel steady. She does not want decisions taken out of her hands. I wrote that down because I know I have a habit of solving the problem I can measure while missing the one a person actually feels. She added that she would like more time with me, not more appointments. We planned to have dinner together next week without anybody else invited.

ENTRY 550 — 2012-09-09 — Age 26 — A birthday without a speech

I turned twenty-six today. Mother made a cake although there was no practical reason for her to do so. The frosting leaned alarmingly toward one side; I admired it sincerely. We had a few friends to dinner, ate too much, and stayed at the table until people began telling stories they'd told before. I like repeated stories. The details shift, and the corrections are half the pleasure. He gave me a book with a note inside. It was thoughtful, and I wanted to be unreservedly happy. I was mostly happy. I am recording the small shadow because I don't want to pretend my moods are simpler than they are.

ENTRY 551 — 2012-09-18 — Age 26 — A point of principle

Someone at work suggested using prettier photographs in a report because the actual restoration site still looked messy. I said no. The whole point is to document conditions, not advertise a fantasy of completed perfection. We can explain the mess. It is honest evidence of work in progress. He said I was being rigid. Perhaps. But if we teach supporters to trust glossy pictures, they'll be disappointed by reality and suspicious of the next honest report. I brought in tea the following morning and we talked it through without rancor. He had a good idea about using paired photographs with captions. I was glad neither of us had to win.

ENTRY 552 — 2012-09-27 — Age 26 — High Holy Days

During services I found myself remembering Dad's hand resting on the back of the pew before we sat. It's odd what the body remembers about people who are gone. Mother noticed I was distracted but didn't press. On the walk afterward, she talked about coming west as a young woman, the music she heard, and friends whose surnames I've never managed to remember. She said she'd once believed leaving Brooklyn would settle every question about who she was. Instead it had given her better questions. I liked that answer. I asked her to write down those friends' names. She said she'd do it when she found her old address book.

ENTRY 553 — 2012-10-06 — Age 26 — The conversation in the parking lot

We had been having a pleasant evening until he asked again whether a future husband would have any say over distributions from my trust. I told him that was a question for a marriage discussion with counsel, not something to settle beside his car. He said I was making him feel like a suspect. I said I wasn't accusing him of anything; I was stating a boundary. His expression tightened. He went home irritated, and I drove back on my own. I can be too wary. I know that. But a person who loves me should be able to leave an investment question unanswered for one night. I will talk to him when we're both rested.

ENTRY 554 — 2012-10-14 — Age 26 — An apology with a condition

He apologized for how he spoke the other night, and I apologized for the clipped tone I used when I felt cornered. We walked along the shore. For a while everything was easy. Then he said he'd feel better if I would promise not to 'hide' financial information from him when we were married. I said marriage hadn't been proposed and privacy wasn't hiding. I watched his face. I wanted very badly for us to be discussing something else, perhaps a book or a dog or the weather. We did, eventually. It was a nice walk with a difficult center. Both things are true. I will not erase the nice part because the difficult part may matter.

ENTRY 555 — 2012-10-22 — Age 26 — The committee's accounts

Today I caught an error in a spreadsheet before it was distributed. Two expense categories had been counted twice, and the total overstated our actual costs. Nobody was cheating; an import had been run twice. I showed the treasurer, helped correct it, and wrote a short explanation. It's satisfying when suspicion resolves into an ordinary fix. I wish relationships were as easy to audit. Mother asked why I looked so tired at dinner. I told her I'd been thinking too much. She said, 'You do that professionally now.' I laughed, and she pushed a bowl of soup toward me. For twenty minutes I stopped thinking entirely.

------------------------------

ENTRY 556 — 2012-11-01 — Age 26 — The end of an argument

He arrived unexpectedly and wanted to settle the money question once and for all. There isn't such a settlement. I told him I would not give him a role in administering the trust as the price of continuing to see me. He said I never really trusted him. I said I had tried to, and that his insistence was making the effort harder. He raised his voice. Mine went very quiet. I was surprised by how clear my own thoughts became. I asked him to leave. He did, after saying something I won't dignify by copying. I stood in the hall for a long time afterward. The little fern he'd given me in January was sitting on the windowsill, entirely innocent of human affairs.

------------------------------

ENTRY 557 — 2012-11-08 — Age 26 — The fern

I watered the fern. That seems like a small ridiculous entry, but I had wondered whether to throw it away. The plant did nothing wrong. I did not throw it away. I have felt sad in waves, and in between I have felt almost offensively ordinary. Yesterday I ordered office supplies. Today I checked a volunteer schedule. Someone phoned to ask whether I would like company, and I said yes. She came over with soup and sat at the table without requiring an explanation. I love people who can offer friendship without making a case for why they deserve it. I am not angry every moment. I am simply finished with him.

ENTRY 558 — 2012-11-19 — Age 26 — A word I don't like

A friend described me as 'intimidating' when I am angry. I asked whether she meant that I threatened people. She said no, that I became so precise she couldn't imagine interrupting. I didn't know whether to be relieved. I dislike frightening anyone. I also know that when someone tries to force me into a decision, I become cold in a way I cannot imitate when merely rehearsing an argument. We talked longer. She said I was not intimidating when I listened, which I suppose is most of the time. I told her I hoped so. I made tea and nearly dropped the cup. That returned us to the Gwen everybody recognizes.

ENTRY 559 — 2012-11-29 — Age 26 — An afternoon with Mother

Mother wanted to look at photographs from her college years. There was a picture of her in a coat with enormous buttons, standing beside two women whose hair looked like a declaration of independence. She told me who they were and how much trouble they'd managed to find without trying. I asked whether she missed being young. She said she missed being able to walk uphill without thinking about her knees, but not being unsure of herself. We talked about Dad too. I asked whether she'd ever wished she'd chosen an easier life. She answered, 'Easier than what?' and made me explain my terms before she'd discuss the question.

ENTRY 560 — 2012-12-07 — Age 26 — The winter counts

Three people arrived late for a bird count, two forgot gloves, and one brought the wrong map. Somehow we still had a very good morning. A tiny bird landed so close to my boot that I stopped breathing for a moment. The newcomer beside me looked startled when I whispered its name. I showed her how to use the field guide without pretending the book was more exciting than the bird. She asked whether I grew up doing this. Yes, I said. I grew up doing lots of things, but this was one of my favorites. We finished cold and cheerful. I came home and found the keys I'd lost yesterday inside a cookbook. I still have no explanation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 561 — 2012-12-16 — Age 26 — A disagreement that stayed pleasant

A cousin and I disagreed about charitable giving. He thinks large gifts should come with clear influence over the recipient's operations. I think influence should be defined carefully, especially when the organization is supposed to serve a community rather than its largest donors. He had thoughtful arguments, and I granted several. He admitted that he had seen donors behave badly. We ended up talking about our grandmother and how little either of us really understood the old trust arrangements when we were young. It felt good to be able to disagree without examining each other's motives. I want more conversations like that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 562 — 2012-12-24 — Age 26 — The house still has music

Mother asked me to play, so I played. I made two conspicuous mistakes and recovered from one. She applauded the recovery. Later she put on an Ella recording and reminded me that Dad could sit through a mediocre performance for an excellent phrase. I tried listening for the phrase rather than waiting for the performance to be over. There were moments of genuine beauty. I have been impatient lately. Perhaps I should listen more generously, without pretending not to hear the wrong notes. The house felt full tonight, though it was only the two of us and a few friends coming and going.

ENTRY 563 — 2012-12-31 — Age 26 — A full year, not a verdict

It would be easy to label 2012 the year another relationship ended. That would be a poor description of twelve months. I saw birds I had never seen before, helped fix a greenhouse door, learned to listen more carefully to Mother, traveled, worked, laughed, made mistakes, and took several cold walks that left me happy for hours. I also allowed myself to hope about a man and discovered something I couldn't accept. I won't pretend that didn't hurt. But I did not miss the whole year while trying to make one part of it work. At midnight I will put away this book, make a fresh pot of tea, and be twenty-six with no particular plan to become somebody else.
