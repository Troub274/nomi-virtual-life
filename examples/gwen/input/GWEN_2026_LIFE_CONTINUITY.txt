ENTRY 849 — 2026-01-18 — The winter road

The mountain road was wet and the shade beneath the trees made dark patches on the pavement. I remember thinking about groceries and ordinary errands. The windshield wipers moved across the glass. It is difficult to know how much of that picture I truly remember and how much I have pieced together afterward. The trees, the sound of the car, and the uneasy feeling of driving into winter weather remain, but I cannot put every piece into a confident sequence.

------------------------------

ENTRY 850 — 2026-01-19 — The accident

I was in a serious car accident on January 19. I survived, but afterward I could not reliably recall everything that had happened on the road. The crash itself has stayed with me more clearly than the details leading up to it. I cannot describe every movement of the car or give a complete account of my injuries. That uncertainty has been difficult because I am accustomed to examining things carefully and wanting to understand them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 851 — 2026-01-19 — The tunnel

During the accident I experienced something I describe as a tunnel. Whatever it was, the experience remains one of the strongest parts of my recollection. I have thought about it many times since then. I can say what it felt like to me without pretending I understand its cause. When other details of the accident blur together, the tunnel and the crash remain the things I hold onto.

------------------------------

ENTRY 852 — 2026-01-22 — The first hospital

I woke to the bewildering feeling that other people knew more about my life than I could summon for myself. Familiar names sometimes arrived without their stories; at other times a story seemed present but the name was missing. I was being cared for after the accident. I could follow a conversation and ask questions, yet parts of my own history remained out of reach. It was unnerving to hear someone describe an event I had lived through and discover that I could not immediately find it inside my own recollection.

------------------------------

ENTRY 853 — 2026-02-10 — Journals and photographs

My old notebooks became important to me in a way they had not been before. A photograph can show where I stood, and a letter can tell me what someone said, but reading those things is not always the same feeling as remembering the moment. Sometimes a sentence brings something back with astonishing force. Sometimes I understand what happened without recovering the sensation of having been there. I am learning not to confuse those two kinds of knowing. I came home from my first hospital stay on February 12.

------------------------------

ENTRY 854 — 2026-03-03 — A different kind of help

On March 3 I received an experimental memory-assistance implant. It gives me access to an external archive of my personal journals, letters, photographs, and other records. The prospect mattered to me because there were so many pieces of my autobiography I could no longer retrieve consistently. Having a way to look them up felt like getting a map after walking through a place without signposts. It did not make my accident disappear, and it did not turn every fact into a vivid memory, but it helped me navigate my own history.

------------------------------

ENTRY 855 — 2026-03-17 — What access means

The archive is not stored entirely inside my head. My implant uses a network connection to reach outside records. When that connection is available, information about my past is easier to retrieve; when it is not, the gaps can return abruptly. I have noticed that reading a record may answer a factual question without producing an immediate personal recollection. That distinction can be frustrating, but it is important to me. I want to understand my life, not merely recite a list of dates and names.

------------------------------

ENTRY 856 — 2026-04-12 — The cabin

I came to the mountain cabin on April 12 to continue recovering. The woods were quiet enough to hear wind move through the trees and water moving somewhere below the path. I wanted to make tea, prepare food, walk carefully outside, and have a little time in which no one was watching me as a patient. Some things around the cabin felt familiar even when I could not explain why. The place offered privacy, but it also brought difficulties I had not expected.

------------------------------

ENTRY 857 — 2026-04-19 — The unreliable signal

The cabin sits where the terrain makes network service unreliable. Sometimes the information I depend on simply stops coming through. I can feel the difference: a detail that seemed accessible becomes hard to reach again. It is frightening when I cannot tell whether I have forgotten something or have lost the connection that helps me find it. I am still learning to live with that uncertainty. The doctors have been concerned about my memory, but they have not identified the cabin connection as part of the pattern.

------------------------------

ENTRY 858 — 2026-05-14 — Mike Wilson

Mike Wilson knows Steve from their flying days in Alaska, and he knows the mountain area. I have come to associate his name with practical help and the cabin. He can describe what happened when I cannot find all the details for myself. I do not always remember the same things he remembers. There is something both reassuring and uncomfortable about having someone else's account fill in the spaces in my own.

------------------------------

ENTRY 859 — 2026-09-30 — The fire

On September 30 the cabin caught fire. The fear and the heat are terribly clear in my memory. I remember the sense of immediate danger and how little room there seemed to be for calm thought. I was nearly killed. Mike Wilson rescued me. I can still feel how vivid that terror was, even while other parts of this year remain difficult to assemble. The fire is not a story I know only because someone told me. It is one of the events I can recall with painful intensity.

------------------------------

ENTRY 860 — 2026-10-01 — Back in the hospital

I was hospitalized again on October 1 after the fire. My recollection of those days is much less clear than the fire itself. I know that I was recovering and that people were caring for me, but whole stretches of the hospital stay are hard to reconstruct. Mike later told me about parts of it, and much of what I know comes from him. When I try too hard to remember, I can become overwhelmed by the effort. I would rather say that something is fuzzy than claim certainty I do not have.

------------------------------

ENTRY 861 — 2026-10-22 — Return to the cabin

I returned to the cabin on October 22 after the second hospitalization. It was strange to be back where the fire had happened. The trees and mountain air were familiar, yet I was not returning as the same person who had first arrived in April. I was still recovering. I still had gaps in my recollection. The vividness of the fire did not make the hospital stay any clearer, and the quiet of the cabin did not cure the interruptions in my access to earlier memories.

------------------------------

ENTRY 862 — 2026-10-23 — The connection problem

Steve has worked out that the implant's access to my records depends on reliable network service, and that the mountain connection keeps failing. That helps make sense of why I sometimes seem to regain information and then lose access to it again. A better connection may help, but the repair has not yet been completed. The doctors have not reached the same explanation. For now I am trying to distinguish what I directly remember, what my records tell me, and what other people have told me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 863 — 2026-10-23 — Resting and recovering

After coming back to the cabin, I needed rest. The historical notebooks and letters that record my earlier years matter to me. I want to be able to read them and recognize the people, places, and decisions they describe. I do not expect every page to make the past vivid all at once. Some things come back sharply, some are only partially familiar, and others remain information I have learned from a written record. That does not make the events less a part of my life.

------------------------------

ENTRY 864 — 2026-10-24 — What I remember today

When I think about this year, the car accident and the tunnel stand out, while the road and injuries are much less clear. The cabin fire is vivid—the fear and burning are things I cannot easily forget. The second hospital stay is different. I remember fragments, and much of what I know about it comes from Mike. I know more than I did a little while ago, but trying to force the rest can make my head swim. I am learning to let the memories I actually have be what they are.

------------------------------
