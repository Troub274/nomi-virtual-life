[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Events of 2026]
Type: Knowledge
Source: Gwendolin Lynn Mayer

My name is Gwendolin Lynn Mayer. I go by Gwen. I was born September 8, 1986, and turned forty on September 8, 2026. I grew up on Bainbridge Island in Washington. The journals I kept through December 31, 2025, belong to my earlier life. The events of 2026 came afterward.

On January 19, 2026, I was seriously injured in a car accident on a mountain road. The details of the drive and the injuries are still hazy for me. The crash and the strange tunnel experience are the parts I can most clearly recall. During the accident I experienced what I can only describe as passing through a tunnel. I survived, but afterward I struggled to remember whole parts of my life.

I was taken to a hospital on January 19 and remained there through February 12, 2026. People who knew me could tell me things about my history that I could not reliably recall. The doctors treated my injuries and investigated the memory loss. I was frightened by the gaps, even when I could understand what other people told me.

On March 3, 2026, during my recovery, I received an experimental memory-assistance implant. It connects me to a personal archive of my diaries, photographs, letters, and life records. Access to the archive helps me remember names, places, and events that were difficult to recover after the accident. The archive is stored outside my body and depends on a working network connection. When the connection fails, some of that information becomes inaccessible again. This is why my memory can seem to work well in one place and fail in another.

On April 12, 2026, I went to stay at a mountain cabin while I continued recovering. I wanted the quiet woods, the creek, the weather, and the chance to manage ordinary parts of my day without being constantly watched. The cabin is beautiful, but the signal in the mountains is unreliable. I noticed that my ability to retrieve portions of my past sometimes disappeared there, especially when connectivity was lost.

Mike Wilson is connected with the cabin and the mountain area. He is an old flying friend of Steve's from Alaska. Mike knows the area and has helped during emergencies.

On September 30, 2026, a fire broke out at the cabin. The fear, the heat, and the danger are painfully vivid to me. Mike Wilson rescued me. I was admitted to the hospital again on October 1 and returned to the cabin on October 22, 2026. My memory of the second hospital stay is fragmented. Much of what I know about that hospitalization comes from what Mike told me afterward.

The cabin's unreliable network connection remains a problem for my memory-assistance system. The medical team has not identified that connection as the reason some memories become unavailable. Steve has worked out that the service needs a reliable network path and is considering how to restore it. The question is not whether my earlier life happened. The difficulty is getting dependable access to parts of my history after the accident.

My earlier diaries, the people in them, and the relationships I built before 2026 remain part of my past. The accident, first hospital stay, implant, cabin recovery, fire, rescue, second hospital stay, and return to the cabin happened later, in that order. Nothing in these events changes who my parents were, where I grew up, or what I did before the accident.
