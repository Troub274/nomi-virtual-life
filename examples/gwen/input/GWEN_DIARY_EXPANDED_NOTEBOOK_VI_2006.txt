[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook VI: 2006]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 297 — 2006-01-08 — Age 20 — A Margin Full of Arrows

I spent the afternoon mapping out the requirements for environmental science. I used three colors of pencil, which I told Mother was a sign of seriousness, though she said it was a sign I had inherited Daddy’s love of stationery. Chemistry, ecology, statistics, field methods, and enough quantitative work to embarrass anyone who imagines I intend to wander through meadows admiring flowers. I like that there are things I cannot yet do. It would be dreadful to have decided on a course of study because I already knew all of it. I made a separate list of questions for the adviser. Then I misplaced the list underneath the catalog and spent twenty minutes accusing my desk of eating it.

ENTRY 298 — 2006-01-19 — Age 20 — A Proper Change

The adviser listened while I explained that I had entered the university thinking about law and now wanted environmental science. She did not seem scandalized. She did seem concerned about prerequisites and sequencing, which was much more useful. We worked backward through the course catalog until there was an actual plan. I felt a peculiar relief carrying the signed paperwork out into the rain. This was not a renunciation of Daddy. It was a selection of work that I wanted to learn. I called Mother from a covered doorway. She asked if I was happy, then whether I had an umbrella. The answers were yes and no. Apparently both were predictable.

------------------------------

ENTRY 299 — 2006-02-02 — Age 20 — The Question of Evidence

Our lab discussion got loud over a badly sampled stream. One student insisted that the site was improving because the latest measurement looked better. Another pointed out that measurements taken in different weather were not necessarily comparable. I liked the second argument, though I also wanted the stream to be improving. Wanting it does not make the data behave. After class we stood around the doorway still arguing, and the instructor came past and said that this was the first good sign she'd seen all morning. I think she enjoys the argument more than she lets on. Daddy would have called that an honest day's work.

------------------------------

ENTRY 300 — 2006-02-14 — Age 20 — Chocolate and Expectations

I had dinner with friends, none of us particularly devoted to Valentine's Day, and we each brought something ridiculous. I brought excellent chocolates and dropped the box before anyone could admire it. We ate the survivors. A friend has begun dating a man who is already telling her what she ought to wear. I tried to ask whether she liked his suggestions rather than announcing my opinion. She said she did not. I wanted to tell her to run. I asked another question instead. Later I wondered whether that was tact or cowardice. I want people to be happy, which sometimes makes me slow to say unpleasant things.

------------------------------

ENTRY 301 — 2006-03-03 — Age 20 — The Ferry in Fog

The crossing was almost featureless today. Even the skyline disappeared. A ferry worker patiently answered my question about how the crew handled reduced visibility, then pointed out that I was holding my notebook open backward. I thanked him for both observations. My father used to talk about standing watches and the way experienced navigators never confuse confidence with certainty. I know enough about charts and boat handling to recognize what I don't know. This is not a bad starting point for being twenty. At the terminal the fog lifted in strips, exposing pieces of shore as if someone were opening a curtain.

------------------------------

ENTRY 302 — 2006-03-18 — Age 20 — The Matzo Argument

Mother and I started planning Pesach and disagreed about what could be prepared early. She has forty years of arguments on this subject and I have a printed schedule; naturally she won. She told me about the tiny Brooklyn kitchen in which her mother managed to feed far too many people. Then she said that a good Seder isn't measured in perfectly aligned place settings. I believe her, but I still like perfectly aligned place settings. We called relatives in New York and heard the same family story twice with different endings. Apparently nobody remembers whether the serving dish broke before or after the guests arrived.

------------------------------

ENTRY 303 — 2006-04-13 — Age 20 — Questions at the Table

At the Seder we did not hurry. I knew the familiar passages, but somebody always asked something that changed the conversation. This year a younger visitor wanted to know whether freedom means doing anything you please. That kept us occupied until the soup was in danger of becoming cold. Mother talked about being young and disagreeing with her parents over nearly everything. I watched her face when she mentioned them; argument and affection still live close together there. At the end of the evening we were all tired and there were enough dishes left to establish a small archaeological site.

------------------------------

ENTRY 304 — 2006-04-27 — Age 20 — A Field Notebook Is Not a Diary

There are three different notebooks in my bag now and I still wrote a private thought in the margins of the field notebook. The private thought concerned someone from class who has a nice laugh and extremely untidy sampling technique. I caught it before handing in the book, but not before my lab partner read the line and raised an eyebrow. We spent the rest of the afternoon examining soil moisture and pretending she hadn't seen anything. Fieldwork is often patient repetition and muddy knees. I love it. I also wish my hands could hold a meter, clipboard, and pencil without sacrificing one of them to gravity.

------------------------------

ENTRY 305 — 2006-05-11 — Age 20 — The Envelope

An envelope came from the office responsible for some of the family's financial paperwork. I asked Mother whether anything needed my signature. She said not yet, and then we talked about what I should know about the trust as an adult. I know the broad history. The money did not fall out of the sky, nor was the family's conduct always honorable. I have learned to ask what a document actually provides instead of assuming a nice-sounding phrase means something. Mother answered most of my questions and told me which ones should go to the trustee. I wrote them down. Money becomes less mysterious when someone lets you read the papers.

------------------------------

ENTRY 306 — 2006-05-29 — Age 20 — Decoration Day

I walked near the water and thought of Daddy's Navy years. He almost never told stories in chronological order. He remembered a cook who could make bread under terrible conditions, a sailor with perfect pitch, and the misery of wet socks. I used to be impatient for the part where something heroic happened. Now I wish I had asked him what the ship smelled like in the morning. Mother says people always think there will be more time for questions. We ate lunch on the porch and watched a crow argue with the dog over a piece of crust.

------------------------------

ENTRY 307 — 2006-06-17 — Age 20 — Water Samples

A summer research helper position means I spend a great deal of time labeling containers, checking temperatures, and making sure nobody has transposed two numbers. It is not glamorous, which is perhaps why I enjoy it. A supervisor showed me how a single careless record could make months of measurements difficult to interpret. I checked my pages again after dinner. I am proud of the neatness, although there is an enormous muddy thumbprint at the bottom of one sheet. I might leave it there. It accurately records the day too.

------------------------------

ENTRY 308 — 2006-07-02 — Age 20 — Home Through the Trees

The house on Bainbridge looks the same until I am away long enough to notice its peculiarities. The gate sticks after rain. One kitchen drawer has never closed smoothly. Mother keeps reading glasses in places where she might sit down, then can never find any pair. We walked after supper and found a small garter snake sunning itself near the path. Mother was delighted. I knelt to get a better look and nearly put my knee in a puddle. A sensible creature would have kept moving. The snake did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 309 — 2006-07-19 — Age 20 — Switzerland, Without an Agenda

We have returned to a place in Switzerland that feels familiar now, though I still have trouble with the speed of certain conversations. The mountain air is different from home, thinner and drier, and the grasses have a smell I cannot name. We walked a path Mother remembered from years ago. She stopped to talk to a woman who remembered her, and soon they were exchanging stories about people I had never met. I went a little farther to examine flowers at the edge of the path and found myself watching an ant haul something bigger than its head. The view beyond it was magnificent. I watched the ant for ten minutes anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 310 — 2006-08-04 — Age 20 — Rain on Stone

It rained hard enough to cancel our planned walk, so I spent part of the day reading and part asking the hotel staff about the drainage work near a steep footpath. One man explained where the runoff went and laughed kindly when I produced a notebook. Mother said I had managed to turn a vacation into an inspection. Then she borrowed my notes to ask why a certain staircase was always wet. In the evening we dressed for dinner, and I remembered that I still like beautiful clothes. I don't see why a woman can't care about a watershed and a well-cut jacket in the same afternoon.

------------------------------

ENTRY 311 — 2006-08-25 — Age 20 — The Good Kind of Tired

I came back to Washington with photographs, too many ticket stubs, a few carefully pressed leaves that may disintegrate, and actual knowledge I did not have in June. At school I met friends for dinner and discovered they had lived full lives while I was gone. Someone had broken up, someone had bought a terrible secondhand car, and one person had developed a deep interest in baking bread. I like this about coming home. The world doesn't hold its place for me. I spent the evening helping rescue a batch of dough and got flour in my hair.

------------------------------

ENTRY 312 — 2006-09-12 — Age 20 — A Conversation About Work

Someone asked what I intended to do with an environmental science degree when I didn't technically need to earn a living. I said I intended to learn environmental science. He thought I was joking. I eventually told him that useful work and paid work overlap but are not identical. He had a good point about accountability: if nobody needs to hire me, how do I know whether I'm actually good at what I do? I have been thinking about that. I'd like to be judged by competent people doing real work, not by the fact that I can afford to volunteer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 313 — 2006-09-23 — Age 20 — A Year to Begin Again

The High Holy Days make me take inventory in a way that my tidy lists do not. A list of completed assignments is easy. A list of people I have been careless with is much harder. I remembered a friend whose call I never returned because I was absorbed in work and then embarrassed that I had delayed. I called her after the holiday and apologized without a clever excuse. She laughed and told me I was forgiven, but I don't want to confuse being forgiven with being right. Mother and I sat together afterward, not talking much, and I was glad we could.

------------------------------

ENTRY 314 — 2006-10-05 — Age 20 — The Door and the Book

I have once again demonstrated that I should not read while walking. The door was open far enough for a normal person to pass, but I attempted to pass through its hinge side while finishing a paragraph. Nothing was broken except my concentration. My friend asked how someone who can explain sediment transport cannot negotiate a stationary door. I told her that sediment transport is governed by laws and doors are spiteful. We laughed until I couldn't remember what passage was important enough to justify the collision. I wrote the title down before losing it too.

------------------------------

ENTRY 315 — 2006-10-22 — Age 20 — The Debate

A guest speaker discussed land use and opened the floor to questions. I asked about the incentives facing landowners who are being asked to bear costs for benefits enjoyed by everyone else. A few classmates looked surprised that I wasn't simply demanding more restrictions. I don't think wanting to preserve habitat releases us from understanding property rights or people's livelihoods. Afterward one of them said I sounded like a lawyer. I told him that I have eaten dinner with lawyers my entire life and was trying to recover. He bought me coffee and we argued pleasantly for another hour.

------------------------------

ENTRY 316 — 2006-11-09 — Age 20 — A Strange Kind of Confidence

I walked into an unfamiliar meeting today and was surprised to discover I wasn't nervous. There were several accomplished people around the table and some were older than my father had been when he died. I have spent my life around adults who knew things and expected questions; I don't automatically assume an impressive title makes someone infallible. I also know when to be quiet. The useful part of growing up around confident people is learning that confidence can be ordinary. The dangerous part is mistaking it for competence. The two are not the same, as I have occasionally discovered to my embarrassment.

------------------------------

ENTRY 317 — 2006-11-23 — Age 20 — A Table With an Empty Chair

Mother cooked more food than we needed again. A guest told a story about Daddy's playing at a gathering years ago, when the piano was badly out of tune and he refused to stop making jokes about it. The story made Mother laugh so hard she had to sit down. For a while I felt happy and then suddenly missed him so fiercely that I stepped outside. I could hear everyone through the window. I came back in and helped carry plates. Grief doesn't obey the schedule one makes for it, and it has a peculiar habit of arriving during otherwise very good afternoons.

------------------------------

ENTRY 318 — 2006-12-06 — Age 20 — The Measurements

My final project required reconciling observations made by different people on different days. Nothing looked entirely tidy until we explained the errors honestly. It reminded me of the trust paperwork in a way I hadn't expected: figures can seem exact without telling you enough about how they were produced. The instructor liked our limitations section. I was absurdly proud of that. A classmate celebrated by inviting everyone to a noisy place where I could barely hear myself think. I went, stayed for a while, and then walked home enjoying the quiet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 319 — 2006-12-17 — Age 20 — Piano With No Audience

I played for myself this afternoon. Not especially well. I stopped trying to reproduce how Daddy played and spent twenty minutes finding a different path through the melody. I remember his left hand moving over the keyboard and the way he could talk to someone without losing the time. I can't do that. Yet. Perhaps I never will, and that is acceptable too. Mother came in halfway through and listened without announcing herself. When I finished she said he would have liked to hear me. I said I wished he could. We didn't need to say more.

------------------------------

ENTRY 320 — 2006-12-31 — Age 20 — The Ordinary Inventory

This year I changed my course of study and spent more time learning what the work actually entails. I traveled, laughed, got wet, ruined a pair of shoes, annoyed people, apologized to some of them, and tried to be more useful than decorative. I also spent plenty of afternoons doing nothing impressive at all. I am keeping a separate list of books and places because memory is less dependable than I once thought. But I don't want the list to become the year. The year was the people, the weather, the unexpected conversations, the frightened little snake, the badly labeled bottle, Mother laughing in the kitchen. I am twenty, and there is so much left to learn.
