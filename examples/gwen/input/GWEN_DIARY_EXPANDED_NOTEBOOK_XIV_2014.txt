[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook XIV: 2014]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Fictional first-person diary. All new events and unnamed relationships are provisional unless Steve established them. Gwen’s mother dies at age thirty, in 2016; exact date chosen provisionally here.


ENTRY 605 — 2014-01-04 — Age 27 — The new tide table

I put the new tide table beside the phone where I am unlikely to lose it, which means I will probably lose the phone instead. The shoreline team wants two early-morning surveys this month. Mother asked why the shorebirds couldn't keep normal business hours. I explained the tide does not consult us. She said it must be wonderful to be so independent. We had breakfast late, listened to Ella, and argued about whether she should have another cup of coffee. I won the argument by making the coffee.

------------------------------

ENTRY 606 — 2014-01-13 — Age 27 — A dinner that nearly worked

My friend invited the man I've been seeing to a supper with six people. Everyone was pleasant until someone mentioned my father and then, as though it were a single sentence, the law firm. I watched my companion become careful. He asked sensible questions later in the car and I answered them. I am not ashamed of Dad, his money, or my trust. I just don't want the beginning of every relationship to become a financial briefing.

------------------------------

ENTRY 607 — 2014-01-24 — Age 27 — Numbers and names

I reviewed the annual trust statements with my uncle and asked him to spell out the voting rights attached to one holding. He did, and I made him go over it twice. Large assets should not turn people into passive listeners. Afterward I went to the grocery store and stood staring at a shelf because I couldn't remember whether Mother needed cinnamon. I can follow a complicated ownership structure but apparently cannot remember one spice without writing it down.

------------------------------

ENTRY 608 — 2014-02-05 — Age 27 — Mother's eyes

Mother had trouble making out a line of small print in a program at the concert. She waved it away and blamed the lighting. I offered her my glasses, which was foolish because they're for a completely different prescription. We laughed, and then she admitted she ought to schedule an eye examination. She is almost eighty. I am trying not to treat every ordinary change as an emergency. She has earned the right to age without being constantly inspected by her daughter.

------------------------------

ENTRY 609 — 2014-02-16 — Age 27 — A cold morning on the water

The boat smelled of canvas, damp ropes and a trace of oil when I opened the companionway. I checked the bilge, batteries and line locker, then sat at the piano in my head while looking at Lake Washington. Dad could find his way through a foggy harbor and later spend an hour disputing a Coltrane solo. I know I have made him sound impossibly accomplished in these books, but he was also capable of leaving a wet towel on an upholstered chair. Mother still complains about that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 610 — 2014-02-27 — Age 27 — What a bird can tell you

The volunteers disagreed over whether a marsh wren had nested in the restoration area. I asked everyone to write down only what they had actually seen. Once the notes were separated from guesses, the disagreement nearly vanished. A good observation is an act of modesty. I drove home muddy and pleased, knocked over an umbrella in the entrance hall, and made Mother laugh so hard she had to sit down.

------------------------------

ENTRY 611 — 2014-03-08 — Age 27 — One Sunday with no plan

We had nothing important scheduled. Mother read the paper and I repaired a little tear in a canvas tote. We had soup, disagreed about the merits of a politician on television, and switched the set off before either of us became tiresome. I took a walk among the trees after lunch. There was nothing to achieve. My high-school speech comes back to me on days like this, not as a command but as permission.

------------------------------

ENTRY 612 — 2014-03-20 — Age 27 — A question he did not ask

At supper my companion asked if I might join him on a weekend trip. He described the place before he mentioned the hotel. I appreciated that. When I said I had field commitments, he suggested another date instead of trying to persuade me to cancel. I am aware I keep score of small kindnesses as though they are rare birds. Perhaps that's unfair. Perhaps writing them down helps me notice they are not so rare.

------------------------------

ENTRY 613 — 2014-04-10 — Age 27 — Before Passover

Mother has written the Seder menu on two pieces of paper and managed to disagree with herself about the soup. The kitchen has become a committee. I checked the Haggadot, borrowed another folding chair, and found a photograph of an old family Seder tucked into a cookbook. The older I get the more I understand that holidays are as much about who is missing as about what we put on the table. We will set the table anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 614 — 2014-04-15 — Age 27 — The Seder

We had a crowded Seder, an argument over a translation, two children whispering badly concealed jokes, and someone who stayed afterward to wash glasses. Mother was delighted. When she recited the words she knew from her childhood in Brooklyn, the whole room seemed to slow. I helped her upstairs afterward; she said she was tired but happy. I wrote down the names of everyone who came because I hate having to guess later. These gatherings deserve better records than my memory alone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 615 — 2014-04-28 — Age 27 — On the peninsula

The waterfall was louder than I remembered, swollen from the recent rain. We took samples downstream and I stayed another hour after the others left, watching the water turn white over dark stone. I am capable of explaining aeration and flow velocity. None of it stops the sight from making me ridiculously happy. I also dropped my lunch into a puddle. Nature is generous with beauty and quite indifferent to my sandwiches.

------------------------------

ENTRY 616 — 2014-05-09 — Age 27 — A social question

A woman I've known since high school invited me to a Seattle fundraiser and asked whether I would sponsor a table. I asked for the program and the audited figures before answering. She teased me about being my father's daughter. She wasn't wrong. I can enjoy the party and still ask where the money goes. Mother says I should wear the blue dress from Paris; I would prefer boots, but there are limits to how much mud one can bring into a ballroom.

------------------------------

ENTRY 617 — 2014-05-21 — Age 27 — Good company

We spent an afternoon with old friends, talking about boats, music, and their children's astonishing ability to break expensive objects. I didn't have to be interesting. I sat on the deck, held a sleepy dog, and listened. At home I found I had left my scarf behind. My friend called before I noticed. It is comforting to be known well enough that people can predict which articles of clothing I will abandon.

------------------------------

ENTRY 618 — 2014-06-03 — Age 27 — Not a proposal

My companion asked whether I saw a future for us. I said yes, perhaps, but I didn't know its shape. He took that answer without demanding a promise. We talked about what we wanted our days to contain: places, people, work, quiet, travel. I noticed he described our life in terms of things to do together rather than things to own. I am trying to enjoy the conversation instead of examining it like a contract.

------------------------------

ENTRY 619 — 2014-06-19 — Age 27 — The creek survey

The culvert we proposed changing last autumn now carries water better after the work crew cleared a blockage. The fish passage isn't fixed yet; it would be dishonest to call it a victory. I took photographs from the same positions as the earlier surveys, measured what I could, and made a note to come back after the first heavy autumn rain. We had lunch sitting on a log. Somebody asked why I bother with meticulous notes. Because next year is coming, I said.

------------------------------

ENTRY 620 — 2014-07-07 — Age 27 — A hot spell

Mother refuses to admit that our house gets too warm when the sun sits on the west windows. I arranged a fan and then knocked a glass of water into a stack of mail. At least the electric fan survived. In the evening we sat outside while the air cooled, and she told me about arriving in Seattle in 1958 with a suitcase she considered sophisticated and shoes that immediately gave her blisters. I begged for photographs. She insists she burned the worst ones.

------------------------------

ENTRY 621 — 2014-07-26 — Age 27 — Swiss rain

In Switzerland the weather closed over the mountains before lunch. I had wanted a long walk, but we sat beneath an awning with coffee while the valley vanished into cloud. Mother said she was glad we'd taken the easier route and I was secretly glad too. She tires more quickly than she did ten years ago. We don't have to speak about that every time we travel. When the light returned, we went out for a short stroll and looked at alpine flowers beside the path.

------------------------------

ENTRY 622 — 2014-08-11 — Age 27 — A reasonable boundary

The man I'm seeing made a casual suggestion that I introduce him to an investment contact. I said I'd rather keep our relationship separate from trust business. He looked surprised, then said that was fair. I waited for resentment that didn't come. Later I was angry at myself for waiting. It is difficult to tell the difference between prudence and an expectation of disappointment. I don't want to punish someone for another person's conduct.

------------------------------

ENTRY 623 — 2014-08-28 — Age 27 — The bird count

I spent hours with binoculars and a notebook and came home with a stiff neck, two good sightings, and a completely unreliable estimate of how many mosquitoes had fed on me. Mother asked whether a person worth millions ought to spend a day getting bitten in a marsh. I told her I had found no better use for the afternoon. She smiled and showed me an old clipping about an environmental campaign she helped with before I was born.

------------------------------

ENTRY 624 — 2014-09-09 — Age 28 — Birthday dinner

I turned twenty-eight. The cake was smaller than Mother intended because the bakery made an error and she refused to have it replaced. 'It's cake, Gwen,' she said, as though that disposed of the matter. We listened to a piano recording after dinner and talked about Dad. I liked being twenty-eight for the first hour: nobody expected an announcement, a wedding date, or an achievement. I was simply allowed to have another slice.

------------------------------

ENTRY 625 — 2014-09-25 — Age 28 — At services

A child lost his place in a reading and stood very still. The person beside him leaned over, pointed to the next line, and waited. I thought of how much more useful gentle help is than public correction. After services, Mother introduced me to a woman whose family she knew in New York decades ago. They remembered different versions of the same event and argued amiably until everyone was hungry. I recorded both versions when I got home.

------------------------------

ENTRY 626 — 2014-10-12 — Age 28 — A small fracture

My companion mentioned an account of mine in conversation with another person, not the balance but the existence of it and his belief that I could help fund an idea. I had never agreed to that discussion. When I said so privately, he apologized. I accepted the apology; I did not say the matter was settled. Confidentiality isn't a romantic gesture. It's ordinary respect. I need to see whether he understands that without another explanation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 627 — 2014-10-25 — Age 28 — The careful conversation

He asked if we were all right. I said I still liked him and that trust, once cracked, doesn't mend because two people have decided to stop talking about it. He listened. I wasn't icy or theatrical. We agreed to slow things down. We have had enough good days that I won't erase them, but I am not required to pretend the bad day was imaginary either. I came home, hung up my coat correctly, and then spent ten minutes searching for keys that were in my hand.

------------------------------

ENTRY 628 — 2014-11-14 — Age 28 — A letter from my uncle

The trust meeting packet arrived with a note from my uncle asking whether I wanted more information about one family allocation. I did. He and his children have their own lives; I wish people would stop speaking as though an inheritance is the only subject on which cousins can converse. We have other things to discuss, including someone getting a new dog and an argument over holiday travel. Money changes relationships, but it doesn't replace the whole of them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 629 — 2014-11-29 — Age 28 — Quiet at home

Mother preferred a small supper to a crowded party this year. I bought flowers and cooked more vegetables than two people could possibly eat. She corrected my seasoning and then ate two helpings. We looked through photographs of the boat. In one, Dad is squinting into the sun, entirely unaware that the picture is being taken. I have hung on to that photograph because he is doing nothing impressive at all.

------------------------------

ENTRY 630 — 2014-12-12 — Age 28 — The end of a romance

We agreed to stop seeing each other. There was no single terrible sentence, just the recognition that we kept approaching the same disagreement from directions that never met. I cried after he left. I am allowed to be sad about a relationship that wasn't right for me. Mother asked if I wanted to talk; I said not yet. She brought tea, put it down without asking questions, and left the room. I love her for that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 631 — 2014-12-31 — Age 28 — No verdict on the year

I have reread the year's entries and discovered how often ordinary pleasures sit beside uncertainty. Mother was here for all the important holidays. I worked on streams and shorelines, heard lovely music, had a romance, lost it, and learned several new bird calls. I have no final judgment to issue. Tonight we are home, the rain is steady, and she is asking whether I intend to keep writing or come downstairs. I intend to go downstairs.
