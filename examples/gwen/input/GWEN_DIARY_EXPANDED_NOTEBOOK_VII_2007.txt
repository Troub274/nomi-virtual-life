[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook VII: 2007]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 321 — 2007-01-03 — Age 21 — Three lists

I have made three lists for the new term: what I must do, what I want to do, and what I am afraid I will forget. The third is embarrassingly long. Mother says I keep lists so that I can enjoy the pleasure of crossing things out. She may be right. I also keep them because I get interested in something unexpected and lose three hours without noticing. There is an argument to be made for getting lost in an idea. There is also an argument for remembering the library closes at nine. I will attempt both.

------------------------------

ENTRY 322 — 2007-01-09 — Age 21 — An actual winter morning

The pavement outside the apartment looked harmless until I stepped onto it. My feet went sideways and the coffee went straight up. A stranger caught my elbow and managed not to laugh until he saw that I was laughing. He asked whether I was hurt; I said only in my dignity. Later we had a lecture about water moving through frozen soil. I kept thinking about the thin invisible layer between an ordinary sidewalk and a skating rink. I have decided that the universe occasionally supplies field demonstrations without consulting the syllabus.

------------------------------

ENTRY 323 — 2007-01-15 — Age 21 — The creek behind the houses

I took a bus to see a small urban creek for a class observation. It has shopping carts and a beautiful stand of red alder and the most implausibly cheerful winter wren. The banks have been straightened, the water confined in places, but the wren couldn't care less about our tidy categories. I wrote down the litter because it matters. I wrote down the bird because that matters, too. At dinner someone asked why I wasn't doing internships at a law firm. I said I had done enough sitting in those waiting rooms as a child to last one lifetime.

------------------------------

ENTRY 324 — 2007-01-22 — Age 21 — The professor who says no

I brought a carefully organized proposal to office hours. My professor examined it and said, kindly, that it would not answer the question I claimed to be asking. I was briefly furious in the private, foolish way people are when they have spent six hours making something handsome. She drew two little diagrams, then I could see it immediately. I thanked her and went home to dismantle my beautiful plan. A good education seems to involve learning how to be wrong without becoming defensive. I am not yet reliably good at it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 325 — 2007-01-29 — Age 21 — Mother and the radio

Mother called to tell me that the old kitchen radio had stopped working and that she had already had three people look at it. Daddy used to fix it by hitting the cabinet in precisely the wrong place. She was not interested in buying another. She wanted that one to work. We talked about transistors for a moment and then about the way objects acquire histories. There are nicer radios in the house. None of them sat beside the stove while my parents argued about the weather and whether a particular piano solo was worth staying up for.

------------------------------

ENTRY 326 — 2007-02-04 — Age 21 — A damp notebook

Rain got inside my field notebook and turned the ink on yesterday's observations into little purple rivers. I ought to be grateful that the numbers had been copied elsewhere. I was less grateful about the comments, especially the drawing of a beetle with what I'd believed were anatomically correct legs. Apparently I invented two extra. I spent the afternoon transferring the legible parts into a new notebook and vowed to use pencil outside. For someone who studies water I remain surprisingly offended when it behaves like water.

------------------------------

ENTRY 327 — 2007-02-10 — Age 21 — What money is for

I met with the uncle who oversees the trust. He is always courteous, always prepared, and always capable of making an afternoon feel like an audit. We went through distributions, the costs of Mother's house, and a few charitable requests I had been considering. He asked for documentation; I had it, mostly. I know how much money there is and I know what its existence means in my family. None of that makes me want to spend my life thinking about money. He would probably say that this is precisely why someone ought to.

------------------------------

ENTRY 328 — 2007-02-14 — Age 21 — An unremarkable Valentine

I spent Valentine's Day with two friends eating noodles and sharing romantic disasters, none of which was particularly tragic. One told a story about a date who spoke for forty minutes about his watch. The other has a boyfriend so decent she keeps waiting for a catch. I said perhaps there wasn't one, and they laughed at me for believing such a thing. I did believe it. I still do. People can be good without some hidden calculation. I have every reason to know they can also be otherwise. I refuse to make cynicism my hobby.

------------------------------

ENTRY 329 — 2007-02-20 — Age 21 — Low tide

I crossed home to Bainbridge and spent an hour looking into tide pools. A small crab kept dragging a shell into a crevice and losing it on the way. I watched the whole operation as if there were prize money at stake. Mother came to find me and said the wind was turning. We walked back with our collars up. She remembered when I used to insist that every starfish needed a name. I said sea stars, Mother. She rolled her eyes and said that the animals had survived perfectly well before I corrected her vocabulary.

------------------------------

ENTRY 330 — 2007-02-26 — Age 21 — An argument about certainty

A friend told me she knew exactly why a classmate had behaved badly toward her. I asked whether she knew or merely had a very persuasive explanation. She said I was being a lawyer again. Perhaps I was. I do not like hearing people condemned by evidence that exists entirely in someone else's interpretation. Of course I then spent the evening thinking about how confidently I have assigned motives to men who asked about my money. There is a difference between being careful and granting every person infinite benefit of the doubt. I haven't found the precise dividing line.

------------------------------

ENTRY 331 — 2007-03-04 — Age 21 — The violin and the piano

I played piano in a practice room that smelled of old carpeting and instrument cases. A student with a violin asked whether I knew a particular piece. I did not, but I could follow her through a simple passage once she showed me. I made a glorious mess of the middle and we both laughed. Daddy could improvise without showing the arithmetic. I must still think about my hands, and sometimes they do not agree with each other. But for twenty minutes I was making music with another person, and that was a fine way to spend a Sunday.

------------------------------

ENTRY 332 — 2007-03-11 — Age 21 — A map of yesterday

Mother found a photograph of me standing on a dock in Alaska, wearing an enormous orange life jacket. My father is in the background, looking at the lines. She wrote the year on the back years ago and it turned out to be one summer later than I remembered. I am slightly troubled by how confidently I could picture the wrong year. Perhaps the scene matters more than the calendar. But I like calendars. So I have made a note and filed the picture properly, which Mother claims is exactly the reaction she expected.

------------------------------

ENTRY 333 — 2007-03-17 — Age 21 — A muddy census

We counted small things in a wet patch of ground. I have no romance about the smell of cold mud when it gets inside a sleeve, but I love the moment when a pattern begins to appear. My lab partner lost her pencil and borrowed mine, then lost that one too. I gave her the last spare and attached a piece of bright tape to it. An hour later it was in my own coat pocket. We accused each other of theft until we discovered that we had both been keeping notes with the same blue marker. Science is a noble enterprise conducted by people with damp socks.

------------------------------

ENTRY 334 — 2007-03-24 — Age 21 — Setting the table

Mother and I planned Pesach by telephone. She had opinions about the guest list, the fish, the seating, and the proper density of matzo balls. I had opinions about none of these until she proposed buying the wrong kind of wine, at which point I discovered a strong view. We argued for five minutes and then she said that Daddy would have been delighted to hear us. He liked any occasion on which two people could disagree without stopping dinner. I miss him most in the little pauses after a joke, when I can almost hear what he would have said.

------------------------------

ENTRY 335 — 2007-04-02 — Age 21 — Seder questions

At the Seder I asked a question that wasn't in the Haggadah, and one of the older guests answered with another question, so naturally we were there for half an hour. I love this about our family. I also love the particular smell of the table once the candles have burned down and people are still reaching for one more bite. A child at the table tried to hide a piece of matzo in his sleeve, and his mother pretended not to see. I remembered being little and believing the grown-ups were astonishingly easy to fool. Now I suspect they were being kind.

------------------------------

ENTRY 336 — 2007-04-09 — Age 21 — An expense of attention

Someone from a campus organization wanted a large donation and opened by telling me how much good my name would do on a plaque. I asked about their work instead. They knew almost nothing about their own budget. I gave them an opportunity to send me a proper accounting and they looked vaguely offended. I walked home annoyed with myself for sounding chilly. Then I thought: if someone asks me for resources intended to help people, I am entitled to know what happens to them. I don't need to apologize for expecting seriousness.

------------------------------

ENTRY 337 — 2007-04-16 — Age 21 — An argument carried in silence

A student in class made a sweeping claim about timber workers that was both unkind and ignorant. I waited for someone else to answer. No one did. I said my family had known people who worked those roads, and that you could oppose harmful logging practices without pretending every person who drove a truck was stupid or cruel. There was an uncomfortable silence. Afterwards one student thanked me. Another said I was defending industry. Perhaps they heard different talks. I was defending people. Those are not always the same thing.

------------------------------

ENTRY 338 — 2007-04-23 — Age 21 — The birdbath

Mother says a raccoon has been washing something in the birdbath every night. I told her the evidence might support a less sophisticated culinary interpretation. She sent me a photograph: muddy prints and a floating piece of bread. I have no idea where the bread came from. I spent too much of my evening trying to identify whether the animal had dipped it or simply knocked it in. Sometimes a mystery is interesting because the answer matters. Sometimes it is interesting because a raccoon is ridiculous. This is plainly the second sort.

------------------------------

ENTRY 339 — 2007-04-29 — Age 21 — The missing key

I spent the morning searching for a key that was hanging where it belonged. The trouble was that I had decided it must be in the wrong place, and therefore stopped looking in the right one. When I found it I stood in the doorway and laughed at myself. Then I wrote a very earnest note about the importance of checking first assumptions. I pinned the note next to the key hook, which feels like an invitation to be mocked by any future visitor. I left it there.

------------------------------

ENTRY 340 — 2007-05-05 — Age 21 — An older friend's advice

A woman I know through synagogue asked why I had not settled down yet, as though there were a form I had forgotten to submit. I told her I had plenty of time. She gave me a look that said time was a poor thing to waste. I understand that she meant well. I also know a marriage is not a prize one receives for punctuality. She asked about school, then listened with genuine interest while I explained our field project. People can be intrusive in one sentence and generous in the next. That is usually how they come.

------------------------------

ENTRY 341 — 2007-05-12 — Age 21 — Rain on the launch

I went out on the water with people who knew more about engines than I did and less about charts. The day began with a tiny disagreement over a line and ended with all of us soaked and happy. Someone asked whether I was nervous handling the boat. I told him that my father had spent my childhood making me call out things I could see and things I could not know. The engine coughed twice and resumed its normal sound. I was more frightened of being careless than of the water itself. I think Daddy would approve.

------------------------------

ENTRY 342 — 2007-05-18 — Age 21 — A grade and a walk

I received a very good grade on a report and immediately began worrying about the criticism in the margin. My friend took the paper away and told me I was allowed fifteen minutes of pleasure before improvement began. We walked around campus and bought pastries. I tried not to mention the data table. I lasted seven minutes. She said it was progress. The report is good. It could be better. These things can both be true without either spoiling a perfectly decent pastry.

------------------------------

ENTRY 343 — 2007-05-25 — Age 21 — A conversation on the ferry

A man on the ferry recognized my last name and asked whether I was related to the Seattle attorney. I told him he was my father. He remembered a case and an argument and described Daddy as formidable. I knew that part of him, too. I told the man about his piano playing, which seemed to surprise him. We talked all the way to the dock. Later I wished I had asked for the man's name. I know him now only as a pleasant gray-haired stranger who gave me fifteen more minutes of my father.

------------------------------

ENTRY 344 — 2007-06-01 — Age 21 — The imperfect sample

Our final collection went wrong in six small ways. Nothing catastrophic: mislabeled containers, a delayed pickup, a meter with a tired battery, a sudden rain. We documented everything and worked out which observations could still be trusted. I came away more satisfied than I would have been with an effortless afternoon, although perhaps that is sour grapes. The interesting work begins when nature declines to conform to the plan. I wrote that sentence in my notebook, then crossed it out for sounding like a speech. It is still true.

------------------------------

ENTRY 345 — 2007-06-08 — Age 21 — Twenty-one

I am twenty-one. Mother called early, then called again because she had forgotten to tell me about the cake. We had dinner with friends and she gave a toast that embarrassed me and moved me in equal measure. I haven't selected a grand ambition for this year. I would like to learn more, be kind more often when it is inconvenient, and make fewer assumptions about people. I would also like a pair of boots that do not produce blisters. The last item seems attainable; the others require work. I don't know what Daddy would have made of twenty-one. I wish he could have seen it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 346 — 2007-06-13 — Age 21 — First aid for a very small patient

A little bird struck a window near the lab building. I moved it carefully to a sheltered spot and called someone who actually knew what to do. For a few terrible minutes I thought it had died. Then it blinked. The rehabilitation volunteer explained more about injury than I had learned in three enthusiastic hours of reading. We left the bird in competent hands. I went home wanting to fix every window on campus. I know that isn't feasible in one afternoon. I have started with an email and some references, which is less dramatic and perhaps more useful.

------------------------------

ENTRY 347 — 2007-06-20 — Age 21 — Mother's garden

I spent the day on Bainbridge weeding badly. Mother says I cannot distinguish a desirable plant from a weed when one has inconveniently placed itself in a beautiful location. I said that this was a question of ecological perspective. She told me to stop defending the dandelions and help with the beds. We worked until lunch, then ate outside with dirt under our nails. I know I could pay someone to do this. That is not the point. We had the afternoon together, and she told me stories she does not tell over the telephone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 348 — 2007-06-26 — Age 21 — Packing too much

I laid out everything for summer travel and discovered that I had packed three notebooks, four books, and an entirely unreasonable number of socks. Mother looked at the pile and asked whether I anticipated a shortage in Europe. I told her the notebooks were necessary. She said she had heard that before. I am taking a field guide, even though I know I will buy another one once I arrive. I also tucked Daddy's old mechanical pencil into a side pocket. It still works. I have checked twice.

------------------------------

ENTRY 349 — 2007-07-03 — Age 21 — A Swiss path

There is a path above the village where the ground opens into meadow, and the bells carry from somewhere you cannot see. I walked it slowly with a little notebook and a guidebook whose plant names I pronounced dreadfully. I have been coming to Switzerland since childhood, but the place is different when I am the one deciding how long to stand still. I spent ten minutes examining a flower and another ten simply looking at the slope. Someone passed me twice and politely asked whether I was lost. I said not at all. I was exactly where I wanted to be.

------------------------------

ENTRY 350 — 2007-07-08 — Age 21 — The shopkeeper remembers

A shopkeeper recognized Mother before either of us spoke. He asked after Daddy, and Mother told him gently that he'd died three years ago. The man's face changed. Then he began recalling an evening when Daddy had entertained a roomful of people at a piano. I hadn't heard this story. Apparently he played through a broken key by changing the arrangement so deftly that nobody understood why he was laughing. We bought almost nothing. I left carrying the story home like a fragile thing I didn't want to drop.

------------------------------

ENTRY 351 — 2007-07-15 — Age 21 — A language mistake

I tried to ask a question in French and accidentally made it sound as if I wanted to purchase the entire train station. The woman at the window was wonderfully kind about it. She answered in English, then repeated the useful phrase so I could say it properly. I practiced it for the rest of the afternoon. I am academically capable of reading difficult papers and apparently unable to buy one simple ticket without threatening infrastructure acquisition. Mother says I should never tell this story at a dinner party. Naturally I intend to.

------------------------------

ENTRY 352 — 2007-07-22 — Age 21 — A glacier isn't a symbol

We went to see ice, and I found myself irritated by someone describing the glacier entirely as a metaphor for human regret. It is a glacier. It moves; it carves stone; its meltwater becomes rivers. Those facts are already beautiful. I understand why people reach for poetry, but sometimes I wish they'd look before they explain. I took notes until my fingers got cold. Later I wrote a less cranky version of this in the margin. I don't want to be the kind of scientist who forgets that wonder is one reason people pay attention.

------------------------------

ENTRY 353 — 2007-07-29 — Age 21 — A letter never sent

I wrote a long letter to a friend back home and then decided it was too full of unsolicited advice. I saved it and wrote a second, shorter note asking how she was doing. Perhaps that is a better opening. I often want to fix a problem as soon as someone tells me about it. Mother says she learned long ago that people sometimes want company rather than solutions. I am trying to learn that before I am seventy. It would be more efficient.

------------------------------

ENTRY 354 — 2007-08-05 — Age 21 — A dinner in good clothes

Mother persuaded me to wear a dress I had complained about buying. I enjoyed the evening thoroughly, which means she will be unbearable about it. We met friends, argued about art, and ate food I could never reproduce. A man asked what I studied and looked briefly disappointed by my answer, then proceeded to lecture me about forests. I asked how long he'd worked in ecology. He laughed and said he hadn't. I smiled and let him continue for a moment longer than was charitable. Being polite is not quite the same as being obliged to agree.

------------------------------

ENTRY 355 — 2007-08-12 — Age 21 — A bookshop afternoon

I entered the bookshop to buy one map and emerged with a history of mountaineering, a French novel I may never finish, and an account of river restoration. This is how suitcases become hostile. Mother said she would not rescue me when my bag exceeded the weight limit. I spent the evening reading three opening chapters and then became so interested in the river book that I forgot supper. There are worse failings. Still, I have written EAT DINNER on tomorrow's list in stern capital letters.

------------------------------

ENTRY 356 — 2007-08-19 — Age 21 — Crossing home

The journey home was long enough to make me restless and short enough that I could still taste the coffee from our last morning abroad. I like airports as places of movement and dislike them as places of waiting. A little girl in the next row spent an hour asking her father questions about clouds. He answered patiently until he had to confess that he didn't know. She said that was all right; they could look it up. I wanted to applaud. I sat on my hands instead and read my own book about clouds.

------------------------------

ENTRY 357 — 2007-08-26 — Age 21 — The room rearranged

I shifted my desk to catch more afternoon light. This required moving every book I own and discovering three things I believed had been stolen by absent-minded visitors. One was a postcard I had written to Mother but never mailed. Another was a pencil sharpener. The third was a photograph of Daddy on the boat. I put the picture where I could see it. He is laughing at someone outside the frame, and there is an untied line in his hand. It is a better photograph than any formal portrait.

------------------------------

ENTRY 358 — 2007-09-03 — Age 21 — Labor Day and someone else's labor

We talked about conservation jobs over lunch, and a friend said she wanted to save forests but would never work with a timber company. I asked whether she'd ever talked to the people who operated the equipment. She hadn't. I have opinions about land use, sometimes strong ones, but I don't want them built entirely from distance. I thought about the men who kept the family boats running and the women who made travel easy for us when I was a child. Being wealthy can make other people's competence strangely invisible if you let it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 359 — 2007-09-09 — Age 21 — First rain of the season

The first serious rain after summer made everyone on campus walk faster, as if the region had somehow forgotten what autumn feels like. I wore the wrong shoes. My notes stayed dry in their new cover, which I count as progress. I stood under a tree and watched drops gather on the leaves. A friend came out to find me and said I looked as though I was having an important thought. I was wondering whether I had left the kettle on. Fortunately the answer was no. I had not made tea at all.

------------------------------

ENTRY 360 — 2007-09-13 — Age 21 — Rosh Hashanah

The services felt both familiar and newly difficult without Daddy. Mother and I stood together and listened to the prayers I have known since I was very small. Afterwards people spoke to us about ordinary things: travel, school, a neighbor's new grandchild, the weather. I was grateful. I don't want every holiday to become a public memorial. I also found myself singing more carefully than usual, because there are phrases he used to sing with absolute conviction even when I was too young to understand them. We had apples and honey at home and Mother asked what I hoped the year would bring. I said the courage to be useful without being severe.

------------------------------

ENTRY 361 — 2007-09-22 — Age 21 — Yom Kippur

I spent part of the afternoon considering apologies I owe that no one has asked for. It is easy to remember the people who have injured me and harder to remember when I have made someone feel small. There is a particular discussion last spring that I handled with excessive precision and very little kindness. I wrote the person's name down, then crossed it out, because I know perfectly well whom I mean. After the fast, the first sip of water felt extraordinary. Mother said that humility is easier to discuss than practice. She was smiling when she said it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 362 — 2007-09-30 — Age 21 — Questions about maps

Our instructor wanted us to compare maps made by people with different purposes. A road engineer, a wildlife biologist, and a hiker can draw the same place and make different things visible. This pleased me enormously. It isn't necessarily deception. Attention is selective. We spent the afternoon labeling features and arguing about scale. I walked home thinking about family stories and how every person at a dinner table could tell a different true account of the same evening. If I ever become impossible to live with, it may be because I am still asking what the map leaves out.

------------------------------

ENTRY 363 — 2007-10-06 — Age 21 — A stranger at synagogue

An older visitor sat beside me after services and asked about my parents. When I told her my father's name she remembered the firm, then told me something wonderfully mundane: he once stood in a hallway with his shoes wet through and refused to leave until a young associate had finished explaining a complicated case. I could see him so clearly. She had no idea how grateful I was. I wrote down the details when I got home. The big things are in the newspapers and files. It is the small things I am afraid of losing.

------------------------------

ENTRY 364 — 2007-10-12 — Age 21 — A date with too many questions

I went for coffee with a pleasant young man who became very curious about the house on Bainbridge, the family firm, and the money behind a scholarship event. Perhaps he was only making conversation. I answered gently and redirected us to books and hiking. We did have a good conversation about both. I came home uncertain whether I'd been unfair in my thoughts. I want to be open. I also know that some questions arrive far too early to be innocent. I haven't decided which kind these were. I hope I won't have to.

------------------------------

ENTRY 365 — 2007-10-18 — Age 21 — The small botanical victory

The plants we set into a neglected strip earlier in the year are doing better than I expected. Several have not survived. One that looked hopeless in July has produced new growth. I wrote the numbers down before letting myself be pleased. A volunteer asked whether she should pull everything that wasn't on the plan. I told her to wait until we knew what it was. There's an admirable temptation to tidy an unruly patch of ground. Sometimes the result is simply destruction with a clean edge.

------------------------------

ENTRY 366 — 2007-10-25 — Age 21 — Three versions of the same story

At supper Mother told a story about her college days, then interrupted herself twice to correct the year and the order of events. I know she was in Seattle by 1958 and that the world she entered was not the one her Brooklyn family expected. She laughed about the way she dressed and then grew thoughtful about a friend she hadn't seen for decades. I asked whether she missed those years. She said she missed some people and some chances, but not the uncertainty. I wrote it down because I hadn't expected that answer from her.

------------------------------

ENTRY 367 — 2007-11-01 — Age 21 — A cold anger

A friend confessed she'd been telling an acquaintance about another woman's private troubles because she thought the stories were entertaining. I asked her to stop. She laughed and said I was being too serious. Something in me became very still. I told her it wasn't her story to sell, even for the price of attention, and I left. Later I was troubled by how cold my voice had sounded. I do not regret what I said. I regret that she couldn't understand why it mattered. I haven't called her yet. I may not.

------------------------------

ENTRY 368 — 2007-11-07 — Age 21 — An apology with no audience

I apologized to the person I had spoken harshly to in the spring. She looked surprised and said she'd almost forgotten it. I didn't tell her I had rehearsed the conversation three times while walking to the meeting. She accepted the apology and we talked about an assignment. Nothing grand happened. There was no music swelling in the background and no lesson announced. I walked away lighter anyway. I hope I remember that doing the right thing sometimes feels ordinary once it's done.

------------------------------

ENTRY 369 — 2007-11-14 — Age 21 — Numbers and names

The charitable budget came up again. I have begun separating the things I care about passionately from the things I am merely flattered to be asked to support. Those categories are not always obvious at first. I would rather fund good habitat work quietly than have my name announced at every gathering. Yet I do think institutions need dependable supporters, and the synagogue has given our family a community for years. I am trying to learn generosity that doesn't depend on being admired. I suspect this will take practice.

------------------------------

ENTRY 370 — 2007-11-21 — Age 21 — The broken umbrella

My umbrella turned inside out near the bus stop. I fought it briefly, lost, and arrived at lunch looking like a damp dishcloth. A friend said she couldn't decide whether I was brilliant or helpless. I told her that people could manage both. We spent the afternoon studying together, and I understood one difficult statistical problem only after she explained it using beads from a broken bracelet. I bought her coffee and thanked her properly. I have learned more from people who can explain things simply than from people who can make them sound complicated.

------------------------------

ENTRY 371 — 2007-11-29 — Age 21 — The music room

Mother asked me to play something after dinner. I chose a piece Daddy liked, then abandoned it halfway and played something else. I felt disloyal for about ten seconds, which is absurd. He was never precious about music; he loved hearing people discover what they could do. Mother listened with her eyes closed. When I stopped, she told me that he used to practice after everyone else had gone to bed because he didn't want the house to hear him making mistakes. That may be the best thing anyone has told me about his musicianship.

------------------------------

ENTRY 372 — 2007-12-04 — Age 21 — A serious paper

I spent the evening reading two papers that disagreed about whether a particular restoration method had worked. They didn't disagree quite as much as the abstracts made it seem. The disagreement lived in the assumptions, the sampling, and the choice of what to measure. I marked up the margins and felt more awake at midnight than I had at noon. I know many people would find this excruciating. I find it soothing when an argument can be examined carefully instead of won by whoever speaks most confidently.

------------------------------

ENTRY 373 — 2007-12-11 — Age 21 — The animal shelter

I spent several hours helping at the shelter and came home smelling of disinfectant and dog. A young animal was frightened of everyone and wouldn't take food while I stood nearby. I backed away, sat down, and waited. Nothing happened for a long while. Then it ate. I don't know if my waiting helped or whether it had simply become hungry enough. It would be easy to invent a story in which it trusted me. I would rather know the truth, even if the truth is less flattering. Still, I was glad it ate.

------------------------------

ENTRY 374 — 2007-12-17 — Age 21 — The professor's recommendation

A professor said that if I intended to continue in this field, I needed to learn to write clearly for people who weren't scientists. I said I had grown up listening to legal arguments and thought I knew something about persuasive language. She said excellent, then asked whether I could explain our work in one paragraph to a neighbor. I attempted it and failed magnificently. She let me try again. I am now writing a second version of my report without assuming the reader knows every technical word. I wish I'd learned this a year earlier.

------------------------------

ENTRY 375 — 2007-12-23 — Age 21 — An almost empty ferry

The ferry was quieter than usual, and Seattle looked sharp against the cold sky. I thought about people moving back and forth between the city and the island, each carrying private errands and enormous hopes that look the same from a distance. I bought a small gift for Mother and a used book for myself. I was supposed to buy wrapping paper and forgot. The gift is presently wrapped in pages of an old map, which I think looks lovely. Mother will probably ask why I couldn't simply use paper like everyone else. I will tell her the mountains deserved another journey.

------------------------------

ENTRY 376 — 2007-12-31 — Age 21 — What did I do this year?

I counted my entries and was startled by how many small things I'd nearly forgotten already: the stranded bird, the wet notebooks, the look on Mother's face when the Swiss shopkeeper spoke about Daddy, the kindness of a stranger at the coffee shop, the shelter animal finally eating. I also remember my birthday and the difficult conversations and the classes that made me work harder than I expected. I used to think important years announced themselves loudly. This one was a steady accumulation of small decisions. I am twenty-one, still learning, still a little too quick to trust, and more grateful than I know how to say. I want to keep writing, especially when nothing remarkable happens.
