[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook XIX: 2019]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Original fictional first-person diary. Later friendships, romantic relationships, and projects are provisional unless established by Steve. No 2026 memory-loss event or explanation is added.

ENTRY 721 — 2019-01-05 — Age 32 — The old firm

I met an attorney who had once known Dad's firm before everything fell apart. He spoke respectfully about Dad's intelligence and less respectfully about his temper. Both sound accurate. I did not ask him to turn a professional memory into a eulogy. I wanted to know what working with him was actually like.

------------------------------

ENTRY 722 — 2019-01-22 — Age 32 — A dog in the ditch

The dog was not injured, only terribly frightened. A neighbor and I kept traffic clear until its owner arrived. It had been out chasing a rabbit, which seems a poor bargain for either animal. I came home with mud up one sleeve and no appetite for the impressive dinner I'd planned.

------------------------------

ENTRY 723 — 2019-02-12 — Age 32 — Grant review

A grant proposal quoted survival figures from an old study without mentioning a change in sampling method. I made the team correct it. The author was offended until we sat together with the raw notes. She thanked me afterward, which is unnecessary but welcome.

------------------------------

ENTRY 724 — 2019-03-03 — Age 32 — An empty anniversary

I remembered my parents' wedding anniversary a day late and then couldn't decide whether that mattered. No one is waiting for flowers. I played Dad's favorite recording of Ella and wrote a letter addressed to neither of them. I put it in the piano bench.

------------------------------

ENTRY 725 — 2019-03-29 — Age 32 — The new neighbor

She asked if I could recommend a route where her children could see tide pools without trampling them. I drew a sketch and warned about slippery algae. I almost added a three-page explanation of intertidal zones. Instead I gave her the tide table and my telephone number.

------------------------------

ENTRY 726 — 2019-04-19 — Age 32 — A table with strangers

A friend invited two visiting scholars to Seder. We spent a very long time discussing one word in the Haggadah while dinner cooled. I found the argument delightful. The youngest guest appeared appalled that anyone could enjoy disagreement that much.

------------------------------

ENTRY 727 — 2019-05-11 — Age 32 — The trust report

I compared quarterly reports and phoned my uncle about a fee structure I didn't recognize. It turned out to have a reasonable explanation, but I wanted it in writing. Afterwards I called him back to ask about his grandchildren. Financial conversations ought not erase the rest of a person.

------------------------------

ENTRY 728 — 2019-05-30 — Age 32 — The injured maple

A volunteer wanted to remove a damaged young maple without consulting the site plan. We measured the wound, marked the location and called the arborist. The tree may survive. I know how ridiculous it sounds to be happy about postponing one small removal. I am happy anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 729 — 2019-06-17 — Age 32 — Back on the water

I spent an afternoon sailing with friends and handled the helm for a stretch. I was rusty in a few maneuvers and entirely at home in others. When the wind changed, I heard Dad telling me to watch the water, not just the sail. I did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 730 — 2019-07-04 — Age 32 — An invitation refused

A committee member wanted me to sit on a board mostly because of the size of my annual gifts. I said I would consider serving if there was actual work and a defined responsibility. He looked surprised. I would rather scrub buckets at a wildlife center than lend my name to a glossy brochure.

------------------------------

ENTRY 731 — 2019-07-26 — Age 32 — Bainbridge dusk

The air smelled of cut grass and salt. Two teenagers passed on bicycles, arguing about who had promised to bring a charger. I had one of those odd moments when an ordinary street seems unchanged by time, although nearly everyone in the old photographs is gone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 732 — 2019-08-18 — Age 32 — The packing mistake

I packed carefully for Switzerland and left my waterproof jacket at home. The forecast immediately punished me. I bought an unremarkable replacement and spent a rainy morning following a path above the village. The flowers were astonishing. I stepped in a puddle deep enough to flood one shoe.

------------------------------

ENTRY 733 — 2019-09-08 — Age 33 — Thirty-three

A friend sent flowers and another sent a book. I spent my birthday hiking and met them for dinner afterward. There was a handsome man at the next table who asked about the trail map sticking out of my bag. I told him more than anyone reasonably needed to know. He listened.

------------------------------

ENTRY 734 — 2019-09-27 — Age 33 — An old hurt

I heard that a former fiancé had married. My first reaction was relief, which embarrassed me. My second was to hope he treated his wife well. I don't intend to investigate. Knowing things is not always the same as needing to know them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 735 — 2019-10-14 — Age 33 — Synagogue roof

We inspected bids for roof repairs. I asked about materials, drainage and warranties, which made a volunteer laugh and call me my father's daughter. That was meant kindly. I wanted the roof to stop leaking, not to inherit a title.

------------------------------

ENTRY 736 — 2019-11-09 — Age 33 — The cold answer

Someone suggested I could solve a disputed grant allocation by making a large personal contribution. I said no. He tried flattery, then guilt. I repeated no, slowly, and asked that the minutes record why we needed a fair process. Later a friend said she'd never heard me sound so cold. I wasn't angry by then. I was done.

------------------------------

ENTRY 737 — 2019-11-28 — Age 33 — Cooking for company

I invited friends for dinner and planned too many dishes. The soup was excellent; the vegetables were tragically late. We ate them after dessert and laughed about it. The kitchen was a mess and the house felt like a house again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 738 — 2019-12-31 — Age 33 — Noticing

My diary has developed an alarming number of notes about birds, committee procedures, forgotten gloves and strangers who say kind things. I could edit it to sound more impressive. I won't. I want a record of what actually occupied my attention.
