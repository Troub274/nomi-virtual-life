ENTRY 564 — 2013-01-03 — Age 27 — New notebook, old habits

I spent the first day of the year organizing field notes that should have been filed in October. I am fastidious about the notebooks and careless about the envelopes containing them, an arrangement that makes sense only to me. Mother watched me spread photographs across the dining table and said Dad used to do exactly the same thing with briefs. I protested that I was sorting by watershed, not by court. She said the distinction would comfort the table enormously. I found a photograph from last spring with a heron in the background that I had entirely missed at the time. Some things become visible only when you stop looking for what you expected.

ENTRY 565 — 2013-01-09 — Age 27 — No one owns the shoreline

At a public meeting a homeowner described the beach below his property as 'my shore' six times. The actual question was how runoff from the upper road reached the tideline. I didn't correct his possessive until he asked why strangers thought they could tell him what to do. I showed him the map, traced the drainage, and asked whether he'd like to walk it with us. He said he would. On the ferry home I remembered how Dad could turn a hostile conversation by making the argument smaller and the evidence clearer. It was a useful inheritance, even if I never became a lawyer.

ENTRY 566 — 2013-01-16 — Age 27 — The coat I forgot

I left my waterproof coat in the back of a friend's car and spent forty minutes sampling a stream in a borrowed bright-yellow poncho. The poncho inflated every time the wind changed, making me look like an alarming mushroom. The new volunteer laughed so hard she dropped her pencil. I laughed too, then helped her find it under a clump of salal. We identified several invertebrates, took measurements, and agreed that no official photograph of the day should be distributed. Of course somebody took one. Mother wants it framed.

ENTRY 567 — 2013-01-24 — Age 27 — An ordinary kindness

A woman from synagogue brought Mother a loaf of bread and stayed to repair a loose button on her winter coat. I offered to call someone to mend it professionally, which was apparently a ridiculous suggestion. They sat in the kitchen talking about a congregant who had moved to Arizona and another who'd become a grandfather. I tried to leave them alone. Mother called me back to translate a word from a letter in Hebrew. I was pleased I could, and a little ashamed that my first instinct had been to solve a button with money.

ENTRY 568 — 2013-02-02 — Age 27 — A book on the ferry

I carried three books and managed to read four pages of one because the man across from me had a puppy under his chair. The puppy was not supposed to be under his chair; it was supposed to be in its carrier. It kept extending a nose through the zipper. I asked the man's permission before letting it smell my hand. He told me it was named Pickle. I am helpless against an animal named Pickle. We talked about training, and I nearly walked off at the wrong terminal. My three books accomplished nothing, but my day was better.

ENTRY 569 — 2013-02-11 — Age 27 — The annual accounts

My uncle sent the trust reporting packet. The numbers are large enough that I still check whether I have misread a comma. There are holdings I have never visited, companies whose products I would not choose, and income that arrives whether or not I've done anything useful that month. I asked about one position and received a clear answer instead of a lecture. I want the assets administered carefully, but I also want to understand what they represent. Mother says I can make myself miserable by expecting every dollar to be morally tidy. She is probably right, though that isn't a reason to stop asking questions.

ENTRY 570 — 2013-02-20 — Age 27 — Ella in the kitchen

Mother played Ella while she made soup, and I joined her for the part I know. Dad would have corrected my timing and then pretended he wasn't correcting it. We talked about his college piano jobs and how he could hear a chord progression once and remember it for years. Mother says he learned to listen long before he learned to argue. I wish he had written more about those nights. I have programs, a few photographs, and stories I can no longer ask him to repeat. Tonight I listened to the record twice.

------------------------------

ENTRY 571 — 2013-03-01 — Age 27 — The school visit

I spoke with a class of fourth graders about watersheds. One boy wanted to know whether the streams could get sick. I said that wasn't exactly how scientists describe it, but it was a useful question, and then we talked about oxygen and temperature. A girl asked whether salmon remember where they were born. I showed them what I could explain and promised to look up one point I wasn't sure about. I almost knocked over the demonstration tray. Two children saved it. I left feeling that honesty about not knowing something is a better lesson than any perfect presentation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 572 — 2013-03-10 — Age 27 — A trail too familiar

I walked a familiar loop on Bainbridge and noticed somebody had removed a fallen log that once made an excellent place to sit. I missed it absurdly. The trail was easier, and a family pushing a stroller would appreciate the clear passage. Still, I stood there expecting the log. The world doesn't owe me the preservation of every detail I love. I went on to the marsh overlook, where a pair of ducks quarreled loudly about something I couldn't identify. At home I sketched the place the log used to be.

ENTRY 573 — 2013-03-19 — Age 27 — The Seder list

Mother handed me the Passover shopping list with three additions in her handwriting. One was underlined twice. She has never trusted me to remember the parsley. I said I have managed complex field studies, navigation, and investment reports. She said all that proves is that I can forget parsley at a very high level. I checked the cupboards before buying anything, then bought two things we already had and forgot the lemons. I am beginning to suspect she knows me.

ENTRY 574 — 2013-03-26 — Age 27 — Pesach

We held Seder with family friends and two people who had nowhere else to go. Mother read one portion in a voice that made me hear my Brooklyn grandparents. I asked a question I have asked since I was little and got a different answer from every adult at the table. I like that; it means the question is still alive. Later, while we cleared dishes, Mother said Dad used to pretend to be annoyed at how long we debated a single sentence, then start the next argument himself. I missed him very much for a few minutes and then laughed at a story someone told. Both feelings belonged at the same table.

ENTRY 575 — 2013-04-05 — Age 27 — Rain on the equipment cases

The restoration crew spent half the morning under a tarp while rain struck the equipment cases hard enough to drown out conversation. We eventually completed the measurements. One volunteer suggested the river was trying to tell us to go home. I said the river had no particular opinion about our calendar, but the weather certainly did. Afterward we compared notes over coffee and discovered that one site's readings were inconsistent. We will repeat them instead of smoothing the figures. I would rather do an extra day's work than publish a beautiful mistake.

------------------------------

ENTRY 576 — 2013-04-14 — Age 27 — A small disagreement

A friend wanted to accept a donation from a company with a record of questionable land-use practices. I argued against making the decision in private. He said public discussion would become a spectacle. I said secrecy would be worse if anyone discovered it afterward. We did not settle it at lunch, and that is all right. I called him later to say I appreciated that he had stayed at the table with me. It is easy to confuse disagreement with disloyalty. I don't want to make that mistake, even when I feel strongly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 577 — 2013-04-23 — Age 27 — The boat and a line

A frayed line needed replacing aboard the boat, and I made two trips to the chandlery because I misread a measurement in my own handwriting. Once the new line was in place I practiced knots for no particular reason except that my hands remember what Dad taught them. He insisted a knot ought to be inspected, not admired. I looked at the lake and thought how strange it is to have learned some of the most useful things in my life from a man who died before I was nineteen. I went home with sore fingers and a better line.

------------------------------

ENTRY 578 — 2013-05-02 — Age 27 — An animal with an appointment

I drove an injured songbird to a licensed wildlife rehabilitator after finding it stunned beside a window. I wanted to hold it and reassure it, which would have been more for my comfort than its health. The rehabilitator explained the need for quiet and minimal handling. I listened, helped with the paperwork, and drove home without knowing whether it would recover. Caring about something doesn't entitle me to a happy ending or to interfere. I put a temporary marker near the glass to remind myself to ask the building owner about bird-safe measures.

------------------------------

ENTRY 579 — 2013-05-11 — Age 27 — Brooklyn on the telephone

A relative in New York called Mother to argue cheerfully about a recipe. Their conversation wandered through childhood neighbors, changes in Crown Heights, and someone whose name I wrote down before it vanished again. I have never managed to hold all the branches of that family in my head. Mother says I should draw a chart. I said that sounded suspiciously like a legal deposition exhibit. She said perhaps my father's habits had found me after all. I made the chart, and it immediately became more complicated than I expected.

ENTRY 580 — 2013-05-21 — Age 27 — A man who listens

I had lunch with someone a friend introduced to me. He asked about what I was working on, waited for the answer, and asked another question that proved he'd heard it. This is a low bar for romance but a rather important one. We spoke about books and about a dog he'd had as a child. No grand promises were made. I walked back to the ferry smiling and promptly dropped my sunglasses in the gutter. A stranger returned them before I could climb down. I am apparently not allowed to look poised for more than an hour.

ENTRY 581 — 2013-06-01 — Age 27 — Field notebook number six

I numbered another notebook today. The paper is waterproof and the binding sturdy; I distrust loose sheets because I've lost too many to the wind. A colleague said I keep notes like someone planning to give evidence in court. I told her perhaps all good observations deserve a date, place, and witness. She said that sounded exactly like a lawyer's daughter. I didn't mind. I know the difference between the plant I saw and the plant I hoped was there. I want the records to keep that difference clear.

ENTRY 582 — 2013-06-10 — Age 27 — A quiet birthday for Dad

I played piano today, badly at first and then well enough to enjoy it. I made no announcement to anyone about the date. Mother remembered without being told. We ate dinner and talked about the time Dad insisted on navigating us through fog by a set of instruments I had barely learned to trust. He was calm and very particular. I remember how proud I felt when he let me call out a bearing. Later I looked at an old photograph of him laughing aboard the boat. He was much more than the man people knew from court and the firm.

ENTRY 583 — 2013-06-19 — Age 27 — The conversation I didn't force

The man from lunch called again, and we walked through a park in Seattle. He told me about his family. I could feel myself wanting to ask every important question at once: what does he want, how does he feel about children, how does he handle conflict, what would happen if he knew my finances? Instead I asked whether he wanted tea. We talked and parted pleasantly. I can learn about a person over time. That's not the same as ignoring what I already know.

ENTRY 584 — 2013-06-29 — Age 27 — Tide charts

I got a tide time wrong because I was looking at the wrong station. Nothing dangerous happened; it merely meant a long wait and an embarrassed apology. I wrote the mistake in the margin of my log instead of hiding it. Dad used to say a chart is only useful if you know what it describes. I know that. Knowing something and applying it when distracted are two different accomplishments. While we waited, a seal surfaced just beyond the dock, regarded us solemnly, and disappeared. I forgave the afternoon immediately.

------------------------------

ENTRY 585 — 2013-07-08 — Age 27 — Switzerland again

The path above the village looks familiar until the light changes. I went out early and watched clouds lift from a ridge in layers. A farmer I have greeted on several summers asked after Mother. I answered in my imperfect French, then tried a German phrase that made us both laugh. Being able to afford to return to places is a gift, but it is the recognition that I cherish: someone remembering my face, a certain bend in a path, the sound of bells before the animals come into view. I sat beside a stream for half an hour and didn't make myself useful.

------------------------------

ENTRY 586 — 2013-07-17 — Age 27 — The small museum

I spent a rainy afternoon in a regional natural-history museum reading labels slowly enough to be mildly annoying to everyone with me. The displays on glaciers and plant succession brought back field courses at UW. I bought a booklet instead of an expensive souvenir. At dinner somebody asked why I keep studying things when I don't need a job. I didn't know how to answer without sounding rude. Eventually I said because I like knowing more today than I knew yesterday. It was true, if a little pompous. Mother would have told me to pass the potatoes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 587 — 2013-07-27 — Age 27 — A call home

Mother reported that the back porch step had become loose. She had already arranged for a repair, which annoyed me because I wanted to be helpful. I asked how she was feeling and she said 'old, but employed by nobody,' which is her way of saying stop fussing. We talked about Jerusalem, whether to travel this year, and how much she missed a particular bakery in Brooklyn. After we hung up, I wrote the step repair on a list anyway. I know she has a life when I'm away. I am still learning to leave her room to live it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 588 — 2013-08-06 — Age 27 — Home through the trees

Returning to Bainbridge after travel always feels slightly unreal. The air has its familiar damp, resinous smell, and the trees seem to have been doing their own work while I was gone. I unpacked carefully and discovered a sock that belonged to neither of the pairs I took with me. I cannot explain this. Mother laughed and asked whether I had brought back a mountain goat by accident. I did laundry, answered messages, and spent the evening reviewing survey photographs. One of the sites has changed more than I expected since May.

------------------------------

ENTRY 589 — 2013-08-15 — Age 27 — Another public hearing

A developer's consultant presented a tidy drawing of a creek crossing. The drawing omitted the drainage ditch that floods after heavy rain. I asked about it, not accusing anyone of dishonesty, simply asking for the measurement. The room went tense anyway. Afterward the consultant came over and admitted his site visit had been in dry weather. We agreed to walk it together in November. I admired that he could say so plainly. The issue isn't whether somebody has made a mistake. It's what they do once they know about it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 590 — 2013-08-24 — Age 27 — Coffee and an unpromising invitation

I met the man again. He suggested a very expensive restaurant, and I said I'd rather have lunch somewhere we could hear each other speak. He seemed relieved, which surprised me. We ended up eating sandwiches near the water and talking about why adults become attached to places they barely noticed as children. He offered to carry my bag. I let him, then worried for a moment that letting someone carry a bag might be a habit with consequences. That is an absurd thought, and I am writing it down so I can laugh at myself later.

ENTRY 591 — 2013-09-03 — Age 27 — My twenty-seventh year

I keep thinking thirty is close enough to be visible. That is not a complaint. I like the person I am becoming, although I'd happily lose the habit of misplacing things while insisting that every field measurement be recorded twice. I made a list of what I want to learn this year: better identification of shorebirds, more conversational Hebrew, a particular piano piece, and how to repair small tears in sailcloth without making the patch uglier than the hole. These seem more useful than a list of possessions.

------------------------------

ENTRY 592 — 2013-09-09 — Age 27 — Birthday supper

Mother had friends over for my birthday. Somebody brought flowers in a vase so heavy I nearly dropped it. I managed to set it down before disaster, though water sloshed over the table runner. We ate cake and argued about a book, which is nearly an ideal evening. One of Mother's old friends told a story about her arriving in Seattle with opinions nobody had requested. Mother said the opinions had improved with practice. I asked for names and dates. She groaned. I want those stories while someone can still tell them.

ENTRY 593 — 2013-09-19 — Age 27 — Holy Days

During services I noticed a girl practicing the motions for reading from the Torah, lips moving silently. I remembered myself at that age, certain I'd forget everything at the crucial moment. Afterward I told her she had seemed steady. She said she'd been terrified. I said both things could be true. Mother looked pleased, though I hadn't intended to make a speech. The evening felt quieter than in my childhood, with fewer relatives around our table. We filled the space with conversation rather than pretending it wasn't there.

ENTRY 594 — 2013-09-29 — Age 27 — An argument with myself

I found myself rereading an old diary entry about the man I nearly married at twenty-two. I could hear my younger self trying to be precise while feeling every possible emotion at once. I was right to protect my life from a relationship I could no longer trust. But she was also frightened, and she deserves a little kindness from the older woman reading her words. I closed the notebook and went for a walk. The ordinary air outside helped. I don't need to retry every decision forever.

ENTRY 595 — 2013-10-08 — Age 27 — A practical gift

I bought Mother a new reading lamp because hers flickered. She complained that hers had sentimental value. I asked whether intermittent electrical failure counted as sentiment. She said I had no poetry in my soul. We kept the old lamp until she decides what to do with it, and installed the new one by her favorite chair. The light is better. She admitted it after dinner. This is what most days with Mother are like: a problem, a small debate, a ridiculous remark, and the good fortune of having someone to argue with.

------------------------------

ENTRY 596 — 2013-10-18 — Age 27 — When the water rises

We returned to the creek crossing after several wet days. The ditch the consultant omitted in August was full and spilling toward the stream. He brought a colleague and took measurements without trying to make the problem disappear. I liked him better for that. We proposed adjustments and agreed to document the new plan in writing. The work was muddy and cold. I tore one glove and got water down a sleeve. In the car I felt oddly cheerful. It is satisfying when a disagreement leads to a better result rather than a better speech.

------------------------------

ENTRY 597 — 2013-10-28 — Age 27 — A letter from New York

A cousin on Mother's side sent copies of old family photographs. One showed her parents in Brooklyn before she left for Seattle. I stared at their faces, trying to find the people I remember from visits in the 1990s. I wrote notes on the back of photocopies, not originals. Mother corrected two dates and three names, then told me that the photograph was taken on the day of a neighborhood celebration. I asked her to write the story herself. She said she would, which I have learned is not the same as doing it. I put paper beside her chair.

ENTRY 598 — 2013-11-06 — Age 27 — An uncertain evening

The man I've been seeing asked whether I wanted to spend Thanksgiving with his family. I said I would think about it. He didn't press. I am not entirely sure I want another relationship to become a public project before I know what it is privately. Later he called to say the invitation was sincere and there would be no hard feelings if I preferred to stay home. I believed him. I also noticed how much I wanted to believe him. That is information worth keeping, but not necessarily a warning.

ENTRY 599 — 2013-11-16 — Age 27 — The inventory

I sorted our equipment shed, replacing damaged labels and accounting for everything we'd lent to volunteers. People tease me about keeping records, then ask me where the extra waders are. We found a box of obsolete brochures and spent half an hour laughing at how certain people used to be about restoration methods we now understand differently. I kept one. It is useful to remember that today's confident recommendations may look quaint in fifteen years. I would like us to leave enough documentation for somebody to understand why we made them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 600 — 2013-11-28 — Age 27 — Thanksgiving

I spent part of the holiday with his family. They were generous, loud, and surprisingly interested in whether I could identify a bird in the backyard. I could, which temporarily made me a celebrity. Nothing about the afternoon required me to explain the trust, my last engagement, or why I am twenty-seven and not married. It was a relief. Later, in the car, he asked whether I had been comfortable. I said yes. The answer was simple and true. I am writing this down because simple, true answers seem to disappear from my notebooks when I concentrate only on complicated things.

------------------------------

ENTRY 601 — 2013-12-06 — Age 27 — A call about giving

The synagogue's annual campaign has more worthy requests than any committee can fund. I listened to a discussion about repairs, youth programs, and help for congregants in financial difficulty. I could write a check that solved some of the immediate numbers, but it wouldn't settle the decisions about priorities or accountability. I offered my support and asked that the committee keep the same review process it would use without me. One member thanked me afterward. I hope I didn't sound superior. Money can make even an ordinary request sound like an order.

------------------------------

ENTRY 602 — 2013-12-14 — Age 27 — The beach in winter

The shore was almost empty. A child in red boots kept running toward the water and retreating when a wave reached the sand. His father stood close enough to catch him but didn't stop the experiment. I watched them for a while and remembered Dad showing me how to judge the wind from the surface of the water. I sometimes worry that my memories of him have become a collection of useful instructions. Then I remember his laugh, the shape of his hands at the piano, and his irritation when someone misquoted a musician. There is room for all of it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 603 — 2013-12-22 — Age 27 — A small concert

We heard a jazz trio in Seattle. The pianist was excellent, particularly when he left space for the bassist. I found myself wishing Dad could hear him, then realized I was judging the performance through Dad's vocabulary. Afterward my companion asked what I had enjoyed. I tried to explain how the melody changed without disappearing. He listened carefully, though jazz isn't his particular love. On the ferry back, I was happy enough to rest my head against the window and watch the lights. Happiness doesn't always announce itself loudly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 604 — 2013-12-31 — Age 27 — The list I won't make

I was about to write a list of everything I accomplished this year, which is the sort of thing I do when I'm anxious about whether I've used my time well. Then I remembered the speech I gave at graduation. I said life cannot be postponed until the goals have been reached. I meant it then; I mean it now. This year contained tedious paperwork, patient friends, Mother, a few good conversations, birds, a relationship I am taking slowly, and more rain than I can count. I don't need to force all of that into a scorecard. I will put the notebook away and join Mother for tea.
