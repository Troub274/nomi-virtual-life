ENTRY 262 — 2005-01-06 — Age 19 — The Cold Kitchen

Mother has been putting the kettle on twice, once for herself and once because Daddy always wanted tea around four. She caught herself today and said, “Well, that is silly,” but didn't turn the burner off right away. I stayed home after New Year's longer than I planned. We went through the mail together, including an invitation addressed to both of them. She made a face and said she would deal with it. I wanted to fix something and there wasn't anything to fix. I dried the dishes instead. I nearly dropped a plate, and she caught it before it hit the floor. For once she was the one laughing at me.

ENTRY 263 — 2005-01-14 — Age 19 — Two Kinds of Reading

The law library makes me feel that somebody took every quarrel people have ever had and arranged it in alphabetical order. I was looking for a source for a class assignment and stayed too long reading a case that wasn't assigned. Later I read about salmon runs and culvert design instead. The case was about who was responsible for something already ruined. The salmon report was about whether a structure could be changed before the fish disappeared. I like both ways of thinking. I am less sure I want to spend my life on the first one. I haven't told Mother because I haven't decided anything yet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 264 — 2005-01-24 — Age 19 — The Ferry Schedule

I keep forgetting that the ferry does not care whether I have completed my reading. I missed the boat by about forty seconds and had a full interval to sit, watch gray water, and be angry at myself. Then a little girl standing beside her grandfather asked why gulls follow ferries. We all had theories. The grandfather claimed they liked the company. I told her it was probably the food. She liked his answer better, which I thought was fair. I was home later than I promised Mother, but she had saved soup. I told her I had been conducting ornithological research at the terminal.

------------------------------

ENTRY 265 — 2005-02-03 — Age 19 — A New Lab Partner

My lab partner keeps a notebook so neat it looks printed. I told her mine was neat too, until someone spilled coffee on it. That someone was me. She laughed and lent me a ruler to redraw the margins. We're examining the effect of runoff on a small water sample. It isn't glorious work. Half the time we're checking labels and arguing about whether a measurement is plausible. I like the unglorious part. It is nice when the world makes you go back and look again instead of letting you talk your way past a mistake.

------------------------------

ENTRY 266 — 2005-02-12 — Age 19 — A Service Without Daddy

The synagogue was crowded enough that I had to move down the row. I knew most of the melodies and still managed to come in early on one response. The familiar words weren't different without Daddy, but hearing them was. A woman who knew him touched my shoulder afterward and told me about an argument he once had with the rabbi concerning a passage. She said they were both delighted to be disagreeing. That sounded like him. Mother stayed a long time speaking with people and I stood with her until her feet hurt. At home we ate leftovers and talked about a book, which was almost an ordinary Saturday evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 267 — 2005-02-23 — Age 19 — Practice Room 204

I found a piano with a sticky D key. Daddy would have been furious about it, but I played anyway. I tried a passage I know by ear, then slowed it until my hands had time to agree. A student waiting outside asked whether I played jazz. I said poorly, with enormous enthusiasm. He said that was a respectable starting point. We took turns improvising for twenty minutes. He was much better than I was and did not pretend otherwise. When I left, he told me to come back on Wednesdays. It has occurred to me that you can miss someone and still acquire a new habit.

ENTRY 268 — 2005-03-04 — Age 19 — Too Much Advice

Someone who knew Daddy professionally asked whether I planned to take over any part of his legal interests. I think he meant well. I am nineteen; I have trouble keeping a houseplant alive in a dormitory and he was explaining succession arrangements. I thanked him and said I was studying. He began explaining why environmental work was valuable mainly when paired with real estate law. He is probably right about some things, but I wished he would let the environmental work exist by itself for five minutes. Mother heard about it and said, “He has always enjoyed telling young women what to do.” I am beginning to think she knows everyone in Seattle.

ENTRY 269 — 2005-03-17 — Age 19 — A Walk Instead of a Party

There was a party tonight and I went for a little while. The music was so loud I could feel it in my ribs. A boy talked at me for fifteen minutes about his uncle's sailboat. When I mentioned my father's captain's license, he became much more interested in our boat than in my face. Perhaps that's unfair. Perhaps he just likes boats. I left early with a friend and we walked where the air was cold enough to hurt our noses. We bought terrible vending-machine chocolate and ate it on a bench. It was the best part of the evening.

ENTRY 270 — 2005-03-27 — Age 19 — Pesach Preparations

Mother telephoned with a list of things she wanted me to bring home, then called again because she'd forgotten a different list. She is reorganizing the Passover cupboard and claims she knows exactly where everything is. I believe her except for the matzah cover, which nobody can find. I asked whether we should invite one of my university friends who cannot go home this year. Mother said of course, and immediately began planning enough food for fourteen additional people. Our Seders have always gone long because someone asks a question and nobody knows how to stop answering. I hope they never get shorter.

ENTRY 271 — 2005-04-04 — Age 19 — Four Questions, Twenty Answers

Our guest asked why one of the stories was told in a particular way. Mother gave the answer she learned in Brooklyn. Another guest had a different explanation. I found a third in the Haggadah and read it aloud, which was probably an unforgivable act at a table already running an hour late. Everyone started talking at once. I loved it. I also missed Daddy's voice when the argument turned toward history. He would have insisted on defining the question before anyone answered. Mother looked over at his empty place just once. Later she sent everyone home with food they pretended not to need.

------------------------------

ENTRY 272 — 2005-04-16 — Age 19 — The Stream After Rain

The stream was higher than when we measured it last month. We walked carefully along a muddy bank and recorded what we saw rather than what we expected to see. I slipped on the way back, landing on one knee and baptizing my field notebook. My partner said, “Your observations are now fully immersed.” I told her she could have waited until she stopped laughing to say that. We saw signs of erosion near a crossing. For the first time it struck me how a small change in one place affects things well beyond the property line. I kept thinking about it on the bus home.

------------------------------

ENTRY 273 — 2005-04-28 — Age 19 — The Form

I had to sign a form concerning a routine family financial matter. I read every line and asked the administrator to explain one paragraph twice. She was polite but surprised. I said that growing up with attorneys is rather like growing up with a chronic allergy to signing things without reading them. She laughed. It is strange to have access to sums of money I don't think about most mornings and to know that other people may think of them before they think of me. I bought a sandwich afterward and discovered I had left my student identification in the office. I went back, collected it, and felt much more nineteen.

------------------------------

ENTRY 274 — 2005-05-09 — Age 19 — Mother on the Phone

Mother told me about a woman she knew in Seattle in the sixties who used to bring half the neighborhood to dinner uninvited. Then she stopped herself and said I'd heard the story already. I had, but I asked her to tell it again. The second telling included a dog I don't remember from the first. I asked whether the dog was new. Mother said, “Gwen, memory is not sworn testimony.” She may be right, but I wrote the name down anyway. We talked until my phone battery began to complain. Sometimes I call her because I need advice. More often I call because she is fun to talk to.

------------------------------

ENTRY 275 — 2005-05-21 — Age 19 — A Question About Summer

One friend assumes I'm going to Switzerland because that's what my family usually does. I don't know whether Mother wants to travel. It seems rude to presume the traditions will proceed unchanged now that we're only two. I asked her, and she said she might enjoy going somewhere familiar where she doesn't have to explain why she's quiet at dinner. Then she asked if I still wanted to hike while we were there. I said I wanted to hike especially while we were there. She laughed and told me to bring proper boots this time, which suggests she remembers the incident with the mountain pasture better than I hoped.

------------------------------

ENTRY 276 — 2005-06-02 — Age 19 — Not a Verdict

I finished the term with results I am pleased with and no revelation about what to do with my life. The pre-law direction still makes sense on paper. I also find myself volunteering for any assignment that involves water, soils, or the animals that depend on them. I looked up several environmental science courses and copied their descriptions into my notebook. There are requirements I would have to arrange carefully. I can do that. The harder part is saying out loud that I might want to change direction. I miss Daddy's habit of asking what problem I'm actually trying to solve.

------------------------------

ENTRY 277 — 2005-06-12 — Age 19 — The Anniversary Week

I had expected to know exactly how the week would feel. That was foolish. Yesterday I was angry at a broken zipper and today I stood in the kitchen remembering the way Daddy cleaned his glasses with a cloth he carried in his pocket. Mother and I visited his grave and brought flowers. She spoke to him as though updating someone about the day's appointments. I told him about my classes and the piano room with the sticky key. We went for lunch afterward. I worried that laughing over dessert would be disrespectful, then remembered how offended he was by people who made themselves solemn out of duty. We laughed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 278 — 2005-06-24 — Age 19 — The Boat

We visited the boat on Lake Washington. The cabin smelled faintly of polish and old canvas, just as it always has. A maintenance person went through a list with Mother; I took notes because she handed me the clipboard without asking. The lines needed attention, and one cabinet latch sticks. I knew enough to follow the discussion but not enough to pretend I could manage everything alone. I spent a few minutes at the helm after the others went ashore. Daddy once told me the purpose of a checklist was to leave less work for your memory, not to prove you had a poor one. I made a better list before we locked up.

------------------------------

ENTRY 279 — 2005-07-05 — Age 19 — Waterfalls Are Not Quiet

We took a trail in the mountains and stopped at a waterfall so loud we had to lean close to hear each other. Someone had described it as a peaceful spot. It was magnificent, but peaceful isn't the word I'd choose. Cold spray settled on my sleeves. I watched the water strike a ledge and split into sheets and thought about all the paths water finds if you stop trying to tell it where to go. Mother said I was looking at the same rock for ten minutes. I said there was a great deal happening on that rock. She went to sit down and read her book.

------------------------------

ENTRY 280 — 2005-07-16 — Age 19 — A Familiar Hotel

There are people here who remember me as a child in mittens. I find this flattering until they begin describing things I did at seven. One woman asked if I still collect stones. I told her yes, but now I call it geology and pretend it is academic. Mother enjoys these reunions. She can become very quiet at breakfast, then spend an hour talking with someone she has known since before I was born. We walked after dinner, and the mountain air smelled of wet grass. I took photographs of flowers I don't know the names of. I plan to find out rather than make up names that sound scientific.

------------------------------

ENTRY 281 — 2005-07-29 — Age 19 — A Problem with Perfect

A man at dinner explained that some people are “born to do important things.” He kept glancing toward Mother, and I wondered whether he meant my family or my academic record. I asked what constituted an important thing. He said influence. I asked whether a person who restores a damaged wetland but never appears in a newspaper has influence. He said I was arguing semantics. Possibly. I think words are where many arguments begin. Mother later told me I had been courteous, though she had seen the exact moment I stopped liking him. I did not know it was visible.

------------------------------

ENTRY 282 — 2005-08-09 — Age 19 — No Fixed Itinerary

We spent an entire morning doing very little. Mother read, I wrote letters, and clouds traveled across the mountains. This afternoon we went into town and I bought a small notebook because I had filled the last one. The clerk asked whether I kept travel journals. I said I kept journals about everything, which sounded pretentious, although it is true. I wrote down the smell of bread outside the shop and the way Mother squinted at a map she refused to admit she couldn't read without glasses. Later I realized I had also written down the price of tea. I don't know why I wanted that detail. I kept it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 283 — 2005-08-21 — Age 19 — Home Again

The house on Bainbridge has the peculiarity of feeling too big at night and exactly the right size at breakfast. Mother has left stacks of mail for us to sort. I found a handwritten note from someone at the synagogue thanking her for a donation and asking if we could help with a particular program. She set it aside to answer personally. I cleaned the dust from the piano keys and played badly for a while. Our dog sat nearby with an expression suggesting he preferred silence. He may be right. I am glad to be home anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 284 — 2005-09-03 — Age 19 — Campus, Again

I moved back into the rhythm of university with a box of books and an entirely unreasonable collection of pens. Mother insisted on checking whether I had sheets. I reminded her I had survived an entire year. She said surviving was not the same as remembering sheets. She was correct; I had forgotten one set. I unpacked the desk first, then the books, then the clothes. A friend knocked and demanded I come outside for dinner. I promised I would be ready in five minutes and took seventeen. She says this is a trait of mine rather than an isolated scheduling failure.

------------------------------

ENTRY 285 — 2005-09-14 — Age 19 — The Adviser

I met with an academic adviser and admitted that the law track no longer seemed inevitable. She didn't look shocked, which disappointed a small theatrical part of me. She asked what classes I wanted to be in when nobody was watching. I said ecology, water science, and the kind of fieldwork that gets your shoes dirty. She made me list prerequisites and deadlines. It turns out changing your mind is not a lightning bolt. It is forms, schedules, chemistry, mathematics, and the willingness to take some classes out of the usual order. I came away with a plan to examine rather than a declaration to announce.

------------------------------

ENTRY 286 — 2005-09-25 — Age 19 — Apples and Pages

We had a lovely holiday dinner, with too much food and several opinions about whether a particular recipe had been better last year. Mother says it was. I remember it being rather dry. I kept that opinion to myself because I am acquiring tact, at least intermittently. I listened carefully during services and thought about the idea of beginning again without pretending the preceding year did not happen. That is different from erasing anything. I went back to campus with leftovers and a book I had borrowed without asking. Mother called to remind me it was hers. I told her I had every intention of returning it after reading it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 287 — 2005-10-04 — Age 19 — A Day Without Noise

I am finding it harder to be alone with my own thoughts when the campus is always full of people. I walked somewhere quiet after class and sat on a bench with my notebook closed. A squirrel came close enough to inspect my shoe. I did not feed it. It seemed insulted. There is a difference between being lonely and wanting silence, and I think I understand the difference better than I did last year. Sometimes I miss Daddy most when someone tells a bad joke he would have improved. Sometimes I don't think of him for hours. Both things are true.

------------------------------

ENTRY 288 — 2005-10-16 — Age 19 — The Field Trip

We counted and recorded things that did not have the courtesy to remain still: insects, birds, moving water. One student was determined to get a perfect list. The instructor said the point was to make careful observations and acknowledge what we might have missed. I liked that answer. I came back with damp socks, a cold nose, and notes written at odd angles because my clipboard kept slipping. I also came back wanting another field trip. A friend asked what I was smiling about. I said nothing, then tried to explain why a muddy afternoon examining a drainage channel had made me so happy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 289 — 2005-10-30 — Age 19 — An Awkward Invitation

A boy from a seminar asked me to coffee. I like him, though I'm not certain whether I like him in the way that makes coffee complicated. We went anyway. He asked about school, music, and the island. It was pleasant until someone passing the table made a remark about my last name. His face changed for an instant, and I found myself looking for signs that probably weren't there. I hate that I do this. He may have been surprised, nothing more. When he asked to meet again, I said yes. Wanting to be sensible is not the same as wanting to be suspicious of everyone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 290 — 2005-11-08 — Age 19 — A Small Collision

I walked into a half-open door while reading a page of notes. It made a noise loud enough that a stranger asked whether I needed assistance. I did not, except perhaps from someone willing to confiscate my reading material while I moved from one room to another. My forehead is fine. My dignity may recover by next week. I told Mother and she said, “You are entirely your father's daughter.” I asked whether he walked into doors too. She said he once forgot a piano stool was behind him and sat down beside it. I wish I could ask him about that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 291 — 2005-11-19 — Age 19 — The Hard Conversation

I told Mother I was seriously considering an environmental science degree instead of continuing toward law. She listened, asked a few questions about the courses, and then said, “Darling, you don't have to spend your life proving anything to either of us.” I had prepared a speech and had nowhere to deliver it. She added that she still hoped I would find someone wonderful and have a family, which was not remotely the subject under discussion. I said she could wish for that without assigning me a schedule. She agreed. Then she asked whether I'd eaten dinner. This is how most of our grand debates end.

------------------------------

ENTRY 292 — 2005-11-27 — Age 19 — Dinner for Two

Thanksgiving at home was quieter than the holidays I remember from childhood, but we cooked too much anyway. Mother told a story about a terrible apartment she lived in when she first moved west. I asked what made it terrible. She began listing defects with such affection that I suspected she had liked it. We ate, washed dishes, and argued amiably about whether the dog was getting fat. In the evening I looked through an old album. There is a photograph of Daddy on a boat with his sleeves rolled up, looking as if he is arguing with the weather. I wish I knew what he was saying.

------------------------------

ENTRY 293 — 2005-12-06 — Age 19 — The Registration Decision

I registered for another term that will let me test the environmental direction seriously. I have not made a grand announcement. The program requirements are real, and I need to be sure I want the work as well as the romance of being outdoors. Chemistry does not become charming simply because you love forests. Neither does statistics. I worked through a set of problems tonight and enjoyed the satisfaction of finally understanding one that had defeated me for an hour. If I change my field, I want it to be because I enjoy learning the difficult parts too.

------------------------------

ENTRY 294 — 2005-12-18 — Age 19 — The Piano Conversation

The student from the practice room found me again and asked what I had been listening to lately. I said Ellington, Ella, some Coltrane, and something contemporary he had recommended. We argued over whether a particular performance was technically perfect but emotionally dull. He played a few measures to make his point. I was not convinced, but I understood him better. Daddy would have loved the argument. I am beginning to realize that remembering somebody doesn't always feel like looking backward. Sometimes it is continuing a conversation with a new person.

------------------------------

ENTRY 295 — 2005-12-24 — Age 19 — The Winter Walk

The path near home was slippery with wet leaves. I had the good sense to wear proper boots and the poor sense to try to photograph a bird while stepping over a root. Mother caught my sleeve. I told her I was certain the bird had done that deliberately. We found a little patch of sunlight by the water and stood there longer than the temperature justified. Later we drank tea and watched an old film. The house felt quiet in a comfortable way for a while. It is odd how much I value the ability to sit beside Mother without trying to make conversation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 296 — 2005-12-31 — Age 19 — Not a Resolution

I used to make lists on New Year's Eve: courses, books, places to go, things to achieve. I still have lists. There is one in the back of this notebook, and it is extremely orderly. But I don't want the year to be only a march between checked boxes. In 2005 I learned to talk with Mother as another adult, found new friends, remembered old ones, went back into the mountains, and admitted I might want a different education than the one I originally chose. I also missed a ferry, ruined a notebook, walked into a door, and played more piano than I expected. It wasn't a spectacular year. It was mine. That seems like enough.
