[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook XXV: 2025]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Original fictional first-person diary. Later friendships, romantic relationships, and projects are provisional unless established by Steve. No 2026 memory-loss event or explanation is added.

ENTRY 829 — 2025-01-08 — Age 38 — A new field notebook

I bought a waterproof notebook because I am tired of watching ink migrate across wet paper. The first thing I wrote was the date and the second was a grocery list. A promising beginning for advanced environmental science.

------------------------------

ENTRY 830 — 2025-01-29 — Age 38 — Familiar places

I took the ferry to Seattle and walked past buildings I knew from childhood. I stopped for coffee where an old friend had recommended and discovered the shop had changed hands. The city changes without consulting me, which I continue to regard as an administrative oversight.

------------------------------

ENTRY 831 — 2025-02-14 — Age 38 — A good friend

I spent the afternoon with a friend whose marriage is happy, complicated and occasionally loud. She made no attempt to pair me with anyone. I appreciate that more than flowers. We went to the waterfront and watched dogs drag their owners toward the tide.

------------------------------

ENTRY 832 — 2025-03-02 — Age 38 — The shore survey

Our team revised the monitoring schedule after two winters of inconsistent storm sampling. I asked for clear backup responsibilities. People have lives, children, colds and cars that won't start. A good project should be able to survive all of those.

------------------------------

ENTRY 833 — 2025-03-21 — Age 38 — A question at the synagogue

A young woman asked why I still give so much to the congregation when I do not agree with every decision the board makes. I told her disagreement isn't exile. I grew up here among people who argued over everything, including where to put the chairs.

------------------------------

ENTRY 834 — 2025-04-13 — Age 38 — Pesach again

I hosted a busy Seder and got the salt water in the wrong bowl. Nobody suffered. A child asked if I believed every word of every interpretation we'd read. I said I believed asking was part of belonging. Later I wrote down her question more carefully because I wanted to think about it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 835 — 2025-05-03 — Age 38 — A little accident

I caught my sleeve on a door handle while carrying tea and managed to pour most of the cup into a potted plant. The plant may recover. I apologized to it out loud. A friend heard me and has promised never to let me forget it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 836 — 2025-05-24 — Age 38 — The woodpecker

A woodpecker drummed on the side of an old shed and I stood listening for longer than I had intended. I watched it move along the boards and wondered how many tiny lives inhabit wood we call dead. I nearly missed an appointment because of a bird. Again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 837 — 2025-06-12 — Age 38 — Re-reading Dad

I opened a notebook of Dad's with diagrams of knots and a few comments about wind. His handwriting is precise until he gets excited, then it leans badly to the right. I practiced two knots and found I remembered one better with my eyes closed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 838 — 2025-06-30 — Age 38 — Grant meeting

I said no to a proposed publicity campaign that would have diverted money from field staff. The chair asked whether I understood how fundraising worked. I told him I understood it perfectly and wanted a credible account of what we had accomplished. We agreed on a smaller campaign.

------------------------------

ENTRY 839 — 2025-07-19 — Age 38 — An island walk

I spent the afternoon walking among cedar and fern. A dog approached, stopped at a respectful distance and waited for its owner. I wished more humans behaved that well. I went home with a leaf in my hair and discovered it only after a video call.

------------------------------

ENTRY 840 — 2025-08-05 — Age 38 — Switzerland postcards

I sorted old postcards from summers in Switzerland. Mother's notes are full of weather, meals and eccentric hotel guests. Dad invariably writes about trains and the quality of the piano. I copied several into this notebook. I don't need a particular reason to preserve them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 841 — 2025-08-22 — Age 38 — A short sail

We went out in settled weather and returned by early evening. I took the helm and remembered how much I love the small practical decisions of sailing. I checked the lines twice. Someone asked whether I ever get bored on the water. I don't understand the question.

------------------------------

ENTRY 842 — 2025-09-08 — Age 39 — Thirty-nine

I turned thirty-nine today. Friends came for dinner, and somebody brought an enormous chocolate cake. I put the extra slices in the freezer. At some point we stopped discussing birthdays and began arguing about whether crows recognize particular people. An excellent evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 843 — 2025-09-23 — Age 39 — The holiday melody

During the High Holy Days I found myself singing a melody Dad learned after converting. He used to pause in the wrong place and then smile as though he'd done it deliberately. I remember the smile more clearly than the mistake. I also remember Mother correcting him.

------------------------------

ENTRY 844 — 2025-10-11 — Age 39 — The small creek

A routine inspection led to a long conversation with a local resident about drainage changes. She showed me a photograph from before the road widening. I asked permission to copy it for the project file. She said yes and told me the name of the dog sitting in the corner of the picture.

------------------------------

ENTRY 845 — 2025-10-30 — Age 39 — What the trust can do

I reviewed next year's giving plan. The synagogue's annual support remains part of it, as do ecological projects with measurable needs. I am conscious of how easily money can distort what people say to me. I cannot repair that by refusing to use it. I can ask careful questions and keep the decisions mine.

------------------------------

ENTRY 846 — 2025-11-12 — Age 39 — Her voice

I listened to an old recording of Mother telling a story about Brooklyn and arguing with Dad about the year. For once I could hear them both, interrupting and laughing. I left the file exactly as it is instead of trimming the noise. The interruptions are the best part.

------------------------------

ENTRY 847 — 2025-11-27 — Age 39 — A good ordinary day

I read in the morning, answered correspondence after lunch, took a walk in the rain and cooked a decent supper. Nothing happened that would deserve mention in a biography. This is why I keep a diary.

------------------------------

ENTRY 848 — 2025-12-31 — Age 39 — The last page of the year

The house is quiet, the rain is tapping at the window and my notebook is nearly full. I have friends to call, conservation work to return to, and places I still want to see. I am not going to write a grand prediction about next year. I only want to remember this evening accurately: the lamp was warm, the tea was too strong, and I was here.
