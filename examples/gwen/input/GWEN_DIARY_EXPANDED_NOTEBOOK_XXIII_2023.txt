ENTRY 793 — 2023-01-12 — Age 36 — The old photograph

A photograph turned up of Dad in uniform, young enough that I scarcely recognized him. I know he volunteered for the Navy as a teenager, but the face in the photograph made that fact feel different. I held it beside one of him at the piano and wondered what happened between the two pictures.

------------------------------

ENTRY 794 — 2023-01-30 — Age 36 — The committee chair

I turned down another ceremonial appointment. I offered instead to review project outcomes twice a year and pay for a qualified field position if the board approved it. One man looked irritated that I preferred actual work to being photographed with a shovel.

------------------------------

ENTRY 795 — 2023-02-18 — Age 36 — Rain reading

I spent the afternoon reading by the window and neglected laundry. The book made a compelling case, then overstated its conclusion so badly I almost threw it across the room. I wrote a note in the margin and resumed. A mind ought to be able to enjoy being challenged without being gullible.

------------------------------

ENTRY 796 — 2023-03-08 — Age 36 — Lunch with a cousin

We met without discussing distributions for nearly an hour, which may be a family record. He told me about one of his children learning violin. I told him about Dad's terrible habit of correcting recordings. We laughed. It felt ordinary and good.

------------------------------

ENTRY 797 — 2023-03-26 — Age 36 — A gull with a hook

A rescue volunteer asked for help keeping a path clear while they handled a hooked seabird. I did exactly what they asked and nothing more. Afterwards we went for tea. The bird was taken for care. I wrote down the responder's name so I could thank her properly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 798 — 2023-04-07 — Age 36 — The familiar table

Pesach brought some of the usual arguments and one entirely new one about whether the youngest child should lead a reading. We found a way that pleased the child, if not every adult. The food was late. That may be the most authentic part.

------------------------------

ENTRY 799 — 2023-04-24 — Age 36 — A short lecture

I spoke to students about watershed measurements and tried to keep my explanation practical. One student asked if rich donors get to pick the science. I said they shouldn't, including when the rich donor is me. That produced a silence I was not sure how to interpret.

------------------------------

ENTRY 800 — 2023-05-19 — Age 36 — A Saturday in the garden

I weeded badly, rescued two worms from the path and got sunburned on one wrist. The garden looks better but not professionally maintained. I don't need professional perfection every time I touch the earth.

------------------------------

ENTRY 801 — 2023-06-06 — Age 36 — Out on the sound

I navigated for friends on a day sail. I checked the chart and weather before we left, and we returned early when the wind changed. A small child asked why I knew the names of the buoys. I told her my father was a captain and expected me to pay attention.

------------------------------

ENTRY 802 — 2023-06-28 — Age 36 — The concert

An excellent quartet played a piece that reminded me of the piano at home. I sat very still afterward while everyone else began applauding. I am not certain whether that was appreciation or simply being slow to return to the room.

------------------------------

ENTRY 803 — 2023-07-15 — Age 36 — A Swiss garden

I returned to a garden I'd seen as a child and found the paths smaller than memory. The mountains were exactly as large. I sat on a bench and wrote a letter to a friend about the plants, then forgot to post it until the following week.

------------------------------

ENTRY 804 — 2023-08-03 — Age 36 — The letter from an admirer

A man I barely knew sent a rather extravagant invitation after a conservation dinner. I wrote back kindly declining. He replied with a business proposal. That clarified matters quickly. I did not bother pretending to misunderstand it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 805 — 2023-08-25 — Age 36 — The meeting ran late

We talked about shoreline access until nearly dark. I brought a map home covered in pencil marks and later discovered a cookie crushed inside it. The budget will survive. The cookie did not.

------------------------------

ENTRY 806 — 2023-09-08 — Age 37 — Thirty-seven

I had dinner with friends and wore earrings Mother would have loved. The waiter brought an extra dessert because someone mentioned my birthday. I did not tell him what the trust was worth. He was kind anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 807 — 2023-09-25 — Age 37 — The holidays

During services I realized I could remember the prayers without looking down, yet wanted the book open in my hands. I kept it there. Habits are sometimes more comfortable than efficiencies.

------------------------------

ENTRY 808 — 2023-10-17 — Age 37 — A cold conversation

A committee member tried to pressure a junior scientist into changing her findings. I told him plainly that he could question methods but not demand conclusions. My voice went quiet and everyone listened. I was shaking afterward. Anger takes effort even when it sounds controlled.

------------------------------

ENTRY 809 — 2023-11-10 — Age 37 — A warm kitchen

Friends came over after a wet hike. We ate soup, hung jackets everywhere and argued about which bird we'd heard. I couldn't find the serving spoon because I had left it in the refrigerator. It was a good evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 810 — 2023-12-31 — Age 37 — Looking back

A lot of what mattered this year will never appear in a report: one child understanding a map, a repaired fence, a friend calling on a hard day. I have been careful with figures and less careful with serving spoons. I suppose that remains me.
