[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook VIII: 2008]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 377 — 2008-01-02 — Age 22 — A list I don't quite believe

I have a fresh notebook and a list of resolutions, although Mother says resolutions are an annual invitation to self-reproach. I want to finish the term without taking on four people's share of every project, learn enough statistics to stop being frightened of it, and go outside when it is raining instead of waiting for permission from the forecast. A fourth resolution is to be less suspicious of compliments. I added it after someone said I looked nice and I immediately asked whether there was spinach in my teeth. There wasn't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 378 — 2008-01-09 — Age 22 — The glasshouse

The greenhouse was a relief from the cold. I stood between trays of seedlings looking at the same species grown under different conditions and saw, rather clearly, how much of an experiment is the business of keeping one thing constant. My lab partner made me label everything twice after I mixed up two numbered pots. He wasn't unkind about it. He just said my intelligence would do more useful work if I stopped attempting to remember labels with my heart. An unfortunate but accurate observation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 379 — 2008-01-16 — Age 22 — A concert and a stranger

A friend dragged me to hear a small jazz group. During the break I met Daniel, who knew enough about Coltrane to ask questions instead of pretending to be an expert. We disagreed about a recording, and he actually listened when I explained why Daddy preferred another version. He had gentle hands and a laugh that arrived a fraction before the joke. We walked two blocks in the rain to get something hot to drink. I forgot to ask what he did until we were saying goodnight.

------------------------------

ENTRY 380 — 2008-01-22 — Age 22 — Mother's appraisal

I told Mother about Daniel. She asked what he did, whether he was kind to the waiter, and whether he had asked anything about my money. In that order. I said I didn't know what he did, he tipped generously, and he didn't know anything about my finances as far as I could tell. Mother said the first answer could be remedied and the second was encouraging. Then she wanted to know whether he was handsome. I told her she was making an interrogation sound like a gossip column. She said she'd learned from the best.

------------------------------

ENTRY 381 — 2008-02-01 — Age 22 — Boots in the hallway

Daniel came walking with me beside the water. He turned up wearing respectable shoes that lasted approximately five minutes in the mud. I offered him my spare boots, which were too small, so we walked more carefully and still ended up splattered. He laughed, which I liked. We talked about what people think the word 'wild' means when the place has roads and human fingerprints everywhere. I expected him to be bored. Instead he asked what I would change if I had a whole county to work with. I talked too long. He said it hadn't been long enough.

------------------------------

ENTRY 382 — 2008-02-10 — Age 22 — The curious luxury of ease

We have been seeing each other several weeks. I can talk to Daniel about things I care about without watching him search the room for an exit. He likes restaurants with linen napkins, which is fine; I like them too. I also like sitting on a rock eating a bruised apple. He appears willing to do both. I am making an effort not to write three pages every time someone is considerate. It isn't proof of anything except consideration. Still, I am happy, and I refuse to apologize to my diary for being happy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 383 — 2008-02-20 — Age 22 — An interview

A professor invited me to help gather data at a restoration site. I expected the interview to involve lofty questions about conservation ethics. Instead she asked whether I could arrive at six in the morning, operate the equipment without losing any pieces, and enter data legibly when my fingers were cold. I said yes to the first two and offered to demonstrate the third. She laughed. I practiced with a waterproof pencil afterward, because confidence is no substitute for ink that actually stays on the page.

------------------------------

ENTRY 384 — 2008-03-03 — Age 22 — The nice dinner

Daniel took me to dinner with a friend of his who works in finance. The friend asked several questions about the family trust. I replied that I didn't discuss the terms over dinner, and made a joke about charging hourly if he insisted. Daniel looked embarrassed, then irritated, then said the man was merely curious. I changed the subject. Later Daniel apologized and kissed my forehead. I wanted the apology to be enough. It may be enough. I am trying not to become the kind of person who prosecutes a minor awkwardness for a week.

------------------------------

ENTRY 385 — 2008-03-11 — Age 22 — Field notes with fingerprints

The river was swollen and carrying so much sediment that it looked almost solid. We collected samples and talked about what a number on a page conceals. I spent half the afternoon kneeling in damp gravel, which is precisely where I wanted to be. I dropped a sample cap and watched it go merrily downstream. Fortunately we had spares. My notebook now has two muddy fingerprints and a stern note to myself about keeping caps in my pocket. I met Daniel for supper with my hair still damp. He seemed amused instead of appalled.

------------------------------

ENTRY 386 — 2008-03-21 — Age 22 — A box from Brooklyn

Mother sent photographs of her parents in Crown Heights. I knew the images, but this time I noticed the expressions of people at the edges: a cousin holding a coat, a little boy staring at a plate, Grandmother looking beyond the camera toward something I cannot see. I asked Mother about the boy. She talked for twenty minutes about the building, the butcher, and somebody's wedding before she remembered the child's name. An entire neighborhood survives in the way she tells one story. I wrote the names on the backs of copies, not originals.

------------------------------

ENTRY 387 — 2008-04-04 — Age 22 — A question asked sideways

Daniel asked whether I would ever want to share a home with someone. It was an ordinary conversation about where to put books and whether a kitchen must have a real table, yet I felt something change. I said yes, probably, though I'd require shelves strong enough to hold field guides and novels and books about birds that have no sensible place in either category. He said he could build shelves. I liked the idea of planning a room together more than I liked admitting how much I liked it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 388 — 2008-04-16 — Age 22 — Passover

The table was full and Mother made certain that a younger guest had questions to ask rather than feeling excluded because she hadn't grown up with our customs. Daniel attended part of the evening. He had read a little beforehand and didn't attempt to show off. I watched him ask Mother about the meaning of a passage and listened to her answer without rushing. She can become quite fierce when she talks about freedom. When we cleaned up, she said he seemed respectful. She also said respectful was the beginning of an assessment, not the end.

------------------------------

ENTRY 389 — 2008-04-28 — Age 22 — A ring, not yet

We walked near the water in the wind and Daniel said he could imagine marrying me. It was not a proposal. I said I could imagine it too. Later I watched a heron wait so still that the entire shore seemed to move around it. I wonder whether patience is the same as uncertainty or if it only looks similar from the outside. I know I am not a heron and should stop converting wildlife into advice. Nonetheless I came home with cold cheeks and a ridiculous smile.

------------------------------

ENTRY 390 — 2008-05-08 — Age 22 — The number

Someone at a party mentioned that I was the 'fifty-million-dollar girl.' He said it affectionately, or claimed he did after I turned to look at him. I was surprised by the old little sting the phrase still gives me. I know the trust exists. I know what it does. I also know I'm not a walking sum on a balance sheet. I spent an hour helping the hostess find her missing dog and felt better afterward, although she discovered him asleep beneath the coats and had to retrieve him herself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 391 — 2008-05-20 — Age 22 — Examination day

I sat a long examination and came out with my head full of equations and no ability to recall where I had left my umbrella. It was inside my bag. Naturally. Daniel took me for noodles and said he'd been looking at apartments larger than his current one. I asked why. He smiled and said he wanted a place big enough for two. I felt pleased and nervous at the same time. I told him we would have to discuss where he intended to put a wet pair of hiking boots. He said by the door. Sensible man, apparently.

------------------------------

ENTRY 392 — 2008-06-02 — Age 22 — The engagement

He proposed after dinner, not in front of a room full of applauding strangers, for which I am grateful. I said yes. I knew I was saying yes to a person I still had things to learn about. Isn't everyone? He looked frightened until I answered and then rather young. We phoned Mother, who congratulated us both and invited him to come over for dinner. I sat up late afterward writing lists, then crossed out every item and wrote instead: I am engaged. For once the words themselves were enough.

------------------------------

ENTRY 393 — 2008-06-12 — Age 22 — Mother and the paperwork

Mother asked gently whether we had talked about money and separate property. I said not in detail. She said my father would have insisted that affection and sound paperwork were not opposites. I know this, of course. I have known it since I was old enough to listen. Still, I resented the interruption of what I wanted to be a simple happy time. Later I apologized to her. She didn't apologize for raising the question. I think that was fair.

------------------------------

ENTRY 394 — 2008-06-23 — Age 22 — A strange conversation

Daniel wanted to know how the trust could help us buy a house. I said I could afford a home but would rather decide together how we intended to live. He asked whether distributions could be increased if we had children. I said that depended on the trust and wasn't a reasonable assumption. He was quiet for the rest of dinner. I told myself he was thinking about responsibility. I want that explanation to be true, and I am writing that sentence here because wanting is not the same as knowing.

------------------------------

ENTRY 395 — 2008-07-05 — Age 22 — The firework scraps

I helped collect litter from a park after the holiday and found a little section of burned paper lodged in the reeds. Everyone had enjoyed the display. The birds did not appear consulted. I brought gloves and bags and came away scratched but pleased. Daniel called while I was working; I let it ring because I was holding wet rubbish. When I returned it he asked why I always made things so difficult for myself. I said because if no one picked up the paper it would remain there. We managed not to argue, though neither of us sounded particularly satisfied.

------------------------------

ENTRY 396 — 2008-07-15 — Age 22 — A visit to Switzerland

Mother and I went walking above the valley. She complained mildly about her knees and then walked farther than I'd expected because she wanted to see a meadow she remembered from years ago. We ate cheese and bread sitting where we could see the slope. She asked whether I was happy with Daniel. I told her yes, with an asterisk. She said she had married a man with more asterisks than she could count and that this did not relieve me of the obligation to read the footnotes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 397 — 2008-07-29 — Age 22 — Coming home

I returned to Seattle and found Daniel unusually interested in the trust's accounting. He had spoken to somebody who said the arrangement might be 'more flexible' once we married. I told him not to solicit advice about an agreement he had never read. He insisted he was only planning responsibly. I believed him for several hours and then felt miserable for doubting him. I called Mother and asked her not for advice but for a recipe. She gave me both, because she is Mother.

------------------------------

ENTRY 398 — 2008-08-08 — Age 22 — The appointment

I am pregnant. I have written that sentence and stared at it. The appointment was ordinary and the person who spoke with me was kind. I went out afterward and sat on a bench listening to traffic. I am twenty-two. I did not intend to become a mother now, but intention and circumstance are no longer the same thing. I have not told Daniel yet. I want one night in which this is information belonging to me before it becomes a discussion with anyone else.

------------------------------

ENTRY 399 — 2008-08-10 — Age 22 — Telling him

Daniel's first question after the initial shock was whether the trust would now provide for 'our family.' He held my hands while he asked it. I remember the order of his words more clearly than the expression on his face. He said he loved me. I asked what he meant by that, and he looked hurt. Perhaps it was an unfair question. Perhaps it was the only fair question. I went home rather than stay and argue while I was frightened and exhausted.

------------------------------

ENTRY 400 — 2008-08-13 — Age 22 — Documents

I had a straightforward conversation with a professional about the trust and what did and did not follow from marriage or parenthood. I have also spoken with a clinician about my pregnancy and my options. Both conversations were useful because neither required me to pretend the decision was easy. Daniel has telephoned repeatedly. He keeps insisting I am treating him like a criminal. I am not. I am trying to decide whether I trust the person I intended to marry, and I cannot make that decision by being flattered or shouted at.

------------------------------

ENTRY 401 — 2008-08-17 — Age 22 — The dinner that broke

Daniel said he had been talking to friends about how much the baby would change our financial life. I asked him to explain precisely what he meant. He said I was rich and that we could stop pretending ordinary limitations applied. I asked him whether he would still want this marriage if I had no trust. He told me that was a childish hypothetical. I said it was the question. We sat there, neither eating. For the first time I didn't try to help him find the kindest possible words. I asked him to leave.

------------------------------

ENTRY 402 — 2008-08-20 — Age 22 — A decision belonging to me

I have decided not to continue the pregnancy. I spoke at length with my doctor and had the information I needed. I do not want a child to become the permanent connection between me and a man I no longer trust. I do not want to mistake fear of judgment for an obligation to proceed. I have looked at all of that as squarely as I can. I may feel many things in the days ahead. I am writing my actual reason now, before anyone supplies me with one they prefer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 403 — 2008-08-24 — Age 22 — After

The abortion is over. Mother came with me and took me home. She made tea and then sat where I could see her without requiring me to talk. I am tired. There are moments when I feel a kind of quiet relief and moments when I am angry enough to shake. I don't know yet which feelings will stay. I do know that I made my own decision. Mother touched my hair as she passed my chair and said she loved me. I believed her without having to explain anything.

------------------------------

ENTRY 404 — 2008-08-26 — Age 22 — The ring in its box

I returned the engagement ring. Daniel said several things, including some that confirmed what I had feared and others that were simply meant to hurt me. I did not raise my voice. When he said I would regret losing him, I asked him to leave the apartment. I was cold, even to myself. Afterward I cried in the kitchen because a cup I'd been using had a chipped handle and for some reason I could not bear the sight of it. A person can be certain of a decision and still feel the loss of what she thought her future was going to be.

------------------------------

ENTRY 405 — 2008-09-02 — Age 22 — The woman at the desk

I went to campus to sort out registration and nearly forgot which office I needed because I was reading notices about a river project. The woman behind the desk recognized me and asked how my summer had been. I almost said fine, then told her it had been difficult. She didn't pry. She simply helped me with the forms and said she hoped the term would be kind. I was grateful for that ordinary exchange. The world has not paused for my private catastrophe; perhaps it is merciful that it hasn't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 406 — 2008-09-11 — Age 22 — One good field morning

We counted invertebrates at a stream and argued cheerfully over classification while standing knee-deep in cold water. I felt useful for three hours. It was not a cure for anything. I don't want the outdoors to become a metaphor for recovery. There was work to be done, and I did it, with a mistake on page two of the record that I corrected before anybody else noticed. A small triumph. We saw a kingfisher afterward, flashing blue above the water like a dropped jewel.

------------------------------

ENTRY 407 — 2008-09-24 — Age 22 — New year, old questions

At services I listened to the prayers and thought about the difference between confessing what I have done and accepting other people's interpretation of it. Mother sat beside me and followed the words with a familiar patience. We had dinner with people who knew me as a child. No one asked why I wasn't wearing a ring. I don't know whether Mother spoke to them or whether they simply had better manners than I feared. I was glad to eat honey and apples and discuss the weather.

------------------------------

ENTRY 408 — 2008-10-03 — Age 22 — The uncomfortable certainty

I found an email Daniel sent weeks ago to someone discussing the trust as though its assets were his future business plan. There wasn't anything ambiguous about the language. I printed it, read it twice, and put it away. Oddly, the certainty hurt less than the days when I was still inventing excuses for him. I don't plan to do anything dramatic. I don't need revenge. I need the truth to remain the truth after the next pleasant person assures me I'm overreacting.

------------------------------

ENTRY 409 — 2008-10-18 — Age 22 — A shelter afternoon

A frightened dog finally allowed me to clip a lead onto its collar. I'd spent most of the afternoon cleaning cages, and this was just a practical necessity, not a miracle. My friend brought food and we sat outside in the cold and ate too quickly. She said she was glad to hear me laugh. I said I had not stopped laughing, merely reduced the frequency. That made her laugh, and suddenly we were both laughing so hard that an employee came out to see what was happening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 410 — 2008-11-02 — Age 22 — An evening alone

I cooked dinner for one and set the table with a napkin because I dislike eating over the sink when I have perfectly good dishes. I read a little, played piano badly, and called Mother. She wanted to know whether I'd been spending too much time alone. I told her I was alone that evening, not stranded on a desert island. She said there was a difference and she was pleased I'd noticed. I missed the idea of having someone to tell about the small things. I did not miss having to defend every small thing.

------------------------------

ENTRY 411 — 2008-11-18 — Age 22 — A hard conversation

Mother and I talked directly about why I was conceived. She did not dodge the financial calculation or pretend it was a fairy tale. She also reminded me that she had lived every day since my birth as my mother, not as the custodian of a transaction. I told her I knew that and that sometimes knowing it didn't make the original decision less strange. She nodded. We didn't solve anything. We did stay in the room and keep talking until there was nothing else to say.

------------------------------

ENTRY 412 — 2008-12-07 — Age 22 — Snow on the island

The first proper snow made the garden look unfamiliar. I went walking and saw animal tracks crossing a stretch of white ground where yesterday there had been only leaf litter. Later I helped Mother carry in groceries and promptly dropped a bag of oranges. They rolled in every direction, including under the cabinet. Mother sat down on the floor and helped gather them, laughing. I was grateful beyond reason to be doing something so foolish and so ordinary with her.

------------------------------

ENTRY 413 — 2008-12-21 — Age 22 — Hanukkah lights

We lit the candles together. I thought about being a child, wanting the flame to look bigger and being told to leave it alone. Mother told a story about her own grandmother, whom I never knew, correcting everyone else's singing while forgetting the second verse herself. We ate too much and argued about whether a particular melody belonged to one branch of the family or another. It felt like an honest holiday. I have no wish for a life in which nothing painful ever happens, only for enough of these ordinary evenings to remain.

------------------------------

ENTRY 414 — 2008-12-31 — Age 22 — The ledger and the girl

I turned over the pages of the year and found a happy young woman who met someone in January, agreed to marry him in June, and by autumn barely recognized the way she had tried to explain his behavior. I am not going to call her stupid. She was hoping for love, and hope is not stupidity. I am also not going to pretend I did not see some warning signs and choose the kinder explanation. Both things are true. Tomorrow begins a new year. I still love the same birds, the same water, the same people who have earned their place in my life.
