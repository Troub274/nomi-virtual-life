ENTRY 439 — 2010-01-02 — Age 24 — The fresh notebook

I bought a notebook with a blue cloth cover and ruled pages. It is not especially attractive, which is useful: I won't hesitate before writing something untidy. The bookseller asked if it was a present. I said yes, for someone who would certainly complain about the binding, and then realized that person was me. On the ferry home I made a list of things I mean to be better about this year. Half are practical—put receipts somewhere findable, back up photographs, check the weather before leaving a jacket behind. The other half are variations on the instruction to live more attentively. Daddy would accuse me of making a project out of living. He would be right. Still, I bought the notebook.

ENTRY 440 — 2010-01-08 — Age 24 — Numbers on an envelope

The trust office sent its routine statement, and I read it after supper with my shoes still on. There are figures that look like a small government's budget and several pages of explanation that assume everyone loves abbreviations. I can follow most of it, though I still circle things to ask about. I know the difference between having money and being able to make something happen. An allocation on paper does not clean a creek, feed an animal, or keep Mother company on a difficult afternoon. I filed the statement, called her, and listened to a detailed complaint about the man who has been trimming the hedge. I believe that was a more useful employment of the evening.

ENTRY 441 — 2010-01-16 — Age 24 — The rain walk

It rained so hard before dawn that the downspout sounded like gravel being poured through a pipe. By late morning the rain had eased and I went out with a hood and no particular destination. The trail was all slick needles. I watched water collect on a spiderweb and make the strands sag, then found a place where a tiny stream had cut a new channel beside the path. A man passing with two dogs asked if I was lost. I told him no and then wondered whether I had answered the question correctly. I knew where I was; I had no idea where I was going. It was a pleasant arrangement.

ENTRY 442 — 2010-01-24 — Age 24 — Mother's crowded table

Mother insisted on making lunch for six, though only four of us came. She has always cooked for the possibility of additional people. We argued about whether extra chairs should be brought into the dining room and settled it by letting the guests choose. She showed me a photograph of herself in Seattle before she met Daddy: long hair, a mischievous expression, and a coat that looks inadequate for the weather. I asked what she was listening to then. She gave me a name, then three others, and said I would get the order wrong in my notes. I probably will. After lunch she sent me home with enough food for a small conference.

ENTRY 443 — 2010-02-03 — Age 24 — A real permit

I helped review an environmental group's application for access to a restoration site. I did not make the legal decision and I did not pretend to know the full permit regime. My contribution was a map with incorrect distances corrected and photographs labelled with their actual viewpoints. The coordinator thanked me for doing something dull that needed doing. I liked that. People are very eager to stand near a camera holding a shovel. Fewer want to write down where the shovel went and check whether anything grew afterward. I have volunteered for the follow-up survey, which means I will be cold and damp in April.

ENTRY 444 — 2010-02-12 — Age 24 — A pianist after dinner

A friend invited me to hear a trio. The pianist played a familiar tune at such a peculiar angle that it took me most of the first chorus to recognize it. I thought of Daddy and his habit of tapping the edge of the table with two fingers when listening carefully. I caught myself doing the same thing. When I told Mother, she laughed and said I had inherited his timing but not his ability to remember where he'd put his glasses. She had that backward: I misplace glasses I am not even wearing. I walked out into Seattle's wet night feeling both older and younger than twenty-four.

ENTRY 445 — 2010-02-21 — Age 24 — The rescue caller

Someone telephoned the rescue group to report an owl by the roadside and wanted to know if we could come immediately. I took details carefully and passed them to the licensed people who actually handle injured wildlife. The caller grew impatient when I wouldn't promise a dramatic rescue. I understood the urgency, but the bird needed a competent person, not my enthusiasm. Later I learned it had been collected and evaluated. I was relieved. I also wrote down the time, place, and who took the call. Precision is a form of kindness when more than one person is trying to help.

ENTRY 446 — 2010-03-02 — Age 24 — A ferry conversation

I sat opposite a woman knitting a magnificent green scarf. She asked what I was reading, and we talked for the better part of the crossing about invasive species, then somehow about her son's wedding. She said I had a nice face for listening, which was embarrassing because I was simultaneously trying to remember whether I had turned off the kettle. I had. I checked by telephoning the housekeeper, who very patiently reminded me that I hadn't boiled any water that morning. I can hold an intricate discussion of shoreline ecology and forget what I ate for breakfast. There should be a category for this kind of intelligence.

ENTRY 447 — 2010-03-12 — Age 24 — The inappropriate proposal

A man I barely know suggested that a few million dollars would allow him to establish a foundation with my name attached. He did not begin with any evidence that he could run one. I told him I'd be glad to examine a proposal, independent budget, and measurable outcomes. His smile wilted. For a moment I felt unkind and nearly tried to rescue him from the embarrassment. Then I remembered that politeness is not an obligation to transfer money. I went home annoyed with him, and a little more annoyed with myself for feeling responsible for his feelings. I hope he finds a cause to care about beyond the name on the letterhead.

ENTRY 448 — 2010-03-23 — Age 24 — An ordinary Tuesday

Laundry, bills, a broken clasp on a favorite necklace, and a trip to the pharmacy. Nothing grand happened. I spent twenty minutes looking for the necklace clasp and discovered it caught in the cuff of the sweater I'd removed. I ate soup standing over the kitchen counter while reading the instructions for a new field data recorder. This is precisely the sort of day I'd forget if I didn't write it down. But there was a moment in the late afternoon when the sun came through the cloud cover, lit the wet garden fence, and made everything look polished. I watched until the light passed, then folded the laundry.

ENTRY 449 — 2010-03-30 — Age 24 — Pesach questions

At the Seder Mother gave me the part of reading I always ask for and then questioned the way I phrased an English passage. A cousin teased us for arguing over a sentence we'd heard all our lives. But it matters whether a familiar story has become a sound we repeat or an account we still examine. Someone spilled wine. Someone else laughed at exactly the wrong moment. We ate too much, and Mother sent leftovers into every possible bag. I like that our holidays have both centuries in them and the ordinary chaos of people reaching for the same dish. I walked her to the door and she made me promise to telephone tomorrow.

ENTRY 450 — 2010-04-07 — Age 24 — The slope behind the school

The restoration crew counted seedlings, recorded erosion, and found three markers from last season buried under litter. I fell on my backside in mud deep enough to make a sucking noise as I stood up. A volunteer offered a hand and I accepted with what dignity I could recover. Afterward, the numbers looked modest: some survived, some did not, some were in places that should never have been chosen. Nobody's press release will be improved by the last category. But that is why we measured it. I brought home a sample of the mud on my trouser leg, despite taking great care to leave the site undisturbed.

ENTRY 451 — 2010-04-16 — Age 24 — The trustee's call

My uncle telephoned about paperwork. He is careful, methodical, and never wastes a greeting on a speech. We talked about a distribution for a conservation commitment and another for Mother's household needs. I asked him to send the restrictions in writing. He said he would, as though that had always been inevitable. After we hung up I thought about his five children and how peculiar it is for families to conduct some of their relationships in birthday cards and others in pages of legal definitions. None of us invented the arrangement. All of us have to live sensibly inside it.

ENTRY 452 — 2010-04-26 — Age 24 — A boy with questions

At a weekend nature program a child asked whether salmon knew which creek was theirs because somebody had told them the address. I almost gave a technical explanation and stopped myself. We talked about smell, journeys, and what it would mean to recognize home without reading a map. He thought it was unfair that fish got a secret sense. I agreed it would be convenient. Later I wrote his question in my field notebook—not as data, but because it was good. I wonder whether Daddy's explanations sounded as extravagant to me when I was little. Probably. I certainly kept asking him anyway.

ENTRY 453 — 2010-05-06 — Age 24 — The mother's doctor

I accompanied Mother to a routine appointment. I carried a notebook and wrote down the follow-up instructions because both of us are excellent at remembering the part we prefer. She objected that she wasn't an invalid. I said I wasn't treating her as one; I was taking notes. Afterwards we had lunch at a place she likes and she ordered dessert before I had finished looking at the menu. She was in fine spirits and gossiped shamelessly about an old friend. I won't mistake the pleasure of today for a guarantee about tomorrow, but I refuse to turn every appointment into a tragedy either.

ENTRY 454 — 2010-05-14 — Age 24 — Seattle in spring

I went into Seattle for errands and stayed longer than expected. I passed a shop window with a coat Mother would adore and bought it after checking the size with her. The clerk tried to flatter me into buying another. I resisted, which will surprise no one acquainted with my closets. On the ferry back I watched a crew member secure a line with quick practiced movements. Daddy would have approved of the tidy work. I almost went over to ask what knot he'd used, then recognized it. Being brought up around boats has its advantages, though I still manage to snag my own jacket on a cleat.

ENTRY 455 — 2010-05-25 — Age 24 — What I like about my work

Someone asked whether I considered myself employed. I said I did useful work, some paid and some not, and was fortunate not to have to arrange my life around a salary. The answer seemed to disappoint him. He wanted a title. I could have provided three grand ones and spent the afternoon feeling fraudulent. Instead I told him about monitoring streams, reviewing grants, and helping other people with better credentials do field work. He asked what the results were. At last we had arrived at a question I liked. I promised to send him the annual report and actually did.

ENTRY 456 — 2010-06-04 — Age 24 — A phone call from Brooklyn

An older relative from Mother's side called to ask after her. We spoke about Crown Heights, people whose names I know mostly from stories, and a family recipe everybody claims is the only proper one. When Mother took the phone her Brooklyn vowels came back in a way I never hear at charity dinners. She talked for forty minutes without looking at the clock. I am always surprised by the different ages a person can be in the same room: eighty-something at the table, twenty-five in the cadence of a remembered street. I made a note to ask her more questions while she is willing to answer them.

ENTRY 457 — 2010-06-13 — Age 24 — The waterfall route

We walked to a waterfall I'd visited before. On the approach I stopped repeatedly for mosses, mushrooms, and a beetle so small that my companion accused me of hiking at geological speed. The falls were high after rain and loud enough to defeat conversation. I liked that. We sat on damp stone, ate sandwiches, and watched spray drift across the pool. On the way out I missed a root and stumbled spectacularly without falling. My companion applauded. This is why I prefer friends who can laugh without being cruel. We were home in time for supper, wet and content.

ENTRY 458 — 2010-06-24 — Age 24 — A letter from the school

A local school asked for help supporting an outdoor-education program. I requested their budget and met the teacher who actually runs it. She brought photographs of children measuring water temperature with enormous seriousness. One boy had labelled his sheet WATER IS COLD, a conclusion I could hardly dispute. We discussed equipment, transport, and how to make repeat visits possible instead of funding one spectacular field trip. By the end I had agreed to contribute and to volunteer occasionally. The teacher thanked me twice. I asked her to thank the bus driver, who had apparently kept the whole operation running during a snowstorm.

ENTRY 459 — 2010-07-03 — Age 24 — The boat again

I spent a day checking equipment on the boat with a patient old acquaintance who knows more than I do about every noise an engine makes. We found a tired hose and replaced it before it could become a story. I kept hearing Daddy's voice saying to inspect the thing you most want to assume is fine. We ate lunch on deck. I missed him acutely, then became absorbed in a discussion of charts and barely thought about grief for two hours. I do not believe that is betrayal. It would have pleased him to see me take proper care of the vessel instead of turning it into a shrine.

ENTRY 460 — 2010-07-14 — Age 24 — Travel notebooks

I was sorting older notebooks before a trip and found a sketch of a Swiss path with nearly illegible labels. Evidently I had been trying to record wildflowers and the precise place I'd dropped my lunch. I could identify the flowers more easily than the lunch, though I remember the shame of having bread roll downhill while strangers watched. I transferred the place names carefully into a new index. It is a luxury to visit the same places again and again, and I do not want familiarity to make me inattentive. It is still possible to see something new on a road you've walked twenty times.

ENTRY 461 — 2010-07-26 — Age 24 — Summer in Switzerland

The morning air was cool enough that I wore a sweater until the sun reached the terrace. I walked a familiar hillside and recognized a bend in the path where Mother once insisted we stop because Daddy was moving too fast. I didn't need that memory to enjoy the morning. A farmer was repairing a gate; I watched the little economy of movements and stayed out of his way. Later I compared plant notes with an old guidebook and found one identification I no longer believed. I corrected it. Having written a thing in ink at seventeen does not oblige me to defend it at twenty-four.

ENTRY 462 — 2010-08-05 — Age 24 — The language mistake

I attempted a polite question in French and produced something that made a waiter pause, then smile with heroic restraint. I knew enough to recognize that I'd gone wrong but not enough to recover gracefully. He answered the question I probably intended. I apologized and tried again. Nobody died. My dinner arrived. I wrote down the phrase as soon as I returned to the room. People often assume travel makes a person sophisticated. Mostly it provides a large international audience for one's errors. It also gives one a chance to learn from them.

ENTRY 463 — 2010-08-17 — Age 24 — A small animal

A cat appeared near the guesthouse and decided I was someone likely to provide attention. I offered affection rather than food and it accepted this arrangement with visible disappointment. An older woman told me its name and the household it belonged to. It had no need of rescuing, which is a fact I remind myself of whenever an animal looks plaintive. We sat together for ten minutes. I scratched its ears, and it left without ceremony when a better opportunity presented itself. I admired its honesty.

ENTRY 464 — 2010-08-29 — Age 24 — Home by water

Returning to Bainbridge after travel is always a peculiar relief. I had missed the smell of wet cedar and the slight impatience of ferry boarding. I also discovered that I'd left a library book under a cushion before departing. It was overdue, naturally. I paid the fine without offering an explanation involving international commitments. Mother wanted to hear everything about the trip and interrupted most of the answers with stories of her own visits. We made tea, spread photographs across the table, and disagreed about which day had been the sunniest. This is a form of travel I like just as much as the journey itself.

ENTRY 465 — 2010-09-08 — Age 24 — A field notebook full of errors

I reviewed a season's measurements with the project lead. Two sites had inconsistent recording intervals, and we couldn't just make the numbers behave by drawing a smooth line through them. We marked the uncertainty. One young volunteer looked horrified, as though admitting imperfect data invalidated the whole project. I told him it made the report more useful. Then I found I'd misfiled my own photographs under the wrong site code. He helped me repair the index and was gracious enough not to comment. I owe him at least lunch.

ENTRY 466 — 2010-09-18 — Age 24 — Rosh Hashanah

The synagogue was crowded with people I know in several different ways: as children from long ago, as adults from committees, and as names Mother still associates with their parents. I stood beside her for part of the day and thought about the year I had intended to have and the one that arrived. When people speak of a fresh start, I don't imagine we discard everything behind us. We bring our mistakes and obligations along. Afterward I listened to an old family story I'd heard at least five times and noticed a detail I'd missed. I asked a question. Mother said I was impossible. She was smiling.

ENTRY 467 — 2010-09-27 — Age 24 — A hard conversation about a gift

The synagogue's treasurer asked whether my annual contribution could be pledged earlier for planning purposes. I said yes in principle, but asked for the timetable and what obligations the commitment would create. It isn't a sign of weak faith to read the papers. I care about the congregation, and I want the people managing its work to be able to make reliable plans. We worked it out without drama. Later I met a child in the hall who asked why adults always looked tired after meetings. I said we sometimes make very simple things difficult. He considered this obvious.

ENTRY 468 — 2010-10-06 — Age 24 — A cup on the stairs

I was carrying two mugs upstairs when my heel caught the runner. I saved myself and lost the tea, which is a fair trade. It ran down three steps, soaked a little mat, and made the house smell wonderful while I cleaned. Mother happened to telephone in the middle of it. I told her what I'd done. She suggested I stop walking with two mugs. I said I would consider her radical proposal. For some reason that made me laugh until I nearly slipped again. I finished the cleaning and wrote DO NOT CARRY TWO MUGS in the notebook. This instruction will have approximately no effect.

ENTRY 469 — 2010-10-17 — Age 24 — A man at the fundraiser

A pleasant man spent an evening talking to me about travel and conservation. I enjoyed him. He asked intelligent questions, listened to the answers, and did not appear remotely impressed by the room we were in. Then he asked a careful question about my trust. I answered generally and watched him change the subject. I don't know what that means. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps plenty. I refuse to cross-examine every person who speaks to me, but I am allowed to notice the order in which they ask things. We exchanged telephone numbers. I came home pleased with the conversation and wary of my own pleasure.

ENTRY 470 — 2010-10-29 — Age 24 — He calls again

The man from the fundraiser called to ask whether I'd like to join a small group for a hike. I said yes. We talked about boots, weather and the length of the route, not money. I was glad. Afterward I caught myself rehearsing replies to questions he hadn't asked. I put the phone down and went out to refill the bird feeder instead. A chickadee arrived before I'd shut the lid. Birds are marvellously direct about what they want, although perhaps that is an argument against trusting them too much. I am trying not to turn a simple invitation into a courtroom case.

ENTRY 471 — 2010-11-07 — Age 24 — A decent hike

Our group hiked in chill weather and came home with muddy trousers. The man was considerate without making a production of it. He helped an older walker over a rough section and did not attempt to make the gesture visible to me. I liked that more than anything he said. I also noticed he knew very little about the trees but asked honest questions. We stopped at a viewpoint and passed around chocolate. My hand slipped on the wrapper and I dropped a square into the leaves. Nobody made a speech about it. Perhaps there are people one can simply enjoy knowing before deciding what they mean.

ENTRY 472 — 2010-11-18 — Age 24 — Cold air, clear thoughts

The relationship, if it deserves that word yet, is still no more than a few conversations and two walks. Mother asked whether I was seeing anyone and I said I was seeing trees, birds, and sometimes a man among them. She regarded this as an evasion, which it was. I don't want a committee to review him before I know whether I like him. I also don't want to hide ordinary happiness as though it were evidence of foolishness. I walked home thinking about this and passed my own driveway by accident. I blame the dark, although the moon was bright.

ENTRY 473 — 2010-11-27 — Age 24 — An old family photograph

We spent an evening looking through photographs and came upon one of Daddy in Navy uniform. He looked so young I felt almost intrusive staring at it. Mother told me one small story about his return and then another about the first time she heard him play piano. She gave no grand interpretation of either. I put the photograph back carefully. When I was a child he was always an old man relative to the fathers at school; in that picture he was sixteen or seventeen and could not possibly have known who he would become. I wish I'd asked him more about what he expected from life then. Perhaps he would have told me to listen to the music instead.

ENTRY 474 — 2010-12-06 — Age 24 — The ordinary committee

We spent ninety minutes deciding the order of three agenda items. I was irritated, then realized I was partly responsible because I kept trying to improve the wording. Governance is not glamorous. I sat on my hands through the final revision and let the chair finish. On the way out another member told me she appreciated my careful questions. I said she might appreciate fewer of them next month. She laughed. I am learning that having the resources to support a project does not entitle me to dominate every conversation about it. I hope that lesson sticks better than the one about carrying two mugs.

ENTRY 475 — 2010-12-15 — Age 24 — Something nice without a plan

The man I have been seeing brought me a book about western rivers, not a lavish present. He'd remembered a passing remark about a chapter I wanted to read. I was touched and then suspicious of being touched, which is tiresome. We had supper and talked about his work and my latest surveys. He was a little shy when I thanked him. I don't know where this will lead, and tonight I don't need to know. I am allowed a pleasant evening without either a wedding plan or a security analysis. I wrote that down because I will need reminding.

ENTRY 476 — 2010-12-24 — Age 24 — The house lights

Mother's house was warm and full of people coming and going. We aren't celebrating Christmas as a religious holiday, but plenty of our old friends do, and she has always been delighted to feed them when they appear. Someone brought cookies shaped like stars. A neighbor asked after the boat. We played records and argued about whether one solo was better on the original recording or in a later live performance. Daddy would have had an opinion so definite that he would have repeated it three times. I found myself defending the later recording and then laughing because I sounded just like him.

ENTRY 477 — 2010-12-31 — Age 24 — No grand resolution

My new notebook is almost full. I have spent a great many pages on birds, ferry crossings, misplaced objects, Mother's appointments, holidays, grant budgets, and conversations whose importance I cannot predict. That seems appropriate. I am still inclined to think well of people until I have reason not to. I am also better at saying no without making a little apology to go with it. Nothing about this year requires a dramatic final sentence. Mother is well enough to argue about the next trip, the boat needs its usual attention, and there is a book waiting beside my bed. I am going to read it now. Happy New Year, Gwen.
