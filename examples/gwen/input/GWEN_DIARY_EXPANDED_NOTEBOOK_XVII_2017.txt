Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Original fictional first-person diary. Later friendships, romantic relationships, and projects are provisional unless established by Steve. No 2026 memory-loss event or explanation is added.

ENTRY 685 — 2017-01-03 — Age 30 — The blue folder

I found Mother's blue folder under the stack of kitchen calendars. Inside were menus from dinners no one could remember and a receipt for a chair she had complained about for twelve years. I almost threw the receipts away, then sat on the floor reading them. I have put practical records in one drawer and things I am not ready to categorize in another. That is not a filing system. It is what I can manage today.

------------------------------

ENTRY 686 — 2017-01-21 — Age 30 — A stranger at the ferry

A woman offered me the last dry seat on the ferry when the wind shifted rain under the shelter. She did not know who I was. We talked about a dog that refused to board without its stuffed rabbit. I laughed more than I expected. The ordinary kindness of strangers feels enormous at the moment.

------------------------------

ENTRY 687 — 2017-02-05 — Age 30 — Too much ivy

I pulled ivy from a cedar at a volunteer work party and learned again that impatience is no substitute for loosening the roots. I brought mud into the car despite three towels. Somebody teased me about the vehicle, then apologized when I told him there was nothing to apologize for. I'd rather have dirt on the floor than ivy around that tree.

------------------------------

ENTRY 688 — 2017-02-23 — Age 30 — The piano bench

I opened the bench and found Dad's pencil markings on old scores. He circled a difficult measure in red and wrote 'not faster—cleaner.' I played the phrase badly three times. Then I tried it slowly. The room felt inhabited for a moment, though of course I was the only one there.

------------------------------

ENTRY 689 — 2017-03-12 — Age 30 — A trust meeting

My uncle sent a carefully prepared packet. I asked questions about distributions, fees, and the investment policy. It was perfectly civil, if a little stiff. I know what he thinks I should do and I know what I want the money to do. I need to be both polite and attentive; neither requires pretending not to understand the figures.

------------------------------

ENTRY 690 — 2017-04-11 — Age 30 — A smaller Seder

We set fewer places this Pesach. I had written the names on slips of paper to decide where everyone should sit, and kept moving one slip back to its old position. A friend gently took it away. Nobody tried to cheer me up. We ate, read, argued about a translation and washed dishes together.

------------------------------

ENTRY 691 — 2017-04-29 — Age 30 — A heron on the pier

The heron stood so still I wondered whether it had already decided I was part of the dock. It struck into the water and came up with a fish. I wrote down where the tide was on the pilings. Dad would have asked about wind and current. Mother would have asked if the bird looked pleased with itself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 692 — 2017-05-18 — Age 30 — A day without appointments

I put my telephone on silent and walked until the trail became a little steep for the shoes I'd chosen. At the overlook I ate an orange with cold fingers. I am trying not to turn every day into another set of tasks. I went home when I was tired, not when I had finished anything.

------------------------------

ENTRY 693 — 2017-06-07 — Age 30 — The board proposal

Our restoration committee wanted a donor letter full of promises no field scientist could defend. I marked the questionable claims. The chair called me difficult. I told him the stream would not improve because our adjectives were beautiful. We rewrote the letter and nobody died of embarrassment.

------------------------------

ENTRY 694 — 2017-06-26 — Age 30 — Brooklyn voices

I called relatives in New York. Somebody remembered Mother dancing at a gathering before she ever moved west. Three people disagreed about the song. I wrote down all three versions. Memories are not a jury trial; I don't need to declare a winner.

------------------------------

ENTRY 695 — 2017-07-14 — Age 30 — The spare life jacket

A child visiting the marina asked why there were so many sizes of life jackets. I showed her the labels and how the straps work, without letting her climb aboard. Her questions were good ones. I remembered Dad checking mine every single time, even when I was old enough to be annoyed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 696 — 2017-08-09 — Age 30 — Switzerland without Mother

I went back to a walking route we used to take together. The innkeeper remembered her and did not make a speech about loss. She just asked whether I wanted the same room. I took a different one. In the morning the meadow was wet and the bells sounded from farther up the slope.

------------------------------

ENTRY 697 — 2017-09-08 — Age 31 — Thirty-one

I ate a late birthday breakfast with friends. One brought field guides, another a rather silly scarf covered in otters. They know me well. I broke a glass while thanking them, which may be my way of keeping sentiment from becoming unbearable.

------------------------------

ENTRY 698 — 2017-09-30 — Age 31 — The synagogue steps

I stayed after services to help fold tables and stack prayer books. Someone who knew Mother told me a story about an argument they had years ago. It ended with both women laughing, and I felt grateful to hear about Mother being irritating rather than saintly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 699 — 2017-10-17 — Age 31 — Not a case to win

A man I'd been seeing asked why I wouldn't tell him more about the trust. I said because it wasn't relevant to whether we enjoyed having dinner. He grew offended at the boundary itself. I didn't shout. I went home and wrote down exactly what he said so I could consider it fairly tomorrow.

------------------------------

ENTRY 700 — 2017-11-06 — Age 31 — One year

I visited Mother's grave and told her about a clogged culvert, a badly behaved dog, and an argument over the synagogue budget. I didn't tell her how sad I was; I think that part would have been obvious. Afterward I bought groceries and forgot the bread.

------------------------------

ENTRY 701 — 2017-11-23 — Age 31 — Rain on the skylight

The storm arrived all at once. I made soup and watched the kitchen skylight fog. I am still learning how much food one person needs, especially when she buys vegetables as if expecting a dinner party. I froze half of it in clearly labeled containers. Mother would have mocked the labels and asked for some to take home.

------------------------------

ENTRY 702 — 2017-12-31 — Age 31 — Inventory

I have traveled, worked, prayed, cried, gone to meetings and made a few new friends this year. None of it adds up to replacing anybody. It adds up to a year. I have kept the old house, though I may eventually decide otherwise. Tonight I can hear the clock ticking in the hallway and am glad I have not stopped writing.
