[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook XV: 2015]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

AUTHORSHIP NOTE: Fictional first-person diary. All new events and unnamed relationships are provisional unless Steve established them. Gwen’s mother dies at age thirty, in 2016; exact date chosen provisionally here.


ENTRY 632 — 2015-01-06 — Age 28 — A familiar cough

Mother has had a cough that lingers longer than she'd like. She saw her physician, who suggested follow-up if it didn't improve. I am recording what actually happened because my mind likes to jump ahead. She was annoyed that I wanted to know the date of the appointment. We watched an old film and she fell asleep during a scene she insists is her favorite. I put a blanket over her knees and left the television low.

------------------------------

ENTRY 633 — 2015-01-19 — Age 28 — Field season planning

There is always a way for a project to turn from creek restoration into committee correspondence. I counted thirty-seven unread messages and considered moving into the marsh permanently. Instead I answered them, updated the sampling calendar, and arranged for a replacement meter. Someone made a useful suggestion that I had initially dismissed; I wrote back to thank her. This job is easier when I remember I am not the only person who can think.

------------------------------

ENTRY 634 — 2015-02-02 — Age 28 — A lesson in leaving things alone

An injured-looking gull on the beach was standing oddly, but it flew strongly when another person came near. I called the rehabilitation contact for advice instead of trying to catch it. I hate doing nothing, and sometimes doing nothing until more is known is the correct thing. The gull was gone when I returned. That does not tell me whether it is safe. I wrote down its markings in case someone else sees it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 635 — 2015-02-16 — Age 28 — The coat from Paris

Mother asked me to help sort her closet. We found a coat she bought in Paris years ago, beautifully made and slightly ridiculous for our weather. She told me about a shop assistant who recognized her three seasons in a row. I asked why she'd kept the coat if she rarely wore it. She said because not everything valuable has to be useful. Then she asked why I keep a collection of chipped stones from creek beds. I conceded the argument.

------------------------------

ENTRY 636 — 2015-03-03 — Age 28 — An appointment

Mother let me drive her to a medical appointment. We had coffee afterward and made jokes about the waiting room magazines being older than either of us, which isn't possible and therefore improved the joke. She is still very much herself: opinionated, curious, and stubborn about opening her own car door. She wants help when she asks for it. I am learning to offer once instead of five times.

------------------------------

ENTRY 637 — 2015-03-16 — Age 28 — Synagogue committee

We discussed the building repairs and a scholarship fund. I have increased support over time, but money shouldn't entitle me to control every decision. I asked the committee to send clear reports and to protect the assistance recipients' privacy. One member worried aloud about costs; another worried about embarrassment. Both were thinking of real people. I went home glad the conversation was difficult enough to require care.

------------------------------

ENTRY 638 — 2015-04-04 — Age 28 — Pesach with fewer chairs

This year's Seder was lovely, though Mother tired before we finished clearing away. I tried to persuade her to leave the dishes until morning and she gave me a look that belonged to the Brooklyn girl who crossed the country to live as she pleased. We compromised: she sat and instructed me in the proper storage of things I have stored every year since adolescence. I let her win on the serving platter.

------------------------------

ENTRY 639 — 2015-04-22 — Age 28 — Earth Day without speeches

I spent the day with volunteers removing invasive plants beside a creek. There were children, retirees, two employees from a company I have criticized publicly, and one woman who brought excellent sandwiches. Nobody asked us to agree on everything. We worked until we were tired and then counted bags. The land looked temporarily worse where the roots had been pulled, which is often how repair begins. I washed mud off my hands and felt content.

------------------------------

ENTRY 640 — 2015-05-09 — Age 28 — Mother's stories

We recorded another conversation about her early years in Seattle. She corrected her own dates twice and laughed about a dance she attended in a dress she sewed herself. I asked about the music, the people, the work at the firm, the first apartment. I did not interrogate her about the affair; she already knows I understand that part. Today she wanted to remember a friend whose laugh was like a squeaky gate. I wrote the friend's name down carefully.

------------------------------

ENTRY 641 — 2015-05-22 — Age 28 — A difficult morning

Mother was exhausted after errands and asked to go home early. I canceled a meeting and stayed with her. She protested that she did not need a keeper. I said I needed company, and she allowed herself to be persuaded. We ate toast and talked about a neighbor's roses. Nothing dramatic occurred. I am trying to remember that time spent together need not be justified by a crisis.

------------------------------

ENTRY 642 — 2015-06-05 — Age 28 — Lake Washington

I took the boat out with a friend who has better patience for docking than I do. There was a crosswind, one line in the wrong place, and a few moments when my father would have had something very specific to say. We corrected it without damage. Later I told my friend about Dad's Navy years and his piano jobs in college. The friend said he sounded exhausting. I laughed because sometimes he was.

------------------------------

ENTRY 643 — 2015-06-19 — Age 28 — A small trip

Mother and I took a short trip rather than a long overseas holiday. She chose the comfortable hotel and I chose a walk beside the water. We had lunch looking at boats and she remarked that I still watch them the way I did at six. She bought me a scarf I do not need. I bought her a book she already owns. We exchanged them at dinner and decided we were excellent shoppers.

------------------------------

ENTRY 644 — 2015-07-03 — Age 28 — How much help is enough

My uncle called about trust documents, and we also talked about Mother's expenses. I asked for a clear distinction between payments made for my benefit, gifts from my own distributions, and bills I could simply pay myself. The structure matters even when everyone involved is family. Mother would be furious to hear that I discussed her like an accounting item, so I won't. I'll tell her I made sure the paperwork is right, because that's what I did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 645 — 2015-07-19 — Age 28 — The garden bench

We moved a bench into the shade because Mother prefers sitting outside where she can hear birds. She told me I had placed it at the wrong angle, then moved it two inches with a look of triumph. I sat beside her. A hummingbird came and went so quickly that neither of us had time to identify it properly. We spent fifteen minutes disagreeing about its coloring. I cherish these entirely inconclusive conversations.

------------------------------

ENTRY 646 — 2015-08-02 — Age 28 — The plan changed

I had been scheduled for several days of fieldwork, but Mother needed more help and I arranged coverage. My colleagues were kind. I dislike being the person who changes plans, though I am always happy to rearrange things for others. We reviewed the site notes over the phone. Then I made soup and realized I'd added salt twice. Mother ate it anyway and advised me not to become a professional cook.

------------------------------

ENTRY 647 — 2015-08-18 — Age 28 — The old address book

I copied names and numbers from Mother's address book while she watched and supplied corrections. Some of the people are gone, some have moved, some she hasn't seen in twenty years. She wanted the book kept, not replaced with an app. I agree. Her handwriting contains a history of changes: pencil erased, ink overwritten, a note saying 'call after six' beside the name of someone who has been dead for years.

------------------------------

ENTRY 648 — 2015-09-09 — Age 29 — Twenty-nine

Mother asked what I wanted for my birthday. I said an afternoon without anyone asking what comes next. She said that seemed an easy gift. We went out for lunch, and she told me a story about my father that I hadn't heard: he once practiced the same musical phrase at the piano so long she threatened to disconnect the electricity. He answered that pianos predated electricity. I laughed until I couldn't finish my tea.

------------------------------

ENTRY 649 — 2015-09-23 — Age 29 — High Holy Days

Mother wanted to attend services and did, though she rested more than usual. I stayed beside her after the congregation dispersed. We talked about forgiveness, which in our family can become an argument with historical exhibits. She said forgiveness isn't the same as claiming people didn't do what they did. I wrote that sentence down. It sounds simple, but I want to remember her exact words.

------------------------------

ENTRY 650 — 2015-10-08 — Age 29 — Her opinion of me

Mother says I take too much responsibility for other people's comfort. I said I have a talent for falling over furniture instead, which keeps expectations modest. She said she wasn't joking. We had a long conversation about helping without smothering, including whether I should continue staying over so often. I agreed to listen when she tells me what she actually wants. That may be harder than any technical problem I have worked on.

------------------------------

ENTRY 651 — 2015-10-25 — Age 29 — A quiet evening

We listened to a record and she told me which musicians Dad would have argued about. I can hear his voice when she imitates him, and it makes me laugh. I made notes about the recording so I can find it later. After she went to bed I washed the cups and sat looking at the garden. I'm not sure whether this is a good year or a hard year. Both descriptions fit.

------------------------------

ENTRY 652 — 2015-11-07 — Age 29 — Not everything gets finished

I postponed an environmental site visit because the family calendar was crowded with appointments. A volunteer offered to cover the measuring station and did excellent work. I thanked her and sent a note to the team. I have to fight the belief that things only count when I personally do them. Mother once said the same about her years at the law firm: you learn to delegate or you drown in everybody's paperwork.

------------------------------

ENTRY 653 — 2015-11-19 — Age 29 — A change in the house

Mother has started keeping a chair near the door so she can sit while putting on shoes. She wanted a nice one, not something that looked like hospital equipment. We found a wooden chair with a good back and a seat at the right height. She inspected the finish as severely as a judge. We brought it home and she told me the entire house was improving because of her age. I told her not to get any ideas about the kitchen cabinets.

------------------------------

ENTRY 654 — 2015-12-02 — Age 29 — A letter I should have sent

I wrote to an old friend from UW whom I hadn't contacted in months. She wrote back almost immediately with news of her work and two children, and asked if I was all right. I said yes, mostly, and told her about Mother's health without making it sound like a weather report. She offered to visit. I felt relieved. Caring for someone can make your world smaller unless you make a point of keeping the door open.

------------------------------

ENTRY 655 — 2015-12-16 — Age 29 — First winter storm

Wind took a branch down near the driveway. No one was hurt. I called the arborist rather than attempting heroics with tools I have no business using. Mother said my father would have fetched a saw. I replied that he would also have hired three people afterward to correct what he'd done. We both laughed. The power flickered once and came back. I checked the flashlight batteries as Dad taught me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 656 — 2015-12-31 — Age 29 — One more year

We stayed home, ate a late supper, and listened to music. Mother seemed tired but content. I am twenty-nine and apparently still capable of wanting her to be here forever, which is a childish wish I will not apologize for. The notebook has more entries about her than usual. That's because we have been spending so much time together. I want it to remain a record of living, not a countdown. Tomorrow we will make breakfast and complain about the rain.
