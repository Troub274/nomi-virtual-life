[Gwendolin Lynn Mayer — Personal Diary, Expanded Notebook XI: 2011]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 478 — 2011-01-03 — Age 25 — An old promise

The first task of the year was not a resolution but a promise I made to a woman at the restoration group: sort the photographs from last autumn before anyone forgets which bank they were taken from. I spent the morning comparing the pictures with my muddy notebook. One filename said north when I meant south. That small error would have sent a volunteer to the wrong place in March. I fixed it and made tea. The man I've been seeing telephoned while the kettle boiled. He asked whether the bird feeder survived the wind. I was pleased he remembered the feeder, but I didn't tell him that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 479 — 2011-01-09 — Age 25 — In the rain with another person

We walked in rain that never decided whether to become a storm. He brought a spare pair of gloves for himself and offered me one when mine soaked through. We ended up wearing mismatched pairs, which pleased me more than it should have. He asked why I kept stopping to look at ordinary trees. I showed him the moss on one trunk and the tiny seedlings lodged in a split. It is astonishing how much living can happen in a few inches of bark. We were quiet for a long stretch without being uncomfortable. I liked that, and afterward I tried very hard not to draw a conclusion from it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 480 — 2011-01-16 — Age 25 — The cousins at lunch

Lunch with two cousins and an uncle who regards punctuality as a moral virtue. He asked about my work and seemed relieved when I could describe a budget without becoming indignant about money. One cousin is very busy with children and showed me thirty photographs before we ordered. I loved the photographs; I also wanted to eat. The other asked whether I was getting serious about someone. I told him I was serious about a deadline on Thursday. The family can tell the difference between an answer and an evasion. They are mostly gracious enough to permit both. On the ferry I wrote down the children's names again so I wouldn't mix them up.

------------------------------

ENTRY 481 — 2011-01-23 — Age 25 — My father's records

I found a folder of Dad's old piano arrangements tucked into a box of navigation books. Some are written in a precise hand, others contain scratches and little impatient arrows. He had put a tiny question mark beside a chord he disliked. I played it badly, revised it, and played it badly another way. Mother says he used to work something out at the piano after a hard day, sometimes for twenty minutes, sometimes until supper grew cold. I sat on the bench looking at his pencil marks for a long while. A person's handwriting can feel more immediate than a photograph. I'm keeping the folder where I can reach it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 482 — 2011-01-31 — Age 25 — A permit and a stapler

The conservation meeting was ninety percent practical work and ten percent people saying 'vision' as though the word completed the application. We needed a right-of-entry letter and photographs from the lower creek. I offered to help with both. Afterward I realized I had taken the office stapler home in my field bag. I drove it back the next day rather than pretending it had migrated on its own. The coordinator laughed and said she had been wondering where it went. I am capable of following complicated grant requirements, yet apparently unable to distinguish a stapler from equipment I own.

------------------------------

ENTRY 483 — 2011-02-06 — Age 25 — A story from Crown Heights

Mother told me about a winter morning in Brooklyn when a neighbor brought hot food up three flights of stairs because her grandmother had taken ill. She remembered the smell better than the words the neighbor used. I asked why she never tells that story when people ask about the wild years in Seattle. She said nobody ever asks the right question. I had asked about food, not rebellion. We sat a long time while she talked about relatives who are now gone and a corner shop that probably isn't there anymore. I wrote down the names when I got home. Their lives ought not vanish just because I never met them.

------------------------------

ENTRY 484 — 2011-02-12 — Age 25 — The check at dinner

We had dinner in Seattle. He reached for the check before I'd even seen it, then told me we could split it if I preferred. Such a small moment and such an absurd amount of thought on my part. I let him pay and bought dessert elsewhere. He didn't seem to mind either choice. I told myself to stop turning ordinary courtesy into a test, but my mind kept looking for the fine print. He asked about my work and then complained that I had asked him too few questions about his. Fair complaint. I was more interested in the person in front of me than in solving the problem of what he might someday want.

------------------------------

ENTRY 485 — 2011-02-20 — Age 25 — The pair of boots

I tried on new hiking boots in a shop where a young clerk knew much more about fit than the price of anything. She made me walk a little ramp and explained how my toes should feel going downhill. I had gone in intending to buy the most durable pair; I left with the pair that actually fit. On the way out I knocked a display of socks sideways and spent five minutes restoring it while the clerk politely insisted she could do it. The boots are plain brown. I hope they last years. I also hope no sock display remembers me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 486 — 2011-02-27 — Age 25 — Not everyone needs a foundation

A donor lunch made me vaguely cross. A speaker suggested that every substantial gift should have a building, scholarship, or program carrying the donor's name. I asked whether the creek cared what name appeared on its signs. That was not especially diplomatic, but I was tired of the discussion. Later I apologized for the sharpness, not the question. Mother says I sound most like my father when I become very quiet before making a point. I would rather not be remembered for the quietness. I'd rather be useful. We funded monitoring equipment without naming it after anybody.

ENTRY 487 — 2011-03-05 — Age 25 — A Saturday of nothing much

I slept late, bought groceries, cleaned the refrigerator, and put three books in a stack that now looks precarious enough to count as furniture. I read in the afternoon until rain blurred the windows. An old friend called and spoke for forty minutes about a problem at work. I gave no advice until she asked. It felt like an accomplishment. I ate toast for supper because I couldn't be bothered to cook the vegetables I'd bought. This is the sort of entry nobody would put in a biography, but I was here and the day happened. The toast was excellent.

ENTRY 488 — 2011-03-13 — Age 25 — Counting returning birds

We helped with a count along a stretch of shore. The tide was lower than expected, and the mud smelled of salt and decomposing leaves. A new volunteer confidently identified a bird that was too far away for any of us to be certain. I admired his enthusiasm and suggested writing 'unconfirmed' rather than making the list more impressive. Later I made the same mistake with another bird and corrected my own notes. There is a particular pleasure in a record that tells the truth, including what it cannot tell. We saw a seal near the point just before packing up. Nobody needed a clipboard to enjoy that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 489 — 2011-03-21 — Age 25 — A look at Mother's stairs

Mother says the stairs are perfectly fine. I say the loose edge of the runner is an invitation to break a hip. We settled the dispute by getting someone to secure it. She watched him work and remarked that she used to climb fire escapes in Brooklyn without anybody panicking. I told her I would happily avoid panicking if she stopped creating opportunities. She was laughing when I left, and I was too. Later I felt foolish for worrying so much about one carpet edge. Then I remembered Dad and decided there is no virtue in ignoring a small, fixable danger.

------------------------------

ENTRY 490 — 2011-03-29 — Age 25 — A letter from Jerusalem

A friend sent a postcard from Jerusalem with a note too cramped to read easily. She had written about the sound of people preparing for Shabbat and the pleasure of recognizing a bakery from an earlier visit. I stood at the kitchen counter trying to decipher her last sentence. It was about a cat that apparently chose her table for its afternoon nap. Travel memories attach themselves to the strangest anchors. When I think of Jerusalem I remember stones warmed by sun, my mother's pace in a crowded street, and a shopkeeper correcting my Hebrew with a grin. I sent my friend a long email in return.

ENTRY 491 — 2011-04-06 — Age 25 — A quiet disagreement

At a project meeting I disagreed with a consultant who wanted to omit the failed plantings from a public summary. He said the losses would confuse people. I said the absence of losses would mislead them. We went back and forth politely until he suggested the numbers be placed in an appendix instead. I agreed provided the report pointed to it plainly. An hour later I was embarrassed by how much the exchange had irritated me. But losing plants isn't shameful; hiding the result is. I came home, made soup, and spilled half the salt into the pot. Some failures are more immediately detectable than others.

------------------------------

ENTRY 492 — 2011-04-15 — Age 25 — Getting ready for Pesach

Mother has a list for the Seder that could run a small municipal department. I came early to help and was assigned the things she knows I can do without argument: check the table, carry chairs, call two relatives, and label serving dishes. We discussed the order of readings. She changed her mind twice and declared that she had never changed it at all. I love her. When the house quieted, I sat with the Haggadah and remembered being little enough to practice the questions while swinging my legs beneath the table. I still have questions. I am relieved about that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 493 — 2011-04-20 — Age 25 — The Seder table

The room filled with voices, food, and the familiar scramble for enough serving spoons. A child stumbled over a line of Hebrew and started again without anyone making a fuss. Mother looked pleased. An uncle objected to an interpretation and we ended up talking about freedom for much longer than expected. The conversation was untidy, affectionate, and occasionally exasperating. I carried plates and nearly knocked over a water glass with the elbow I was trying to keep out of the way. Afterward, Mother sat in her chair with her shoes off and announced that next year we would invite fewer people. She has said this every year that I can remember.

------------------------------

ENTRY 494 — 2011-04-29 — Age 25 — An honest walk

He asked whether I was reluctant to be introduced to his friends. I said yes, then immediately wished I'd said something more graceful. We walked along the water while I explained that my life comes with a great deal of other people's curiosity and that I don't like making new acquaintances feel like applicants. He listened and told me he understood some of it, not all of it. That was a much better answer than claiming he understood everything. We made no promise about the future. We bought coffee and sat watching a ferry pull away from the dock. I was glad we'd talked.

------------------------------

ENTRY 495 — 2011-05-07 — Age 25 — A dog with opinions

At the rescue event a large old dog leaned his full weight against my leg and refused to move. His handler said he'd chosen me. I suspect he had chosen the shady spot beside my chair. Either way, we kept each other company for nearly an hour. Children petted him gently after being shown how. One boy asked why old dogs turn gray. I told him it is a reward for surviving younger dogs. The handler laughed. I came home covered in fur and thinking about how animals can ask very little of us and still take a great deal of time, patience, and care.

------------------------------

ENTRY 496 — 2011-05-14 — Age 25 — The trust papers again

My uncle sent a packet about quarterly allocations. I read the letter twice, then the attachments, then telephoned with three questions. He answered without surprise. I think he has grown used to my wanting to know not merely whether something is permitted but who has to carry it out and what record remains. He asked after Mother and I gave him the honest answer: she's sharp, opinionated, and gets tired more easily than she admits. The money is an instrument, not an explanation of anyone's character. I wish more people who discuss my money would remember that it belongs to a web of obligations as well as possibilities.

------------------------------

ENTRY 497 — 2011-05-22 — Age 25 — A thunderstorm in Seattle

Thunder arrived while I was at a friend's apartment. We watched rain turn the street into a silver sheet and listened to a neighbor playing trumpet very badly somewhere below. I liked the trumpet; it seemed determined to survive the weather. When the storm passed, I found my umbrella exactly where I had left it, which is unusual enough to record. We ate pasta, argued about whether a documentary had been fair to its subjects, and ended the evening discussing somebody's cat. No one mentioned the trust. It was a perfectly ordinary evening, and I found myself unexpectedly grateful for it.

ENTRY 498 — 2011-06-01 — Age 25 — A young volunteer

A teenager helping on the creek crew asked whether environmental science was a good way to change the world. I told her it is a good way to learn what is actually happening in one piece of it. Change requires other things too: neighbors, budgets, law, stubbornness, maintenance, sometimes luck. She looked disappointed until we found the first healthy new growth from last autumn's planting. She photographed it with great ceremony. I told her to take a second photo from the same marker next month. Perhaps the least glamorous part of helping the world is returning to see whether the first attempt worked.

------------------------------

ENTRY 499 — 2011-06-09 — Age 25 — Aboard the boat

I went aboard to check a few things I'd been putting off. The cabin smelled of varnish, clean rope, and the stale air of a closed space. I found one of Dad's old penciled notes folded into a navigation book. It was about an unremarkable repair, not an inspiring sentence. I read it three times anyway. I can remember him correcting my grip on a line, saying firmly that a rope doesn't care how much you wish it would behave. The man I've been seeing offered to help with the boat sometime. I said perhaps. Some spaces I share easily. This one takes a little more thought.

------------------------------

ENTRY 500 — 2011-06-18 — Age 25 — One badly planned picnic

We planned a picnic for six people and somehow nobody brought a knife. There was bread, cheese, fruit, a very large cake, and no practical way to divide half of it. A friend used the edge of a clean plate to cut the cake, which worked better than expected. Then the wind took three napkins and my hat. I recovered the hat from a bush with more haste than dignity. We laughed until our faces hurt. It was a good afternoon. I am writing down the missing knife because someone should remember it next time, although I know perfectly well we will forget something else.

------------------------------

ENTRY 501 — 2011-06-27 — Age 25 — Summer in Switzerland

The air in the village smelled of cut hay and the sharp green scent that comes after rain at altitude. I went walking early and spent far too long identifying flowers beside a path I'd taken on earlier visits. A woman tending her garden recognized me before I recognized her. She asked after Mother in careful English, and I answered with my uncertain French and an apologetic smile. In the afternoon I called home. Mother wanted to know whether I had eaten a proper lunch and whether my shoes were waterproof. There are evidently some questions a woman will ask her daughter until the end of time.

------------------------------

ENTRY 502 — 2011-07-04 — Age 25 — An Alpine morning

I took a long uphill route and reached a meadow while cloud shadows moved over the grass. Cows rang their bells somewhere out of sight. I sat on a stone eating bread and cheese and thinking about nothing important. Later, I sketched a drainage line and wrote notes about where the vegetation changed. I have been in expensive places and grand rooms, but I can't remember any chandelier giving me the satisfaction of finding water emerging unexpectedly from under rock. I came down late, sunburned in a narrow stripe above my collar, and had to admit I had forgotten sunscreen yet again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 503 — 2011-07-13 — Age 25 — The little museum

A rainy afternoon sent me into a small local museum. I spent forty minutes with a display of old agricultural tools while other visitors moved through in six. One tool's purpose escaped me until a volunteer explained it. I love being corrected by someone who actually knows the object. At supper I told my companions about it and managed to knock the salt shaker into my glass. Everyone has become accustomed to my turning an interesting explanation into a small housekeeping event. We talked about Swiss farming, then the weather, then nothing more ambitious than where to walk tomorrow.

------------------------------

ENTRY 504 — 2011-07-22 — Age 25 — The wrong path

A path marker looked familiar, and I turned without checking my map. Twenty minutes later I realized the stream was on the wrong side of the trail. I wasn't lost in any serious sense; I could see the valley and retraced my steps. Still, I was annoyed that I had allowed recognition to substitute for attention. At the junction I stopped and looked properly. There were two signs, clear as could be, and I had walked straight past one. Being clever doesn't exempt a person from being careless. At least the detour led me past a patch of wildflowers I might otherwise have missed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 505 — 2011-07-31 — Age 25 — A letter to Mother

I wrote Mother a proper letter instead of an email. I described the mountain walks, a musician playing outside a café, and the bread I have been eating with such enthusiasm that my trousers are beginning to complain. I left out nothing but a minor blister that has already healed. She would immediately turn it into a footwear inquiry. At the end I told her I wished she were sitting across the table so we could disagree about some trivial thing. I thought it sounded sentimental, but sent it anyway. She telephoned two days later and said the handwriting was getting worse.

ENTRY 506 — 2011-08-08 — Age 25 — Home and the ferry

I returned to Bainbridge with a bag full of notes and an inexplicable collection of ticket stubs. The ferry crossing felt like a familiar breath. I like being elsewhere, and I like coming back. Both things are true. A friend met me for supper. We discussed a new survey project, the disappearance of a favorite bakery item, and somebody's difficult landlord. I unpacked late and found a chocolate bar flattened beneath my field notebook. The chocolate was still edible. The notebook now smells faintly of cocoa, which might improve the experience of entering data.

ENTRY 507 — 2011-08-16 — Age 25 — A matter of photographs

I spent hours sorting site photographs, searching for the same angle from the previous year. The vegetation had grown, the light had shifted, and the trail had been rerouted after a washout. The differences were real, but they were not all evidence of ecological change. I made notes about each one. A colleague said I was being fastidious. I agreed. That is the point. If I am asking people to give money or effort based on a result, I should know which parts of the result we can defend. I went home and forgot the groceries I'd bought in the car for twenty minutes. Fortunately nothing suffered but the lettuce.

------------------------------

ENTRY 508 — 2011-08-25 — Age 25 — The man and his plans

He asked if I could imagine a future in which we traveled together more often. The question was kind, but it made me uneasy. I answered that I could imagine trips and also imagined having time alone. He looked a little surprised. We talked about the difference between planning something and claiming it before it exists. I was proud of my honesty and afterward worried I had been unnecessarily severe. He sent a cheerful message the next morning about a bird we'd seen. I was relieved, then annoyed at myself for needing relief. It is difficult to know someone while simultaneously trying to protect a future you haven't lived.

------------------------------

ENTRY 509 — 2011-09-03 — Age 25 — Labor Day water

We took a small boat out for an afternoon with friends. I checked the weather, listened to somebody discuss a new outboard, and helped with lines because standing idle on a boat feels wrong. A guest asked whether I learned all this on vacations. I said my father taught me, and that vacations were often where he tested whether I'd listened. We came back in the late light. At the dock I misjudged the distance and planted a shoe in a puddle so deep it looked deliberate. I was damp but happy. Nobody's wealth prevented the water from getting into my sock.

------------------------------

ENTRY 510 — 2011-09-12 — Age 25 — The annual conversation

Mother and I discussed her plans for the autumn, the synagogue calendar, and whether she ought to take fewer obligations onto her shoulders. She informed me that I am not her mother. I said no, but I am her daughter and am permitted to care whether she gets exhausted. She conceded that point with visible reluctance. We then spent twenty minutes choosing the proper flowers for a friend who has been ill. She remembered a detail about the friend's garden I had forgotten. The conversation felt like being handed both a warning and a gift: she needs help sometimes, and she still knows far more than I do about a great many things.

------------------------------

ENTRY 511 — 2011-09-22 — Age 25 — Rosh Hashanah

At services I recognized voices before I saw faces. There are people whose singing has been part of the furniture of my life for as long as I can remember. Someone asked after Dad. They knew, of course, that he'd been gone for years; they meant they still remembered him. I thanked them and was glad they said his name. At lunch a young girl wanted to know why people wish for a sweet year when years contain sad things. I told her I thought wishing was not the same as promising. She considered this, accepted a piece of apple, and went away. I wrote the question down. It deserves a better answer than I gave.

ENTRY 512 — 2011-10-01 — Age 25 — A cool temper

A committee member implied that the field team had overstated its expenses. I asked him, calmly, which line he believed was inaccurate and opened the receipts on the table. He could not identify one. I waited. He eventually said he had meant only that some costs 'felt high.' I told him that a feeling is a reason to check a figure, not a reason to accuse a person. There was an uncomfortable silence. I didn't raise my voice. I didn't need to. Later I felt sick about the exchange, though I still believe every word. I dislike that cold part of myself, not because it is always wrong, but because it is so final when it arrives.

------------------------------

ENTRY 513 — 2011-10-09 — Age 25 — Yom Kippur afternoon

I spent the afternoon thinking about apologies I've given too quickly and apologies I've withheld too long. There are people I have forgiven who did not deserve further access to my life, and people I have avoided because I was embarrassed to explain myself. Those are different things. Mother and I sat together quietly after services. She held my hand for a few minutes as though I were still a child. I didn't pull away. When we ate later, everything tasted more vivid than usual. I was tired and not inclined to make a philosophy out of it. I wrote only that I wanted to be more precise with my kindness.

------------------------------

ENTRY 514 — 2011-10-17 — Age 25 — The wrong name

I introduced a volunteer to another volunteer and used the wrong first name. I knew both names and could have written either without hesitation. My mouth simply chose one without consulting me. Everyone was polite. I turned red and tried to explain, which prolonged the misery. The woman whose name I'd misplaced laughed and said her sister does the same thing. Later we worked together placing stakes at a monitoring site, and it became entirely ordinary. I mention it because I am tempted to remember only the embarrassment, when most of the afternoon was useful and pleasant. The stakes are in correctly, and we saw a kingfisher.

------------------------------

ENTRY 515 — 2011-10-25 — Age 25 — Questions at the synagogue

A younger member asked me how the synagogue decides which programs receive funding. I showed her what I could and directed her to the treasurer for the rest. She seemed to think that because I give a substantial amount I could simply tell everyone what to do. I told her I hope that isn't true. Institutions get brittle when one person's checkbook substitutes for everyone's judgment. Afterward I had a very good conversation with her about children's education. She had ideas I hadn't considered. I put her in touch with someone who can actually implement them. I came home glad that she had asked questions rather than just thanking me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 516 — 2011-11-02 — Age 25 — An old friend from school

I met a woman I knew in high school, someone I once envied for having exactly the confidence I lacked. She confessed that she had been terrified of everything then. We compared memories and discovered how badly we'd misunderstood each other. She remembered my BMW and thought I was effortless. I remembered her ease in groups and thought she was fearless. Neither impression held up. We drank coffee until the cups were cold. The conversation did me good. I don't think she wants anything from me except perhaps another coffee next month. That is a surprisingly luxurious feeling.

------------------------------

ENTRY 517 — 2011-11-06 — Age 25 — A notebook in my pocket

The small field notebook that lives in my coat pocket has become almost illegible. There are measurements, grocery reminders, names of birds, and the phone number of a woman who offered to lend us a soil auger. I copied the useful things into their proper places and found a note saying simply 'ask Mother about blue bowl.' I couldn't remember what it meant. When I called, Mother remembered immediately: it had belonged to her aunt in Brooklyn. We spent half an hour talking about that aunt and the kitchen where the bowl stood. I nearly lost a family story because I'd made a poor note. I am adding context to my notes from now on.

------------------------------

ENTRY 518 — 2011-11-11 — Age 25 — Veterans Day

I looked at Dad's Navy photograph again. I've known for years that he volunteered young, and still I have trouble reconciling that boy with the white-haired man who made me practice knots until I could tie them without looking. A veterans' ceremony in town included names and ordinary stories that would never appear in history books. One man spoke about the friend who taught him to repair an engine. I thought of all the skills war left people with, alongside the damage it caused. Dad seldom offered grand speeches about service. He did teach me to prepare properly and look after the people beside me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 519 — 2011-11-20 — Age 25 — A small disappointment

He canceled our plans because of a work obligation and told me late. I was disappointed and said so. He apologized and asked if I wanted to reschedule. I did. I write this because five years ago I might have treated either his mistake or my disappointment as proof of something enormous. It was neither. We had supper two nights later and he seemed as tired as I had been. I do not know whether this relationship will last. I do know that people must be allowed to have inconvenient Wednesdays without being put on trial.

------------------------------

ENTRY 520 — 2011-11-28 — Age 25 — The house at dusk

I went to Mother's house just before sunset. From the drive I could see her moving across a lighted room, carrying a stack of papers. She looked small from outside, which startled me because she never feels small when we're talking. We ate leftovers and listened to a record she had kept since before I was born. She told me a story about a friend who once insisted a singer had ruined a song by improving it. I laughed until I nearly knocked over a glass. Mother put her hand over mine and moved the glass away without interrupting the story. Some forms of love are automatic.

------------------------------

ENTRY 521 — 2011-12-04 — Age 25 — Winter shore

I walked a winter beach with a friend and we found more plastic fragments than shells. We filled a bag with what we could safely remove and left the rest for a properly organized cleanup. I dislike the impulse to do a grand, theatrical thing when the quieter useful thing is within reach. We watched gulls work a stretch of shore, entirely untroubled by my opinions. The air was cold enough to make my nose ache. On the return walk we talked about books, families, and why good boots are worth paying for. Mine have already earned their keep.

------------------------------

ENTRY 522 — 2011-12-11 — Age 25 — A difficult dinner

Someone at dinner made a joke about charitable organizations as though everyone who worked for one were either foolish or dishonest. I asked which organizations he meant. He named none. I felt that cold, careful mood approaching and decided to put it aside because I didn't actually know the man well enough to judge him. We talked about the limits of his own experience instead. He turned out to have had one frustrating encounter and generalized from it. I still disliked the joke. I was also glad I hadn't delivered the lecture I was preparing. There are times when curiosity is a better first instrument than anger.

------------------------------

ENTRY 523 — 2011-12-18 — Age 25 — Gifts and expectations

I bought Mother a book she mentioned in passing and then spent an absurd afternoon worrying she might already own it. She does not, apparently. For two cousins I bought small things connected to conversations we'd had, not elaborate gifts that would force anybody to reciprocate. I know what it is like to have an object arrive carrying someone else's expectations. The post office was crowded and a child behind me narrated every item in my basket. He was quite accurate until he guessed that the book was for my sister. I said no, my mother. He asked how old she was. I said old enough to enjoy a good book.

------------------------------

ENTRY 524 — 2011-12-24 — Age 25 — A tune in the kitchen

I tried playing one of Dad's arrangements again, badly but less badly than last January. I had left the music on the piano, and Mother came into the room while I was working. She didn't interrupt. When I finished, she said he would have enjoyed hearing me wrestle with the middle section. I asked whether that was praise. She said it was a report. We made tea and talked about the musicians he admired, and she corrected my memory of which recording he had played most often. I love that she still has stories I haven't heard. I hope she never feels required to tell them all at once.

------------------------------

ENTRY 525 — 2011-12-31 — Age 25 — A life kept in small records

There are many pages in this year's notebook. Most of them concern small things: bird counts, arguments over figures, Mother's stairs, a dog that chose my shade, misplaced names, wet socks, and dinners that might become memories or might remain dinners. I have also let someone know me a little better. I haven't decided what we are building, and perhaps I don't need to decide tonight. I keep thinking of the graduation speech I gave years ago, about not missing your life while working for a goal. I still believe it. If anything, the years have made the instruction harder and more necessary. I shall spend tonight with a book and tomorrow outdoors if it isn't pouring. Happy New Year, Gwen.
