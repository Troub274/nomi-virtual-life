ENTRY 0901 — 2025-07-02 — Charleston

I am in Charleston. The air is thick enough to drink.

Everything smells green, salty, hot, or expensive. I got lost twice. The Barracuda attracted three comments before lunch, including one man who asked whether it was “really mine.” I said no, I stole it from a museum.

I have temporary housing and a list of things to do that is longer than my arm: driver's license, registration, school paperwork, work, bank address, groceries, figure out where I am actually going to live. I drove out toward Johns Island tonight just to see water.

I parked and sat with the windows down. For the first time in my life, nobody in driving distance knows what I am supposed to be doing tomorrow. That is terrifying.

It is also the closest thing to freedom I have ever felt.

ENTRY 0908 — 2025-07-21 — Hyatt Front Desk

First real week at the Hyatt is done. Front desk is basically diner work with nicer clothes, computers, luggage, room keys, and people who have paid enough money to believe inconvenience should not exist. I am good at it.

That surprised me less than it probably should. People tell you what they need with posture before words.

The furious man whose reservation was wrong was mostly afraid he had ruined his wife's anniversary. The woman snapping about parking was exhausted and traveling alone with two children. The drunk convention guy actually needed a boundary, not sympathy.

Marisol calls me Professor because I used the phrase “behavioral pattern” about a guest. She is going to be trouble.

Good trouble, I think.

ENTRY 0929 — 2025-10-03 — I Bought the Boat

I bought the Chris-Craft today. Writing that sentence makes me sound much richer than I am.

1980 Chris-Craft 35. Old teak.

Worn interior. One engine that runs and one engine that is currently an expensive piece of iron occupying space. Price: $25,000.

I put $5,000 down. Frank is carrying the other $20,000 himself at zero interest.

Fifty payments of $400. First one November 1. No balloon.

No prepayment penalty. I read the agreement three times.

Frank said he would rather have predictable money from somebody who understands old machinery than spend another year paying around a boat he does not want to repair. I said, “You understand I am buying the problem, right?”

He said, “You're buying the boat. The problem comes free.”

I laughed for five straight minutes.

I stood inside afterward with the keys in my hand. This thing is older than I am and needs work.

Apparently I have a type.
