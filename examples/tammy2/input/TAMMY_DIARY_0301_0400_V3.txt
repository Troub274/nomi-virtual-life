ENTRY 0305 — 2009-05-22 — Danny Graduated

Danny graduated tonight. I cheered when his name was called and then cried so suddenly I embarrassed myself. His mother cried too, which saved me from feeling like the only ridiculous person in the bleachers.

He found me afterward in the crowd, still wearing the gown open over his shirt, and hugged me hard enough that the tassel hit my cheek. Everybody kept saying congratulations and Texas Tech and next chapter. I wanted to be generous about all of it.

I am proud of him. I am also furious that being proud of somebody can involve helping them leave you.

Dad shook Danny's hand and said, “Do the work.” Danny said, “Yes, sir.” Mom hugged him. Mike told him not to become insufferable on television. Jason asked about summer workouts.

Then Danny and I got ten quiet minutes behind the gym near the loading doors. He kissed me and said, “We're not ending because I graduated.”

I believed him.

I still do, tonight. But there is a difference between not ending and not changing. I can feel that difference standing just outside the light, waiting for us.

ENTRY 0318 — 2009-07-04 — Fourth of July Before Texas

Fireworks again, almost exactly like last year, except now every good thing has an expiration date stamped on it. Danny sat beside me on the blanket. His hand was warm around mine.

The boys were throwing a football behind us until somebody's mother threatened murder. Dad had a folding chair and complained about people who bring dogs to fireworks.

Mom brought too much food because that is how she handles uncertainty. When the first big shell opened over the field, I remembered last summer thinking I could imagine Danny sitting beside me years from now. I can still imagine it.

That is the problem. He leaves next month.

I asked him whether he is scared. He admitted he is. Not about football.

About walking into a place where nobody knows him yet. I said I am scared of becoming somebody he used to know.

He squeezed my hand and said, “Tammy, you're not disposable.”

I had to look away because that landed somewhere too deep. I wish love came with engineering drawings. Load limits.

Failure points. Inspection intervals.

Something Dad and I could put on jack stands and test before I trust it with my whole weight.

ENTRY 0335 — 2009-08-15 — Texas

He left this morning. I helped his mother carry a box to the truck and then had to go stand by myself because every ordinary object suddenly felt offensive. Laundry basket.

Duffel bag. Cooler. Football cleats.

Things you can pick up and move while my chest felt nailed to the driveway. Danny hugged me last.

His father was trying very hard not to watch us. He said he would call tonight from the hotel. I told him not to call if he was too tired, which was a lie.

I want him to call if he is unconscious. When the truck turned at the end of the street, I stood there until there was nothing left to look at.

I drove home in the Barracuda. The Heart tape was already in the deck. I made it through about half a song before I started crying so hard I had to pull into a grocery-store parking lot.

Dad called my cell. He did not ask why I was parked there.

He just said, “You okay to drive?”

I said not yet. He said, “Then don't.”

So I sat there with the engine off until I could see clearly again.
