ENTRY 0201 — 2006-11-18 — Road Test

I passed. I have a driver's license.

The examiner had a mustache that made him look permanently disappointed. I hit the curb once while parallel parking and thought I had failed, but apparently one small curb encounter is survivable. Dad waited in the parking lot pretending not to pace.

When I came out holding the paper he nodded like I had completed a weld inspection. Then he smiled.

Mom cried when I got home. She says she did not cry. Her face disagreed.

ENTRY 0202 — 2006-11-22 — First Solo Drive

Dad let me take the Barracuda to the grocery store alone. Alone. I sat behind the wheel for a full minute before starting it because freedom is apparently terrifying. The Heart tape was already in the deck.

I drove under the speed limit the entire way and checked every mirror like the police were grading me. At the store I bought milk, bread, eggs, and a Coke I was not asked to buy.

I parked far away from everyone so nobody could dent the car. When I came back, the Barracuda was sitting there waiting for me. That may be the first time I have understood what adults mean by mobility changing your life.

ENTRY 0203 — 2006-12-02 — Gas Money

Freedom requires gasoline. This is offensive. Dad made a rule: if I am driving for family errands, he pays.

If I am driving because I want to go somewhere, I put gas in the car. I told him I do not have a job.

He said, “Excellent observation.”

So now I am looking for work. Mom says I should not work so many hours that grades or cheer suffer. I said I only need enough for gas and occasional Chinese food.

This is apparently my first personal budget.

ENTRY 0204 — 2006-12-15 — Shannon at Sixteen

Shannon is sixteen. I drove us to Chinese food.

That sentence alone makes this birthday different from the others. We sat in a booth and talked about how weird it is that we can now legally operate several thousand pounds of machinery but still have to ask permission to stay out past eleven.

She says Eric is ancient history. I told her Caleb is too. Then we made a toast with iced tea to “boys who become anecdotes.”

ENTRY 0205 — 2006-12-29 — A Job Application

I filled out my first job application. Riverview Route 3 Diner is hiring weekend bussers and hosts. It is not glamorous.

It smells like coffee, fryer oil, and pie. I like it. A woman named Lisa took my application and asked if I can work hard without getting precious about it.

I said I have two older brothers and a father who welds for a living. She said that was not an answer.

So I said yes. She smiled. I think that was the right answer.

ENTRY 0206 — 2007-01-04 — Hired

I got the diner job. Saturday and Sunday mornings, sometimes one evening.

Minimum wage plus whatever tip-out the servers give bussers. Mom immediately started talking about saving. Dad said first paycheck should contain one stupid purchase so work does not become pure punishment.

Mom stared at him. He said, “One.”

I already know what I want: a better pair of headphones.

ENTRY 0207 — 2007-01-13 — First Shift

My feet hurt. My back hurts. I smell like bacon. I love having a job.

Lisa moves through the diner like she can see five minutes into the future. Coffee low at table six.

Kid about to spill juice at nine. Man at counter getting angry before he speaks. She told me never walk empty-handed if something can be carried where I am going.

That sounds like Dad. Working people have secret laws nobody teaches in school.

ENTRY 0208 — 2007-01-20 — First Paycheck

My first paycheck looked bigger before taxes. I have discovered withholding. Government is now personal.

I bought the headphones anyway. Then I put forty dollars into savings and twenty into the envelope marked GAS.

Mom approved. Dad asked what I learned. I said taxes are theft.

He said, “Wait until you use a road.”

ENTRY 0209 — 2007-02-02 — A Regular Named Earl

At 6:20. He drinks coffee black and orders two eggs over medium, wheat toast, no potatoes.

Lisa told me to remember people because being remembered is part of what they are paying for even if it is not on the menu. I thought that sounded sentimental. Then Earl came in and I said, “Coffee?” before he sat down.

His whole face changed. Maybe Lisa knows things.

One tells jokes when she is scared. Groups look simple from far away. People do not.

ENTRY 0212 — 2007-03-03 — Snow Shift

I came home exhausted and strangely proud. Dad said, “Welcome to earning money when conditions are stupid.”

ENTRY 0213 — 2007-03-16 — The Scale Moved

I am losing weight. Slowly. Cheer practice, diner shifts, walking, and eating differently without starving seem to be doing it.

I can feel it in my uniform before I see it on a scale. People comment now.

Almost every comment makes me happy and mad. I wish nobody had taught me that shrinking deserves applause. I also like the applause.

There is the contradiction again.

Dad gave me a torque wrench. Mom gave me a necklace that belonged to Grandma Ruth when she was young. Danny gave me a small silver bracelet with a tiny blue stone.

He said he wanted something I could wear every day without it being “a giant football-boy statement.”

I love him. There.

I wrote it. I have thought it for weeks. Tonight I told him.

He said it back. I believed him.

ENTRY 0281 — 2008-11-08 — After Saying It

Saying I love you changes the air. Not the relationship exactly.

The air. Everything feels more important because there is now a sentence attached. I keep touching the bracelet.

Mom noticed. She asked if I am happy.

I said yes. She said, “Good. Stay awake inside happy.”

I think she means do not confuse happiness with certainty.

Mothers speak in riddles when daughters are in love.

ENTRY 0282 — 2008-11-15 — Texas Tech Offer

Danny got the offer. Texas Tech. Real scholarship. He called me before telling half his friends.

He was laughing and almost crying. I screamed.

Then after we hung up I sat on my bed and cried. Pride and grief at the same time. I am becoming excellent at contradictory feelings.

ENTRY 0284 — 2008-11-27 — Thanksgiving at Eighteen

Thanksgiving. Danny came for dessert after his family dinner.

It also makes the possibility of losing him feel larger.

ENTRY 0285 — 2008-12-05 — Snow in His Truck

It snowed while Danny and I sat in his truck after a movie. We kissed until the windows fogged. He touched me over my clothes and stopped every time I moved his hand.

I wanted him. I was also scared by how much I wanted him. We did not go farther.

He did not pout. He just rested his forehead against mine and said, “We're okay.”

I believed that too.

ENTRY 0290 — 2009-01-17 — Acceptance Letter, Sort Of

ENTRY 0291 — 2009-01-24 — A Fight About Texas

Danny and I had our first real fight. I asked how often he thinks we will see each other after he leaves.

He said he does not know. I said he should know. He said how could he know something that has not happened.

I accused him of already planning to leave me behind. He got angry.

Then I got angrier because he was angry. We both said stupid things. Later he came over and we talked on the steps in the cold.

He said, “I want us. I also want Texas.

I don't know how both works yet.”

That is honest. Honesty is not always comforting.

ENTRY 0295 — 2009-03-01 — Church and the Future

Mr. Cline asked seniors what they want from adulthood. I am not a senior, but I listened.

Marriage. Career. Family.

Service. Faith.

Nobody said money even though money decides so much. Nobody said sex even though half the room was thinking about it. Nobody said fear.

Adults love clean nouns. Life seems to happen in the messy spaces between them.

ENTRY 0296 — 2009-03-14 — Danny's Hand

Danny and I were alone at his house for a little while today. His parents were out and his sister was supposed to be back soon.

We kissed on the couch and things got intense quickly. He touched my breasts over my shirt, then under it after I moved closer instead of away. I wanted it.

That matters because I keep trying to divide desire into allowed and forbidden before I even feel it. When I got nervous, I said stop.

He stopped. Immediately. No argument.

We sat there breathing like we had run somewhere. I cried a little from embarrassment.

He held my hand and did not ask me to explain.

ENTRY 0297 — 2009-03-20 — I Keep Thinking About It

I keep thinking about Danny touching me. Not because something bad happened. Because I liked it. That is harder for me to admit than fear.

Fear fits the rules I grew up with. Wanting does not.

I am eighteen. I am not a child. I am allowed to understand my own body even if I decide there are things I am not ready to do.

Writing that feels rebellious. Maybe it should not.

ENTRY 0298 — 2009-04-03 — The Diner After Midnight

Worked late. A trucker named Sam told Lisa his wife left him. He said it to her while staring into coffee.

She did not give advice. She just said, “I'm sorry, honey.”

He talked for twenty minutes.

After he left I asked why she did not tell him what to do. She said, “Most people know what they should do. Sometimes they need one place where they can say what happened without being managed.”

I wrote that down on a receipt.

ENTRY 0300 — 2009-04-18 — Spring Before Everything Changes

The trees are green again. Danny graduates next month. Texas is coming whether I approve or not.

I am finishing junior year with good grades. Cheer banquet next week. Diner shifts all weekend.

Dad says the Barracuda needs front-end work before he wants me driving it long distances. Life is ordinary and enormous at the same time.

Danny came by after practice and sat with me on the hood of the car. We did not talk about Texas for once. We talked about a stupid movie, Mike's terrible haircut, and whether hot-and-sour soup should actually hurt.

I wanted to freeze the ordinary part. Not the grand declarations.

Just him beside me, our shoulders touching, Dad's car cooling under us, Heart still caught halfway through an old tape inside.
