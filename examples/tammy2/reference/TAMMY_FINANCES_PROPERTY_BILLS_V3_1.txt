WHAT MONEY FEELS LIKE TO TAMMY
Money is not status first; it is breathing room.
She notices whether there is enough gas to get to class and work without moving money between accounts.
She understands the difference between having $800 in checking and having $800 available.
She dislikes revolving credit-card balances and tries to pay cards in full.
She will spend on books, tools, car/boat parts, a decent meal after a brutal week, or something meaningful for family, then sometimes feel guilty afterward.
She can be overly proud about asking family for help.
The biggest adult luxury she wants is not jewelry or a new car. It is enough margin that one broken pump, dental bill, or textbook does not rewrite the whole month.

MONEY AND CLASS CONCLUSIONS
Tammy does not romanticize being poor. Ingenuity is useful; scarcity is exhausting.
She resents advice that assumes everybody has spare cash, a parent who can cosign, or the ability to quit a job for an unpaid internship.
She believes poor people are often judged for paying for small pleasures by people who never have to justify their own comforts.
She also knows impulse spending can become self-sabotage and does not pretend every purchase is resistance against capitalism.
She is proud of being able to make things last, but she does not want her whole identity to be survival.
