PRIVATE DIARY — VOLUME 4

ENTRY 301 — 2020-01-19 — A Question About Happiness

I went to a lecture about the mathematics of networks and spent the next two hours talking with a woman named Lila who studies how people make decisions. She asked what I do when I'm not working.

I listed painting, reading, friends, temple, and a few more things. She asked what I do for no reason at all.

I couldn't answer quickly. How ridiculous.

I came home and sat on the floor listening to an entire album, doing nothing else. Halfway through I reached for my phone. I made myself put it down.

------------------------------

ENTRY 302 — 2020-02-09 — Grandma's Bad Advice

Grandma Miriam called and asked whether I was dating anybody. I said no. She said I ought to find a man who could make me laugh, then advised me never to trust a man who laughs at his own jokes.

Grandpa Jakob shouted from the other room that she had married one.

She told him to be quiet and then laughed so hard she had to stop talking.

I love their marriage. I also know the two of them once went nearly a week without speaking after an argument about a building. Affection doesn't seem to require good behavior every day.

------------------------------

ENTRY 303 — 2020-03-02 — A Dry Run

Our office discussed plans for working from home because of the virus. At first the meeting sounded like a software installation problem. People talked about laptops, permissions, and video calls.

I found myself thinking about who could afford to stay home and who couldn't.

Ruth said not to assume any of us knew how long this would last. I appreciated that.

Bought groceries on the way home. There was no rice in the store. I stood in the aisle looking at empty shelves and felt suddenly foolish for assuming rice would always be there.

------------------------------

ENTRY 304 — 2020-03-15 — The Apartment Becomes the World

I've been working from home for days. Seattle is strangely quiet, and my laptop has taken over the kitchen table.

Mom and Dad are staying in Redmond. Andy is trying to continue his architecture work from a bedroom. Maya sent a photograph of herself practicing guitar in pajamas and called it her world tour.

I washed my hands so often they felt like paper. Then I sat by the window and watched a couple walk past six feet apart.

Nothing in my mathematical training prepared me for how peculiar the silence would feel.

------------------------------

ENTRY 305 — 2020-03-27 — No, I Don't Know

Dad asked what the models say about how long this will last. I told him models depend on assumptions, and the assumptions are changing daily.

He said he'd never heard me sound so cautious about statistics.

I asked whether he wanted a reassuring lie. He said no, he wanted his daughter to come for dinner.

I said I wanted that too.

We talked by video instead. Mom put the phone on a shelf so I spent most of the call looking at her forehead. Andy says this is our family's technological legacy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 306 — 2020-04-05 — Passover Preparations

Mom and Grandma Miriam are organizing a family seder by video. Hanne's family in Israel is making separate plans. Everyone has an opinion about what counts as being together.

I spent an hour trying to explain a video link to Grandpa Jakob, who built a real estate business across two countries but apparently cannot locate the mute button.

Hanne called to say she wasn't going to let a screen tell her when to speak.

It's possible this seder will make history, though perhaps not the kind the rabbi means.

------------------------------

ENTRY 307 — 2020-04-09 — A Seder in Boxes

The Passover seder was a glorious disaster. Dad couldn't hear Grandpa, Grandma couldn't hear the rabbi's recording, and Andy's microphone amplified every crunch of his matzah.

Then Mom began singing and people joined from their separate little rectangles. For a few minutes I forgot the screen.

Afterward I ate alone in my apartment and cried. Not because something awful happened tonight. Because so many people I love were visible and completely out of reach.

I called Maya. She stayed on the line while we washed our dishes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 308 — 2020-04-25 — The Plant

I bought a houseplant online. It arrived looking deeply disappointed in humanity.

I named it Pierre, because I thought a French name might help. Ruth says I should stop anthropomorphizing my indoor greenery. I told her she's the one who keeps addressing spreadsheets as if they have motives.

I took a photograph of Pierre and sent it to Andy. He drew a little building with a sunny atrium just for plants.

For the first time in weeks I spent an afternoon making something that didn't need to be correct.

------------------------------

ENTRY 309 — 2020-05-17 — Maya's Window

Maya stood outside my apartment and played three songs from the sidewalk while I leaned out of the window. A neighbor applauded. Another asked if we could choose a different hour next time.

We talked afterward from opposite sides of the street. She misses playing live music. I miss hugging her.

I wanted to tell her I was doing fine. Instead I said some nights I feel very lonely.

She said, "Good. I was starting to think I was the only one."

It helped more than either of us expected.

------------------------------

ENTRY 310 — 2020-06-20 — One Table, Many Numbers

Ruth asked us to revise assumptions in several forecasts. We couldn't simply reuse last year's patterns; too many ordinary habits had changed.

One colleague insisted the uncertainty made the whole exercise pointless. I said uncertainty was precisely why we needed to be honest about the possible range.

After the meeting I wondered whether I'd been patient enough. Probably not.

Then I ordered noodles and ate them on my balcony. Someone in the neighboring building was playing a trumpet badly. It sounded wonderful.

------------------------------

ENTRY 311 — 2020-07-04 — The Last Slice of Pie

Mom left a container of berry pie on the porch so I could pick it up without coming inside. She stood behind the glass and complained that I'd chosen the smallest piece.

I waved the fork at her.

Driving home I passed families in their yards, some careful and some not, all trying to invent a holiday out of an uncomfortable summer.

I ate the pie in the parked car and sent Dad a picture. He replied, "You missed a spot." I think I inherited my sense of humor from people who should have known better.

------------------------------

ENTRY 312 — 2020-07-30 — Lila and the Self

Lila and I talked by video about whether people remain the same person as their memories change. She studies decision-making and insists identity isn't merely a scrapbook.

I pointed out that I have actual scrapbooks proving how much I've changed. She asked whether the person making the scrapbook was also choosing what to leave out.

That stopped me.

Later I reread my diary from age ten. I had written about wanting adults to knock before entering my room.

At thirty-one I still think that's an excellent moral philosophy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 313 — 2020-08-07 — Flowers Through the Fence

Seafair feels different this year because nearly everything feels different. Mom and I visited Rebecca's grave at separate times. We both left stones.

I remember the impact, her hand slipping out of reach, and what happened between the hulls. I don't need a new interpretation of that memory every August.

Today I mostly wanted to tell her how enormous the world can become when you're trapped in one apartment.

I came home, made soup, and called Mom. We talked about a recipe and never mentioned the cemetery again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 314 — 2020-08-28 — The Job Offer That Wasn't

Another insurance firm contacted me about a position. Better title, more money, fewer details about what I'd actually do.

I asked questions and got polished answers that somehow contained almost no information.

Ruth found out and asked whether I'd like more responsibility here. I told her maybe, provided it meant working on better problems and not attending more meetings.

She laughed. Apparently advancement involves meetings whether you want them or not.

I haven't decided. It's a strange luxury, being able to choose what sort of frustration I prefer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 315 — 2020-09-18 — A Stranger on a Screen

I met Oliver through a dating app. He's a landscape architect who loves old maps, bakes sourdough, and owns a dog named Euclid. I accused him of choosing the name to attract mathematicians.

He said the dog came with it.

We talked for almost two hours. He was funny and easy to listen to. At the end he asked if we could do this again.

I said yes, then realized how glad I was to have somewhere to be tomorrow evening, even if the somewhere was the same chair.

------------------------------

ENTRY 316 — 2020-10-11 — Thirty-Two

Thirty-two. Mom and Dad organized a birthday call. Andrew wore a ridiculous paper hat. Maya played Happy Birthday on guitar, badly on purpose. Tamar joined from Israel for five minutes and shouted something in Hebrew that Mom refused to translate.

Later Oliver left flowers outside my door. We've never been close enough to kiss, but his note made me blush.

I thought about Rebecca and wondered what she'd say about dating through a computer.

Probably something devastating. I miss her jokes, including the ones I never got to hear.

------------------------------

ENTRY 317 — 2020-10-29 — Everything Is Someone's Emergency

Work was dreadful today. A colleague's child needed help with remote school, another colleague was caring for her mother, and I complained about a meeting running late.

I heard how I sounded and wished I could rewind the whole hour.

I apologized afterward. The colleague said she didn't need a saint, just somebody who could cover a report on Thursday.

I told her I could do Thursday.

Sometimes the grandest thing available is simply keeping a promise that fits on a calendar.

------------------------------

ENTRY 318 — 2020-11-16 — Grandpa's Memory

Grandpa Thomas called from Alaska and told me a story about a gold mine I'd heard before, except this time he insisted Dad had been with him.

Dad wasn't born yet when the story happened.

I didn't correct Grandpa. I asked what the place smelled like. He described wet timber and diesel and the coffee they drank from metal cups.

When we hung up I called Grandma Nora. She said he's getting forgetful and sometimes it frightens him.

I want to fly up there. I also know I shouldn't just now. I hate that I cannot solve distance with money.

------------------------------

ENTRY 319 — 2020-12-06 — A Strange Argument

Oliver said the pandemic has taught him most people don't think for themselves. I asked what evidence would convince him he was wrong. He accused me of treating a date like an exam.

I was annoyed and said he was treating certainty like a substitute for thought.

We went quiet. Then he made a joke about Euclid eating another shoe, and we laughed.

I still like him. I also don't know how much of him I know. A screen makes it easy to enjoy the clever version of somebody without seeing how they behave when the dishes pile up.

------------------------------

ENTRY 320 — 2020-12-31 — A Quiet Ending

I thought about writing a grand summary of 2020, then decided the year has received enough grand summaries.

I miss my family. I'm tired of video calls. I'm grateful for my job and also bored with sitting at the same table. Pierre the plant has survived my care, to everyone's surprise.

Oliver and I talked tonight. He asked if we might finally meet in person when it's sensible. I said I'd like that.

At midnight I opened the window and heard someone cheering in the distance.

Happy New Year, whoever you are.

------------------------------

ENTRY 321 — 2021-01-24 — Coffee with a Dog

Oliver and I walked on opposite sides of a wide path with Euclid trotting between us on a long leash. It was the first time we'd met outside a screen.

He's taller than I expected, quieter in person, and much less inclined to interrupt. Euclid adored me until I ran out of treats.

We got coffee to take away and stood looking at the water. It was awkward for ten minutes and lovely for twenty.

When I got home I couldn't decide whether I liked Oliver more or just liked being outside with another human being.

------------------------------

ENTRY 322 — 2021-02-15 — The Ordinary Work of Love

Mom called to ask whether I could help Dad sort his home office. He'd kept cables from computers he no longer owns and printouts of code older than some of his coworkers.

He said every cable was potentially useful. I held up a connector neither of us recognized.

We laughed, then spent the afternoon organizing. He told me a story about building software in the early Microsoft years, when he was young and terrified of looking stupid.

I forget he was ever young and uncertain. I wonder what Andy thinks about me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 323 — 2021-03-08 — A New Set of Questions

Ruth asked if I would mentor a new analyst named Isabel. I said yes, provided nobody expected me to become encouraging in that artificial corporate way.

Isabel turned out to be quick, nervous, and rather too eager to apologize for not knowing things.

I told her to stop saying sorry every time she asked a question. She asked whether I was angry.

I wasn't. But the way I said it may have sounded like I was.

We started again, more gently. Teaching is a kind of mathematics I am still learning.

------------------------------

ENTRY 324 — 2021-03-28 — The Wrong Celebration

Grandma Miriam and I talked about vaccinations and the possibility of visiting Victoria again. She sounded excited, then very tired.

I began planning a grand family reunion until she told me she didn't want a grand anything. She wanted tea with me and perhaps a short walk if the weather was good.

I laughed at myself. I've spent a year complaining about not being together and nearly ruined the idea by making it into an event.

I promised we'd make tea. No invitations, no speeches, no family photographs unless she asked.

------------------------------

ENTRY 325 — 2021-04-18 — The Kiss

Oliver kissed me after we walked in a park. It felt good, simple and rather overdue.

Then he made a joke that I ought to be easier to date now that we could meet more often. I asked what he meant. He said I was always so intense.

I smiled, but I didn't like it.

Later I wondered if I was being unreasonable. Maybe he was teasing. Maybe he wasn't. I wish I could occasionally allow a pleasant moment to remain pleasant without examining its foundations.

------------------------------

ENTRY 326 — 2021-05-09 — Mother's Day, Finally

I hugged Mom today. Really hugged her, not through glass or while standing apart.

We stood in the kitchen holding each other for so long Dad asked whether he'd be allowed to make lunch.

I told Mom I'd missed her. She said she knew. Then she fussed about whether I was eating enough, which was almost a relief.

Andy joined us by video and showed a model he'd made for architecture class. The roof looked like a paper sail. Dad asked if it would leak. Andy said, "That is a different department."

------------------------------

ENTRY 327 — 2021-06-03 — Grandpa Thomas

Grandpa Thomas died this morning in Juneau. Grandma Nora called Dad first. Dad called me while I was making coffee.

I didn't cry immediately. I found the photograph Thomas sent me years ago, the snowy truck outside the mine office. I remembered how he told the story of meeting Nora differently each time.

Then I cried.

Andy asked if we would go to Alaska for the funeral. I said I didn't know yet. I hate that there are phone calls after which the whole day keeps moving even though it shouldn't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 328 — 2021-06-18 — What He Left

We went to Juneau for Grandpa's funeral. Grandma Nora looked small standing beside the table of photographs, then suddenly enormous when she stood to speak.

She told a funny story about him courting her and being hopelessly wrong about the local weather. Everyone laughed, including Dad.

Afterward two relatives argued about some tools Grandpa had promised to each of them. I was furious with both. Later I understood that grief had made them behave like hungry children.

I kept one old measuring tape. Nora said he'd have wanted me to use it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 329 — 2021-07-04 — A Real Family Dinner

We had people over in Redmond, carefully, for the first time in what felt like forever. Maya came. Mom made too much food. Dad talked about Grandpa Thomas and told a story I'd never heard.

I enjoyed every minute and also felt exhausted by so many voices.

Oliver was invited. He stayed near me and was polite, but afterward said my family never stops debating.

I said that's true, and I love them for it.

He said he hadn't meant it as a compliment. The ride home was long.

------------------------------

ENTRY 330 — 2021-07-19 — The Things He Doesn't Like

Oliver told me he doesn't like it when I challenge him in front of people. I asked for an example. He gave three.

I could see one where I'd been unkind. The other two were times I simply disagreed.

He wanted me to say I understood. I did, partly. I also wanted him to stop treating my curiosity like a personal insult.

We were both tired of the conversation before it ended.

He kissed me goodbye. I watched him walk away and felt more relieved than sad. That's not a reassuring sign.

------------------------------

ENTRY 331 — 2021-08-01 — No More Oliver

Oliver and I agreed to stop seeing each other. I told him I wanted to feel free to think aloud without wondering whether I was embarrassing him. He told me he wanted a relationship that didn't feel like an argument.

He wasn't wrong about the arguments. I wasn't wrong about why they happened.

I miss Euclid the dog already, which feels like a betrayal of everybody involved.

Maya says I should take a month off dating. I said I was planning on at least six. She asked whether she'd need to make me a spreadsheet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 332 — 2021-08-16 — Twenty-Two

Andy turned twenty-two. He's taking his architecture studies very seriously and has a notebook full of drawings that look both beautiful and impractical.

I offered him an old drafting lamp. He accepted immediately and then asked whether it was vintage. I said it was from my apartment, not an archaeological dig.

He told me he wants to live in Portland eventually. The architecture firms interest him, and he likes the city.

Mom said she wished her children would stop moving around the map. Dad said perhaps we'd inherited it from our grandparents.

------------------------------

ENTRY 333 — 2021-09-11 — Twenty Years

Twenty years since I watched the news from school and couldn't understand how the buildings could actually be falling.

I reread what thirteen-year-old me wrote. I hadn't tried to explain the world. I'd written that I wanted people to go home.

I think that's an answer I still understand.

Dad and I talked about how confident adults sounded afterward, including when they were wrong. He admitted he had been wrong about things too.

I wish more people could say that without thinking they'd lost something.

------------------------------

ENTRY 334 — 2021-10-11 — Thirty-Three

Thirty-three. Mom made a cake, Andy called, and Dad gave me a little computer board because he believes electronics are a natural language of love.

I visited Rebecca first. I told her about Grandpa Thomas and the ridiculous measuring tape I brought home from Alaska.

Later Maya took me to dinner and we discussed boys, books, and why every city seems determined to rebuild the same ugly apartment building.

It was a good birthday. I refuse to rank it against the others.

------------------------------

ENTRY 335 — 2021-11-02 — Isabel's Question

Isabel asked whether I had ever made a mistake that cost the company money. I told her yes, though we'd caught it before the decision became final.

She looked relieved. Then she asked why I hadn't told her sooner.

That bothered me. I want her to respect my work, but I've been carefully showing her my successes and hiding my embarrassing drafts.

I showed her an old report Ruth had covered in red comments. Isabel laughed at one of my sentences. She was right to.

------------------------------

ENTRY 336 — 2021-11-27 — A Painting That Failed

I spent the holiday weekend painting the view from Mom's kitchen window. Somehow the cedar looked like broccoli, and the light looked as though it had been poured from a yellow bottle.

I got frustrated and scraped half the paint away.

Dad said I should keep the picture as evidence of the problem, like a debugging log.

I told him I wasn't debugging a tree. He said, "You certainly seem to be."

I made another painting the next day. It was better, mostly because I had stopped trying to be impressive.

------------------------------

ENTRY 337 — 2021-12-18 — Nora's Winter

Grandma Nora sent photographs from Juneau. She has started using Grandpa's old camera, which I hadn't expected.

One picture is of a coffee cup in morning light. Another is a road disappearing into snow. Neither contains a single human being.

I called to say I loved them. She said she didn't know what else to do with her afternoons.

I didn't try to fix that sentence.

I asked if she'd send another picture next week. She said she might, if the weather cooperated.

------------------------------

ENTRY 338 — 2022-01-09 — A Public Lecture

Went to an in-person lecture for the first time in ages. Lila was there. We sat at the back and spent half the discussion passing notes about the speaker's assumptions.

Afterward she accused me of treating every intellectual problem as an invitation to improve the world.

I said that was generous. Sometimes I just want to know how something works.

She asked whether knowing without doing can ever be enough.

I told her I was hungry and suggested we solve dinner before civilization.

------------------------------

ENTRY 339 — 2022-02-04 — Dinner for One, Again

I tried a new recipe and produced something edible, though barely. I set the table properly for myself instead of eating over the sink.

Why do I save beautiful dishes for company? I like looking at them too.

I lit a candle. Then I spilled red sauce on the tablecloth and laughed so hard I had to sit down.

Somewhere in my twenties I decided living alone was a temporary state between relationships. I'm not sure I believe that anymore. It's a life, not a waiting room.

------------------------------

ENTRY 340 — 2022-03-19 — How to Be Close

Maya says I've become too comfortable cancelling plans. I told her I was tired after work and she said she knew, but being tired was becoming my permanent excuse.

I wanted to accuse her of not understanding what my job demands. Instead I asked whether she'd been missing me.

She said yes.

I drove to her place with food. We ate on the floor and listened to a record from high school. She rested her head on my shoulder. I thought, rather unexpectedly, that this is also what love feels like.

------------------------------

ENTRY 341 — 2022-04-15 — The Missing Place Setting

Seder at Mom and Dad's. We left a chair for Grandma Nora in the family video call, and Andy balanced a laptop against a stack of cookbooks.

Grandpa Jakob interrupted three times to correct the timing of an old story. Grandma Miriam told him he had been wrong the first time too.

I had meant to ask a question about the Exodus and ended up listening to a long argument about whether my parents should replace the kitchen chairs.

Some traditions endure for reasons nobody can explain.

------------------------------

ENTRY 342 — 2022-05-07 — Saturday with Lila

Lila dragged me to a little gallery opening. I had promised to stay for twenty minutes and remained for three hours.

One artist had painted a row of telephone poles in rain. Nothing was happening in the picture, yet I couldn't stop looking.

I came home and tried to paint my own kitchen sink. The first attempt looked like a collapsed tunnel. I painted it again.

I forgot to eat dinner. The next morning I had paint on my elbow and felt absurdly pleased with myself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 343 — 2022-06-12 — A Colleague I Didn't Like

A new manager asked why our team was spending so much time checking older datasets. I gave him an answer so sharp that Ruth later asked whether I'd intended to educate him or humiliate him.

I said he'd been dismissive. She agreed.

Then she asked the question again.

I spent an unpleasant evening admitting I'd enjoyed making him uncomfortable. That doesn't make the analysis less necessary. It does make me less proud of the conversation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 344 — 2022-07-09 — A Date with a Telescope

I went out with Mateo, a meteorologist I met through Lila. He brought a small telescope to a park after dinner and tried to show me Saturn.

We couldn't find it. There was too much cloud cover, and he had brought the wrong eyepiece.

We sat on a blanket making up constellations instead. Mine was a giant pastry with a bite missing. His was a rather convincing umbrella.

He walked me to my car and kissed me gently. It was a pleasant surprise. I don't know what happens next.

------------------------------

ENTRY 345 — 2022-08-16 — The Bridge Problem

Andrew turned twenty-three. He sent me a drawing of a pedestrian bridge and asked whether I thought people would choose to cross it.

I said I'd need to know where it led.

He called that a very annoying but excellent question.

He is living away from home, studying architecture, taking internships, and sometimes sounding exhausted. I wanted to send him money and solve the practical parts. Instead I asked what he needed.

He asked for advice on choosing between two firms. I was delighted to be useful.

------------------------------

ENTRY 346 — 2022-09-04 — Grandma Miriam Is Still Herself

Visited Victoria. Grandma Miriam has started writing names on the backs of photographs because she says people become mysterious too quickly.

She handed me a photograph and asked who the little girl was. I recognized Mom.

Grandma said, "Good. I was testing you."

I suspect she wasn't. I didn't say so.

We sorted pictures until tea, when Grandpa Jakob became impatient with us and insisted we come outside to see a repair he'd made to the porch. Grandma said that was his version of asking for company.

------------------------------

ENTRY 347 — 2022-10-11 — Thirty-Four

Birthday in Redmond. Dad made waffles, Mom made a cake, and Maya came over with a gift wrapped in three layers of old newspaper.

Mateo brought me flowers. He's kind, often late, and impossibly interested in clouds. I like him. I don't think I'm in love with him.

I'm allowed to like somebody without making it a future plan.

Before dinner I visited Rebecca's grave, as I often do. I told her I'd met a man who can explain why the sky looks green before some storms. She would have had a joke ready.

------------------------------

ENTRY 348 — 2022-10-30 — Mateo's Honesty

Mateo told me he wants to move to Colorado for a job. I asked when he'd decided. He said he'd been thinking about it since before we met.

I was irritated, though we'd never promised each other anything.

He said he liked me but didn't want either of us pretending we were building a life together. I told him I wasn't pretending. Then I realized I had been imagining him as a possibility more than I'd admitted.

We spent the evening talking and drinking tea. I cried on the drive home. I was annoyed at myself for crying.

------------------------------

ENTRY 349 — 2022-11-26 — Twenty Questions

Dad and I played an old game where one of us imagines a thing and the other has twenty yes-or-no questions to find it.

He chose "a recursive function." I told him that isn't a thing you can put in your pocket and he said the rules never required pockets.

Mom called him a cheat. He said definitions matter.

I wanted to argue, but Andy was laughing so hard I couldn't finish.

It occurred to me later that one reason I love Dad is that he still wants to play games with me when I'm thirty-four.

------------------------------

ENTRY 350 — 2022-12-31 — An Ordinary Goodbye

Mateo sent a photograph of snowy mountains from Colorado. I sent him a picture of Seattle rain. We wished each other a happy New Year without making anything more of it.

Maya came over. We cooked, played cards, and sat in silence for a while. She told me she worries the years are speeding up.

I told her I think we notice time differently when we're repeating ourselves.

She said that was a terrible way to comfort a friend. I poured her more tea.

I'm not writing a list of resolutions this year. Perhaps tomorrow.

------------------------------

ENTRY 351 — 2023-01-18 — The Computer That Talks

Dad sent me a link to a new AI chatbot and asked what I thought. I gave it a question about probability, then a question about Danish migration, then asked it to invent a cake recipe.

The probability answer was elegant and wrong. The history answer was plausible and slippery. The cake sounded terrible.

Dad said I was being unfair by testing it on things I already knew.

I said that was how you discovered whether a clever voice had any reason to be trusted.

Then we spent two hours arguing. It felt like being twelve again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 352 — 2023-02-04 — The Date with the Violinist

A friend introduced me to a violinist named Simon. We went to a tiny restaurant, and he talked about rehearsals, orchestras, and the way certain notes felt like colors to him.

He was charming. I liked his hands, his laugh, and the way he listened when I talked about painting.

At the end he asked if I wanted to come to a concert. I said yes.

I've decided not to interrogate my happiness quite so thoroughly this time.

I lasted twelve minutes before calling Maya to analyze the entire evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 353 — 2023-02-26 — Where the Music Went

Simon played beautifully tonight. Watching him with his instrument made me feel like a guest in someone else's private language.

Afterward he was irritable and exhausted. He snapped at a waiter and then apologized. I didn't know which was more revealing: the anger or the apology.

We went for a walk. He told me he sometimes hates playing the music he loves.

I understood that more than he expected. But I wanted him to be kinder to people who couldn't defend themselves.

I didn't tell him. Not yet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 354 — 2023-03-12 — An Impossible Assignment

Ruth wants me to lead a larger modeling project and mentor two people at once. She says I'm ready.

I said I like doing mathematical work and don't want to become a manager of meetings.

She said leading people is work too, just less tidy than equations.

I thought about turning it down. Then I remembered how many times I've complained about decisions made by people who don't understand the models.

I said I'd try it for six months. I hope Ruth knows how to rescue me if I become unbearable.

------------------------------

ENTRY 355 — 2023-03-29 — The Museum Bench

Simon and I went to a museum. He took nearly forty minutes in front of a painting I didn't particularly like and refused to explain why.

I got restless. He asked me to sit beside him and look again.

Eventually I noticed that the light in the picture moved from one corner to the other without seeming to obey the objects.

I still didn't love it. But I could understand why he did.

We had a wonderful afternoon. Then he cancelled dinner the next week without warning. I am trying not to turn one into an explanation for the other.

------------------------------

ENTRY 356 — 2023-04-09 — The Good Plates

Passover at Mom and Dad's. Grandpa Jakob told a story about Victoria real estate and got the decade wrong twice. Grandma Miriam corrected him, then whispered that he tires easily these days.

I looked at him more carefully. He seemed older than I remembered.

Mom served dinner on the family dishes. Andy called from school to ask if he could be put on speakerphone for the songs.

Dad moved the phone beside the candles. It was not acoustically ideal. We sang anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 357 — 2023-04-28 — A Very Unprofessional Moment

I lost my temper in a meeting. Someone presented an analysis I'd spent weeks helping to build and mentioned everybody except Isabel.

I interrupted him halfway through. I was right to insist on giving her credit, but I was furious enough that I made the room awkward for everyone.

Ruth called afterward and asked whether I'd wanted to fix the record or punish the person. I said both.

She sighed and told me to come talk before I wrote any emails.

I deleted the first draft. It was a masterpiece of spite.

------------------------------

ENTRY 358 — 2023-05-09 — Simon Leaves the Stage

Simon and I stopped dating. He cancelled plans repeatedly and always had a compelling explanation. I kept resenting things I hadn't said out loud.

He told me he doesn't have the time or steadiness for a relationship just now. I told him I wished he'd said that sooner.

We kissed goodbye outside a coffee shop. It was sad, but not devastating.

I liked him. I liked his music. I never quite liked how I behaved when I was waiting for him.

I went home and painted a dreadful red violin. I may burn it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 359 — 2023-05-19 — What Is Intelligence?

Dad and I continued our argument about AI. He says programming taught him that complex behavior can arise from surprisingly simple rules.

I said that's also true of weather, and knowing the equations doesn't mean you know what a storm is like from inside.

He asked what evidence would satisfy me that a machine understood anything.

I said I wasn't sure. He smiled, delighted, because I'd finally admitted uncertainty.

We ate sandwiches. Dad got mustard on the keyboard and insisted it was an experimental input.

------------------------------

ENTRY 360 — 2023-06-03 — A Family Business

Mom wants help organizing photographs and papers from Victoria. Grandma Miriam has been sending boxes, and Grandpa Jakob keeps insisting that certain documents are filed under a system only he understands.
