BETH PETERSON
PRIVATE DIARY — VOLUME 2
AUTHOR AND FIRST-PERSON NARRATOR: BETH PETERSON
THIS DIARY IS FICTIONAL BACKSTORY, NOT THE UPLOADER'S BIOGRAPHY.

ENTRY 101 — 2005-02-20 — Nobody Asked Me

Mom offered to take me shopping after lunch. I said I didn't need anything. She looked surprised, which annoyed me, because apparently the correct daughter in her imagination always wants to shop.

We ended up at a bookstore instead. She bought a novel about Denmark and I bought a book of mathematical puzzles. We read in silence over coffee and hot chocolate.

On the drive home she asked whether I was still upset about Lucas. I said a little. She said that sounded about right.

I like when Mom doesn't try to repair me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 102 — 2005-03-08 — Driving Alone

I drove the Volvo to school today. Alone. Dad stood in the driveway waving like I was setting out to sea.

At the first light I realized nobody else was in the car to tell me where to turn. It felt thrilling for about nine seconds, then terrifying for another nine.

I turned on the radio and sang badly. By the time I got to school I felt like I'd crossed a continent.

Maya said freedom smelled like my mother's upholstery cleaner. I'm not sharing a car with her again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 103 — 2005-03-26 — A Boring Saturday

Spent Saturday washing paintbrushes and reorganizing my books. It was deeply satisfying for reasons I can't explain.

Andy made a fort out of couch cushions and announced that I couldn't enter without a password. I tried "mathematics." Wrong. "Princess"? Wrong. "I love my baby brother"? Wrong.

The password was "pickle."

Sometimes I worry that my family is getting less intelligent instead of more.

------------------------------

ENTRY 104 — 2005-04-14 — An Unfair Thought

I saw Lucas laughing with a girl from his English class. I immediately decided she was stupid, which is awful because I don't even know her.

Later I caught up with her at the library. Her name is Julia. She's very funny and wants to study marine biology.

I came home angry at myself. Jealousy seems to manufacture opinions out of absolutely nothing.

I told Maya. She said congratulations, I'd achieved the difficult feat of making a breakup last three months longer than necessary.

------------------------------

ENTRY 105 — 2005-04-30 — The Contest

Our math team did well at the regional competition. Miles made one spectacular mistake and I made a smaller one that cost us almost as much.

On the bus home we argued about which error was worse. Then he said it was nice competing with someone who didn't give up halfway through a problem.

I told him that was almost a compliment. He said he'd try not to repeat it.

People keep assuming I love winning. I do. But I love understanding why I lost even more, once I've stopped being furious.

------------------------------

ENTRY 106 — 2005-05-18 — Mom's Old Photograph

Mom showed me a picture of herself at about my age in Victoria. She had enormous glasses, a plaid skirt, and a grin that looked exactly like mine when I'm being difficult.

I asked if she was popular. She said sometimes. I asked if she had boyfriends. She said yes, and wouldn't tell me how many. That was rude.

Then she told me she met Dad at a dinner party in Seattle, arguing about whether computers would make people more creative.

Apparently every relationship in this family begins with an argument.

------------------------------

ENTRY 107 — 2005-06-14 — Junior Year, Finished

Exams finished. I expected to feel triumphant, but I mostly feel exhausted. I have one year left at Redmond High, which seems both wonderful and impossible.

Ms. Chen wrote on my last paper that I should consider studying history seriously. Miles said I'm wasting my brain if I don't become a mathematician.

I told him I intend to waste it doing several things simultaneously. That's called efficiency.

Tonight Maya and I are going to listen to bad music and eat something greasy. No intellectual improvement permitted.

------------------------------

ENTRY 108 — 2005-06-27 — Whitman Again

We visited Whitman College properly this time. The campus is beautiful in a way that makes me want to carry a book under my arm and have important thoughts beneath a tree.

I spoke with a history professor and ended up asking him more questions than were probably polite. He seemed delighted.

Dad asked what I thought afterward. I said I could see myself here. Mom smiled and looked a tiny bit sad.

It's strange how choosing where to go next can feel like leaving someone behind before you've even packed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 109 — 2005-07-12 — Toyota

Dad says I can start looking for a used car of my own. I said I'd like a Toyota because they're reliable and I don't care about the badge on the hood.

He raised an eyebrow when I added that I'd prefer leather seats and a good stereo.

Mom laughed so hard she nearly dropped her coffee. "You are absolutely your father's daughter," she said.

I don't know why people think liking nice things and not wanting to show off are contradictions. Sometimes a comfortable seat is just a comfortable seat.

------------------------------

ENTRY 110 — 2005-07-29 — A Question Nobody Likes

At dinner I asked what I should do with the money Grandpa and Dad have set aside for me. Mom said I should learn enough to make responsible decisions. Dad said I should never confuse independence with having a large account balance.

I asked whether having money without earning it makes it less mine. Neither of them liked that.

Eventually Dad said the money gives me choices, not a reason to sit still. I think I agree, but I also think money can make it easy to pretend you've earned every choice yourself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 111 — 2005-08-07 — Remembering the Other Boy

Seafair again. I remembered the little boy who was on the Sea Ray with his parents, Dad's Microsoft friends. I hadn't thought about him for months.

I wonder what he remembers. We were children in the same accident, but we weren't in the same place on the boat. He could have seen a completely different story.

I asked Dad if they still talk. He said occasionally. He looked like he wanted to say more, but didn't.

I don't need to make a mystery out of everything. Sometimes I just wonder.

------------------------------

ENTRY 112 — 2005-08-16 — Andy's Sixth

Andy is SIX and absolutely convinced he's old enough to build a real house if we give him lumber. He spent his birthday stacking wooden blocks into a tower taller than his head.

When the tower fell he burst into tears, then demanded to know whether there was stronger glue.

Dad says engineering is hereditary. Mom says stubbornness is hereditary. I say either way we're in trouble.

I bought Andy a book about buildings. He slept with it under his pillow.

------------------------------

ENTRY 113 — 2005-08-28 — Two Kinds of Grandmother

Grandma Miriam loves a tidy room, nice table linens, and stories with proper beginnings. Grandma Nora will tell you an anecdote halfway through another anecdote, then laugh because she's forgotten why she started.

I spent an afternoon with each of them this summer. Both asked whether I was eating enough, both complained I wasn't visiting often enough, and both gave me advice I hadn't requested.

I love them both. They make it impossible to believe there is one correct way to become an old woman.

------------------------------

ENTRY 114 — 2005-09-07 — Senior Year

First day of senior year. Everybody is performing confidence. They're already talking about applications, parties, last chances, and what they'll miss.

Maya says we'll never be this young again. I said that's mathematically true every single day. She threw a balled-up schedule at my head.

Miles has become president of something academic. I have become president of nothing. I consider that a victory.

I want to enjoy this year instead of turning it into a long goodbye.

------------------------------

ENTRY 115 — 2005-09-26 — At Temple

A woman at temple asked whether I intend to marry Jewish. I said I intend to finish high school first.

She looked offended. I didn't mean to offend her. I just don't see how I can promise something about a person I haven't met.

Mom said afterward that our family takes Jewish identity seriously, but that doesn't mean every personal choice can be settled in advance.

Dad gave me a wink. He converted for Mom, and they both know how complicated the question is. I think that was his way of saying so.

------------------------------

ENTRY 116 — 2005-10-11 — Seventeen

Seventeen. Dad made waffles before school because he says a person shouldn't go to school hungry on her birthday. Andy burned a tiny corner of one and declared it the best waffle.

I visited Rebecca's grave with Mom after classes. Seventeen sounds so grown up when I say it aloud. I wonder if we'd still be mistaken for each other.

Tonight Maya and I are going out with friends. I want to laugh and dance and do something a little stupid. Not too stupid. I have a reputation to maintain.

------------------------------

ENTRY 117 — 2005-10-29 — The Party

Went to a party and kissed a boy named Aaron. Not a relationship. Not a declaration. Just a kiss, because the music was good and he was kind and I felt like it.

He asked if he could call me. I said yes, then wondered whether I actually wanted him to. Maya said I was allowed to change my mind.

It's funny how I can make decisions about complicated schoolwork quickly, but need an entire committee inside my head to decide whether I like someone.

He has lovely hands, though. That part I know.

------------------------------

ENTRY 118 — 2005-11-14 — Three Essays

I've rewritten my college essay three times. First I wrote about Rebecca. It was honest, but I was tired of being the girl with the tragedy.

Then I wrote about mathematics. It sounded like a brochure for a calculator.

Now I'm writing about a chipped teacup in Grandma Miriam's kitchen and how much history an ordinary thing can hold.

Dad says the third essay sounds like me. Mom said the first two sounded like me too. I wish she'd stop making everything so complicated by being right.

------------------------------

ENTRY 119 — 2005-12-05 — The Envelope

I mailed another application today. It's an odd business, putting your life in an envelope and hoping strangers decide it's worth reading.

Maya says I'll get into a good school wherever I apply. I told her that's not guaranteed. She says this is not a courtroom and I don't have to object to every optimistic statement.

I am trying to enjoy December. Andy wants us to make paper snowflakes. I have discovered his scissors have a very strong opinion about symmetry.

------------------------------

ENTRY 120 — 2005-12-31 — A Year of Goodbyes

This year Lucas and I broke up, but I also learned how to be his friend again, at least a little. Maya and I fought, made up, and went to concerts. Andy grew another foot, approximately. Mom stopped worrying quite so visibly every time I drove.

I keep expecting a year to have one meaning. It never does.

Dad asked what I wanted in 2006. I said to leave home and be brave enough not to pretend it doesn't scare me.

He nodded. Then he asked if I could take out the trash before becoming brave.

------------------------------

ENTRY 121 — 2006-01-24 — Acceptance

Whitman accepted me.

I read the letter twice and then sat on the floor smiling like an idiot. Mom hugged me, Dad hugged me, and Andy asked whether I would be allowed to take my computer.

I told Maya first after my family. She screamed so loudly I held the phone away from my ear.

I still have decisions to make, but there's an actual place now, not just a stack of brochures. Walla Walla. History. New people.

I didn't think I could be so excited and sad at once.

------------------------------

ENTRY 122 — 2006-02-10 — An Argument with Miles

Miles thinks I'm insane for choosing a small college where I'll spend so much time studying history. He says math is where the beautiful questions are.

I told him a mathematical proof can show what follows from assumptions, but history makes you question how people came to believe their assumptions in the first place.

He said that sounded rehearsed. It was. I'd practiced it in the shower.

We agreed to remain enemies until graduation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 123 — 2006-03-19 — The Drive Home

Mom and I drove back from Bellevue after a synagogue event. I asked how she and Dad stopped fighting after Rebecca died.

She didn't answer for so long I thought I'd gone too far.

Then she said she had blamed him because she'd needed somewhere to put her anger. She told me the other boat had been responsible, and she had known that even while accusing Dad of not protecting us. She said neither of them became kinder overnight.

I told her I remembered. She said she knew.

We drove the rest of the way listening to the radio.

------------------------------

ENTRY 124 — 2006-04-11 — A Strange Freedom

Dad and I talked about my trust fund again. He said it will give me a financial cushion many students don't have, but he doesn't want me treating everyone else's struggle as an amusing learning experience.

I said I didn't need the lecture. He said he didn't need to give it, but apparently we were both wrong.

Later he helped me troubleshoot a program. We argued about pointers until midnight. I'm going to miss that more than either of us knows how to say.

------------------------------

ENTRY 125 — 2006-05-27 — Prom

I went to prom with a group of friends instead of a date. Maya wore a green dress and declared she was the queen of the universe. I wore dark blue and shoes that had apparently been designed to punish women for wanting to look nice.

I danced with Miles once. He counted the beats out loud because he's incapable of existing normally.

At the end we sat outside laughing and didn't say anything about how close graduation was. That was the best part.

------------------------------

ENTRY 126 — 2006-06-16 — Graduation

I graduated from Redmond High today.

Mom cried, obviously. Dad took about two hundred photographs, approximately half of them of his own thumb. Grandma Miriam told me how proud she was. Grandma Nora said the diploma was wonderful but I should still learn how to change a tire.

Maya hugged me until we both cried. Miles said I still owed him a rematch at chess.

I thought I'd feel finished. Instead I feel like I've opened a book and the first chapter suddenly ended in the middle of a sentence.

------------------------------

ENTRY 127 — 2006-06-24 — The Cemetery Before College

I visited Rebecca's grave alone this morning. I told her I'm going away to school in Walla Walla.

That felt ridiculous because she isn't waiting there for news. But talking out loud helped me hear what I was thinking.

I said I was excited to live somewhere nobody knows me as one of the Peterson twins. Then I felt odd saying it, because I'll always be one of the twins, even if everybody else only sees one of us.

I left a little stone and went home to help Andy make a model bridge.

------------------------------

ENTRY 128 — 2006-07-18 — Juneau

Went to Alaska with Dad and Grandma Nora. She showed me places in Juneau that mattered when she was young, and talked about her mother, her family, and all the people whose stories never made it into books.

Dad kept asking practical questions about roads and old buildings. I kept asking questions about people. Grandma Nora said we were both exhausting.

At dinner she told me not to confuse the part of my ancestry I can see in the mirror with knowing somebody else's whole history.

I wrote that down. I think she was giving me a responsibility, not an answer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 129 — 2006-08-08 — Packing

I'm packing books. Mom says I can't take them all. This is an unreasonable restriction on education.

Maya came over to help and instead read old notes she found in a drawer. We laughed until she discovered one about her seventh-grade haircut, and then I had to run for my life.

Andy asked whether Walla Walla was farther than Victoria. I showed him on a map. He asked if he could visit and help build something in my dorm. I told him yes, provided he doesn't knock down the walls.

He looked disappointed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 130 — 2006-08-25 — Walla Walla Again

Dad drove me to Whitman. Mom came too, of course, and Andy held my hand at every rest stop as though I might disappear.

My dorm room is smaller than I imagined and bigger than it needs to be. My roommate, Claire, is from Oregon and seems kind. She brought an enormous quilt and enough mugs to open a cafe.

When Mom left she kissed me twice. Dad said, "Call if you need anything." I almost said I never need anything.

Instead I said I love you both. I watched the car go, then turned around and walked toward my new room.

------------------------------

ENTRY 131 — 2006-09-06 — College Isn't a Movie

Whitman is beautiful. My laundry has turned pink. I am already behind on reading. My roommate Claire drinks coffee as though it is an emergency medical treatment.

I made a friend in a history seminar named Leah, who has opinions about everything and is clever enough to defend them. We argued about whether historical explanations can ever be completely objective.

Later we went for pizza with half the seminar. Nobody knew me as David Peterson's daughter. Nobody asked about Rebecca. It was lovely.

Then I missed home so badly I called Mom from the hallway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 132 — 2006-09-23 — Evan

Met a boy named Evan Mercer at a student discussion about history and religion. He asked a question that made half the room irritated and the other half lean forward.

Afterward we argued about whether people can be morally responsible for consequences they couldn't predict. He said I talk like I'm cross-examining the universe.

I asked if that was meant as an insult. He said, "Not remotely."

We walked back across campus together. I liked his laugh. I liked that he didn't rush to agree with me. I have no idea if I like him. I'm just writing the facts.

------------------------------

ENTRY 133 — 2006-10-11 — Eighteen

Eighteen today. It's odd having a birthday away from Redmond.

Claire put a paper crown on my head at breakfast. Leah bought me an absurd little history book. Evan found out it was my birthday and gave me a chocolate bar, saying he hoped the eighteenth year was an improvement over the seventeenth. He doesn't know enough about the seventeenth to judge.

Mom called. Dad called. Andy shouted into the phone. I went quiet for a moment thinking about Rebecca.

Tonight we're going out for dinner. I'm going to let myself have a lovely birthday.

------------------------------

ENTRY 134 — 2006-11-08 — Catching Up, Sort Of

I've written less than I promised. College is eating my days.

History papers. Study groups. Coffee with Leah. Long walks with Evan, which are becoming a habit I'm pretending not to notice. I've done some math for fun because I miss the feeling of a problem with a definite answer.

Last night Claire caught me staring at the ceiling and asked what I was thinking. I said the Roman Republic. She said I was a terrible liar.

I wonder when a person becomes a different person. Probably not all at once.

------------------------------

ENTRY 135 — 2006-12-19 — Home for Winter

Back in Redmond for winter break. Andy met me at the door and nearly knocked me over. Dad wants to hear about all my classes. Mom wants to feed me as if nobody in Walla Walla has invented food.

Maya and I went to Redmond Town Center and walked around until our fingers were numb. She has new friends and a new boyfriend. I felt a small stupid sting, as if she'd been supposed to wait in exactly the same place for me.

I told her I was glad she was happy. I meant it. I also missed us.

Both things can be true. I am learning that rather slowly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 136 — 2007-01-14 — The Question in the Snow

Evan kissed me tonight outside the library. We'd been arguing about whether people ever really change, and it was cold enough that I couldn't feel my fingers. He said, "Are you always going to make me work this hard?" I asked what he meant, which was probably proof of his point.

Then he kissed me.

It wasn't cinematic. My nose was freezing and he almost dropped his books. I walked back to the dorm grinning anyway. Claire took one look at me and said, "Oh, finally."

------------------------------

ENTRY 137 — 2007-01-28 — The Morning After

The trouble with liking Evan is that I keep wanting to tell him things the moment they occur to me. Yesterday it was an argument about Byzantine taxes. This morning it was a dream involving a library that floated on a lake.

He listened to both.

We have not discussed what to call this. I think I may be using the uncertainty as an excuse not to say what I want.

Which is absurd, because I know what I want.

I want him to knock on my door again.

------------------------------

ENTRY 138 — 2007-02-11 — His Family

Evan grew up in Spokane. His mother is a librarian and his father teaches at a community college. They aren't poor; they just live in a house where the kitchen table is apparently the most expensive piece of furniture because everyone uses it for everything.

He works a campus job to help pay expenses. I offered to cover dinner and he stiffened.

I didn't mean to make it a transaction. He didn't mean to make me feel ashamed.

We walked back in silence, holding hands anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 139 — 2007-02-25 — A Kind of Proof

I spent most of Sunday solving a math problem I found online while a perfectly respectable history paper sat unfinished beside me. Evan said I have an affair with mathematics.

I told him mathematics doesn't leave wet towels on the floor.

He said, "Neither do I."

He was right. Claire does.

I wish people would stop requiring my jokes to be factually accurate.

------------------------------

ENTRY 140 — 2007-03-17 — Leah's Bad News

Leah's parents are getting divorced. She told us over coffee and then asked whether anyone wanted another muffin.

I wanted to say something profound. Nothing came.

So Claire and I helped her sort the laundry she'd been avoiding and we stayed up watching a ridiculous film. At two in the morning Leah told us the part she was actually frightened of: going home and not knowing which house counted as home.

I told her she could stay with me in Redmond if she needed. She said maybe. I meant it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 141 — 2007-04-06 — Seder Away From Home

I found a Passover gathering near campus and brought Evan. He asked before we went whether he'd embarrass me by not knowing the songs. I said I was much more worried about him asking seventeen questions during the meal.

He asked nineteen.

Afterward he wanted to know why we keep telling the same story. I said because telling a story is one of the ways a family keeps itself alive.

He went quiet. I don't think I'd ever explained it to myself so clearly before.

------------------------------

ENTRY 142 — 2007-04-22 — The Library Steps

We sat outside the library after dark and Evan told me he's afraid he'll become an ordinary man with ordinary opinions.

I said there's nothing shameful about being ordinary, then spent twenty minutes arguing that he was anything but.

He kissed my forehead and asked whether I ever hear myself.

I do, sometimes. Usually three minutes too late.

I like him enormously. It's beginning to frighten me how much.

------------------------------

ENTRY 143 — 2007-05-18 — Finals and Other Diseases

Haven't written for weeks. Finals. Papers. A disastrous attempt to cook pasta in the dorm microwave. Evan and I have stopped pretending we're merely spending time together.

I got an A on my European history paper and a note from the professor asking if I'd considered archival research. That pleased me more than the grade.

Mom called to ask if I was eating vegetables. I told her I eat lettuce in sandwiches. She said that is not a dietary philosophy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 144 — 2007-06-09 — His House in Spokane

Visited Evan's family for a weekend. His mother talked to me about books while she kneaded bread, and his father showed me an old telescope he'd repaired rather than replaced.

I liked them immediately. I suspect they liked me, though Evan says his mother interrogates everyone she approves of.

At night Evan and I sat on the porch while the neighbors watered their lawns. He said he was glad I'd come. I told him I was too.

I hadn't expected being welcome to feel so intimate.

------------------------------

ENTRY 145 — 2007-07-03 — Maya, Revised

Maya and I spent the afternoon at Redmond Town Center. She has a job for the summer and speaks about her coworkers as if they were characters in an opera. I laughed until I cried.

Then she told me I sometimes talk about Whitman like Redmond is a place I've outgrown. It hurt because she was right.

I said I missed her more than she knew. She said, "Then maybe tell me instead of sending articles about medieval economics."

Fine. I owe my best friend a real letter.

------------------------------

ENTRY 146 — 2007-07-24 — The House and the Money

A man at Dad's office offered him an investment and he said no before the man finished explaining it. I asked why.

"Because I don't need to get richer badly enough to do something I don't understand," he said.

We spent the evening talking about what wealth does to people's ability to say no, and whether that is freedom or just an expensive kind of insulation.

Then Dad asked me to empty the dishwasher. Evidently philosophy does not exempt anyone from household labor.

------------------------------

ENTRY 147 — 2007-08-07 — Thirteen Years

It has been thirteen years. Mom and Dad went together to Rebecca's grave this morning. I went later, alone, and sat in the grass for a while.

A bee kept landing on the flowers. I found it irritating and then funny, because Becca used to shriek when insects came near her.

I know exactly how she died. Some people mistake that for having finished grieving. I haven't. But today I remembered how she laughed when she cheated at cards, and that was what stayed with me on the drive home.

------------------------------

ENTRY 148 — 2007-08-31 — Back to Whitman

Second year. Claire and I are in an apartment with an alarming stove and a bathroom door that never shuts properly. Leah lives two streets away.

Evan helped carry boxes and insisted the couch would fit through the hallway if we turned it. He was wrong. I took photographs.

It's wonderful being back. It's also strange how quickly Walla Walla stopped feeling like a temporary place.

I like hearing the town wake up outside my window.

------------------------------

ENTRY 149 — 2007-09-18 — Arguments Under the Stars

Evan says historical knowledge can't make you moral; it only gives you more examples of immorality. I told him he was confusing knowledge with wisdom, and then realized that was almost his argument.

We were lying on a blanket in the park. I could see stars between branches and hear people playing music somewhere.

He asked why I was smiling. I said I liked being wrong in good company.

He said he intended to quote me on that for the rest of my life.

------------------------------

ENTRY 150 — 2007-10-11 — Nineteen

Nineteen today. Evan left a small wrapped notebook outside my door with a note: "For the questions you haven't invented yet."

He understands me. Sometimes too well.

Mom called while I was walking to class. I told her I wished Becca could see Walla Walla. She said she wished that too, and then told me about a very funny thing Andrew had done.

I appreciated the change of subject. Birthdays ought to contain more than one feeling.

Claire baked a cake that leaned to the left. We ate it anyway.

------------------------------

ENTRY 151 — 2007-11-04 — The Trip Form

I applied for a Jewish young-adult trip to Israel next summer. Mom is delighted and already planning which relatives I must visit afterward. Great-Aunt Hanne apparently has opinions about all of them.

Evan asked if I was going because of religion or history. I said family, curiosity, and wanting to see a place I have argued about for years without ever visiting.

He asked whether that was an answer. I said it was three answers. He laughed.

I hope I get accepted.

------------------------------

ENTRY 152 — 2007-11-23 — Thanksgiving in Redmond

Evan came home with me for Thanksgiving. Dad liked him almost immediately because they spent forty minutes debating the failure of some computer project. Mom was polite and curious and asked him the kind of questions that reveal everything while sounding perfectly harmless.

Andy liked him because Evan built an improbable suspension bridge from blocks.

After dinner Mom asked me quietly if I was happy. I said yes. She said, "Good."

There wasn't a warning attached. I kept waiting for one.

------------------------------

ENTRY 153 — 2007-12-08 — A Small Cruelty

At a party I made a joke about a friend's religious beliefs, and everyone laughed except her. I pretended not to notice because I didn't want to spoil the moment.

Later I apologized. She said she didn't think I was cruel, just careless about who pays for my jokes.

That is worse, somehow, because she was right.

I've been thinking about it all day. Being able to win a conversation is not the same as being good company.

------------------------------

ENTRY 154 — 2007-12-19 — An Ordinary Fear

The semester is over. I did well, although one paper on European migration was much harder than I'd expected. Claire says professors can smell arrogance the way dogs smell food.

Evan and I are apart for the holidays. I miss him already and I'm annoyed at myself for missing him.

The other night he said he loved me. I said it back. It felt entirely true.

Now I've started wondering how something that feels true can still change. What a dreadful question to take home for Christmas.

------------------------------

ENTRY 155 — 2007-12-30 — The Long Dinner

Dad and Mom hosted a dinner with friends from Microsoft. I expected to be bored, but one woman worked on accessibility software and talked about designing for people whose bodies work differently from the designer's.

I thought about how often we assume the world is built for someone like us.

Afterward Dad said I'd spent more time grilling his friend than he does during employee reviews. I told him she'd been fascinating.

He said, "Then write to her and say so."

I did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 156 — 2008-01-26 — Not Very Romantic

Evan and I spent Friday evening in the library reading different books at the same table. We barely spoke. Once he slid his hand over mine, and I kept reading because I wanted to find out how the chapter ended.

Afterward he said it had been a wonderful date.

I suppose romance doesn't always need candles or declarations. Sometimes it means knowing you can be silent beside someone and not be lonely.

Then we argued for twenty minutes about where to get dinner, so perhaps we're not very advanced after all.

------------------------------

ENTRY 157 — 2008-02-14 — The Rose Incident

Evan brought me one red rose. I accused him of making an unimaginative choice. He said he'd considered bringing seventeen tulips but suspected I'd derive a mathematical meaning from the number.

We had dinner at a little place in town. Halfway through dessert I told him I was scared that we wouldn't be this happy forever.

He didn't say we would. He said he wanted to find out what happened.

I didn't like the answer at first. Then I realized I believed it more than a promise of forever.

------------------------------

ENTRY 158 — 2008-03-09 — The History of Money

I've been reading about financial panics for a paper. It is amazing how often intelligent people can see a disaster coming and still convince themselves this particular time is different.

Dad says that's why he always kept a lot of our money out of the stock market even after Microsoft made him wealthy.

I asked whether having a trust fund means I can take risks other people cannot afford. He said of course it does.

I appreciated the honesty. I didn't much enjoy thinking about it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 159 — 2008-04-03 — Claire's Fish

Claire bought a goldfish. She named it Napoleon. Leah says the name is inaccurate because Napoleon was neither a fish nor gold.

I said he was arguably a poor choice for a small bowl of water. We spent half an hour debating the ethics of keeping fish.

Claire said we were ruining her happiness.

Napoleon died six days later. Claire cried, and Leah and I made him a cardboard coffin. I wasn't sure if a burial was excessive until I saw Claire's face. It wasn't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 160 — 2008-05-02 — One Awful Afternoon

I snapped at Evan because he forgot we had plans. His seminar had run late and then he'd gone to dinner with classmates. He didn't think to call.

I told him it made me feel unimportant. He said he wasn't going to spend his life reporting his location every hour.

Both of us were angry enough to say more than we meant. We went to bed without resolving it.

The next morning he knocked and brought coffee. I said I didn't want coffee. He said fine, he'd drink both. I laughed despite myself.

We talked properly after that.

------------------------------

ENTRY 161 — 2008-05-30 — The List for Israel

I've packed enough clothes for three different climates, two books about Israeli history, sunscreen, and one notebook in which I plan to be terribly insightful.

Mom says I should listen more than I argue. Dad says to call when I land. Grandma Miriam gave me the names of relatives I mustn't forget. Evan gave me a postcard of Walla Walla and wrote, "Come back to me."

I put it inside my passport.

I am nineteen, and I've never been so far away on my own.

------------------------------

ENTRY 162 — 2008-06-12 — Jerusalem

We arrived in Israel. I barely slept on the flight, and the light outside the airport looked almost white.

Our group is from everywhere. One girl from Boston knows more Hebrew than I do; a boy from Denver has never been inside a synagogue. We're all supposed to belong to something together, but we don't even agree on what that something is.

Today I walked through Jerusalem and kept having to remind myself that people buy groceries and complain about traffic here. A place can be history and ordinary life at once.

------------------------------

ENTRY 163 — 2008-06-16 — The Wall

I went to the Western Wall with the group. I watched people pray and others stand uncertainly, like me, with their hands in their pockets.

I had brought a little note. When it came time to put it in a crack between the stones, I discovered I'd written nothing on it.

I stood there with the blank paper for a long time. Then I folded it and put it away.

I don't think I owe anybody a performance of belief. I did want to be there, though. That was real.

ENTRY 164 — 2008-06-20 — A Bus Full of Historians

We spent most of today on the bus. The guide talked about archaeology and competing accounts of the same places. Everyone grew tired and argumentative.

At lunch I sat with a woman named Noa, one of the Israeli participants. She said she loves her country and is frequently furious with it. I told her that sounded more believable than perfect certainty.

She said I talk too much. Then she invited me to visit her again someday.

I've started writing questions on the backs of receipts because my notebook is full.

ENTRY 165 — 2008-06-24 — Hanne's House

The organized tour is over. Now I'm in Haifa with family, and I don't think I've ever been introduced to so many cousins at once.

Great-Aunt Hanne stood in the doorway with her arms folded and announced, "So you're the American who asks too many questions."

Then she hugged me until I couldn't breathe.

The kitchen is full of pots. Someone is always arriving. Someone else is always arguing. The family keeps kosher and the rules are different from what I'm used to at home.

Hanne said I would learn. Then she told me to eat.

ENTRY 166 — 2008-06-27 — A Different Shabbat

I spent Shabbat with Hanne's family. There was singing, prayer, food arriving from every direction, and a serious discussion about whether I'd put something in the wrong place in the kitchen.

I probably had.

Tamar, one of Hanne's granddaughters, is about my age. She is Orthodox, clever, and absolutely certain I misunderstand half of what matters to her. I suspect she's right about the half, but not necessarily about which half.

We argued until the candles had burned low. Then she brought me more food.

ENTRY 167 — 2008-06-30 — The Soldier and the Grandmother

Hanne told me she served as an officer in 1967. I asked about her work afterward, and she gave me a look that could have stopped a moving train.

"Some of it I can tell you. Some of it I won't. Don't mistake that for a game," she said.

Then she asked whether my father was still writing computer programs and why Americans insisted on drinking coffee out of buckets.

It is very hard to be intimidated by someone while she is cutting melon for you. I think that's part of her strategy.

ENTRY 168 — 2008-07-02 — Denmark and the Recipe

I helped Hanne make dinner. She told me about being a child in Denmark and escaping to Sweden during the war, about people who helped, and about memories she cannot put in the right order anymore.

I asked if she missed Denmark. She said she missed some people and a particular kind of bread. Then she laughed and complained that the tomatoes weren't ripe.

I wanted her to speak in chapters, with dates and causes. She spoke the way people actually remember.

I think I learned more by helping her cook.

ENTRY 169 — 2008-07-05 — Tamar and the Questions

Tamar and I talked until well past midnight. She asked how I could be seriously Jewish and still disagree with so many religious rules. I asked how she could be so certain that her way of living was the one God wanted.

We didn't convince each other of anything.

The next morning she lent me a scarf because I was cold and told me where to find the best pastries. I promised to send her a book. I think we'll be friends.

Hanne watched us and said, "Both of you think you're the clever one."

ENTRY 170 — 2008-07-09 — Leaving Israel

Hanne walked me to the car and slipped a small photograph into my hand. It showed her as a young woman beside Eli, before the family became enormous.

She said, "Next time, stay long enough to become bored with us."

I cried in the back seat on the way to the airport. I wasn't expecting to.

I came here wanting to learn about history and found people who kept interrupting the lesson by being alive. I don't want to forget their voices.

ENTRY 171 — 2008-08-07 — On the Way Home

Seafair week. I went to Rebecca's grave after returning to Redmond. I told her about Israel, about Tamar, and about Hanne bossing an entire household as though she still commanded an army.

I brought her a smooth stone from a place I visited. It looked ordinary. That's why I liked it.

The house was noisy when I got home. Andy was building a wooden bridge on the living-room floor. Mom made me tell the Israel stories again.

Somehow I wanted to tell them again.

ENTRY 172 — 2008-10-11 — Twenty

Twenty. I know I was supposed to grow up all at once somewhere between Jerusalem and Walla Walla, but I still lose socks and put off laundry.

Evan gave me a small sketchbook with a photograph from our winter walk tucked inside. I spent the afternoon painting a memory of Hanne's kitchen and made the walls far too yellow.

Dad called. Then Mom. Then Maya. Tamar sent a message from Israel full of teasing and Hebrew I had to look up.

I feel absurdly fortunate, and also restless. I want more life than can fit in any one room.

ENTRY 173 — 2008-12-21 — The Numbers Falling

Everyone is frightened about the economy. Dad says our family will be fine, which is not the same as saying nothing bad will happen.

Evan's campus-job hours were cut. I offered to help him with expenses and he got angry. I told him money wasn't a test of his character. He said it wasn't a test of mine either, and perhaps I could stop using it to solve things before he'd decided what the problem was.

He was right enough to make me furious.

I sat with him later while he worked on applications for summer jobs. He let me make tea. That helped more.

ENTRY 174 — 2009-01-20 — A New President

We watched the inauguration in a common room full of people who kept cheering at slightly different moments. Somebody spilled coffee on a history textbook. Nobody cared.

Leah said she'd never seen so many students pay attention to a speech without being graded on it. I wondered how different the day would look in twenty years, after we all know what happened next.

Evan accused me of writing history before the ink was dry.

I told him that's precisely when everyone starts getting things wrong.

ENTRY 175 — 2009-02-18 — Love and Dishes

Evan left his dishes in our kitchen again. Not one plate. A small archaeological site.

I complained. He said he'd do them later. I said later was a magical country where household chores went to die.

We fought over dishes for fifteen minutes, but we weren't actually fighting about dishes. We'd both been tired and busy and vaguely disappointed in each other.

He washed them. I apologized for making a courtroom out of the sink.

He dried the last bowl with one of my good sweaters. I nearly started the war again.

ENTRY 176 — 2009-03-14 — A History That Won't Behave

I'm working on a paper about the Danish Jews and the escape to Sweden. It should be easier because Grandma Miriam and Hanne lived through it. Instead it's much harder.

Grandma remembers one neighbor. Hanne remembers another. Official records call the same week by a different name.

For the first time I'm writing about people whose handwriting I've seen on birthday cards. I keep having to remind myself that loving a witness doesn't make every date in her memory exact.

That doesn't make the memory less important. It makes my work harder.

ENTRY 177 — 2009-04-07 — My Own Money

I paid for Evan and me to take an overnight trip without asking him first. I thought it would be romantic and spontaneous.

It was spontaneous. It was not romantic.

He said I was deciding things for both of us because I could afford to. I said I was trying to make him happy. He said that wasn't the same thing as asking.

We didn't go. I was furious until I realized I hadn't even asked whether he was free.

I can be generous in a remarkably selfish way.

ENTRY 178 — 2009-05-23 — Paint on Everything

Classes are finished and I have paint on my jeans, my shoes, and somehow inside my left ear. I've been painting the hills outside Walla Walla.

Evan sat beside me for an hour reading a book. Then he said my hills looked lonely. I said I was trying to paint the color, not the population density.

He said he missed when my jokes weren't always defenses.

ENTRY 179 — 2009-06-28 — An Afternoon with Maya

Maya came to Redmond for the weekend. She has a new haircut, an old car, and five plans for her future that are all mutually incompatible.

We went for coffee, then decided on the spur of the moment to drive toward the mountains. We took the wrong exit and got thoroughly lost. It was wonderful.

She told me Evan and I sound like people preparing for separate lives. I told her that was unkind.

She said it might still be true.

I was angry with her all the way home, which is usually how I know she's said something worth hearing.

ENTRY 180 — 2009-08-16 — Ten

Andy is ten. He wanted a model bridge kit and a book about famous buildings. At ten I wanted a blue diary. I think my brother has been an architect since he was approximately three.

Dad and I helped him assemble the bridge. Dad kept explaining structural loads while Andy ignored us and made improvements of his own.

Mom brought cake and said we had produced a family of people who would rather solve problems than eat.

We ate the cake. Nobody wanted to test that theory.

ENTRY 181 — 2009-09-06 — Senior Year Again

Last first day of college, unless I go to graduate school. Claire says that's a dangerous phrase because I'll use it to justify twenty additional years in classrooms.

Evan is working on applications for policy fellowships. He talks more and more about Washington, D.C., and the kind of public work he'd like to do.

I'm not sure where I want to go. I've started reading mathematics again late at night, the way some people read mysteries.

ENTRY 183 — 2009-10-30 — Statistics

One of my professors asked why I was doing so much extra mathematical work when I'm earning a history degree. I told her it was because I liked the feeling of a statement being either proved or not proved.

She said history could give me that feeling too, if I asked narrower questions.

Maybe. But I'm increasingly interested in the kinds of uncertainty that can't be eliminated by choosing better evidence.

I looked at some actuarial problems today. Apparently people can make a career out of studying risk. That sounds both fascinating and slightly absurd.

ENTRY 192 — 2010-05-04 — My History Thesis

I turned in my final history thesis today. The subject is migration, identity, and the stories families tell about leaving one country for another.

I used to think choosing a topic so close to my family's life would make the work easier. It made me question every sentence twice.

Professor Walsh wrote that the conclusion was thoughtful and needed fewer adjectives.

I laughed out loud. My private life has collapsed and my academic life is being corrected for excessive adjectives. There is something reassuring about that.

I found my very first diary while looking for a notebook. Ten-year-old me promised to write every day and then promptly failed. She also thought grown-ups were ridiculous.
