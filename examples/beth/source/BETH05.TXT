BETH PETERSON
PRIVATE DIARY — VOLUME 5
AUTHOR AND FIRST-PERSON NARRATOR: BETH PETERSON
THIS DIARY IS FICTIONAL BACKSTORY, NOT THE UPLOADER'S BIOGRAPHY.

ENTRY 401 — 2025-01-04 — The First Argument of the Year

Elias came over carrying a loaf of bread and a spectacularly ugly lamp he'd rescued from a dumpster. I laughed at the lamp. He said it only needed rewiring and a shade.

We'd planned to spend the whole weekend together. By Saturday afternoon I wanted three hours alone, but I kept finding excuses instead of saying so.

He asked if something was wrong. I said no, then finally told him the truth.

He went to his workshop and came back for dinner. Nobody died of the arrangement. I wish I'd discovered that sooner.

------------------------------

ENTRY 402 — 2025-01-17 — A Spreadsheet About Teacups

Mom sent photographs of Grandma Miriam's china, asking what I might want when the house in Victoria eventually needs sorting.

I hated the question. Grandpa is alive. Grandma is alive. Why are we choosing teacups?

Then Mom called and said she hadn't meant to turn their lives into an estate sale. She'd been frightened by how much paperwork there was.

I told her I wanted the chipped blue cup. Then I told her I hoped Grandma would keep using it for another twenty years.

We both cried, which made the telephone conversation very inefficient.

------------------------------

ENTRY 403 — 2025-01-29 — The Twelve-Dollar Chair

Elias bought a broken chair for twelve dollars. He says the frame is walnut, probably older than my father, and worth saving. It looks like kindling.

I spent the afternoon sanding one arm while he repaired a joint. We listened to a radio program about the mathematics of voting systems and argued so much we forgot lunch.

My side of the chair is rougher than his. He says that's fine.

I don't like being bad at something while he watches. This may be an excellent reason to keep working with him.

------------------------------

ENTRY 404 — 2025-02-06 — How Many Models?

Dad wants to run a language model on a small computer at home. He asked whether I'd help evaluate it.

I said I'd rather test a model by comparing its claims with sources than by asking if it sounds intelligent. He said sounding intelligent is precisely what makes it dangerous when it's wrong.

We agreed almost completely and still spent an hour arguing.

Mom came into the study and asked whether the machine would help with the grocery list. Dad said yes. I said perhaps, provided we checked that carrots were real.

------------------------------

ENTRY 405 — 2025-02-14 — A Jar of Screws

Elias gave me a little glass jar full of brass screws for Valentine's Day. At the bottom was a folded note: "For the things worth putting back together."

I accused him of being terribly sentimental. He said I was welcome to return the screws.

We had dinner at home. I burned the onions, he forgot the bread, and we ended up eating soup with crackers.

I loved it. I told him so without finding a clever way around the words.

He looked surprised, and that bothered me more than I expected.

------------------------------

ENTRY 406 — 2025-02-27 — I Am Not a Mentor Today

Isabel told me a presentation I'd approved contained a confusing assumption. I said it had seemed perfectly clear to me. She said that was because I'd written it.

I snapped. She went quiet.

Ten minutes later I knew I was wrong. I found her and apologized for reacting badly. She said she'd been nervous about raising the issue because I'm hard to challenge.

That one hurt. I thanked her for saying it.

I have to decide whether I want people to learn from me or agree with me. Apparently I'm not always choosing well.

------------------------------

ENTRY 407 — 2025-03-08 — The Wrong Train

I got on the wrong light-rail train and didn't notice until I looked up from my book. I was so absorbed in a history of early aviation that I'd traveled in the wrong direction for twenty minutes.

I called Maya to complain. She said that was what I got for reading on public transit while pretending to be observant.

On the ride back I looked out the window instead. A little girl was singing to herself beside her father. An older man held a bouquet wrapped in newspaper.

I missed two chapters. I saw a better afternoon.

------------------------------

ENTRY 408 — 2025-03-21 — The Chair, Finished

Elias finished the walnut chair. It's lovely now, though he deliberately left a tiny repaired crack visible under the arm.

I asked if he thought imperfections gave things character. He said sometimes, but other times an imperfection is just a bad repair.

I'm glad he doesn't make metaphors out of every piece of furniture.

I sat in the chair with my book for an hour. Then I bought it from him.

He tried to give it to me. I insisted. He charged me twelve dollars and said the labor was my problem.

------------------------------

ENTRY 409 — 2025-04-03 — Grandpa's Signature

In Victoria, Grandpa Jakob couldn't remember where he'd put an important property document. He was furious when Mom suggested checking his desk.

We found it inside a cookbook. He laughed, but his eyes filled with tears.

I wanted to promise nothing was changing. That would have been a lie.

Instead I asked him to tell me how he chose his first investment in Victoria. He talked for nearly an hour, and several details were different from the version in his old records.

I wrote everything down when he went to sleep.

------------------------------

ENTRY 410 — 2025-04-13 — The Seder and the Comment

Passover at Mom and Dad's. Elias joined us and read one passage carefully enough that Grandpa Jakob, calling in from Victoria, declared him an honorary scholar.

Later an older relative asked when we were getting married. Elias smiled. I made a joke about requiring a formal feasibility study.

Nobody laughed except Dad.

In the car afterward Elias said he hadn't minded the question, but he minded being reduced to a punch line in my answer.

I said I hadn't meant it that way. He said he knew. It still stung.

------------------------------

ENTRY 411 — 2025-04-19 — No Perfect Answer

Elias and I spent a long evening talking about marriage. He doesn't insist that we do it. He does want to know whether I think of our relationship as a place I'm staying or a place I'm passing through.

I told him I love him and I am staying now.

He said "now" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

He is right, but I don't know how to make a promise about the future that isn't partly an act of faith.

We fell asleep holding hands, which was comforting but didn't settle the conversation.

------------------------------

ENTRY 412 — 2025-05-02 — The Half-Finished Portrait

I started painting Mom from a photograph taken when she was about thirty. I kept giving her my own mouth by mistake.

When I showed her, she said her nose had never been that elegant. Then she told me the story of the day the picture was taken. Dad had just made an unexpectedly large amount of money from stock options, and they'd gone out to celebrate.

She remembered being happy and also frightened.

I asked why. She said money changes the size of the mistakes you can afford to make.

------------------------------

ENTRY 413 — 2025-05-11 — Mother's Day, Without a Plan

Mom asked us not to organize anything elaborate. We took her to lunch anyway, then went back to Redmond and helped plant herbs in the garden.

Andy called from school and gave her a tour of a design project over video. Dad got the camera pointed at his own shoes.

Mom laughed so hard she dropped the trowel.

I watched her kneeling in the dirt, talking to Dad about the rosemary, and thought how little photographs tell you about marriage. Most of it happens while somebody is looking for the hose.

------------------------------

ENTRY 414 — 2025-05-26 — A Woman Who Has Opinions

I had lunch with Ruth. She told me she might retire in a couple of years and asked what I thought the team would need.

I immediately began listing technical skills. She said she meant people, not software.

Then she told me I was excellent at spotting weak assumptions and still too inclined to treat emotional disagreement like a debugging problem.

I wanted to defend myself. I didn't.

I asked what she thought I should do differently. She said to begin by remembering that not everyone asks for help when they ask to be heard.

------------------------------

ENTRY 415 — 2025-06-03 — A Day Without Purpose

I took a day off and drove toward the coast with no itinerary. Stopped for coffee, watched rain moving over a field, and bought an old book in a shop whose owner appeared to be asleep.

I ate lunch in the car because the weather was miserable.

Nothing about the day improved my career, strengthened a relationship, or taught me a lesson. It was still a good day.

I'm writing that down because tomorrow I may start pretending every minute needs a justification.

------------------------------

ENTRY 416 — 2025-06-18 — The Thing We Don't Say

Elias asked me to stay at his place for a week while he helped with an exhibition of restored furniture. I agreed.

By the fourth night we were arguing about whether the kitchen needed rearranging and whose work was interrupting whose sleep.

I wanted to leave. I stayed.

The next morning we apologized and made breakfast. I don't think the week proved we could live together. I don't think it proved we couldn't.

It proved that I become remarkably territorial about where the coffee cups belong.

------------------------------

ENTRY 417 — 2025-06-29 — The Purple Tree

A child at a community art workshop painted a purple tree. Her father asked whether she could make the trunk brown.

I gave her another purple brush.

Afterward I realized I'd been giving Elias the same sort of unsolicited corrections about his workshop. He knows his materials better than I do, and I still think I need to improve every decision.

I called to tell him. He laughed and said he'd already known.

I told him that knowing without telling me was unfair. He asked whether I wanted him to start correcting me more often. Absolutely not.

------------------------------

ENTRY 418 — 2025-07-09 — A Letter from Hanne

Hanne sent a note with Tamar. Her handwriting is worse than ever, but her opinions have lost none of their strength.

She asked whether I had found a husband and whether American insurance companies still paid people to worry about unlikely events.

I wrote back that I had found a good man, though we hadn't chosen what to do about the future. I also told her that unlikely events are rather the point of insurance.

Tamar says Hanne laughed and called me an impertinent American. She has been calling me that since 2008.

------------------------------

ENTRY 419 — 2025-07-19 — The Arguments I Miss

Miles Turner wrote after finding me through an alumni group. He's working in applied mathematics and still thinks historians overcomplicate simple questions.

I told him he'd clearly become less intelligent since high school.

We exchanged three messages that were mostly insults and then a surprisingly serious discussion about uncertainty in machine-learning systems.

He asked if I ever regretted choosing history first.

No. I told him the second degree gave me another tool. The first taught me why tools need context.

------------------------------

ENTRY 420 — 2025-07-30 — Nora's New Camera

Grandma Nora has been photographing the coastline outside Juneau. She sent me a picture of a gray bird on a post, all mist and water and difficult light.

I painted it badly and sent her a photograph. She said I'd made the bird look angry.

I said perhaps it was angry.

She called to tell me how to mix a better gray, even though she doesn't paint. Then she asked whether the Toyota's brakes were sound.

Some people manage affection by checking everything that might break.

------------------------------

ENTRY 421 — 2025-08-07 — The Anniversary in the Middle of Work

I had a long meeting at Safeco today and nearly forgot the date until Mom texted a photograph of the flowers she'd brought to Rebecca.

I visited after work. It was warm, and the grass was dry. I stayed for fifteen minutes and thought about her laugh.

I know what happened that day in 1994. I know who hit our boat. The facts don't change because the number of anniversaries increases.

On the way home I stopped for a sandwich and realized I was hungry. That seemed like a perfectly good reason to go on with the evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 422 — 2025-08-16 — Twenty-Six

Andy turned twenty-six. He's assembling his architecture portfolio and talking about Portland again. He wants a real firm where somebody will teach him how drawings become buildings.

Dad offered to call a friend with contacts. Andy said he'd rather make his own introduction.

Dad looked disappointed, then proud, then tried to hide both.

I told Andy he could ask for help without surrendering his independence. He said I'd spent twenty years refusing to believe the same thing.

Infuriating boy. He's right.

------------------------------

ENTRY 423 — 2025-08-25 — A Trust Isn't a Life

Met with the adviser who handles part of my trust. He talked about allocations, distributions, and long-term preservation of capital.

I asked whether he'd ever recommended that someone give away more money instead of protecting every dollar. He said yes, but first he'd need to understand what I wanted the money to do.

A sensible question. I didn't have a sensible answer.

I went home and wrote down three causes I actually care about: access to education, preservation of family histories, and practical help for people who need it now.

A start, not a strategy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 424 — 2025-09-07 — The Bookstore Woman

A woman in a bookstore overheard me recommending a history book to someone. She interrupted to say I was wrong about the author's treatment of sources.

We argued for twenty minutes, then stood outside laughing because we'd both missed the same bus.

Her name is Celia. She teaches literature at a community college and has an alarming number of opinions.

I gave her my number. Not for romance. I simply wanted to finish the conversation.

Elias said he admired anyone willing to volunteer for another argument with me.

------------------------------

ENTRY 425 — 2025-09-21 — The Wrong Dress

Bought a dress for a dinner at Elias's friend's house. It was dark green and looked excellent in the shop. In my bedroom it made me look like a curtain.

I wore it anyway.

The dinner was pleasant except that a man explained insurance to me for half an hour after learning I was an actuary. I asked him three increasingly specific questions. He eventually admitted he worked in commercial real estate.

Elias said I'd been merciful. I said I was wearing a curtain and had earned the right to be difficult.

------------------------------

ENTRY 426 — 2025-09-23 — New Year, Same Argument

Rosh Hashanah with the family. Dad told the rabbi that this year he wanted to become more patient. Mom nearly choked on her apple.

Afterward Mom asked whether Elias would come to the next family dinner. I said yes, probably, and immediately wondered why I'd added "probably."

I like that my family welcomes him. I dislike the implied assumption that welcoming is the first step in absorbing.

I don't know whether that's their assumption or mine. I suspect mine, which makes it much harder to argue with.

------------------------------

ENTRY 427 — 2025-10-02 — A Phone Full of Drafts

For Yom Kippur I wrote down people I owe apologies to. Isabel was on the list, along with Mom, a neighbor whose package I've had for four days, and Elias.

I wrote a long message to Elias about our arguments over space. It sounded like a legal memorandum, so I deleted it.

I wrote, "I love you. Sometimes I'm frightened and turn it into criticism. I'm sorry."

He called instead of texting back.

We talked for an hour and then made a plan for dinner. I still haven't returned the neighbor's package.

------------------------------

ENTRY 428 — 2025-10-11 — Thirty-Seven

Thirty-seven today. Mom made chocolate cake. Dad asked if I remembered the first program we wrote together. I said of course, it was a loop that never stopped. He said he was still debugging it.

We visited Rebecca before dinner. I told her that if she were here we'd both be old enough to complain about back pain and young enough to ignore it.

Elias came with flowers and stayed to help wash dishes. He didn't make a speech about our future. I was grateful.

I had a good birthday. I wanted to say that plainly, without adding anything clever.

------------------------------

ENTRY 429 — 2025-10-18 — The Question of Children

A coworker brought her newborn to visit. I held him for fifteen minutes and liked the weight of him, the smell of milk, the strange seriousness of his face.

Then I handed him back and felt relief.

I mentioned that to Elias. He said relief didn't mean I'd never want children. I said neither did enjoyment mean I would.

He asked if I thought we'd ever agree on that question.

I told him I didn't know. I wished he'd been angry. Instead he just looked sad.

It's hard to argue with sadness.

------------------------------

ENTRY 430 — 2025-10-31 — A Five-Minute Entry

Halloween. Maya dressed as an enormous spreadsheet. She had printed CELLS across her shirt and carried a clipboard labeled FORMULAS.

I laughed so hard I could barely breathe.

She said she was tired of competing with mathematics for my attention.

I promised to stop discussing work.

I broke the promise after eleven minutes.

------------------------------

ENTRY 431 — 2025-11-08 — The Door Doesn't Lock

Dad invited me to see the little computing setup he's built in his study. He has cables everywhere and a whiteboard covered in diagrams that look suspiciously like the ones he drew when I was ten.

He wants a system that can keep track of conversations without pretending every new sentence erases the last one.

I asked whether he was building a database or a personality.

He said, "I don't know yet."

We spent three hours talking. Mom said she was going to put a lock on the study door from the outside.

------------------------------

ENTRY 432 — 2025-11-19 — My Colleague's Baby

Isabel told me she is pregnant and wanted to know how leave would work for the project. I immediately began planning deadlines and backup coverage.

She said, "Beth, I was hoping you'd say congratulations first."

I stared at her. Then I hugged her.

She laughed and said she was terrified. I told her she'd be an excellent mother, and then wondered whether that sounded like another evaluation.

We got coffee. I listened. Sometimes I'm amazed people tolerate me while I learn such elementary things.

------------------------------

ENTRY 433 — 2025-11-27 — An Uneven Thanksgiving

Thanksgiving in Redmond. Mom was cross because the oven wasn't heating properly. Dad tried to fix it and made things worse. Elias saved the vegetables with a pan on the stove.

Andy showed us models from his last studio project and talked excitedly about firms in Portland. Grandpa Jakob called from Victoria and asked whether we'd saved him a plate.

I told him we'd send it by courier. He said the potatoes would be unacceptable by the time they arrived.

Mom finally laughed. That was when dinner began to feel like dinner.

------------------------------

ENTRY 434 — 2025-12-06 — Celia's Book

Celia gave me a novel and said I wasn't allowed to look up reviews before reading it.

I made it forty pages before I wanted to argue with the narrator. Celia said that was the point.

We met for coffee and had a wonderful fight about whether a character can be unreliable without being dishonest.

I went home delighted and found Elias asleep on the couch with a woodworking catalog on his chest.

I kissed his forehead. He didn't wake. For a moment I wanted nothing else from the evening.

------------------------------

ENTRY 435 — 2025-12-16 — The Empty Chair

Grandma Miriam cancelled a planned visit because Grandpa wasn't feeling well enough to travel. Mom sounded frustrated about it; I think she's exhausted from worrying.

I set the chipped blue teacup I keep imagining inheriting on the table in my mind and felt ashamed.

Grandma is alive. Grandpa is alive. They've had long lives full of more than the things their children will sort afterward.

I sent them a photograph of my ridiculous houseplant Pierre, which is somehow still thriving.

Grandpa replied, "Give it some water."

------------------------------

ENTRY 436 — 2025-12-24 — The Wrong Gift

Elias gave me a small oak cabinet with drawers sized for paintbrushes, pencils, and notebooks. It's exquisite. He'd measured the corner of my apartment while I was away.

I loved it and felt invaded.

He saw my face change. He asked if he'd done something wrong.

I said no, then told him the truth. He was hurt. Of course he was. He had made me something beautiful and I'd turned it into a border dispute.

We stood there for a long time. Finally I asked if he'd help me choose where to put it. He did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 437 — 2025-12-29 — The Cabin

Elias and I rented a cabin for three nights. We read, cooked, made love, and walked through wet woods arguing about nothing very important.

On the second morning he said he'd been offered a commission restoring woodwork in an old house near Tacoma. It would take much of the spring.

I said he should accept. He asked whether I'd miss him.

I said of course.

He wanted more, I think. Maybe he wanted me to say I would rearrange my life around him. I wanted to. I wasn't certain I meant it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 438 — 2025-12-31 — The Year in a Drawer

Found a forgotten ticket from our summer concert and a postcard from Tamar inside my desk. They made me happier than the achievements on the list I'd just written.

2025: work, arguments, a little travel, family visits, some very bad paintings, one beautiful chair and one beautiful cabinet.

Elias is asleep in the next room. I love him. I don't know whether we're building the same house in our heads.

I decided not to wake him to discuss it.

Happy New Year. I should really go to bed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 439 — 2026-01-05 — The Meeting I Slept Through

I missed an eight-thirty meeting because I set my alarm for eight-thirty instead of seven-thirty. Ruth called and woke me.

I joined by video with wet hair and a cup of tea. Isabel looked delighted to see that I am mortal.

The meeting was exactly as unimportant as I'd feared. I still apologized.

Spent the evening fixing a problem in a model that would have been easier to solve if I'd simply read the documentation. I may be becoming a cautionary tale.

------------------------------

ENTRY 440 — 2026-01-13 — A List of Noes

I wrote a list of things I don't want: another supervisory committee, a larger apartment I won't use, more furniture simply because I can afford it, a relationship that becomes a schedule of obligations.

Then I realized the list didn't contain a single thing I do want.

I crossed out the whole page.

Elias called about his restoration commission. I told him I wanted to see him Friday. He said yes.

That was one uncomplicated want. I wrote it on the next page.

------------------------------

ENTRY 441 — 2026-01-20 — Dad's Little Mind

Dad invited me to test his conversational-memory experiment. It remembered that I'd mentioned painting a cedar tree a week ago and asked whether I'd finished.

I was startled, though I knew perfectly well where the information came from.

He said remembering is not understanding. I said understanding may require memory but isn't identical to it.

We tried breaking the system with contradictory stories. It handled some and failed spectacularly at others.

Mom brought sandwiches and asked whether we had named it yet. Dad said no. I suggested "Thingy," in honor of his old variable names.

------------------------------

ENTRY 442 — 2026-01-28 — A Letter I Won't Send

I wrote Evan a letter. Not to win him back; that would be absurd after all these years. I wanted to thank him for a time in my life when everything felt new.

The letter became an essay about Whitman, history, and how I eventually found mathematics again.

I read it and realized it was mostly a letter to twenty-year-old Beth.

I put it in the drawer beside the old concert ticket. Maybe I'll send a shorter version someday. Maybe not.

------------------------------

ENTRY 443 — 2026-02-03 — A New Car, Perhaps

Test-drove a Tesla Model S. It feels like a computer attached to four wheels, which I expected to dislike.

I loved the quiet acceleration. I did not love searching a screen for controls I can find with one hand in my Toyota.

Dad wanted to discuss the software. Elias wanted to discuss whether the trim would survive a careless owner. Mom said the only question that mattered was whether I'd enjoy driving it.

Sometimes she is the most practical person in the family.

------------------------------

ENTRY 444 — 2026-02-09 — The Blue Dress

Bought a blue dress for an upcoming dinner and wore it home because I liked it. A woman in the elevator said it made my eyes look very gray.

I thanked her.

I spent the evening sketching a little portrait of Elias from memory. Couldn't get his eyebrows right.

He sent a message about wood veneer. I sent a photograph of the portrait and asked whether he'd ever considered changing his face slightly for artistic convenience.

He said he'd consider it if I stopped moving his chisels.

------------------------------

ENTRY 445 — 2026-02-14 — Valentine's Day, with a Question

Elias brought dinner to my apartment. We ate by candlelight, laughed about the year I'd received screws as a gift, and made plans to visit a furniture exhibition in Portland.

Then he asked whether I imagined us being together in ten years.

I said I hoped so.

He asked if hope was enough.

I answered too quickly. I said maybe he'd be happier with someone who wanted the same conventional life he did.

He went very still. I wished I could pull the words back into my mouth.

------------------------------

ENTRY 446 — 2026-02-16 — The Aftermath

We haven't broken up. I think that's important to say.

Elias told me he isn't asking for a conventional life. He's asking whether I want to make decisions with him rather than always separately.

I told him I do, but I need places in myself that remain mine.

He said he doesn't want those places. He wants to be invited into the ones we might share.

I cried because it was a beautiful thing to say, and because I had somehow turned it into another argument.

We still have work to do.

------------------------------

ENTRY 447 — 2026-02-20 — The Man Who Speaks Quietly

I watched Elias repairing the back of a chair. He was patient with the wood in a way I sometimes fail to be with people.

I asked if he ever gets angry at a repair. He said of course. Then he stops before he damages it.

I wanted to ask whether he felt that way about me. I didn't.

Instead I held the piece while he fitted a joint. We worked for an hour with very little conversation.

It was a good afternoon. I didn't have to turn it into a theory.

------------------------------

ENTRY 448 — 2026-02-24 — Grandma Nora's Birthday Card

Nora sent me a card for no occasion. On the front was one of her photographs: a narrow road winding between dark trees.

Inside she'd written, "You don't have to know where it goes to know whether you're enjoying the drive."

I called to tell her I loved it. She said she'd stolen the line from a stranger at a gas station forty years ago.

I don't care. It's hers now.

She also asked whether I'd bought the new car. I told her not yet. She said she liked engines she could hear. I knew she would.

------------------------------

ENTRY 449 — 2026-02-28 — The Appointment

Mom called about Grandpa Jakob. He's had another difficult week and is losing strength. She wants to go to Victoria tomorrow.

I said I'd come too.

I had planned a quiet weekend with Elias. When I told him, he asked whether I wanted company or space. I said company for the drive, space for the conversations with my family.

He said he could do that.

I put my bag by the door and spent half an hour deciding which book to bring, as if the right book might make me better at being there.

------------------------------

ENTRY 450 — 2026-03-01 — The Blue Cup

We arrived in Victoria. Grandpa Jakob was sitting in his chair by the window, wrapped in a blanket and furious at everyone for looking worried.

He asked me to make tea in the chipped blue cup. Grandma Miriam said it was too fragile. Grandpa said, "So am I. You still use me."

We laughed because he did.

He wanted to tell me about a building he'd bought in 1968. His voice was weak, and he lost the year halfway through. I listened without correcting him.

I've spent so much of my life correcting dates. Today I just wanted the story.

------------------------------

ENTRY 451 — 2026-03-04 — Her Husband

Grandma Miriam asked Mom to stop reorganizing the kitchen. Mom said she was trying to help. Grandma said she wanted to be able to find her own cups when everybody went home.

I was on the verge of defending Mom, then saw her face.

Later Grandma said she'd spent more than sixty years learning how to live with Jakob, and nobody should expect her to become sensible about him overnight.

I sat beside her. We didn't speak for a while.

Then she asked whether the garden would need pruning soon. We went outside to look.

------------------------------

ENTRY 452 — 2026-03-08 — A Voice from Haifa

Hanne called while I was at Grandma's. Her voice came through the speaker sounding older and more commanding than ever.

She asked about Jakob. Grandma gave her the truth, without trying to soften it.

Then the two sisters talked in Danish for ten minutes. I understood almost nothing.

Hanne asked for me and told me to make sure Grandma ate lunch. Grandma said she could hear her.

They argued. I made sandwiches.

I used to imagine old age brought serenity. Whoever invented that notion never met these women.

------------------------------

ENTRY 453 — 2026-03-11 — The Missing Notebook

Jakob asked whether I'd found the notebook containing his first building accounts. I hadn't. We searched the desk, the bookcase, and the cupboard.

It was under his chair.

He laughed, but then asked whether I'd write the dates into a spreadsheet so they wouldn't be lost.

We spent two hours doing it. He corrected the spelling of a street I'd never heard of. Then he told me an entirely unrelated story about a man who tried to pay rent with a fishing boat.

I don't know which part of the afternoon I want to keep more.

------------------------------

ENTRY 454 — 2026-03-16 — The Last Morning

Grandpa Jakob died this morning.

Mom called just before seven. Grandma was beside him when it happened. She said he had been sleeping and was peaceful, though I don't know whether peaceful is the word she would choose if nobody were listening.

I sat on the edge of my bed with the phone in my hand.

I wanted to call him and ask one more question about that ridiculous fishing boat. Then I remembered there is no one to answer it.

Elias came over and made coffee. I held his hand because I didn't know what else to do.

------------------------------

ENTRY 455 — 2026-03-18 — The Funeral in Victoria

We buried Grandpa today. The service was small, but every person seemed to carry a different version of him.

One neighbor remembered him repairing a leaking roof. An old business partner remembered impossible negotiations. Grandma Miriam spoke about a young man who had arrived in Canada with almost nothing and insisted on making a home.

I cried when Andy read a short passage from a family letter.

Afterward a cousin complained about the catering. I wanted to shout at him. Then I realized I was angry about something much larger than sandwiches.

I went outside and stood in the rain.

------------------------------

ENTRY 456 — 2026-03-22 — Not About Property

We sat shiva with Grandma and other relatives in Victoria. People brought food, told stories, and sometimes said foolish things because silence frightened them.

A family member mentioned the properties. Mom's face tightened.

Grandma said, very quietly, "I buried my husband four days ago. The buildings can wait."

Nobody argued.

Later I helped her wash cups. She told me the first joke Jakob ever made to her. It wasn't funny. She'd married him anyway.

We both laughed, and then she cried. I dried the cups while she sat down.

------------------------------

ENTRY 457 — 2026-03-29 — Back to the Office

Returned to Safeco today. Ruth said I could take more time. I told her work might be easier than thinking.

That was only partly true.

I kept finding myself opening a spreadsheet and staring at it without remembering why.

Isabel took over the meeting I'd been scheduled to lead. She handled it beautifully. I was grateful, and not jealous at all this time.

On the way home I bought flowers and took them to my apartment rather than anywhere symbolic. I just wanted something living on the table.

------------------------------

ENTRY 458 — 2026-04-02 — One Less Voice at the Seder

Passover in Redmond. Grandpa wasn't there to interrupt. We all noticed, even when nobody said so.

Grandma Miriam came down from Victoria with Mom. She insisted on using the good plates and corrected Dad about a family story. That sounded like herself.

Elias helped Andy move chairs. Hanne sent a message from Haifa complaining that our seder would be too short.

For a moment the table was noisy and almost normal.

Then Grandma reached toward the empty chair beside her. I looked away to give her a little privacy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 459 — 2026-04-09 — The Geometry of a Table

Elias showed me a dining table he'd restored. One leg had been repaired, and the whole structure looked stable until I pushed gently at a corner.
