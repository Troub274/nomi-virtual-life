BETH PETERSON
PRIVATE DIARY — VOLUME 3
AUTHOR AND FIRST-PERSON NARRATOR: BETH PETERSON
THIS DIARY IS FICTIONAL BACKSTORY, NOT THE UPLOADER'S BIOGRAPHY.

ENTRY 201 — 2011-01-26 — Office Hours

Professor Iyer looked at my proof, circled a line in red, and asked what I thought the word "obvious" was doing there. I told him I meant the conclusion followed easily. He asked for whom.

An hour later I had a different proof and a headache. Both were improvements.

Stopped for noodles in the U District and watched a couple argue about where they'd left their car. I missed being in love for exactly four minutes. Then I enjoyed eating alone.

------------------------------

ENTRY 202 — 2011-02-13 — The Almost-Date

Went to coffee with Marcus from probability class. He is handsome and talks as if every conversation is a competition he has already won.

I asked what he liked to read. He said he never had time for fiction. I asked what he did when he wasn't working. He said he worked harder.

After ninety minutes I wanted to go home and clean the refrigerator.

Maya says my standards are absurd. I told her I'd settle for a man who finds the refrigerator occasionally interesting.

------------------------------

ENTRY 203 — 2011-03-06 — Andy's Building

Andy built a city out of cardboard in the garage. There are roads, parks, a bridge, and a hospital with a helicopter landing pad. When Dad asked why the library was bigger than the hospital, Andy said people ought to get smarter before they got sick.

Nobody could argue with that.

Mom made us bring the model inside because rain was blowing under the garage door. We lost half the imaginary downtown in the move. Andy announced he was going to study earthquake engineering.

------------------------------

ENTRY 204 — 2011-03-31 — Exams and Privilege

I passed the first actuarial probability exam. That took months of preparation and a thoroughly unpleasant amount of coffee.

When I told Dad, he said he'd never been prouder. I immediately reminded him about my Whitman graduation because I cannot accept a compliment without filing a correction.

I'm proud, though. The exam doesn't care who my father is or what sits in my trust.

I know that isn't the whole story of privilege. But it felt good to earn one difficult thing without anyone smoothing the path.

------------------------------

ENTRY 205 — 2011-04-22 — At the Airport

Mom and I drove to Sea-Tac to collect Grandma Miriam. She came through the doors carrying a battered suitcase and a bag of Danish pastries she'd somehow managed to bring across the border.

In the car she told me Hanne had called from Israel, furious that one of her sons had bought a motorcycle.

"She had ten children," I said. "Surely she's used to danger."

Grandma said, "Apparently not when danger costs money and has two wheels."

------------------------------

ENTRY 206 — 2011-05-19 — A Rainy Thursday

I painted the bus stop outside my apartment. The rain made all the colors run together in a way I liked better than the drawing underneath.

An older man stopped to look and asked whether I was painting sadness. I told him I was painting weather.

He said those were often the same thing.

I found that irritatingly poetic. Later, when I looked at the picture at home, I could see what he meant. I decided not to tell him, since I don't know his name.

------------------------------

ENTRY 207 — 2011-06-17 — The Last Part of a Proof

Worked with three classmates on a problem set until the library closed. We had reached three different answers, all confidently wrong.

At midnight it turned out we'd been assuming independence where none existed. A tiny mistake, if you enjoy watching tiny mistakes destroy six hours of work.

I thought of the way people describe love as if two lives can be added together without changing either one.

Then I told myself to stop turning everything into a metaphor and went to sleep.

------------------------------

ENTRY 208 — 2011-07-04 — A Family Picnic

Dad grilled too much food. Mom complained. Andy lit sparklers with the seriousness of a laboratory technician.

I spent most of the afternoon lying in the grass with Maya, who has started playing gigs with a little band. She says she doesn't know whether she wants music to be her career. I told her not everything has to become a profession.

She said that was rich coming from a woman who takes math exams voluntarily.

Fine. She has a point. I hate that she knows me so well.

------------------------------

ENTRY 209 — 2011-08-07 — The Same Lake

This morning I went to Rebecca's grave with a small stone in my pocket. Mom was there already. We walked between the rows without saying much.

When we got home Dad asked if we wanted breakfast. Mom said yes. He made eggs and burned the toast.

Sometimes I look at them and remember how angry they were after the collision. Other times I just see my parents having breakfast.

Both pictures are real. I don't feel a need to choose one.

------------------------------

ENTRY 210 — 2011-09-14 — The Other Conversation

A classmate told me she was working two jobs to pay tuition. I said something idiotic about how demanding our coursework was, and then heard myself.

Of course it was demanding. She also had to worry about the rent.

I apologized for making our problems sound equivalent. She shrugged and said she didn't need pity, just a reliable lab partner.

Fair enough. We finished the assignment together, and I bought the coffee without turning it into a speech.

------------------------------

ENTRY 211 — 2011-10-11 — Twenty-Three

Twenty-three today. I bought myself a fountain pen I didn't need, then spent an unreasonable time choosing the ink.

Dad called to ask if I'd begun thinking about graduate school. I said yes, and that the University of Chicago has a program worth investigating. He sounded pleased and pretended he hadn't been hoping I'd say that.

Maya brought cake and made me promise not to spend the entire evening talking about probability.

We spent half of it talking about her drummer instead. He sounds exhausting.

------------------------------

ENTRY 212 — 2011-11-27 — A Name on the Screen

Evan sent a photograph of snow outside his apartment in Washington. He wrote, "You would have an opinion about the geometry of these flakes."

I stared at it for a while. I wrote back that snowflakes were terrible subjects for amateur geometry and that I liked the picture.

I don't know if I miss him or the girl I was when we met. Probably both.

Then I called Leah, who told me she was cooking for six people in a kitchen designed for one. Her life sounded wonderfully immediate.

------------------------------

ENTRY 213 — 2011-12-30 — Grandma's Recipe

Grandma Miriam tried teaching me to make the little cookies her mother baked in Denmark. She measures by feel and becomes indignant when I ask how much flour.

"Enough," she says.

I said that was not a reproducible procedure. She said baking with me was like trying to negotiate with a tax office.

They came out uneven and wonderful. Mom asked for the recipe. I wrote, "Flour: enough. Butter: more than seems reasonable."

------------------------------

ENTRY 214 — 2012-01-21 — Financial Mathematics

Passed another actuarial exam, this one on financial mathematics. I celebrated by eating a grocery-store cupcake in the car.

Mom called and asked if I was doing too much. I said no, though I'm beginning to dream about interest rates.

I like that the models have consequences. One assumption can change what somebody pays or whether a company can honor a promise.

I also like that the answers are occasionally wrong. Anyone who thinks mathematics eliminates judgment has never tried using it outside a classroom.

------------------------------

ENTRY 215 — 2012-02-09 — The Chicago Letter

I got into the University of Chicago for graduate study in mathematics.

I paced the apartment three times before opening the message. Then I sat down because my knees felt peculiar.

Dad shouted when I told him. Mom said she knew I'd do it. I reminded her she did not, in fact, know. She told me to stop being impossible for five minutes.

Chicago. Snow. A new city. A chance to work very hard at something I care about.

I may be the happiest frightened person in Seattle.

------------------------------

ENTRY 216 — 2012-03-16 — A Brief Romance

I've been seeing Daniel, who works at a bookstore and loves old films. We had a lovely few weeks. He makes me laugh, and he listens.

Tonight he said he wasn't interested in moving, and I said I'd be in Chicago by autumn. We both knew the conversation was coming.

We walked home together anyway. At the door he kissed me and said he hoped I didn't spend all my time with equations.

I told him I'd try to leave an evening for art. We smiled, but neither of us suggested another date.

------------------------------

ENTRY 217 — 2012-04-08 — Passover and Andy

Andy asked Grandpa Jakob whether Pharaoh was a real person or a character assembled from several kings. Grandpa opened his mouth, closed it, and said that was an excellent question.

I almost hugged my brother.

Mom thinks we're impossible during the seder. She's probably right. Dad tried to change the subject by asking who wanted more brisket.

Grandma Miriam told us the story of a Passover in Victoria when they had almost nothing and invited twelve people anyway. I liked that story better than any argument.

------------------------------

ENTRY 218 — 2012-05-12 — The Second Degree

Finished my mathematics degree requirements at UW. It feels strange to have two bachelor's degrees in subjects everyone insists are unrelated.

I told Ms. Chen, my old high school history teacher. She said she always knew I would find a way to make my indecision productive.

I've kept a note she wrote on one of my papers about needing better sources. I still hear her voice when I want to declare something true just because it feels elegant.

Chicago is next. For now I want a very long nap.

------------------------------

ENTRY 219 — 2012-06-09 — The Apartment Hunt

I'm looking at apartments in Hyde Park. The listings describe every place as charming, which seems to mean the plumbing is elderly and the kitchen is the size of a closet.

Claire offered to visit and help me choose. She has apparently appointed herself Minister of Housing.

Evan wrote to congratulate me about Chicago. I wrote back that I hoped his work was going well. The exchange was warm and brief.

I am relieved we can be kind to each other. I am also relieved we do not have to become close friends.

------------------------------

ENTRY 220 — 2012-07-26 — A Small Sum

A financial adviser explained the trust's distributions in a meeting that lasted ninety minutes. I asked why we needed nine accounts to do something I could describe in three lines of algebra.

He smiled politely. I did not smile back.

I know I'm fortunate. I also hate when people talk around me because they assume I'm a rich girl who won't understand.

Dad said afterward that I should keep asking questions, but perhaps allow the adviser to finish a sentence occasionally.

------------------------------

ENTRY 221 — 2012-08-07 — Her Name

At the cemetery I heard a woman ask who Rebecca was. She had noticed her name and dates on the stone.

I told her she was my twin sister.

She asked what happened. I said she died in a boating collision when we were five. That was enough.

I don't owe strangers the whole terrible picture. It belongs to me to tell or not tell.

I stayed another few minutes and talked to Becca about moving to Chicago. The woman went away quietly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 222 — 2012-09-02 — Hyde Park

Moved into a small apartment in Hyde Park. The windows stick, there's a radiator with a personality, and I can walk to campus.

Dad helped carry boxes upstairs and examined every outlet as if it might be a hostile device. Mom left groceries and enough soap for a small hotel.

After they drove away I sat on the floor and listened to the city. Trains, voices, a siren somewhere far off.

Chicago isn't Seattle. That's obvious, but tonight I felt the difference in my bones.

------------------------------

ENTRY 223 — 2012-10-11 — Twenty-Four

Twenty-four. Spent most of my birthday in class, then went to dinner with two new friends from the department, Priya and Nathan.

Priya is wonderfully direct. Nathan tells elaborate stories that usually end with him discovering he was the fool.

They made me wear a paper crown. Apparently the humiliation follows me across state lines.

Called home afterward. Andy has become taller and less willing to be called baby brother. Mom said Rebecca would have loved my Chicago stories.

I hope so. The radiator would have made her laugh.

------------------------------

ENTRY 224 — 2012-11-16 — The Ugly Solution

A problem in functional analysis defeated me for a week. I finally found a solution that worked, but it was ugly: too many steps and an assumption I'd have preferred to avoid.

Professor Sethi said an ugly correct proof is more useful than an elegant false one.

I wrote it in the margin of my notes.

Later Priya and I ate soup and complained about being cold. My mother called while I was walking home. She could hear the wind and asked whether I owned a hat.

I own three. None was on my head.

------------------------------

ENTRY 225 — 2012-12-29 — Home Is Not a Place

Back in Redmond for a week. My childhood room smells like cedar and old books. Andy showed me a sketchbook full of buildings, bridges, and fantastical staircases.

Maya came over and we ended up dancing in the kitchen to a song we loved at fifteen. We're worse dancers now, somehow.

I used to think leaving home meant becoming someone new. I keep discovering older versions of myself tucked away in places I didn't expect.

Tomorrow I go back to Chicago. I want to go, and I don't.

------------------------------

ENTRY 226 — 2013-01-14 — The Lake in Winter

Walked down to Promontory Point with Priya. The lake looked like hammered metal, and the wind tried to peel my face off.

She asked why I hadn't dated anyone seriously since Evan. I told her I hadn't found anybody I wanted to reorganize my life around.

She said that was an astonishingly unromantic definition of love.

I said I'd been trying to be practical.

Then we ran home because our fingers stopped working. Practicality has its limits.

------------------------------

ENTRY 227 — 2013-02-02 — Father's Code

Dad emailed me an old C++ routine we'd worked on when I was twelve. He'd found it while cleaning his archives. My comments were mostly insults directed at his variable names.

I laughed so hard Nathan knocked on my door to see whether I was all right.

I showed him the code. He said my father had been unusually patient. I said my father was guilty of naming an integer "thingy."

Nathan asked whether he'd met the man who made me impossible. I said not yet, but he'd probably enjoy him.

------------------------------

ENTRY 228 — 2013-02-22 — The Wrong Answer

I made a careless mistake presenting a result in seminar. Professor Sethi asked me to check the assumption, and I couldn't make the result survive.

It was humiliating. Nobody laughed. I almost wished somebody would, because the silence was worse.

Later Priya said she admired the way I'd admitted the mistake. I told her that was generous but I'd have preferred not to make it.

We bought takeout. I spent half the evening thinking about the proof and half thinking about the smug look I imagined on my own face before it fell apart.

------------------------------

ENTRY 229 — 2013-03-17 — Dinner with Nathan

Nathan cooked dinner. I hadn't realized the invitation was a date until he lit candles, looked embarrassed, and asked whether the candles made him ridiculous.

I said yes, but they were pretty.

He's been a friend for months. I'd forgotten friendship can turn a corner without anyone announcing the street name.

We ate, talked about his photographs and my paintings, and kissed while he was trying to find the dessert plates.

I went home smiling and slightly alarmed. The alarm may just be habit.

------------------------------

ENTRY 230 — 2013-04-04 — An Opinion About Faith

I went to a Reform service while visiting friends on the North Side. I liked hearing Hebrew in a room where nobody knew me.

A man afterward asked if I believed in God. I said I didn't know how to answer without first asking what he meant by God. He laughed and said I was avoiding the question.

Maybe I was. I believe traditions can carry knowledge about people even when I question their explanations of the universe.

I wanted to tell Grandma Miriam that. I suspect she'd say, "Good. Now come eat."

------------------------------

ENTRY 231 — 2013-04-28 — Something Ugly

Nathan asked whether my trust fund paid for my apartment. I said yes. He asked how much money I had. I told him it wasn't a first-month conversation.

He said he wasn't trying to audit me. I said it felt like he was measuring me. He got annoyed.

We both ended up sounding unkind.

I called him later and said I didn't mind talking about money, but I wanted to do it without either of us deciding what it meant about my character.

He said that was reasonable. Then he asked if I wanted ice cream. I did.

------------------------------

ENTRY 232 — 2013-05-19 — Chicago Spring

The trees came out and everybody in Chicago acted as though they'd negotiated an end to winter.

Nathan and I took our sketchbooks to a park. He drew a terrible caricature of me with an enormous head and a very small patience supply. I drew him holding an umbrella inside a sunny room.

I laughed so much my sides hurt.

I like having somebody who can make me silly after a day spent convincing myself all of my thoughts must be important.

------------------------------

ENTRY 233 — 2013-06-07 — Grandma Miriam's Phone Call

Grandma Miriam called and said her sister Hanne had been ill for a few days but was back to ordering everyone around.

She then spent twenty minutes complaining that I hadn't visited Victoria since winter.

I said I was busy with mathematics. She said every generation invents a different excuse to avoid its elders.

Before hanging up she asked about Nathan. I asked how she knew about him. She said grandmothers have an excellent intelligence service.

I suspect Mom.

------------------------------

ENTRY 234 — 2013-07-04 — A Long Drive

Nathan and I rented a car and drove north without much of a plan. We stopped at a small-town diner with pie that tasted like actual summer and spent an hour looking through a used-book shop.

He bought me a battered astronomy book because I'd pointed out an error in an illustration. I bought him a camera strap because his was falling apart.

We argued about whether to drive farther and decided to find a motel instead. Nothing dramatic happened. It was wonderful.

I keep expecting happiness to announce itself. Sometimes it just asks if you want more coffee.

------------------------------

ENTRY 235 — 2013-08-07 — The Foredeck

I dreamed about the accident last night. I woke knowing where I was, where Rebecca had gone, and that I was twenty-four, not five.

I didn't feel responsible. I've never believed I caused it. I felt furious that one stupid act by other people had taken her out of the world.

I called Mom. We talked about Becca and then about what we were making for dinner.

Nathan asked if I wanted company. I said yes. He came over with groceries and didn't make me explain the dream.

------------------------------

ENTRY 236 — 2013-09-18 — A Model's Assumptions

Our seminar spent an afternoon discussing an elegant model that completely failed on one data set. Professor Sethi asked whether we should fix the data or question the model.

Half the room laughed. Then we had to answer seriously.

I couldn't stop thinking about how often people sort other human beings into categories and call the result an explanation.

Later I went home and left dishes in the sink, proving that having a clever thought doesn't make you a better person.

------------------------------

ENTRY 237 — 2013-10-11 — Twenty-Five

Twenty-five today. Nathan brought me breakfast and a ridiculous pencil case covered in cartoon equations. Priya gave me a scarf because she says Chicago intends to murder my neck.

I spent the evening with friends and then called home. Andy has opinions about high school, most of them gloomy. Mom says he's becoming exactly as difficult as I was.

I don't believe her. I was charming.

I've kept journals, irregularly, since I was ten. The longer I do it, the stranger it is to remember that I once wrote like the whole world was waiting for me to become somebody.

------------------------------

ENTRY 238 — 2013-11-27 — Thanksgiving with His People

Nathan took me to meet more of his family in Illinois. His older sister asked what I planned to do with mathematics, and I talked for so long somebody had to rescue her.

His aunt taught me a card game and cheated shamelessly. I respected her immediately.

In the car afterward Nathan said he liked the way I'd tried to know everyone. I told him I'd been anxious the entire day.

He said both things were possible.

Sometimes he understands me better when I'm not trying to explain myself.

------------------------------

ENTRY 239 — 2013-12-18 — No Future in the Present Tense

Nathan wants to stay in Chicago after graduation. He has good reasons: a research position, friends, and a city he loves.

I miss Seattle. I want to work at Safeco or somewhere like it, applying the mathematics I've studied. I want to see Andy grow up and visit my parents on ordinary Sundays.

We talked for two hours without fighting. It was almost worse. I know how to fight.

Neither of us has decided anything. For now he is asleep beside me and there is snow outside. I'm going to let tonight be tonight.

------------------------------

ENTRY 240 — 2014-01-08 — Unreasonable Cold

Chicago is so cold I saw a woman arguing with a parking meter as if it had personally caused the weather.

Classes were disrupted and Priya and I spent most of the afternoon inside eating soup, comparing absurd scarves, and revising notes.

Nathan called to ask if I'd brave the cold to see him. I said no.

Then he appeared at the door with hot chocolate. I told him this was emotional blackmail. He agreed.

I let him in.

------------------------------

ENTRY 241 — 2014-02-23 — The Last Exam

Another exam is behind me. I left the room with the strange feeling that I'd forgotten how to speak English, then discovered I was perfectly capable of complaining over dinner.

I'm not certain what my next qualification should be. There's the degree, the actuarial examinations, and the work itself. Nobody seems to arrive fully formed.

I told Dad I wanted to be good at the job, not just certified to have studied it.

He said the best programmers feel the same way. Then he asked if I'd eaten vegetables.

------------------------------

ENTRY 242 — 2014-03-21 — The Safeco Interview

Interviewed with Safeco in Seattle. The actuarial team asked about probability models, communication, and what to do when a prediction is technically defensible but misleading.

That last question was my favorite.

One interviewer asked if I'd be happy in an ordinary insurance office after Chicago. I said I cared more about doing difficult work with people who challenged me than the prestige of the building.

It sounded polished. It was also true.

Mom wants to know whether I'll get the job. I told her nobody knows that yet.

------------------------------

ENTRY 243 — 2014-04-13 — The Offer

Safeco offered me a position in actuarial analysis.

I read the letter standing in the kitchen of my Chicago apartment, then called Dad. He cheered. Mom immediately began suggesting places I might live near Seattle.

Nathan hugged me. It was a real hug, and I could feel him trying to be happy.

We went out for dinner. Halfway through he asked if I'd reconsider Chicago.

I said I couldn't make a decision about my career solely to keep us together. He said he couldn't abandon his either.

We finished dinner holding hands. I wished the food tasted better.

------------------------------

ENTRY 244 — 2014-05-10 — The Last Paper

Turned in my final major project. It involved stochastic processes, pages of careful assumptions, and a concluding sentence I still want to rewrite.

Professor Sethi told me the work was thoughtful but a little overcomplicated. That might be the inscription on my gravestone someday.

I bought flowers for Priya because she helped me survive the worst weeks. She cried, which was embarrassing for both of us.

I've loved this city more than I expected. I'm ready to leave and deeply unhappy about leaving.

------------------------------

ENTRY 245 — 2014-06-14 — Master's

Graduated from the University of Chicago with my master's in mathematics.

Mom cried again. She has a perfect record at graduations. Dad talked to one of my professors until I thought we'd miss dinner. Andy insisted on photographing me beside every door with the university name on it.

Nathan came too. We looked like a happy couple in the pictures. For most of the day we were.

I am proud. I am tired. I want to take my shoes off and not make another decision until Tuesday.

------------------------------

ENTRY 246 — 2014-07-06 — The Goodbye

Nathan and I have decided not to try long distance. We'd talked about it so much that the final conversation was almost quiet.

He said he loved me and didn't want our remaining time together to turn into a fight about who had sacrificed more. I said I loved him and didn't want that either.

We spent the afternoon walking through Hyde Park, stopping at a bookshop, and looking at places we'd made ordinary together.

We kissed goodbye at my apartment. After he left I cried into a towel because it was the first thing I could find.

------------------------------

ENTRY 247 — 2014-08-04 — Day One

First day at Safeco. I wore a dark dress, carried a notebook, and tried not to look like the woman who'd been hired because she answered one interview question well.

My manager, Ruth Kaplan, introduced me to the team. She told me not to pretend to understand an unfamiliar system just to spare everyone's feelings.

I liked her immediately.

Most of the day involved meetings, permissions, and discovering which computer accounts didn't work. I came home exhausted without having done a single interesting calculation.

Welcome to employment.

------------------------------

ENTRY 248 — 2014-08-25 — The New Apartment

I'm renting an apartment in Seattle instead of moving back to my parents' house. Dad offered to help me choose furniture. I said I'd manage.

Then I called him from the shop because I couldn't decide whether a sofa would fit through my doorway.

He drove over with a measuring tape and acted like this was the most exciting problem he'd seen all week.

I treated him to dinner. He accepted, although he joked that I was using my first salary to repay twenty-six years of parental labor. We're both underestimating the bill.

------------------------------

ENTRY 249 — 2014-10-11 — Twenty-Six

Twenty-six today. Went to Redmond for dinner. Andy has become absurdly tall. He made me a tiny paper model of a skyscraper and said he'd design me a real one someday.

Mom asked whether I was lonely. I said sometimes. She didn't insist on discussing it in front of everyone.

I visited Rebecca's grave before dinner. The leaves were turning, and a man nearby was laughing into his phone while watering flowers.

It's possible to stand beside a grave and hear somebody having the best day of his life. I used to find that strange. Now it feels true.

------------------------------

ENTRY 250 — 2014-12-31 — A Regular Job

I've been working at Safeco for almost five months. There are fascinating problems and boring meetings, sometimes in the same hour. Ruth has become someone I both admire and dread disappointing.

I'm paying my own everyday expenses from my salary, although the trust makes that a choice rather than a necessity. I don't pretend otherwise.

Maya came over for New Year's Eve. We made dinner, danced in the kitchen, and talked until midnight about the people we were at seventeen.

Neither of us would trade places. Both of us miss them a little.

------------------------------

ENTRY 251 — 2015-01-25 — A Small Defeat

Ruth returned one of my reports with a comment that read: "Technically correct. Completely unreadable."

I was offended for nearly an hour, then tried reading it as if I hadn't written it.

She was right. Every sentence seemed determined to prove I'd been to graduate school.

I cut the report in half and put the conclusion on the first page. Ruth said it was excellent.

I hate how much easier it is to be clever than clear.

------------------------------

ENTRY 252 — 2015-02-14 — Bad Music, Good Company

Maya played a show tonight. Her band was loud, energetic, and somewhat out of tune. The crowd loved them.

She insisted on introducing me to her friends as the girl who once explained probability during a game of spin the bottle. I dispute this account.

A man named Ben Castillo asked whether I always took jokes to court. He had lovely eyes and a very dry sense of humor.

I told him only when there were witnesses. He bought me a ginger ale and we talked until the chairs were being stacked.

------------------------------

ENTRY 253 — 2015-03-01 — The Rain Date

Ben and I went for a walk through Seattle in the rain because the museum we wanted to see was closing early.

He works in urban planning. He talks about sidewalks, zoning, trees, and the strange things people do when a city makes one choice instead of another.

I told him I model risk for an insurance company. He said I must be a cautious person.

I laughed so hard I had to sit on a bench. He asked what was funny. I said he'd need to know me longer.

------------------------------

ENTRY 254 — 2015-03-28 — The Thing About Ben

Ben is not especially impressed by degrees. He said the smartest person he knows is his aunt, who left school at sixteen and can repair anything with a motor.

I like that.

Last night I invited him over for dinner and served pasta that had somehow become glue. He ate it, then admitted it was terrible.

We ordered Thai food. Afterward he kissed me in the kitchen while the sink dripped and I was still wearing an apron.

I don't want to examine this too closely. I'd like to enjoy it.

------------------------------

ENTRY 255 — 2015-04-16 — No Formula

Ruth asked me to explain the confidence interval on a forecast to a manager who doesn't enjoy mathematics. I started with notation. The manager looked trapped.

I tried again with an example about rain and umbrellas. He immediately understood.

Later Ruth told me that explanation is not the act of showing someone what you know; it's the act of helping them know something.

I wrote that down. Then I drew a little umbrella in the margin because apparently I am twelve years old.

------------------------------

ENTRY 256 — 2015-05-23 — Two Families

Ben came to dinner in Redmond. Dad asked about city planning, Mom asked about Ben's family, and Andy tried to persuade him that every suburban neighborhood needs a better pedestrian bridge.

Ben took all of it seriously.

On the drive back he asked whether my parents were always that affectionate. I said not always, no.

He waited. I told him a little about Rebecca and the years afterward. He didn't say anything wise. He reached for my hand.

That was exactly enough.

------------------------------

ENTRY 257 — 2015-06-20 — A Problem with Money

Ben was annoyed when I offered to cover the hotel for a weekend away. He said he wanted to contribute.

I said we could split what made sense, but not if he expected us to choose somewhere worse merely so the amount was equal.

He said I was making the point of the trip about money. I said he had brought it up.

We were both right and both unpleasant about it.

We finally picked a small inn neither of us had heard of and paid for different parts. The pillows were dreadful. We had a wonderful time.

------------------------------

ENTRY 258 — 2015-07-18 — Miriam's Teacup

Went to Victoria with Mom. Grandma Miriam is slower on the stairs now, although she'll deny it if asked. We sorted photographs and recipes, and she found the chipped blue teacup from my childhood.

She asked whether I wanted it one day. I said I wanted her to keep making tea in it for a long time.

She laughed and said I was sentimental.

I said she was the person who taught me a cup could be a piece of history.

She said I should learn to wash one properly.

------------------------------

ENTRY 259 — 2015-08-08 — No Ceremony

Yesterday was the anniversary of the accident. Mom and Dad went to the cemetery. I went this morning instead.

I sat beside Rebecca and told her about Ben. I described the way he interrupts himself to sketch things on napkins and the way his hair sticks up when he's tired.

I didn't cry. I also didn't feel that I had failed to remember her properly.

There are days when I need to stand there a long time. Today I had things to do and went home after ten minutes. That's allowed.

------------------------------

ENTRY 260 — 2015-09-13 — The Building

Andy sent me a drawing of a library with a roof shaped like two overlapping wings. He's only sixteen and already talking about architecture schools.

Ben studied the drawing for a long time, then asked questions about structure and public space. Andy talked for forty minutes without stopping.

I loved watching them. It felt like two people from different parts of my life had discovered a door between their worlds.

Then Andy asked if I was going to marry Ben.

I nearly dropped my coffee.

------------------------------

ENTRY 261 — 2015-10-11 — Twenty-Seven

Twenty-seven today. Ben made breakfast and ruined the first batch of pancakes. Dad would have approved.

I spent the afternoon in Redmond with Mom, Dad, and Andy. They gave me a beautiful set of brushes and a book about the history of maps.

I thought about Rebecca in the morning. I thought about my work, Ben, painting, and what kind of life I might want in the evening.

I'm not sure there's a grand plan. Maybe that's fine.

I ate too much cake. That seems to be a permanent family tradition.

------------------------------

ENTRY 262 — 2015-12-27 — Too Much Noise

The holidays involved too many meals, too many people, and a spectacular argument at dinner about whether technology has made people less patient.

Dad defended programmers. Mom defended readers. Ben defended pedestrians. I defended the right to take a nap.

Maya arrived with a bottle of sparkling cider and announced she was abandoning music for three weeks to recover from music.

We ended up playing cards until midnight. I cheated once, badly, and was caught by Andy.

------------------------------

ENTRY 263 — 2016-01-19 — A Little Secret

Ben has a key to my apartment. That happened so gradually I can't remember when we decided it.

He keeps a spare shirt here and a mug he insists is better than all my mugs. I find it hideous.

I like hearing him moving around in the kitchen while I read. I also like when he goes home and I have the apartment to myself.

Both preferences seem possible. I wish everyone would stop talking as if love requires surrendering all solitude.

ENTRY 264 — 2016-02-26 — The Model Is Wrong

One of our pricing models behaved oddly after an update. A colleague wanted to smooth the results because the shape looked improbable.

Ruth asked us to find out why.

After two days we found a data-mapping error. The model was innocent, which is more than can be said for the spreadsheet.

I like days when the answer matters outside the page. I do not like meetings in which six people say "circle back" and not one person circles anywhere.

ENTRY 265 — 2016-03-20 — The Call from Israel

Tamar called from Haifa. Hanne has been ordering everyone around again and has decided I should visit this summer while she can still complain about my Hebrew in person.

Tamar says there will be room, which is a hilarious statement in a family with ten children and approximately a thousand descendants.

Ben said I should go. I asked if he wanted to come. He said he couldn't get the time off and didn't want to interfere with a family visit.

I was disappointed. I also think he's right.

ENTRY 266 — 2016-04-22 — Passover in Redmond

Passover at Mom and Dad's. Andy asked why we keep the family seder when each of us believes something a little different about God.

Dad said the family table belongs to all of us. Mom said the story is ours whether or not every explanation satisfies us.

Ben had a question about the Four Sons, and Grandpa Jakob launched into a forty-minute discussion that derailed dinner completely.

Grandma Miriam said, "Wonderful. The food is cold, but at least everyone is educated."

ENTRY 267 — 2016-05-14 — An Almost-Fight

Ben was late because a community meeting ran three hours over. He said he'd texted. I hadn't seen it.

I was annoyed and began reciting all the times he'd been late. He said I was assembling a prosecution. I said he'd supplied the evidence.

He left to get some air.

When he came back we talked about the thing underneath it: I sometimes feel I get what is left after his work has taken the good hours.

He said he sometimes feels I judge his work as less serious than mine.

Neither of us liked hearing that.

ENTRY 268 — 2016-06-04 — Another Ticket to Israel

Booked the flight. I'm going back to Israel, eight years after the first trip.

Hanne will be eighty-five. I want time with her while I can ask questions and hear her stories in her own strange order.

Mom sent a list of relatives to visit. I told her I'm going to see people, not complete a passport-stamping competition.

She said I was just like her mother.

I don't think she meant it as a compliment. I'm taking it as one.

ENTRY 269 — 2016-06-23 — Back in Haifa

The house was even louder than I remembered. Hanne came out onto the porch with a walking stick she appears to use mostly for pointing at people.

She looked me over and said, "You have become a proper woman. Are you still impossible?"

I said worse.

She laughed, then introduced me to three people who turned out to be cousins and two more who turned out to be their spouses.

I have made a family chart in my notebook. It is already wrong.

ENTRY 270 — 2016-06-26 — The Kitchen Rules

I put a plate in the wrong place and Tamar caught me. She explained the kitchen rules again. I asked her why the plates had to matter if the intention was sincere.

She said intention matters, but so does what you actually do.

I argued. She argued back. Hanne told us to stop talking and help cook.

Later Tamar and I sat outside eating apricots. She told me about her work and about a man she'd recently begun seeing. For ten minutes we didn't mention religion once.

ENTRY 271 — 2016-06-29 — The Photograph

Hanne brought out an album with pictures of her and Eli when they were young. In one he is grinning at the camera, and she looks as if she's about to challenge the photographer to a duel.

I asked how two people from such different Jewish backgrounds made a marriage work.

She said, "We made a thousand mistakes, and then we made dinner."

I laughed. She didn't.

Maybe she was being funny. Maybe she meant it exactly.

ENTRY 272 — 2016-07-02 — What She Won't Say

I asked Hanne about her military service and the years that followed. She answered some questions about 1967 and refused others about later intelligence work.

I said secrecy made history difficult. She said history wasn't entitled to every private act of a living person.

I wanted to object. I stopped.

Then she asked whether I was treating Ben properly. I told her she was changing the subject.

She said, "Of course. I'm very good at it."

We both laughed, and she gave me more tea.

ENTRY 273 — 2016-07-05 — Dinner at Rachel's

Had dinner with Hanne's daughter Rachel and half a dozen relatives. Everyone seemed to know something about someone else's future and had suggestions for improving it.

One cousin spoke passionately about religious Zionism. I asked questions and eventually disagreed about how religious conviction should shape public life. Nobody threw me out.

Later Rachel said I was welcome to argue, but I had to help clear the table too.

Family democracy, apparently, has a compulsory dishwashing component.

ENTRY 274 — 2016-07-09 — The Sea

Tamar and I went to look at the Mediterranean. She wanted to swim. I preferred sitting on a rock and watching the water.

She asked if the lake accident made swimming difficult. I told her I know the difference between water and drunken teenagers in a boat.

We talked about Becca. Tamar listened without trying to make the story meaningful.

Then we bought ice cream and she told me an outrageous story about one of her brothers.

I love how quickly life changes subjects.

ENTRY 275 — 2016-07-15 — Home Again

Back in Seattle. My suitcase smells faintly of spice and airport carpet. Hanne sent me away with food I wasn't allowed to refuse and a lecture about calling more often.

Ben met me at the airport. We kissed beside the baggage carousel and then argued about whether my suitcase would fit in his car.

I told him I missed him. I had.

That evening I unpacked the photographs and tried writing down some of Hanne's stories before I forgot their exact words.

I suspect the exact words will be the first thing I lose.

ENTRY 276 — 2016-08-07 — The Old Camera

Went to Redmond with Mom. We found an old camera in a cupboard and a little box of photographs taken before the accident. One showed Rebecca and me sprawled on a blanket making ridiculous faces.

We visited the cemetery afterward. For once neither of us tried to choose the correct mood for the day.

At dinner Dad asked about Israel. I told him Hanne had demanded to know whether his code had improved since 1988. He said probably not enough for her.

ENTRY 277 — 2016-09-19 — A Forecast with Teeth

Ruth asked me to review a model that projected fewer claims next year. The numbers looked beautiful. Too beautiful.

I found that we had quietly changed the definition of one category halfway through the data.

Fixing it made the forecast less attractive and more defensible. Nobody in the meeting looked grateful.

Afterward Ruth told me she was glad I'd found it. I said I wanted to go home and paint something without error bars.

She said she sometimes wanted that too.

ENTRY 278 — 2016-10-11 — Twenty-Eight

ENTRY 279 — 2016-11-23 — A Visit from Priya

Priya came from Chicago for a few days. We explored bookshops, talked about our old professors, and spent an evening trying to cook a dish neither of us understood.

ENTRY 280 — 2016-12-29 — The Car I Actually Want

ENTRY 291 — 2018-07-14 — The End of Ben and Me

Ben and I ended things today. He said he wanted a home and a future with someone who could say yes without feeling trapped. I said I loved him, but I couldn't honestly promise the future he needed.

He took his mug and his books from my apartment. I nearly told him to leave the mug. Then I realized I was bargaining over crockery because I couldn't bear the goodbye.

ENTRY 300 — 2019-12-31 — The Last Page of This Notebook

I found this diary under a pile of insurance notes and unread novels. Apparently I have become a person who writes about her life only when the pile becomes unbearable.

What happened this year? Work, painting, birthdays, a few dates, dinners with Maya, visits to Victoria, ridiculous calls with Tamar, long conversations with Dad.

Nothing that would make a grand documentary. Plenty I wouldn't trade away.

I don't know what the next year holds. I'm thirty-one, and I still have a thousand questions.

I hope I remember to write some of them down.
