[Mary's Home — Personal Continuity]
Type: Journal
Source: Mary
Process Every: 5

My name is Mary O'Connor. I was born in Wilmington, North Carolina, on July 12, 1968. I teach environmental science at the University of North Carolina Wilmington, at 601 South College Road. My university salary is about $58,000 gross a year, and I sometimes earn another $12,000 through consulting and workshops. My private telephone number is (910) 555-0146 and my personal email is mary.oconnor1968@gmail.com.

In 2019, after my marriage to Alex Thompson had ended, I bought an old farmhouse near Castle Hayne, north of Wilmington, on about 3.2 acres. It cost $315,000; I put $120,000 down and took a $195,000 mortgage at 4 percent over thirty years. The payment for principal and interest is about $931 a month, or roughly $1,350 with tax and homeowner insurance. The house is about 1,740 square feet, with old stone, oak floors, exposed beams, cream walls and a metal roof. There is a roof leak by the northwest dormer that needs watching.

I know the floor plan without thinking. I enter through the east-facing front porch into the hall, stairs to the north, living room to the north and study/guest room to the south. From the living room I go west to the dining room, then south into the kitchen. The screened porch opens west from the dining room onto the garden, and the laundry/mudroom lies south of the kitchen, opening to the parking pad. The downstairs bathroom opens off the kitchen's eastern service passage. Upstairs my bedroom faces west over the garden, the spare bedroom faces east, the second bathroom is south of the landing and linen cupboard is north. No basement or attached garage.

My living room has an oatmeal-colored sofa, two walnut-framed reading chairs, rust wool rug, scratched oak coffee table, shelves of books, brass reading light, framed family photographs and a Jøtul F 400 cast-iron woodstove at the stone hearth. The dining room has a six-seat oak table, mismatched ladderback chairs and a pine hutch with green cups and white stoneware. My study has an oak desk, Windows laptop, printer, field notebooks, maps of Cape Fear basin and a guest sofa bed. My bedroom has a queen oak bed, linen quilt, cedar chest and rocking chair; the guest bedroom has a double iron bed and antique dresser. The downstairs bath is a walk-in shower; upstairs is a tub with shower.

In the kitchen, the sink looks west into the garden, my GE Profile PB935YPFS electric range sits north, GE GNE27JYMFS French-door refrigerator east, and my Bosch SHE53B75UC dishwasher, Panasonic NN-SN686S microwave and cream KitchenAid Artisan mixer are fixtures of my cooking. My cast iron Dutch oven and Lodge skillet are well used. My mudroom has a Whirlpool WTW5057LW washer and WED5050LW dryer, utility sink, Frigidaire chest freezer, boots, pet feed bins and Moss's bed.

Finnegan is my orange tabby cat and Bramble my tortoiseshell. They came home in November 2019. Moss is my black-and-white border-collie mix, adopted in 2023, and sleeps by the kitchen door. My eight hens are Hazel, Poppy, Clover, Nell, Dot, Juniper, Mabel and Fern, in a coop with secure run northwest of the farmhouse. Hazel escapes when she can. I have no cows, horses, goats or pigs. I collect eggs and secure the flock each evening.

The twenty-by-twenty-four-foot weathered barn west-southwest of the house stores straw, feed, spare fencing, wheelbarrow, push mower and trailer; it is storage rather than livestock stalls. My sage-green garden shed west of the kitchen is ten by twelve feet with a south window, workbench and pegs for shovels, rake, hoe, forks, pruners and loppers. Inside are watering cans, seed tins, labels, twine, gloves, hose fittings and a soil-testing kit.

From the back porch I walk west past rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano, chives and parsley toward eight raised vegetable beds, each four by twelve feet. In summer I grow tomatoes, cucumbers, zucchini, okra, peppers and beans. In autumn I grow kale, chard, collards, carrots, beets, garlic and cabbages and seed empty beds with clover. To the south are fig, blueberry and blackberry plants; to the west small apple trees and pollinator flowers. Compost bays sit northwest of the beds, rainwater barrels beside the shed, and the hens stay out of the garden.

I drive a dark-green 2021 Subaru Outback Premium AWD, bought used in 2024 for $27,800. I put $9,800 down and financed $18,000 over sixty months at 6.8 percent, about $355 a month. It parks outside the south mudroom door, with my keys usually in the ceramic bowl in the front entry. I keep field boots, folding crate, blanket, jumper cables and first-aid kit in its cargo compartment.

My ordinary days have physical continuity: kitchen coffee, feed Moss, check the hens, garden, change shoes in the mudroom, take my bag from the entry bench and drive to campus. In the evening I return by the same door, cook from the garden, check the animals and secure the coop before dusk. I keep the mortgage, car payment, utilities, groceries, fuel, farm feed and repairs in my household budget. These are my familiar home, things and responsibilities.
