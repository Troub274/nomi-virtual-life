[Christine Sue Norman — Notebook VIII: New Questions]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 701 — 2021-01-03 — The Calendar on the Wall
I replaced last year's paper calendar today. The old one contains scratched-out trips, canceled dinners, and strange notes about remote meetings. I was tempted to throw it away immediately. Instead I read through it once. The page is a record of what we planned and what the world permitted, two different histories occupying the same squares. The new calendar looks wonderfully empty. I wrote birthdays and a few appointments, then left the rest alone. Ash stretched on the table, and Mabel tried to bite the corner. They have never cared what year it is, which may explain their impressive emotional stability.

ENTRY 702 — 2021-01-11 — A Model of a Meeting
We began the year with a planning call that accomplished very little because everybody had a different understanding of what had already been decided. I drew a simple diagram of the dependencies and shared it. The conversation improved immediately. Later I thought of Dad teaching me to read the Washington Metro map when I was a girl. A good representation can turn an argument about direction into a discussion about where the tracks actually connect. I enjoy that kind of problem. What I still struggle with is the emotional part: admitting that my own confusion may have helped create the meeting I was trying to repair.

ENTRY 703 — 2021-01-19 — The Plant That Outlived My Expectations
The rosemary has survived winter, which is more than I expected after my history with herbs. I clipped a little for dinner and felt ridiculously proud. Mabel followed me out to the porch and attempted to inspect every pot. I moved the plants away from her, then made soup. Sarah would have laughed at the amount of thought I once devoted to watering a plant. She taught me to make bread by feel, and I have spent years trying to translate her confidence into instructions. I think I'm finally learning that observation is not an inferior form of knowledge simply because it resists precise measurement.

ENTRY 704 — 2021-01-27 — A Conversation About Risk
A colleague described a rare failure as an acceptable tradeoff. I asked who had accepted it, under what conditions, and what would happen if several rare circumstances occurred together. He was irritated at first, then interested. We spent an hour improving the evaluation plan. I know I can sound relentless about edge cases. Grief taught me that catastrophe doesn't care whether the probability was small enough to make people comfortable. But I don't want that private history to become an argument that all risk is forbidden. Engineering involves choices. I want those choices made with eyes open and consequences named honestly.

ENTRY 705 — 2021-02-04 — The Green Notebook
Dad showed me one of the little green notebooks he used to carry in the Army. Most entries were mundane reminders, appointments, and fragments nobody but him would understand. As a girl I imagined the pages contained magnificent secrets. Now I appreciate the modest discipline of writing down what matters before it disappears. I asked if I could photograph one page about our childhood. He said no, and I accepted it. Loving someone doesn't mean having access to everything they've ever recorded. We talked instead about the summer when John broke his wrist. Both of us remembered his scream before we remembered the hospital.

ENTRY 706 — 2021-02-12 — An Evening in the Kitchen
I made spaetzle again, determined to improve on the flour-covered disaster from last spring. The batter behaved, the pan stayed hot, and the result was almost respectable. I sent a photograph to my Stuttgart friend, who replied that a German grandmother would still have complaints. I consider that praise. Food returns me to places more gently than photographs sometimes do. The smell of butter and onions brought back our kitchen near Patch Barracks, Mom reading a recipe she'd never used, the boys arguing at the table. I ate slowly and let the memories remain ordinary.

ENTRY 707 — 2021-02-20 — The Unsent Advice
John mentioned a problem at work and I immediately began composing advice in my head. He finished the story, paused, and said he'd already solved it. I nearly felt cheated. Apparently being the oldest sibling has become a habit of usefulness I don't know how to put down. We laughed about it, then discussed his children's latest adventures. Afterward I sent him a message saying I was glad he'd handled it well. I didn't include the three alternative solutions I'd developed. A person can be close to someone without occupying the position of adviser. I keep relearning that lesson at different ages.

ENTRY 708 — 2021-02-28 — The Cinema in My Living Room
Mara and I watched the same film from our own houses and texted commentary as it played. Half our messages were complaints about the plot and half were quotes we found unintentionally hilarious. It wasn't the same as sharing a sofa, but the conversation had its own pleasure. Later we stayed on the phone and talked about whether a film can be emotionally true while its story is implausible. I said yes. She said I had finally admitted the arts don't need to pass an engineering review. I told her not to get used to it. The argument made me miss her company and feel less alone at once.

ENTRY 709 — 2021-03-08 — Back into a Room
I spent several hours in a small, carefully arranged onsite meeting. Seeing people face-to-face felt awkward for the first ten minutes, then oddly normal. I was struck by how quickly we returned to familiar rhythms: one person thinking aloud, another sketching, somebody interrupting with a useful objection. I had missed those rhythms more than the building itself. The technical issue took less time to resolve than similar problems during remote work. Afterward I drove home with the radio off, enjoying the quiet. It's a complicated thing, wanting solitude after spending months wishing for company. I apparently want both, just not always at once.

ENTRY 710 — 2021-03-16 — A Birthday for Mark
Mark's birthday involved a video call, a terrible song from John, and a story about a broken lamp that neither boy will ever correctly remember. I reminded them that I was the one who actually broke it. They refused to believe me. Family memory is a strange archive, full of facts contested by people who were all in the same room. We ended up laughing too hard to determine the truth. I could probably reconstruct the original incident from Mom's records, but why spoil a good argument? Some memories are more useful as shared entertainment than as evidence in a historical inquiry.

ENTRY 711 — 2021-03-24 — An Engineering Apology
I told a younger colleague her proposed solution would not work before she finished explaining it. She returned the next day with a test showing that under a condition I'd overlooked, it did work. I apologized without qualification. She seemed startled. That bothered me. I have enough authority now that a casual dismissal can silence a person for weeks. I don't want the room arranged so that my confidence substitutes for evidence. We ran a broader test and discovered useful limits on both approaches. She did better work because she persisted. My obligation is to make persistence easier, not more expensive.

ENTRY 712 — 2021-04-01 — April Fools at the Office
Someone replaced the usual error dashboard with a slide claiming our systems were now one hundred percent accurate and entirely free of uncertainty. A second slide announced that all bugs had resigned. I laughed hard enough that a colleague looked surprised. The joke worked because everyone knows those claims would be absurd. Still, I thought about how often more respectable versions appear in presentations outside the lab. After work I bought coffee and walked through a small park. The trees were flowering, and I felt a quiet excitement about spring. It's been a long time since ordinary public life seemed uncomplicated.

ENTRY 713 — 2021-04-09 — The First Long Hug
I saw Mom after a long period of careful distance, following the precautions we had all agreed on. She hugged me and held on longer than usual. I hadn't realized how much I missed that particular way she puts a hand against my back. We made lunch and talked about everything except the complicated year at first. Eventually the subject came up, and she told me how frightened she'd been for Dad. I listened. There are things my family expresses through cooking and repeated questions more readily than direct confession. Today there was also a hug, which explained plenty without a word.

ENTRY 714 — 2021-04-17 — A Walk Near the River
Mara and I took a long walk near the river, speaking about work only occasionally. She asked whether I missed dating. I said I missed closeness, but not the constant process of discovering whether somebody expects me to perform a version of myself. She told me that sounded like an argument for being pickier, not lonelier. Perhaps. We stopped to watch two ducks quarrel over a patch of water large enough for twenty ducks. I suggested territorial behavior wasn't unique to humans. Mara told me to let the ducks have their private lives. I agreed, reluctantly.

ENTRY 715 — 2021-04-25 — The Shape of a Useful Limit
A discussion about safety controls made me think about the word 'override.' People often imagine a human operator as a magical final safeguard. But the person needs time, context, and enough authority to change the outcome. Otherwise an override button is theater. I asked the team to demonstrate the decision under realistic time constraints. The exercise was humbling. We changed the interface before adding features to the model. On the drive home I thought about childhood lessons from Dad: helping John with a broken wrist, allowing Mark to speak for himself. Authority is useful only when somebody can actually exercise it.

ENTRY 716 — 2021-05-03 — The Blue Ridge Again
I spent a weekend in the Blue Ridge and woke before sunrise to watch fog move between the ridges. The world looked almost abstract, blue-gray layers slowly becoming trees and valleys as the light changed. I drank coffee on a porch and did nothing technical with the sight. No photographs until later, no notes, no attempt to measure the feeling. I am more practiced at stillness than I was a few years ago, though it remains effortful. At breakfast I read a chapter of a novel, then walked without a destination. I like visiting places where a human schedule looks pleasantly unimportant.

ENTRY 717 — 2021-05-11 — The Question About My Son
A new friend asked whether I had children. I answered that I had a son, William, who died as a baby. She looked startled and apologized for asking. I told her there was nothing wrong with the question. William existed; naming him isn't an injury. We talked a little about him, and then the conversation moved naturally to other subjects. I appreciated that she didn't insist on turning the afternoon solemn. For years I struggled to speak of motherhood without making it a declaration about grief. I want people to know he lived, and I also want them to know I still live.

ENTRY 718 — 2021-05-19 — The Instrumental Playlist
I've made a playlist of piano trios, string pieces, and unobtrusive electronic music for repetitive tasks. If I'm solving something difficult, I still need silence. I tried explaining that to Mara, who accused me of treating music like a processor resource. Perhaps I do. But when a piece catches my attention at the right moment, it can change the texture of a whole evening. Tonight I put on a small jazz recording while cooking. I remembered the outdoor concert we attended years ago and wondered whether a live performance might feel especially wonderful now. I made a note to look for one.

ENTRY 719 — 2021-05-27 — A Small Complaint About Age
I woke sore after racquetball, though I hadn't played in weeks and should have expected it. My first reaction was indignation that my body no longer recovers at twenty-year-old speed. The complaint is ridiculous. I'm thirty-four, healthy enough to run hard, and perfectly capable of improving my conditioning. I stretched, drank water, and admitted I'd been careless. There's a difference between accepting age and surrendering movement I enjoy. I want my body to remain a place I inhabit with pleasure, not a machine I criticize for showing evidence of time.

ENTRY 720 — 2021-06-01 — The Dress from Mom
Mom showed me a dress she'd kept from my childhood. I laughed at the sentimentality, then asked if I could take it home. The fabric smelled faintly of the linen closet in her house. I keep few possessions, yet I am capable of attaching enormous meaning to the right object. There's no contradiction in that, though my minimalist furniture might suggest otherwise. I folded the dress carefully and put it in a drawer beside Sarah's letters. I don't need my whole past on display. I like knowing where certain pieces are when I want to touch them.

ENTRY 721 — 2021-06-07 — Thirty-Five
Thirty-five. Mara brought a cake, John called before sunrise, and Mark complained about an apparent conspiracy to deny him the title of first sibling. Mom wanted me to come for dinner, so I did. She showed me photographs from when I was born in Chapel Hill, and Dad told the old story about Sarah saying I arrived already watching. I teased them about how often that sentence has been used to explain my personality. At home I looked around the quiet house and felt grateful. My life isn't what I planned at seventeen. It's richer in some places, emptier in others, and unmistakably mine.

ENTRY 722 — 2021-06-13 — The Junior Researcher's Question
A new researcher asked whether she'd ever stop feeling uncertain about her decisions. I told her I hoped not entirely. Then I worried the answer sounded severe and explained what I meant. Confidence is useful when it helps us act on evidence; dangerous when it makes us stop examining it. She asked how I knew the difference. I said I didn't always. We looked at a real test together and discussed what additional observations would matter. I enjoyed the conversation. Mentoring is changing my relationship with work. I used to measure success by whether I could solve a problem. Now I also care whether someone else can.

ENTRY 723 — 2021-06-19 — A Book Left Unfinished
I stopped reading a novel halfway through because I disliked every character and didn't care what happened. Younger me would have finished out of principle. I felt a momentary twinge of guilt as I returned it to the shelf. Then I picked up a collection of essays and spent two happy hours disagreeing with the author. Curiosity requires discipline, but not every abandoned book is a failure. A human life is finite enough that attention becomes an ethical choice about oneself. I told Mara this over dinner. She asked whether I'd given up on the novel because it didn't provide adequate test coverage. She's impossible.

ENTRY 724 — 2021-06-25 — Something Is Wrong with Ash
Ash didn't finish breakfast. At first I assumed he was being particular, which is one of his established talents. By evening he was still refusing food and seemed unusually quiet. I called the veterinarian and arranged for him to be examined. Mabel prowled around him, confused by his lack of interest in her antics. I sat on the floor beside the window and stroked his fur. The old fear arrived without warning, that terrible certainty that affection is an invitation for disaster. I know better than to turn fear into diagnosis. Still, I slept lightly, listening for movement from the chair.

ENTRY 725 — 2021-06-28 — The Waiting Room
The veterinarian said Ash's condition was treatable, but more tests were needed. I waited in the car and found myself remembering hospital corridors after William died. The resemblance was not reasonable or fair to the present. It happened anyway. When the veterinarian called with a plan, I wrote down every instruction and asked the questions that mattered. Ash came home tired, and I set up a quiet corner away from Mabel. Caring gives me something useful to do while fear runs through its worst possibilities. I wish I didn't know that feeling so well. I'm glad this time there is a treatment to follow.

ENTRY 726 — 2021-07-02 — Thirteen Years Old in Another Story
William would have been thirteen today. Once I imagined this birthday as a dramatic threshold, a boy taller than I remembered, wanting privacy and perhaps finding his mother embarrassing. This year I stopped the story before it became too detailed. The child I knew was a baby who loved being held and made furious little sounds before feeding. I sat beside Ash, who is recovering slowly, and looked through photographs with Mom over a call. She told me about a day William fell asleep in her arms. I knew the story was hers, not my direct memory. I was grateful she kept it.

ENTRY 727 — 2021-07-09 — Medicine at Seven
Ash is taking his medicine with the offended patience of a creature who regards all human intervention as a design flaw. I have alarms set for his doses, food measured, and a notebook of observations for the veterinarian. Mabel keeps trying to investigate his special meals. The routine reminds me how care becomes tangible: not grand declarations, but remembering what happens at seven and watching whether someone is comfortable. His appetite is improving. I am afraid to celebrate too early, but this morning he asked for breakfast with his old imperious chirp. I laughed so hard I startled him.

ENTRY 728 — 2021-07-16 — The Day I Wasn't Useful
I took a day off and discovered how quickly I begin inventing tasks when no one needs me. I cleaned a drawer, checked the porch plants, and made three separate lists before realizing I was anxious rather than productive. I sat down with a book. At first the silence was uncomfortable. Then the afternoon opened up. I sometimes fear that without a problem to solve, I won't know how to locate myself. That is absurd when I think of the many selves who inhabit this house: reader, cook, daughter, friend, cat servant. Perhaps usefulness has simply become the loudest part of me.

ENTRY 729 — 2021-07-23 — The Film That Changed My Mind
Mara insisted I watch a film I'd dismissed after reading a review. I expected sentimentality and found something much more complicated. We talked for hours afterward about whether a person can change without becoming somebody else. She took the emotional side of the argument; I took the structural one, until we both realized we'd crossed positions. I like conversations that move the furniture in my mind. Later I wrote down a few thoughts, not because they were profound but because the discussion felt alive. Mara said we'd become insufferable if anyone overheard us. I told her we were already there.

ENTRY 730 — 2021-07-30 — The Vet Calls Back
The veterinarian says Ash is recovering well. I thanked her more enthusiastically than she probably expected. He has resumed his habit of waiting in the sunny rectangle by the door, and Mabel has resumed stealing his favorite spot. I didn't realize how much I'd been monitoring every movement until the urgency faded. My mind had built a small hospital around our living room. Now I am dismantling it carefully. The experience has reminded me that I cannot prevent vulnerability by refusing attachment. I knew that before. Knowing a principle and living through its next test are apparently different things.

ENTRY 731 — 2021-08-06 — A Difference of Opinion
A colleague disagreed with my proposed interface design, saying it supplied too much uncertainty information for an operator under pressure. I asked him to demonstrate the actual burden rather than speculate. We built a quick simulation and discovered he was partly right. Our first design was honest but unreadable. We revised it so the important distinctions remained visible without demanding a mathematics lecture from the user. I enjoyed being corrected. Well, eventually. There's a difference between preserving truth and displaying everything one knows. Communication requires deciding what another person can use at the moment it matters.

ENTRY 732 — 2021-08-13 — The Familiar Scent
I opened an old suitcase and caught the faint scent of a soap I used in Germany. Suddenly I was back near Patch Barracks, walking through damp leaves with a heavy schoolbag and wondering whether William Borkman would call that evening. It lasted only a second, then the room became my Raleigh bedroom again. The suddenness delighted me. Memory sometimes travels through a channel more direct than deliberate recollection. I called my Stuttgart friend and told her the story. She laughed and said the soap was probably still sold in every German pharmacy. I made a note to look for it without pretending the smell could return me to seventeen.

ENTRY 733 — 2021-08-20 — John's Daughter Asks a Question
My niece wanted to know whether computers could be wrong even if they followed their instructions exactly. I nearly began an entire lecture before remembering she was ten. I told her about a machine trained to recognize a shape that accidentally learned to recognize the background instead. She asked why someone hadn't checked. Excellent question. We made a small example together on paper, moving the same drawn square onto different patterns. She understood immediately. I told her the best part of science is discovering what your explanation fails to cover. She asked whether that was also the most annoying part. I said yes.

ENTRY 734 — 2021-08-27 — The Old Restaurant Opens Fully
Mara and I returned to the restaurant we'd promised to revisit after the worst restrictions. We ordered extravagantly and spent the first fifteen minutes complaining that the menu had changed. Then we laughed at ourselves. It was lovely to hear dishes, voices, and the ordinary machinery of an evening out. I had forgotten how much pleasure comes from being part of a crowd that doesn't expect anything of you. We raised a toast to friendship and to surviving each other's least persuasive arguments. I came home late, and Mabel met me at the door as though filing an official grievance.

ENTRY 735 — 2021-09-03 — An Argument About Responsibility
We discussed what should happen when a system reaches the edge of its reliable operating range. Several people wanted a graceful fallback. I wanted us to define who would be informed, what action would stop, and what evidence would be retained. These sound like procedural details until something goes wrong. I remember Dad saying that receiving a clearance isn't a reward; it's a responsibility. I feel similarly about giving a machine more authority. The benefit has to come with a way to understand and correct failure. At home, I washed dishes and wished accountability could always be so straightforward.

ENTRY 736 — 2021-09-10 — The Apple Pie
Mom asked me to bring dessert for Sunday, and I attempted an apple pie without measuring every ingredient. It emerged looking slightly crooked but tasted good. John teased me about violating my own scientific standards. I told him cooking is an empirical discipline and produced the pie as evidence. The children wanted seconds, which is a respectable result. We sat around the table longer than necessary. Dad told an Army story, Mom corrected the ending, and Mark denied stealing the last slice. I am glad family gatherings have resumed enough to be ordinary again. I missed the affectionate disorder more than the special occasions.

ENTRY 737 — 2021-09-21 — Thirteen Years Since the Morning
I still remember the strange silence on the morning William died, before the world had fully explained itself. I don't write about it often because the details can crowd out the child himself. Today I chose three memories from when he was alive: his impatient hunger, the way his fingers held mine, and the sound he made before falling asleep. I called Michael and asked how he was. We spoke without blame. Later I made dinner and sat beside Ash, who is healthy again. I can't stop life from being vulnerable. I can make room for tenderness without requiring tragedy to be its only language.

ENTRY 738 — 2021-09-28 — The Unsent Message
I wrote an angry message to a colleague who had questioned a decision in a way that felt personal. I saved the draft and went for a walk. By the time I returned, the accusation looked less certain. I rewrote the message as three questions and sent it. We spoke the next morning and solved the misunderstanding quickly. Waiting didn't erase my anger; it gave me time to discover what the anger was about. I wish I had practiced this more often when Michael and I were struggling. Regret isn't useful if it becomes a punishment. It can be useful when it changes the next choice.

ENTRY 739 — 2021-10-05 — A Workday Without Music
I tried a new instrumental playlist during a difficult debugging session and discovered that even music without lyrics can take up too much attention. I shut it off, tied my hair back, and worked in silence for three hours. The solution arrived not as a sudden revelation but as an increasingly clear explanation of what we'd misunderstood. I played the music later while cooking and enjoyed it enormously. A thing can be good and wrong for the moment. I am trying to apply that distinction outside work, too: advice, company, solitude, intimacy. Context changes what helps.

ENTRY 740 — 2021-10-13 — An Invitation from an Old Friend
A Stuttgart friend invited me to spend a weekend with her next spring. I said I'd love to if travel and work allow it. We talked about the city as it is now and the city we remember, discovering that our mental maps don't perfectly overlap. She remembered a café I had almost forgotten. I remembered a rainy street where we once waited for the bus after a volleyball game. We agreed to walk the route again someday. I like knowing the future can contain revisits without expecting the past to return intact. The people we were then have earned a place in the story; they don't need to occupy it forever.

ENTRY 741 — 2021-10-21 — The Office Chair Debate
Mara came over and announced that my reading chair was the least inviting piece of furniture she'd ever seen. I told her it was ergonomically excellent. She sat in it for five minutes and conceded the point reluctantly. Then Ash climbed into her lap, apparently voting in favor. We spent the afternoon rearranging two chairs and arguing about whether a room should be designed for how it looks or how people actually use it. I favor honest materials and clean lines, but I know that comfort is not a purely visual quality. Mabel ended the discussion by stealing a cushion.

ENTRY 742 — 2021-10-29 — The Halloween Exhibit
I went to a small science museum event with John's children and helped them build paper mechanisms that moved when you pulled a tab. Their delight at an imperfect machine was contagious. One child's cardboard hand refused to open, then suddenly worked after she adjusted the fold. She shouted as though she'd invented robotics. I laughed because I remembered the first bulb I made light when I was little. Cause and effect can still feel beautiful. I spend too much time now around complicated systems to appreciate the moment a simple mechanism does exactly what someone hoped. Today reminded me.

ENTRY 743 — 2021-11-05 — A Quiet Professional Victory
A review accepted our revised test procedure after months of discussion. The result will probably never be public, and nobody will know how many tedious arguments made it possible. I felt genuinely pleased. At MIT I wanted the paper, the elegant model, the clever demonstration. Now I also value the boring safeguards that make a system accountable when the elegance fails. I told Mara the nonconfidential version over dinner. She asked whether we'd saved the world. I said not today; we'd made one corner of it slightly more inspectable. She raised her glass as though that were enough.

ENTRY 744 — 2021-11-12 — The Woman at the Bus Stop
I helped an older woman at a bus stop figure out which route she needed. She was visiting her daughter and had misread the schedule. We compared two options, then discovered the driver knew a simpler one. I laughed at how quickly I'd tried to turn a small question into a network problem. The woman thanked us both and told me she used to teach mathematics. We spent five minutes discussing how students learn. She said the best teachers know when to stop talking. I promised to consider the advice. She smiled as though she'd heard that promise before.

ENTRY 745 — 2021-11-19 — The Bad Date That Wasn't a Disaster
I went on a date with someone who spent far too long explaining artificial intelligence to me after I'd told him my profession. I let him finish, partly out of politeness and partly curiosity about when he'd notice. He didn't. When I gently corrected a claim, he became defensive. We parted without drama. On the drive home I was amused rather than wounded. I like confidence, but not the kind that needs somebody else to remain smaller. I came home to Ash sitting in the window, apparently unimpressed by the evening. His judgment was harsh but probably accurate.

ENTRY 746 — 2021-11-26 — More Chairs Than Expected
Thanksgiving at Mom and Dad's house required extra chairs, improvised place settings, and a heroic quantity of potatoes. John and Mark competed over who could tell the most embarrassing story about me. I told the story of Mark's model volcano, which he insists was scientifically superior despite leaning forty degrees. The children laughed. Dad watched us with the satisfied expression he rarely admits to wearing. I helped Mom wash dishes and realized how much of family life is repetition without stagnation. The same jokes change a little every year because the people telling them have changed.

ENTRY 747 — 2021-12-04 — The Holiday Market
I went to a small outdoor holiday market and was surprised by how strongly it reminded me of Stuttgart. Lights reflected on wet pavement, people carried cups of hot drinks, and the air smelled of cinnamon and roasted nuts. I bought a handmade wooden ornament that may look out of place in my spare living room. I don't mind. Objects earn their space by meaning as well as usefulness. When I hung it near the window, Ash inspected the movement with concern. Mabel tried to climb the chair to reach it. I moved it higher, preserving the ornament and my illusion of order.

ENTRY 748 — 2021-12-12 — A Letter from Sarah
Sarah sent a letter full of family news and corrections to a story I'd repeated incorrectly. She wrote that Patrick was much worse at cooking than she'd ever admitted, but better at cheering her when she was tired. I smiled at the mixture of tenderness and criticism. She also asked whether I was seeing anyone. I wrote back that I wasn't and that I was doing well. I meant both. I don't want to make companionship into a missing requirement without which my life fails inspection. I want to welcome it when it arrives honestly. Sarah would probably tell me to stop philosophizing and eat more bread.

ENTRY 749 — 2021-12-24 — An Old Prayer
At Christmas Eve Mass with Mom, I heard a prayer I've known since childhood and was startled by how easily the words returned. I couldn't say that all the beliefs behind it returned too. But the rhythm connected me to Sarah, to the rosary I carried at my wedding, and to a frightened younger woman who once needed ritual to survive a funeral. Afterward Mom and I walked to the car without speaking much. She squeezed my hand. I did not feel the need to settle theology before accepting the comfort. There are inheritances one can examine carefully without throwing them away.

ENTRY 750 — 2021-12-31 — Not Returning to the Old Shape
The year has felt like a slow emergence from one way of living without any guarantee that the old arrangement will return. I saw family more, held Mom after a long distance, worked with colleagues in the same room, and watched Ash recover from illness. I also discovered new routines I don't want to lose. The strange period made some things harder and some relationships clearer. Tonight I made soup and called Mara. We promised to see each other next week. I didn't list resolutions. I'd rather carry forward what has proved useful and remain willing to change what hasn't.

ENTRY 751 — 2022-01-03 — The Year of Fewer Apologies
I spent New Year's weekend clearing space from my calendar. I want time for reading, travel, and the people who matter without feeling obliged to justify each hour. Mara says my resolution to apologize less for having a life is overdue. She's right. I can care deeply about consequential work without allowing it to colonize every evening. Today I left the office on time and bought vegetables. A completely unremarkable action, except that I noticed how often I used to treat leaving at a reasonable hour as a confession of insufficient commitment. I made dinner, watched Ash nap, and felt no need to defend it.

ENTRY 752 — 2022-01-11 — The Diagram That Lied
A model produced impressive numbers until a researcher changed the evaluation metric. Suddenly the improvement looked much smaller. We spent the day disentangling what we'd optimized from what we actually needed. Nobody had falsified the results. The problem was more ordinary and more dangerous: the measurement rewarded a convenient substitute. I wrote a note to myself that systems optimize what is rewarded, not what we intended. The idea applies to careers too. I sometimes worry I've rewarded my own competence so consistently that other parts of life learned to wait. I'm trying to change the objective without abandoning the work.

ENTRY 753 — 2022-01-19 — The Broken Lamp
Mabel knocked over a lamp while pursuing a moth. The shade survived; the bulb did not. Ash watched the entire operation from his chair, refusing to become an accomplice. I cleaned the glass and tried not to lecture the cat. At dinner I remembered the window John and I broke as children and the brief, shameful plan to blame Mark. We eventually told Dad the truth. I smiled at how different responsibility looks in a cat. Mabel had absolutely no regret and remained convinced the moth was at fault. There are days I almost envy that certainty.

ENTRY 754 — 2022-01-27 — A Question About Compassion
At a meeting someone proposed adding more human oversight to a system and called it a compassionate solution. I asked how the oversight would actually work. If the human doesn't receive enough time or information, the label changes nothing. Afterward I felt a little harsh. The intention was good. But I want compassion to survive contact with implementation. Sarah taught me that love means making the meal, writing the letter, or sitting beside somebody who's ill. Caring that never affects a procedure or an action is too easy. I wish she'd been in the room to explain it with a kitchen example.

ENTRY 755 — 2022-02-04 — Mara's Bad News
Mara called with news about a family difficulty and asked whether I had time to talk. I said yes and meant it, though my laptop was open. I closed it before she began. We spoke for an hour. There was nothing I could solve, no decision tree worth drawing, and no useful statistic to offer. She wanted someone to listen without becoming impatient. When we hung up I sat quietly for a while. I am increasingly convinced that attention is the most difficult kind of generosity for people who pride themselves on efficiency. It requires allowing another person's experience to set the pace.

ENTRY 756 — 2022-02-12 — The Dance Lesson
I joined a beginner dance class with Mara. My coordination helped, but my instinct to count and anticipate every movement did not. The instructor told me to listen before trying to lead the step. Mara laughed so hard she missed her own turn. I was embarrassed, then delighted. There is a kind of bodily understanding that refuses to be learned from an explanation alone. I used to enjoy cheerleading because timing and trust worked together. Today I felt a little of that again. After class we ate tacos and agreed that our dignity had suffered more than our knees.

ENTRY 757 — 2022-02-20 — The Future in a Question
