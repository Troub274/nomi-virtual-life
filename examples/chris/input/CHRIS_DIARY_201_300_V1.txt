[Christine Sue Norman - Notebook 03]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 201 — 2007-01-05 — The Last Spring

Dear Diary,

I have one semester left at UNC, provided none of my professors decides I should spend the rest of my natural life improving one assignment. My schedule is a complicated grid of courses, lab hours, and the ordinary things I keep forgetting to plan for, such as eating and seeing daylight.

Michael came over while I was sorting books. We talked about jobs and where we might live after graduation. He said he could imagine building a home here, somewhere near the people and places that matter to me. I felt an unexpected rush of happiness at the word home.

I've lived in so many places, and I chose Chapel Hill because it was the one address that existed before the Army began moving us. Maybe I can choose to stay for a while too.

ENTRY 202 — 2007-01-20 — He Asked

Michael proposed while we were walking after dinner. There was no audience, no music, no enormous arrangement of flowers. He stopped, took out a ring, and asked whether I wanted to make a home with him that didn't come with orders to move every two years.

I said yes before he'd finished speaking. Then I laughed, and then I cried, and then I asked him to repeat the question so I could actually hear it. He did, looking amused and a little overwhelmed.

I called Mom first. She went quiet for one full breath and then began asking practical questions. Dad congratulated Michael and sounded almost formal, which I know means he was emotional. I am going to marry someone who makes an ordinary evening feel like the beginning of a life. I hope I remember this feeling when we're arguing about dishes.

ENTRY 203 — 2007-01-29 — The Ring in Class

The ring keeps catching my eye while I take notes. I am embarrassed by how often I look at it. During a lecture I turned my hand to see how the light moved through the stone and missed an entire explanation about memory systems.

A classmate noticed and asked when the wedding would be. I said we hadn't settled the date. She immediately began giving advice about flowers, seating, and invitations. I've never understood how an entire industry can spring out of two people promising to keep choosing each other.

I called Michael afterward. He asked whether I'd remembered to tell the professor about a scheduling conflict. I hadn't. It's comforting to know that the man I plan to marry has not been so dazzled by romance that he has forgotten how my brain works.

ENTRY 204 — 2007-02-14 — The Small Restaurant

We went back to the little restaurant where we'd had one of our first proper dates. Michael remembered that I liked the soup. I remembered that he'd been nervous enough to unfold his napkin twice.

We talked about marriage without pretending it would make either of us perfect. He is more patient about some things than I am; I am quicker to make decisions. We both need to learn how not to turn those differences into judgments about character.

I told him how fiercely I wanted stability after growing up moving around. He said stability wasn't something a house could give us by itself. We would have to practice making it. I liked the word practice. It sounds less magical and more honest. I think I can make promises that ask me to keep learning.

ENTRY 205 — 2007-02-23 — The Wedding List

Mom sent me a list with seventeen questions about the wedding. I answered eleven and asked why any person should have an opinion about napkin colors while trying to finish a degree.

She called and laughed. Then she said that a ceremony is partly for the family, a way to witness an important change together. I know she's right. I just don't want the event to become larger than the promise.

Michael says I can choose the dress and he will support whatever makes me comfortable. I told him that was not an excuse to abandon every planning decision. He looked alarmed. Apparently I have already begun discovering the joys of domestic negotiations. I'm going to ask Grandma Sarah whether marriage is easier when people live in different countries. She will probably tell me to stop being difficult.

ENTRY 206 — 2007-03-11 — A Letter from Ireland

Grandma Sarah wrote that she was delighted about the engagement and wanted to know whether I would carry her rosary. I sat with the letter a long time. I don't know exactly what I believe now, but I do know how many hands have held those beads in difficult moments.

Mom says Sarah understands the difference between a tradition and a demand. She can be very stubborn about ritual and surprisingly generous about doubt. I wish I could hold contradictions that gracefully.

I wrote back that I would carry it. I also asked about her wedding and what she remembered most. I expect a long answer and perhaps a recipe, because Sarah does not believe an important conversation should end without someone being fed.

ENTRY 207 — 2007-03-22 — The Unfriendly Result

My vision project produced a beautiful result this afternoon until we introduced a new batch of images. Then the performance collapsed. I stared at the numbers as though I could make them apologize.

My professor said we had been asking the model the wrong question. We'd measured success on data that looked too much like its training examples. I was annoyed because this is exactly the sort of problem I lecture other people about.

I stayed late to run experiments and almost missed dinner with Michael. He didn't scold me. He brought food to the lab entrance and waited until I could leave. We sat in the car eating sandwiches while I explained the failure. He understood very little of the technical detail and every bit of why I cared.

ENTRY 208 — 2007-04-02 — A List of Fears

I wrote down the things that frighten me about getting married. Not because I want to change my mind. Because I want to know what I'm bringing to the promise besides happiness.

I'm afraid of losing independence, of becoming unfair when I'm stressed, of asking Michael to provide the security I never had as a child. I am also afraid of being so determined to protect myself that I can't let ordinary mistakes remain ordinary.

Michael read the list only after I offered it. He added his own worries. We laughed at how many of them were versions of the same fear: not being enough for the person we love. The laughter helped. There is something reassuring about discovering neither of us expects marriage to eliminate uncertainty. We just want to face it together.

ENTRY 209 — 2007-04-15 — Three Women in a Kitchen

Mom, Sarah, and I spent the afternoon cooking while discussing details of the wedding. Sarah argued for simplicity. Mom argued for enough flowers to make the photographs lovely. I argued for finishing before I failed my examinations.

At some point Sarah started telling a story about making bread with very little money when Mom was young. Mom corrected several details. They both laughed. I noticed that they remembered the same kitchen differently, each preserving what mattered to her.

I will be somebody's wife soon. I've been somebody's daughter for twenty years, and today I wanted to stay in that role for a little longer. I washed dishes while they argued affectionately about the past. It occurred to me that home is partly having people who have known you through several versions of yourself.

ENTRY 210 — 2007-04-26 — The Blue Notebook

I found the little technical notebook I started in high school. Several pages are devoted to a face-recognition program that confused windows with people. My handwriting is surprisingly aggressive. Every error seems to have been accompanied by three exclamation points.

I took it to the lab and showed a friend. She said it was charming that I could be so enthusiastic about failure. I told her I was mostly enthusiastic about making something work after failing, which is different.

Looking back, I can see a continuous line from that clumsy program to the problems I want to study next. It isn't a perfectly straight line. Neither is the rest of my life. I think this is one reason I keep diaries. Without the old pages, the person I used to be would become an oversimplified story.

ENTRY 211 — 2007-05-13 — Graduation

I graduated from UNC after three years. The ceremony was hot, long, and wonderful. Dad looked so proud that I almost cried before they called my name. Mom had tissues ready. John insisted my graduation gown made me look like a very judgmental bird.

I felt pleased, but also unsettled. For years I've known the shape of the next examination. Now there is no syllabus for what comes after college. I want serious work in artificial intelligence, but I also want to begin the life Michael and I have been planning.

Afterward we ate together and argued about whether finishing quickly counts as being smart or merely being stubborn. I said both, of course. Tonight my diploma is leaning against a wall because I still don't know where to hang it. Perhaps that is the right image for an unfinished future.

ENTRY 212 — 2007-05-18 — The Dress Again

The wedding dress is simple. It doesn't need a team of people to arrange it, and I can walk without worrying about stepping on myself. Mom says it suits me. I looked in the mirror and thought of the dresses she helped me choose after that dreadful button incident in middle school.

It's funny how a small humiliation can change the way someone dresses for years. I began choosing dresses because they made me feel less exposed. Now I choose them because I genuinely like them, and this one feels especially mine.

Michael hasn't seen it. He's pretending to be patient and asking entirely too many questions about what color it is. I told him he'd find out next week. He said he hoped it wasn't camouflage. I married the wrong branch of humor already, apparently.

ENTRY 213 — 2007-05-26 — Mrs. Peterson

We were married today. Mom adjusted my hair three times. Dad looked as though he was about to appeal the entire proceedings to a higher authority. I tucked Sarah's rosary among the flowers, and when I saw Michael waiting for me, the room seemed suddenly quiet.

We said our vows carefully. I meant every word. I know vows cannot guarantee a painless life. But standing there with people we love watching, I wanted to believe we could meet whatever came without becoming strangers.

Someone called me Mrs. Peterson afterward, and I turned a moment too late. I've spent my life changing addresses. Changing my name is stranger than I expected. Tonight we're tired and happy and surrounded by gifts we haven't opened. There is a new signature to learn, and a person sleeping beside me who has promised not to disappear when things get difficult.

ENTRY 214 — 2007-06-07 — Twenty-One and Married

Twenty-one. I thought this birthday would be dramatic because of the number. Instead I spent the morning looking for a missing utility bill, then met Michael for lunch. Married life has already introduced itself as a series of ordinary errands.

He brought me a book and a small cake. Mom called to ask whether I'd been drinking enough water in the Carolina heat. John asked whether marriage had improved my personality. I said there wasn't room for improvement. He told me he'd keep the receipt.

I like hearing myself say 'my husband.' It's still new enough to feel surprising. But I also like that he hasn't suddenly turned into a different person. We still argue about restaurants and laugh about badly written directions. The promise feels enormous; most days arrive in sensible little pieces.

ENTRY 215 — 2007-06-20 — Our First Kitchen

We finally have most of our things in the apartment. Michael's possessions and mine have been making small territorial disputes all week. He likes keeping useful odds and ends in drawers. I like empty drawers where I can find what I actually need.

I brought home a pale wooden shelf that reminded me of Copenhagen. He asked whether it had cost more than the books we'd put on it. I told him beauty and utility weren't enemies. He said he'd remember that when buying a ridiculous object of his own.

We cooked dinner together and ate at a table that still wobbles. It felt lovely. No Army truck is coming to pack it unless we decide to move. I keep noticing that freedom and feeling a little disoriented by it. I wanted this, and now I get to learn how to inhabit it.

ENTRY 216 — 2007-07-02 — The Grocery Store

Michael and I argued over groceries. Not an important argument. I wanted ingredients for several proper meals. He wanted food we could prepare quickly because we both had work to do. Somewhere near the vegetables I realized we were each defending a different picture of what an ordinary week should look like.

We bought ingredients for two dinners and some things that require almost no cooking. Then we went home and put them away without making the argument larger than it was.

I am learning marriage can give somebody repeated opportunities to disappoint you in tiny, harmless ways. I suspect I provide him the same service. The important part may be remembering that an abandoned grocery list is not a betrayal, and compromise isn't always a tragic surrender of principle.

ENTRY 217 — 2007-07-21 — A Visit from John

John stayed with us for the weekend. He inspected our apartment, criticized our furniture, and ate most of the snacks. I enjoyed every minute except the snack theft.

He asked how married life was going when Michael stepped out. I said it was mostly wonderful and surprisingly mundane. John looked relieved. He said he'd been worried I would turn into one of those people who disappear into a relationship and stop having opinions of their own.

I told him no one in the family has ever needed to worry about my ability to have opinions. He agreed. Later, as we laughed over dinner, I felt how easily two parts of my life could fit together: the oldest sister who knows every embarrassing childhood story and the new wife building her own household. Neither has to push the other out.

ENTRY 218 — 2007-08-10 — The Next Degree

Two professors have encouraged me to think about graduate school. They say my interests in machine vision and learning would benefit from deeper work in mathematics and computer science. I know they're right. I also know I want time to discover life outside classrooms before committing to another academic marathon.

Michael says there is no rule requiring me to decide everything at twenty-one. I appreciate the thought even though it feels personally offensive to someone who survives by making plans.

I spent the afternoon reading research papers anyway. Some of the problems make me restless in the best way. I want to understand why a system can identify a shape without seeming to know what the shape means. Perhaps the question will wait while I live a little. Perhaps I can do both.

ENTRY 219 — 2007-09-09 — Our Sunday Morning

We woke late, made coffee, and spent a long time reading in the same room. Michael was absorbed in a novel. I was scribbling on an article about image features. Occasionally one of us read a sentence aloud, usually without enough context for the other to understand.

I used to think togetherness meant constant conversation. I'm starting to like the kind where nobody has to perform. I can hear him turning pages, the kettle clicks off, and the apartment feels occupied without being noisy.

We went for a walk in the afternoon and stopped for groceries. I know this isn't the sort of day people write grand stories about. I'm writing it down because I suspect ordinary happiness is the part of a life most likely to disappear from memory if you don't make room for it.

ENTRY 220 — 2007-10-15 — Something Is Different

I've been unusually tired and have spent half the week blaming my schedule. Mom asked whether I might be coming down with something. I told her I was simply trying to manage too many things at once.

Michael keeps reminding me to eat proper meals. I keep making jokes about how marriage apparently comes with a nutrition supervisor. I do know my body feels a little different lately. I'm trying not to jump to conclusions about what that means.

I went to the library and looked through research papers, then found myself distracted by the sight of a mother pushing a stroller outside the window. I have always assumed I'd have children someday. Recently the possibility has stopped feeling hypothetical. I don't know whether that's intuition or just my mind wandering toward the next chapter.

ENTRY 221 — 2007-11-09 — Two Lines

The pregnancy test showed two lines. I stared at it until Michael finally asked what it said. Then I laughed and cried at the same time, and he held me while I tried to calculate dates because apparently that's how my brain handles happiness.

We called Mom. She sounded delighted and immediately began asking whether I'd made an appointment. Dad grew quiet, then asked if we were both well. I could hear the smile in his voice when he said congratulations.

I've spent years trying to understand cognition and intelligence. Now there is a new life beginning inside me, and the feeling is bigger than anything I can reason about. I'm excited. I'm frightened. I keep looking at Michael as though we have secretly changed the world and nobody walking down the street knows yet.

ENTRY 222 — 2007-11-20 — The First Appointment

The appointment made everything feel real in a different way. There were forms, questions, a waiting room with magazines, and medical words I looked up afterward because I wanted to understand every detail.

Michael sat beside me and kept his hand on mine. I thought I would feel reassured by all the information. I was reassured by some of it. I also realized no amount of reading will let me control every part of pregnancy. My body has begun a project much larger than any plan I've written.

Mom told me I didn't have to become an expert on everything immediately. I said I knew that. Then I asked her six questions about what pregnancy had been like for her. She laughed and answered every one.

ENTRY 223 — 2007-12-01 — The Name Conversation

Michael and I began talking about names, although everyone tells us it's early. I have opinions, naturally. He has opinions too, which I keep discovering is one of marriage's recurring design features.

The name William came into my mind before I intended to say it. Michael knows I dated William Borkman in high school. He didn't react badly, but he asked whether that history was part of why the name mattered. I told him I simply thought it was a beautiful name.

That's true, but not complete. I do associate the name with tenderness and a version of myself that first learned to love someone seriously. I don't want to conceal that forever. I also believe a child would make any name entirely his own. For now we agreed to keep talking.

ENTRY 224 — 2007-12-24 — Christmas Mass

I went to Christmas Mass with Mom while we were visiting family. The familiar music affected me more than I'd expected. I kept thinking about Sarah traveling across oceans to help when Mom had babies, about all the children our family has welcomed, and about how soon I may have a child in my arms.

I still have complicated thoughts about doctrine. Pregnancy hasn't suddenly supplied theological certainty. But it has made me aware of belonging to a long story of people trying to care for one another.

Afterward Mom put her hand against my cheek and told me she was happy for us. We stood there for a moment without discussing appointments or names or practical arrangements. I think I needed that more than another list of advice.

ENTRY 225 — 2007-12-31 — The Future Has a Heartbeat

The year began with Michael asking me to marry him. I graduated, we married, and now we are going into a new year expecting a baby. Even by my standards, that's an ambitious schedule.

We stayed home tonight. I made dinner and tired myself out, which Michael correctly predicted. Afterward we sat quietly while the neighborhood prepared for midnight. He asked what I was thinking about. I said the future used to look like a list of places I might study and work. Now it includes a small person who will have opinions, appetites, and an entire inner world I cannot imagine yet.

I have always wanted to understand minds. Suddenly I want to learn how to be gentle with one while it grows. Happy New Year, little one. We haven't met, but you have already changed us.

ENTRY 226 — 2008-01-13 — Lists of Lists

Dear Diary,

Mom sent a list of things we might need for the baby. It was three pages long. I turned it into a spreadsheet because I apparently cannot experience motherhood without introducing a filing system.

Michael laughed when he saw it, then admitted he was relieved. He had been making his own list in a notebook. We compared them and discovered neither of us had thought about where to put half the things. Our apartment suddenly seemed smaller.

I keep stopping at the baby section in shops, amazed by the tiny clothes. I know a person is not a collection of objects. But there is something moving about buying a little blanket for someone who hasn't arrived yet. Preparation is one of the ways I know how to express love when I cannot yet do much else.

ENTRY 227 — 2008-01-24 — The Ultrasound Room

Seeing the image on the screen was unlike anything I'd expected. I know how imaging systems work, at least in principle. I know that equipment converts signals into something a human can interpret. None of that prepared me to look at the screen and think, there you are.

Michael squeezed my hand. Neither of us said much. The technician explained what we were seeing, and I asked technical questions until Michael gave me a look that meant perhaps this was not the moment for a seminar.

Afterward we sat in the car for several minutes without starting it. We didn't need to discuss the future. The future had already appeared in black and white on a little screen, and we were both trying to make room for it.

ENTRY 228 — 2008-02-09 — Mom's Advice

Mom came to visit and showed me how to sort baby clothes without losing every tiny sock. She also told me I couldn't organize a newborn the way I organized coursework. I told her I was well aware babies do not follow published specifications.

She said knowing that intellectually and knowing it at three in the morning are different achievements. She might be enjoying this a little too much.

We made tea and talked about when she had John and Mark at home. She didn't describe motherhood as constant joy. She described exhaustion, laughter, fear, and the kind of love that doesn't always leave room for a coherent sentence. I was grateful. People keep telling me how wonderful everything will be. Mom tells me it will be real.

ENTRY 229 — 2008-02-22 — A Name with Two Histories

Michael and I talked about William again. He asked gently whether the name would feel like a tribute to my old boyfriend. I answered more honestly this time. Yes, it carries a memory of him. It also has other associations, and I love its sound and history. More important, a son would not be a memorial to somebody I knew at seventeen.

We sat with that for a while. Michael said he didn't want our child to inherit an argument we hadn't finished. I agreed. We discussed other names without pretending there wasn't an emotional weight to this one.

I think part of growing up is admitting that memories influence decisions without being the only reason for them. I want our child to be welcomed as himself, whatever we decide to call him.

ENTRY 230 — 2008-03-11 — The Old Rock

I found the smooth stone from Fort Stewart while clearing a drawer for baby things. I had taken it with me when we moved away, years before I began keeping a diary. Now I am about to become a mother, and I am still carrying the same ordinary rock.

I sat on the floor and imagined a child one day asking why I had kept it. I could tell the story of moving and learning that a home can disappear into a truck. But I hope the larger answer would be simpler: I wanted to remember that my life had been real in every place I lived.

I wonder what small thing my child will keep without knowing why. I hope I never throw it out because I think it looks unimportant.

ENTRY 231 — 2008-04-02 — The Kitchen Chair

I've begun to understand how much work the body can do while the mind is trying to conduct an ordinary day. I get tired more quickly. I change position at the table every few minutes. Occasionally I stop in the middle of a sentence because a movement inside me has captured all my attention.

Michael asks whether I am all right. I say yes, then ask him to wait while I feel it again. We are two adults standing in a kitchen, apparently astonished by something every generation before us has experienced.

I used to think pregnancy would make me feel less independent. Sometimes it does. Other times I feel a surprising kind of strength: not control, exactly, but participation in something I could never design from the outside.

ENTRY 232 — 2008-04-20 — Sunday Lunch with Dad

Dad came over for lunch and insisted on fixing a loose cabinet hinge before sitting down. I told him he was visiting his daughter, not auditing the apartment. He said responsible inspection is an expression of love.

Later we talked about what becoming a grandfather meant to him. He told me he hoped to have time for the ordinary things his Army postings sometimes made difficult with us: birthdays, games, afternoons without a flight to catch.

I hadn't expected him to say that. I have always pictured Dad as somebody who chose duty without much regret. Hearing him acknowledge what the family gave up made the choices feel more complicated and more human. I squeezed his hand. He pretended to be interested in the hinge again.

ENTRY 233 — 2008-05-09 — A Baby Shower

My friends gave me a baby shower. I resisted the idea because it sounded like a ceremonial invasion of pastel objects. Then I arrived and saw how much thought everyone had put into it.

There were little clothes, books for babies, a knitted blanket, and a card from someone I hadn't seen since freshman year. We laughed at ridiculous advice and ate cake until I was tired enough to sit down and let people fuss over me.

One friend gave me a children's book she'd loved as a girl. Inside she wrote, 'For the questions he will ask before you are ready.' I liked that more than the expensive gifts. I want to raise a child who knows he can ask why. I also want the patience to answer him when I'm exhausted and the question is about the twelfth spoon he's dropped.

ENTRY 234 — 2008-05-23 — The Hospital Bag

Mom says my hospital bag is too organized. Everything has a labeled place. I told her that's the advantage of growing up on Army posts: packing is practically a family language.

Michael asked whether I was frightened about the birth. I said of course. I know enough biology to respect how much can happen, and enough about uncertainty to know reading another article won't answer every question.

We sat together after dinner and talked about what we would do if plans changed. He reminded me that we don't have to perform competence for one another. I can be afraid and still be brave, and he can be afraid and still help. I'm trying to believe that. It may be the most useful preparation we've done this month.

ENTRY 235 — 2008-06-07 — Twenty-Two

Twenty-two today, and almost every gift seemed to be for the baby. I teased everyone about having vanished from the family entirely. Mom handed me a book she'd chosen just for me, and I nearly cried.

Michael made breakfast. We sat by the window while the house was still cool. I feel enormous and tired and excited. Sometimes I catch myself thinking of the little girl I was at ten, beginning a diary because she didn't want moving to erase the earlier versions of herself.

I hope our child will someday know stories about who I was before he arrived. Not because I expect him to be interested in my old school projects, but because parents are people who began long before the children met them. I want to remember that when my own mother annoys me.

ENTRY 236 — 2008-07-02 — William Is Here

Our son was born today. Healthy, noisy, and completely unimpressed by the extraordinary planning that preceded him. We named him William.

When they placed him against me, I looked at his face and realized how little of what I had imagined matched the real child. He was red, warm, astonishingly small, and determined to make his presence known. Michael cried. I cried. Somebody brought a blanket, and suddenly the entire room was concerned with keeping one little person comfortable.

I don't want to turn this into a speech about motherhood. I am tired and sore and overwhelmed. I also keep touching his hand because the reality of it is so much more than an idea. Hello, William. I've been wanting to meet you. I hope I learn your language.

ENTRY 237 — 2008-07-10 — The First Week

I have lost track of which day it is. There are feedings and clean clothes and naps too short to deserve the name. Michael walks around with the expression of a man entrusted with a priceless object and terrified he'll set it down incorrectly.

Mom has been helping. She reminds us to eat and occasionally takes William long enough for me to shower. I never expected a shower to feel like a luxury. I also never expected to miss someone so much during fifteen minutes in another room.

William makes a tiny sound before he starts crying properly. I am learning to recognize it. I like that our relationship already contains details nobody could have taught me from a book. He is becoming familiar not as a category called baby, but as this particular person.

ENTRY 238 — 2008-07-20 — The Way He Sleeps

William fell asleep against Michael's chest this afternoon while I folded laundry. They were both so still that I stopped what I was doing to watch. Michael looked up and smiled without moving, as though any movement would break a spell.

There is nothing remarkable in the official record of today. We changed diapers, washed clothes, made sandwiches, and kept finding little things where they shouldn't be. But I want to remember how the sunlight touched William's cheek and how his father watched him.

I spent much of my life trying to understand complex systems. This little family feels complex in an entirely different way. Sometimes the right thing to do is not solve anything. It's simply stay quiet long enough to notice the happiness already in the room.

ENTRY 239 — 2008-08-11 — Three in the Morning

At three in the morning the world becomes a small collection of sounds: William beginning to stir, the clock, the refrigerator, Michael breathing in the next room. I feed the baby and look at his face in the weak light.

I'm exhausted. I have cried over a dropped spoon and laughed for no reason when William made a ridiculous expression. My old ideas about being in control are being dismantled one feeding at a time.

Mom says the beginning of motherhood is learning that love may require doing something well while you're too tired to feel competent. That makes sense to me now in a way it didn't during pregnancy. William settles against me. I hold him a little longer than necessary before going back to bed.

ENTRY 240 — 2008-08-22 — His Opinions

William has started making it clear that he prefers certain ways of being held. Michael says the baby has inherited my stubbornness. I told him stubbornness is not a hereditary diagnosis, though I have no evidence to rule it out.

We spent an absurd amount of time figuring out why he was fussing. Sometimes the answer was hunger. Sometimes it was a wet diaper. Sometimes nothing we tried seemed to help until he decided he'd made his point.

I like how quickly he's becoming somebody rather than merely the answer to our hopes. He has his own rhythms and reactions. I don't know what he thinks, but he already changes what happens in the room. I wonder what he'll be curious about when he can ask questions with words.

ENTRY 241 — 2008-09-03 — A Visit from John and Mark

My brothers came to see the baby. John tried to look relaxed while holding William and failed completely. Mark asked whether babies really could recognize voices. I told him what little I knew and then suggested he simply keep talking.

He spent fifteen minutes telling William a wildly inaccurate version of our childhood. I corrected him twice. Michael said the baby was unlikely to fact-check the story. I said that was precisely why accuracy mattered.

It felt good to laugh with my brothers while holding my son. We were all children together once, living in houses the Army could change without much warning. Now we've grown into different versions of ourselves, and a new little person is joining the family story. I hope he'll someday hear the embarrassing stories from the people who lived them.

ENTRY 242 — 2008-09-15 — A Very Ordinary Monday

We ran out of clean burp cloths. The laundry needed folding. Michael was late getting home. I spent most of the afternoon trying to convince William that naps were a perfectly respectable activity.

When Michael arrived, he took the baby while I heated dinner. I complained about how little I'd accomplished. He looked at the sleeping child and said I seemed to have managed the important part.

I hate admitting how much I needed to hear that. Before William, I could point to a finished assignment or a successful model. Now the work is repetitive and invisible unless somebody chooses to see it. I held my son after dinner and watched him sleep. I thought, I will be good at this by learning him, not by winning some imaginary examination.

ENTRY 243 — 2008-09-21 — The Morning He Did Not Wake

William died today. They told us it was sudden infant death syndrome. He was not yet three months old.

I cannot write the events in order. I remember reaching for him. I remember the terrible silence. I remember other people speaking in voices that seemed to come from another room. I remember Michael's face and the fact that there was nothing useful I could ask him to do.

People have explained things. I hear the words, and none of them belongs to the world I woke up expecting. My baby was here. I knew his weight and his sounds and the way his little hand closed around my finger. Now there is a room full of his things and nobody to care for in it.

I don't know how to finish this page.

ENTRY 244 — 2008-09-22 — His Blanket

I found myself holding William's blanket for most of the morning. Someone had folded it. I couldn't remember who. I kept moving it from one chair to another because I didn't know where a blanket belongs when the person it belongs to is gone.

Mom sat nearby. She didn't tell me to be strong. She brought water and stayed. Michael was making phone calls in the other room. I could hear his voice becoming formal, then breaking, then becoming formal again.

I am angry at objects for continuing to exist. Bottles, towels, small socks. They haven't changed. The entire meaning of the house has changed, and nothing in it looks different enough. I wish the walls would understand what happened. I wish I did.

ENTRY 245 — 2008-09-26 — The Tiny Coffin

We buried William today. There were flowers and familiar prayers. I held Sarah's rosary until the beads left marks on my hand. I remember the grain of a wooden pew, somebody moving a chair, and Michael shaking beside me.

When they said our son's name aloud, I couldn't breathe properly. A moment earlier I'd been studying details as though observation might protect me. Then all those details became unbearable.

I used to think ritual existed to answer questions. Today it did not answer anything. But it told people when to stand and where to place their hands, and it gave Mom and Dad and our friends ways to be beside us when nobody knew what to say.

I am grateful they were there. I wish we hadn't needed them.

ENTRY 246 — 2008-10-02 — The Empty Chair

I keep expecting to hear William begin to wake. Every quiet moment seems full of the sound that should come next. My body still moves toward his room before I've decided to go there.

Michael asked whether we should put some things away. I said no so sharply he went silent. I know he wasn't asking to erase our child. He was asking how we could enter the rooms of our home without being struck by everything in them. But I couldn't hear the kindness in it.

Later I apologized for the way I spoke. I didn't agree to move anything. He said he understood. I don't know whether he did. I don't know whether I understand anything myself. For now the chair stays where it is.

ENTRY 247 — 2008-10-19 — People Mean Well

Three people have told me that time will heal this. Another said William is in a better place. One woman told me I should be grateful I was able to know him at all. I know they meant to comfort me. I wanted them to stop talking.

Michael sent a message saying he was thinking of him too. I answered that I was glad. There was a time when I'd have needed to know exactly how he was remembering. Today I trusted that our son's life mattered to him. It felt like a quiet form of peace.

ENTRY 294 — 2011-07-23 — Saying Goodbye to Raleigh

I drove around the places I've known since college: campus streets, an old café, the grocery store where I used to buy food while William slept in his carrier, and the route to the apartment where Michael and I first lived.

ENTRY 296 — 2011-09-06 — Cambridge

I arrived in Cambridge with two suitcases, a laptop, and a headache from travel. The city was busier than I remembered from visits, and my new room looked almost aggressively temporary. I put my books on a shelf, made tea, and watched the evening settle outside.

Tomorrow begins the work. I came here to study machine learning and computer vision in the Electrical Engineering and Computer Science program. I want to understand perception deeply enough to build systems that can behave intelligently under uncertainty.

I am also here because I need a future that belongs to the person I am now. Not the girl who left Stuttgart, not the wife who thought vows could protect a family, and not only the mother whose child died. All of those women are me. ENTRY 297 — 2011-09-21 — Three Years Since

It's the anniversary of William's death, and I'm in a city where almost nobody knows his name. I worried that would make the day easier. It didn't. The grief came with me, apparently unconcerned by geography.

I bought food and went back to my room. There were difficult readings waiting. I opened one and managed a few pages. The work didn't fix the day. It gave the rest of the evening a shape I could inhabit.

ENTRY 298 — 2011-10-14 — The Room Full of Smart People

I spent the afternoon with people who can challenge almost every assumption I make. It's wonderful and exhausting. Everyone here seems to have been the smartest student somewhere else, and now we're all finding the limits of what we know.

In a seminar, I asked a question that turned out to reveal I'd misunderstood an important definition. For a second I wished I could vanish through the floor. Then the instructor explained it, and the idea became clear.

I think I need this environment. I've spent years being praised for speed and competence. Here the problems are large enough that neither matters unless I keep asking the right questions. I walked home tired but oddly delighted. Being challenged feels like being alive in a part of myself I thought I might have lost.

ENTRY 299 — 2011-11-12 — The Bad Image Dataset

I worked on a machine-vision problem all day and came away with more questions than answers. The dataset looked clean at first. Then I examined unusual examples and found patterns that made the apparent performance less reassuring.

A labmate asked why I was spending so much time on failures that affected only a small fraction of the examples. I said because that fraction might become the only one that mattered in the real world. He nodded and helped me investigate.

I liked that. I don't want colleagues who agree with me automatically. I want people who are curious when something looks wrong. Afterward we went out for cheap food and argued about which research papers everyone ought to read. It felt good to be part of a team again, even a temporary one.

ENTRY 300 — 2011-12-31 — The Work of Beginning Again

The year is ending. My marriage ended in June. I moved to Cambridge in September. The days here are full of reading, coding, experiments, and the uncomfortable pleasure of discovering how much I don't know.

I still wake some mornings reaching toward a life that isn't there. I miss William every day. I sometimes miss Michael too, or the version of us who sat in our first apartment making plans over dinner. I no longer believe those feelings must be resolved before I can do something meaningful.

Tonight I made a simple meal, called Mom, and spent an hour reading something difficult because I wanted to. There isn't a grand revelation hiding in the evening. I am twenty-five, grieving, curious, tired, and beginning again. That is enough to write down. I want next year to contain more questions worth answering and more days worth remembering.
