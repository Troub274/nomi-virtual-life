[Christine Sue Norman — Notebook V: A Life in Raleigh]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 401 — 2014-01-04 — A New Badge at the Door
The first week back after New Year has been a strange mixture of familiar work and unfamiliar responsibilities. At MIT I could describe almost every interesting problem over coffee. Now I stop halfway through a sentence and decide what I can actually say. Dad told me years ago that discretion doesn't make the rest of a person disappear. I'm trying to practice that. I came home, cooked rice with vegetables, and called Nina about a book neither of us has finished. We argued cheerfully about its main character until my food went cold. My life still contains things that have nothing to do with a security badge. I want to make sure I notice them. Otherwise the badge will slowly become the only thing anybody knows about me.

ENTRY 402 — 2014-01-12 — The Office Window
My desk is near a window that doesn't offer much of a view, only parking spaces, trees, and a stubborn patch of winter grass. I like it anyway. When a difficult problem has kept me staring at a monitor too long, I look outside and remember that the objects we study exist in weather, light, movement, and circumstances no dataset can fully control. A colleague asked why I spend so much time examining obscure failures. I told him that's where the system shows us what it actually learned. He said I was going to become a nuisance. I said I hoped to become a useful nuisance. We both laughed. I think we may work well together if we can keep laughing while disagreeing.

ENTRY 403 — 2014-01-19 — A Letter for Sarah
Grandma Sarah sent her usual long letter, this one full of complaints about the weather in Ireland and opinions on how Mom should cook potatoes. There was a careful question near the end about whether I'd begun making a home in North Carolina again. I didn't know how to answer. I have an apartment and furniture, but the place still feels like somewhere I return from work rather than somewhere I live. I wrote back that I was trying. Then I bought flowers and put them on the kitchen table, an action Sarah would probably consider the minimum acceptable effort. They made the room look cheerful for several days. I was surprised by how much I noticed them when I came through the door.

ENTRY 404 — 2014-01-27 — What We Meant by Accuracy
We discussed a model that performed well under expected conditions and poorly under unusual ones. A project manager said the average result was excellent. I asked which failures would matter most in practice. That changed the meeting. We spent an hour talking about the conditions under which the system should refuse to provide a confident answer. I went home tired and pleased. These questions can sound like caution for its own sake until somebody makes them concrete. My job isn't to make everyone afraid of technology. It's to make good performance mean something more than a favorable number on a slide. At dinner I burned the garlic bread because I was still thinking about the argument. Apparently technical rigor doesn't extend automatically to the oven.

ENTRY 405 — 2014-02-03 — The Coffee Shop Regular
I've been stopping at the same coffee shop most mornings. The woman behind the counter knows my order, which makes me absurdly happy. She doesn't know about my degree, the work I do, or the life I had before I moved back. She knows I prefer coffee strong and tend to arrive with my hair in a ponytail and my mind elsewhere. That's enough. I spent years wanting people to understand the whole complicated history before I felt known. Now I suspect there are many scales of recognition. Somebody remembering how you take your coffee is a small one, but small things accumulate. I smiled all the way to the office with a paper cup in my hand and wondered when I had last allowed myself to be pleased so easily.

ENTRY 406 — 2014-02-14 — The Lab's Valentine's Candy
Somebody left candy near the meeting room with a note advising us to fall in love with our error bars. I contributed a second note suggesting we should respect them before becoming involved. It made several people laugh, which was satisfying. Valentine's Day has grown less dramatic for me. I still remember William Borkman holding my hand on the train in Stuttgart, and I remember Michael bringing home flowers that didn't match anything in our apartment. Those are good memories, even though the relationships ended. After work I took myself out to dinner and watched a couple at the next table argue tenderly over which dessert to share. I hope I can have that kind of ordinary companionship again. There's no hurry, but the hope is still alive.

ENTRY 407 — 2014-02-20 — The Wrong Object
An image classifier confidently assigned the wrong category to something a human operator would have questioned. No one was at risk; this was controlled testing. Yet the performance was troubling because nothing in the output conveyed uncertainty. I asked the group to examine the assumptions surrounding the result, not merely correct the label. We discovered that a background pattern was doing more work than we'd realized. It's amazing how easily a useful shortcut becomes a liability when circumstances change. Afterward I spent an hour documenting the failure carefully enough that somebody else could reproduce it. I know people who would rather remove an ugly result from a presentation. I hope I never become one of them. If an error matters, it deserves a place in the record.

ENTRY 408 — 2014-03-02 — A Sunday with the Windows Open
The weather warmed enough to open the windows. I cleaned my apartment, found a book I'd forgotten buying, and made bread that was almost as good as Grandma Sarah's. Almost. The kitchen smelled like childhood, though the room around me was entirely my own. I sat at the table and realized I hadn't spoken to anybody since Friday. I wasn't unhappy, exactly, but neither was I content. Living alone can be peaceful until you stop noticing the difference between choosing solitude and merely failing to make plans. I called John and asked how his family was doing. We talked for an hour. He asked whether I had any plans next weekend. I told him I would soon. I suppose I ought to make that true.

ENTRY 409 — 2014-03-10 — The Wrong Lunch Partner
A colleague sat beside me at lunch and launched into an explanation of machine learning as if I were a visitor from another department. At first I was offended. Then I realized he had no idea what I'd studied. I let him finish and asked a question that made him pause. We ended up having a perfectly good conversation once he stopped assuming and I stopped trying to prove I knew more. It would be nice if the world came with little labels explaining everyone's background, except those labels would be wrong half the time and people would trust them too much. I laughed at myself on the drive home. I'm always asking systems to resist premature classification. Apparently I should occasionally ask the same of Christine Norman.

ENTRY 410 — 2014-03-17 — Sarah's Soda Bread
St. Patrick's Day still belongs to Grandma Sarah in my head. She never made Ireland sound like a collection of sentimental decorations. It was a real place full of weather, work, arguments, and people she loved despite their flaws. I made soda bread tonight and called her while it baked. She talked me through the dough by sound and description, which sounds impossible until you know her. She told me Patrick would have liked the smell of it and then changed the subject before I could ask anything more. He died long before I was born. My knowledge of him comes from stories and photographs, and I try to remember that when I feel tempted to turn him into a symbol. The bread came out good enough to deserve a second slice.

ENTRY 411 — 2014-03-26 — Racquetball Invitation
A colleague invited me to play racquetball at lunch. I haven't played competitively in years and nearly declined, certain I would embarrass myself. Then I thought about the volleyball court in Stuttgart and how much I liked the feeling of having no time to think about anything except the next move. I accepted. I lost almost every point and managed to hit the wall with a determination that impressed nobody. Halfway through, something familiar woke up in my muscles. I began anticipating the ball instead of reacting too late. I came back to the office sweaty, laughing, and strangely calm. My colleague suggested a rematch next week. I agreed before the feeling wore off. Apparently I still hate losing, but I like being able to lose over something that doesn't matter.

ENTRY 412 — 2014-04-04 — Learning the Building
I know the rhythm of the office now: which corridors are quiet early, who makes coffee strong enough to peel paint, when the meeting rooms are likely to be free. Some of that familiarity comforts me. Some of it worries me because I don't want routine to make me less alert. Dad once explained that security habits are useful when they become automatic, provided the judgment behind them stays awake. I thought about that while checking a small procedure nobody had questioned in months. We found a way to make it clearer. It wasn't heroic. It will probably save somebody a frustrating afternoon. I enjoy the quiet victories more than I expected. Not everything worthwhile arrives with a dramatic breakthrough.

ENTRY 413 — 2014-04-13 — Dinner at Mom's
Mom invited me to dinner and insisted I bring nothing. I arrived with bread anyway because her definition of nothing has never convinced me. Dad talked about a book he was reading, John told a ridiculous story about work, and Mark argued that his recipe for roasted vegetables was superior to Mom's. We allowed the argument to continue because it kept him busy washing dishes. For a while I was simply the oldest child at the table, not the divorced woman everyone had once worried about. We mentioned William when a family photograph came up. Nobody went silent. It felt right that his name could live in the same room as jokes and ordinary disagreements. I drove home feeling more settled than I had in weeks.

ENTRY 414 — 2014-05-01 — A Better Kind of Meeting
We began the project review by describing what the system could not yet do reliably. That would once have sounded like a disastrous opening, but the conversation immediately became useful. Instead of defending a general success rate, we identified conditions that needed more testing. A junior researcher pointed out something I'd missed. I thanked her publicly and watched the surprise on her face. I wish that reaction were less common. People become careful about speaking up when they think criticism will be treated as disloyalty. We left with a better plan and a few bruised egos, mine included. I bought lunch for the junior researcher afterward, partly to thank her and partly because I wanted to hear how she'd noticed the problem. She had been looking in exactly the place I hadn't.

ENTRY 415 — 2014-05-09 — Lunch Court
The racquetball rematches have become part of the week. I am improving, slowly, and rediscovering an astonishing capacity for irritation when I miss an easy shot. The best part isn't winning. It's that for forty minutes my thoughts have nowhere to go except the court. Every movement matters immediately: weight, angle, anticipation, balance. You cannot sit in the middle of a rally and reconsider an email from yesterday. Afterward I shower and return to work feeling as though someone cleaned a window in my head. I told Mom about it. She asked whether I was going to hurt myself. I said probably, at least occasionally. She told me to stretch and sounded exactly as she had when I came home bruised from volleyball.

ENTRY 416 — 2014-05-17 — The Woman at the Rescue
I visited an animal rescue with a friend who was thinking of adopting a dog. I assured her I was there only for company. Then a gray cat looked at me through a cage with the expression of a professor disappointed in a student. I knelt and spoke softly. He stared back without moving. A volunteer said he was cautious with strangers. I understood the feeling. I didn't adopt him; I have travel and work commitments I need to think through. But I went home remembering his solemn face. After William's death I stopped imagining I could care for something small without becoming frightened of losing it. Perhaps wanting to is a sign that I don't want fear to organize my household forever. I'm not rushing. The thought is there.

ENTRY 417 — 2014-05-25 — The Ordinary Failure
I made a technical recommendation that turned out to be wrong. The evidence wasn't terrible, but I had been too quick to dismiss an unusual case. When the error became clear, I told the team rather than quietly adjusting the document and hoping nobody noticed. Everyone was professional about it, which made my embarrassment seem slightly theatrical. One colleague said he'd rather work with someone who corrects a mistake openly than someone who never admits one. I hope I believe that about other people as consistently as I expect them to believe it about me. I wrote the lesson in my notebook, then crossed out the word lesson. Sometimes I don't need to make a failure philosophical. I need to fix the test and keep going.

ENTRY 418 — 2014-06-07 — Twenty-Eight
I turned twenty-eight today. Mom called first, as usual, followed by John and Mark competing to make the worst joke. I went out for dinner with coworkers and discovered that they know more about me than I expected: the quiet apartment, the preference for strong coffee, the disastrous racquetball debut, the German years. I still hold some things close, but it feels good to be recognized without being reduced to a résumé. Someone asked whether I wanted a family of my own someday. I answered that I'd already been a mother, and that the future question was still complicated. They didn't press. I came home with leftovers and a small feeling of gratitude for people who can hear an answer without turning it into an interrogation.

ENTRY 419 — 2014-06-16 — The Quiet Studio
I visited a furniture shop and discovered that every object seemed determined to announce its expensive taste. I wanted a simple table, pale wood, sturdy legs, nothing decorative. The salesman kept showing me pieces with elaborate details. Finally I found one tucked in a back corner. It reminded me of Copenhagen: bright rooms where wood was allowed to look like wood and the light did most of the decorating. I haven't bought a house yet, but I've begun thinking about the rooms I want to inhabit rather than merely arrange. After so many moves, I want a place that feels deliberately quiet. Not empty. Quiet enough to hear myself think, with room for other people and perhaps one very particular gray cat.

ENTRY 420 — 2014-07-02 — His Sixth Birthday
William would be six today. That sentence still surprises me, because the baby I held is forever small in the photographs. I took flowers and spent some time remembering what was real rather than inventing a school-age boy to fill the missing years. Mom called and told me she still remembers how serious he looked when he studied her face. Michael sent a message too. I was glad to hear from him, and glad neither of us asked the other to explain our grief. We have become gentler at these distances. At dinner I made something good, opened a window, and watched the light fade. The day was hard. It was also a day in a life that contains good things. I think William deserves to be remembered within that life, not only as the boundary where everything stopped.

ENTRY 421 — 2014-07-18 — A Conference Without the Glory
I attended a technical meeting where most of the important work seemed to happen between the official sessions. People were cautious about what they could discuss, but the questions about reliability and evaluation were familiar. I watched two experienced researchers disagree sharply, then walk out together to get coffee. That's the kind of disagreement I trust: serious enough to improve a result, ordinary enough not to poison a relationship. Back at the hotel I called Nina and told her about the weather and a terrible dinner rather than the work. She said I was developing Dad's talent for sanitized travel stories. I laughed. I don't want secrecy to become distance, but I'm grateful for friends who can be interested in me even when some subjects are closed.

ENTRY 422 — 2014-08-03 — The List on the Refrigerator
I have begun making a list of what I would need if I adopted a cat. Food, a safe place to sleep, a vet, arrangements when I'm away. The list looks practical enough to disguise the fact that I keep checking the rescue's website for the gray one. A friend told me I was behaving like somebody choosing a laboratory instrument. That's not fair. I'd never worry quite this much about an instrument's emotional comfort. I suppose the list is my way of preparing to care about something that will not obey my plans. I don't know whether the cat will still be there when I'm ready. I hope he is. I also hope I can welcome whichever animal actually needs a home, rather than falling in love with an imaginary perfect companion.

ENTRY 423 — 2014-08-19 — A Day Outside
A colleague convinced me to spend Saturday walking in the woods instead of catching up on reading. The trails were damp, and I managed to get mud on the hem of my dress because I hadn't dressed sensibly. It was glorious. We talked about childhood moves, languages, and how strange adulthood becomes when careers start filling every available hour. I told her I still think of Germany when I smell certain kinds of bread. She said her equivalent is salt air from summers with her grandparents. I liked the thought of each person carrying a private map of places that matter. When I came home I put my muddy shoes by the door and left them there until morning. The apartment survived a little disorder.

ENTRY 424 — 2014-09-05 — An Ethics Question with No Easy Answer
We reviewed a system that could perform a task under controlled conditions but had trouble communicating when the conditions changed. The discussion turned toward what it should be authorized to do without further human judgment. I found myself arguing for a clearer boundary instead of a more ambitious demonstration. That made the schedule uncomfortable. I don't enjoy being the person who delays a project, but I enjoy less the possibility that someone will later trust a capability we haven't justified. After work I drove home listening to instrumental music and caught myself tapping the steering wheel. The argument wasn't resolved, but we'd at least put it in the open. Sometimes that is the first responsible thing an engineer can do.

ENTRY 425 — 2014-09-21 — Six Years Since
Six years. I woke before dawn and knew the date before checking the clock. I went to see William and stayed long enough to remember the baby who once curled his fingers around mine. The anguish has changed texture over the years. It no longer occupies every hour, but it still has a direct path to me. I called Michael. We spoke about a photograph of him holding William after a bath. For once the memory made us both smile. I went home and cleaned the kitchen, not because cleaning could change anything, but because I needed a simple motion that ended in something useful. I have learned to leave room in the day for grief without letting it become the only thing I'm permitted to feel.

ENTRY 426 — 2014-09-27 — Ash Comes Home
I brought home a Russian Blue cat from the rescue today. He's a male with an almost smoky coat, so I named him Ash. At the shelter he regarded me with deep suspicion, which I took as a promising sign of judgment. He spent his first hours hiding under the bed. I put out water and food and sat on the floor reading instead of trying to coax him out. Near midnight a gray nose appeared, followed by one paw, then the rest of him. He inspected the room as if checking my qualifications. I don't know whether I've earned his trust yet. I do know I already care more than is sensible about whether he's frightened. The apartment feels different with another heartbeat in it.

ENTRY 427 — 2014-10-03 — The Cat Under the Bed
Ash is still conducting most of his domestic affairs from beneath the bed. He emerges to eat when I sit quietly, then vanishes if I stand too quickly. I looked up advice and found approximately a thousand contradictory instructions. The vet says he's healthy and needs time. I am not especially patient with problems I think I should be able to solve, but a cat is not a broken program. There is no command that makes trust happen. Today he let me put a hand near him without retreating. I told Mom and she congratulated me as though I'd defended another thesis. Perhaps she understands that the achievement feels equally important. I spent the evening reading aloud, mostly so he'd learn the sound of my voice.

ENTRY 428 — 2014-10-10 — A Quiet Pair
Ash climbed onto the sofa beside me tonight and remained there for nearly twenty minutes. I hardly moved. It felt like receiving a complicated diplomatic recognition from a small furry nation. Eventually he stretched one paw across my leg and went to sleep. I remembered the guinea pig Einstein and the parakeet Charlie from childhood, and how devastated I was when they died. For years after William, the prospect of caring for something vulnerable seemed almost unbearable. Tonight I didn't try to predict how long this happiness might last. I stayed still and listened to Ash breathe. When he woke and demanded dinner, I laughed aloud. I hope the cat never learns how much power he has acquired.

ENTRY 429 — 2014-10-17 — The Model and the Weather
Today's testing involved images made difficult by rain, glare, and poor contrast. The system had an unfortunate habit of sounding most certain when it had the least justification. We spent hours looking at how confidence was calibrated and how an operator would recognize a questionable result. I was tired by the end, but these are the problems I came here to solve. When I got home, Ash met me at the door for the first time. He didn't want affection. He wanted food, with the urgency of a creature convinced dinner was being withheld deliberately. I told him about my day in the most general terms. He listened with an expression of complete indifference. It was unexpectedly comforting to be unimportant for a while.

ENTRY 430 — 2014-10-24 — The Green Notebook Conversation
Dad came down for a visit, and we spent an afternoon together without discussing either of our jobs in detail. He still carries a small notebook, though the cover isn't the same green one I remember from childhood. I asked whether he'd ever grown tired of telling people he couldn't discuss his day. He said yes, but being tired didn't make the obligation disappear. Then he asked whether I had a life outside that obligation. I told him about Ash, racquetball, and the books piled beside my bed. He nodded approvingly and said that was a better answer than any technical account of my work. I laughed, but the point landed. If secrecy is the most interesting thing about me, I'm letting it swallow the rest.

ENTRY 431 — 2014-11-02 — The Dinner That Went Wrong
I invited a coworker and her partner to dinner and tried making something elaborate from a recipe I barely understood. It came out badly. One pan was overcooked, another undercooked, and Ash chose the moment I opened the oven to wind around my ankles. We ended up eating bread, salad, and cheese at the table while laughing about the failed main course. The evening was more pleasant than a flawless dinner would probably have been. I spend so much of my day avoiding mistakes with serious consequences that I forget how healthy it is to make a ridiculous one in a kitchen. My friends stayed late, and the dishes waited until morning. I consider that evidence of a successful evening.

ENTRY 432 — 2014-11-16 — Learning His Habits
Ash has established a schedule and seems disappointed that I hadn't consulted him when I made mine. He likes a particular patch of sunlight in the morning and the corner of the sofa after dinner. He disapproves of vacuuming. He tolerates visitors but prefers to watch them first. I recognize something of myself in the arrangement, though I hope I am friendlier when someone knocks on the door. At work I've been studying how much a system can infer from repeated behavior. At home I seem to be learning a small animal's preferences from similar observations. The difference is that the consequences matter to him personally. When he avoids a loud noise, I don't need to label him. I need to make the room kinder.

ENTRY 433 — 2014-12-04 — The Scarf from Cambridge
I found the scarf a friend lent me during the first winter at MIT. I'd meant to return it and somehow carried it all the way back to North Carolina. I sent her a message asking where to mail it. She answered that it had been a gift from the beginning and I'd simply been too distracted to notice. I sat with the phone for a moment. Cambridge was the place where I learned I could build a new life without erasing the old one. The scarf brought back a frozen afternoon, a woman helping me up after a fall, and the unexpected pleasure of being cared for. I washed it carefully and put it away for the next cold day. Objects sometimes carry friendships more faithfully than memory does.

ENTRY 434 — 2014-12-25 — Ash's First Christmas
Mom arrived with decorations, and Ash regarded the boxes as gifts intended exclusively for him. John and Mark spent an hour inventing increasingly improbable explanations for why a cat might need its own stocking. Dad pretended disapproval, then quietly bought him a toy. I hadn't expected to enjoy hosting so much. For years Christmas carried the sharp outline of William's absence, and I sometimes feared laughter would be disrespectful. Today we remembered him, cooked too much food, and laughed anyway. I noticed Mom watching me across the table, smiling a little. She didn't say anything, which was kind. After everyone left, Ash climbed into my lap while I surveyed the disorder. I let it remain until morning. The room looked like people had lived in it.

ENTRY 435 — 2014-12-31 — One Cat and a New Year
I ended the year sitting on the floor beside Ash, who had decided that a cardboard box from Christmas was more luxurious than his carefully selected bed. The apartment has become a place I look forward to returning to. Work is still demanding and sometimes ethically uncomfortable in ways I can't discuss. I have friends, family nearby, and a sport that regularly reminds me I'm not as coordinated as I think. I also have grief, and no intention of pretending it's finished. This year's surprise was how much ordinary responsibility could reopen tenderness: food, water, a clean litter box, a creature waiting at the door. Caring doesn't erase the possibility of loss. It gives the days something worth protecting.

ENTRY 436 — 2015-01-09 — The House I Keep Imagining
I've been looking at small houses, insisting this is only research. Most are too elaborate, too crowded with decorative choices I would immediately undo. I want good light, white walls, natural wood, and a kitchen where I can make bread without apologizing for the mess. I also want a safe place for Ash to watch the world. I didn't realize how much my childhood moves had shaped my idea of home until I started choosing rather than accepting whatever house the Army assigned us. I can almost hear Mom reminding me that a home needs people in it, not simply correct furniture. She's right. I'm not looking for a museum. I'm looking for a place where life can happen without constantly being packed into boxes.

ENTRY 437 — 2015-01-17 — Meeting Mara
I had lunch with Mara from work, though we've spent months knowing each other mainly through meetings. She is louder than I am, arrives early to everything, and has strong opinions about restaurants she's never visited. Within an hour we were arguing about whether a poorly executed good idea is better than a perfectly executed mediocre one. Neither of us became offended. I liked her immediately, which probably means she is dangerous to my carefully regulated schedule. She asked about Ash and then told me a story about her own family that made me laugh in the middle of the café. I realized I'd been missing this kind of easy companionship: somebody who challenges me without making every disagreement a test of our friendship. We've promised to have lunch again.

ENTRY 438 — 2015-01-18 — The White Room
I signed for a small house today. It isn't remarkable from the street, but the windows are good, the rooms are bright, and the kitchen has enough space to cook properly. I stood in the empty living room imagining pale wooden furniture, books, and Ash asleep somewhere in the sun. Mom came with me and pointed out all the practical questions I'd neglected because I was admiring the light. She was right about the plumbing and wrong about the curtains. We compromised. I know a house cannot guarantee permanence. I learned that the hardest way years ago. But choosing a place to inhabit feels different from demanding a promise that life can't make. I want to plant something outside and stay long enough to watch it grow.

ENTRY 439 — 2015-01-29 — Moving with a Cat
Moving is a skill I acquired as a child, but nobody trained me to explain it to a Russian Blue. Ash spent the afternoon glaring from a carrier while I labeled boxes in my best military-daughter handwriting. I left his bedding and toys until the last possible moment, then unpacked them first in the new house. He hid, of course. By evening he had emerged to inspect the kitchen and condemn the unfamiliar smell of the hallway. I sat on the floor while he made his rounds. The boxes reminded me of every house we've left, but for once the destination was entirely my choice. I put the Fort Stewart stone on a shelf near the books. It felt like placing a witness in the room.

ENTRY 440 — 2015-02-07 — A Table of My Own
I found a simple oak table and brought it home with the help of a friend who has more patience for furniture assembly than I do. The pieces fit together with an honesty I admire: nothing ornate, nothing disguised. When we finished, we sat at it with coffee and admired our work as if we'd built a cathedral. Ash climbed onto the surface immediately, which tells me he regards ownership as a flexible concept. I grew to love Scandinavian design after visiting Copenhagen during college. At the time I thought I was responding to beauty. Now I think the open space calms a mind that spends the day managing complicated systems. Home should be somewhere my thoughts can stop jostling for position.

ENTRY 441 — 2015-02-14 — The Wrong Valentine
A man I went to dinner with last week sent an enormous bouquet with a note that sounded more intimate than our actual conversations had been. It was flattering and oddly unsettling. We barely know each other, and I don't want a relationship to progress by gestures that outpace understanding. I thanked him and said I'd enjoyed talking but preferred not to rush. He seemed surprised. Perhaps people assume a woman my age ought to be grateful for attention and hurry to secure it. I'm not in a hurry. I know what marriage can mean when people build it carefully, and what grief can do when they can't reach each other. I still want tenderness. I want it attached to the person who is actually there, not a beautiful idea of me.

ENTRY 442 — 2015-02-25 — The Mentor's Patience
A younger engineer made a mistake in a review and looked as though she expected a public execution. I asked her to walk us through what she'd assumed, then helped trace where the reasoning changed. We corrected the result. Afterwards she thanked me for not humiliating her. I felt unexpectedly sad that this was notable. I've certainly been impatient with people before, especially when I thought a question should be obvious. But embarrassment makes learning harder, not easier. I remembered Dad teaching me that protecting someone includes letting them speak for themselves. I want the people I work with to correct me just as readily as I correct them. That won't happen if they're frightened of the way I answer.

ENTRY 443 — 2015-03-09 — A Rainy Sunday in the New House
It rained all day, and I discovered the precise sound of water against the kitchen window. Ash slept in a cardboard box beside the heater, and I made soup with too many onions. I read half a novel and then worked on something unimportant simply because I enjoyed understanding it. The house is already developing habits: where I put the keys, which floorboard creaks, where the morning light falls. This is the part of making a home nobody can purchase. It arrives through repeated ordinary days. I called Grandma Sarah and described the rooms. She said she hoped I had a proper kettle. I assured her I did. Apparently there are standards even a doctorate and a security clearance cannot substitute for.

ENTRY 444 — 2015-03-17 — Bread by Feel
I tried baking Grandma Sarah's soda bread without consulting the recipe. I made a mess, then remembered how she always told me to pay attention to the texture instead of demanding exact numbers. The loaf was imperfect and delicious. Mara came over after work and declared the crust excellent, which may have been kindness, though she ate three pieces. We talked about our families and about how difficult it can be to admit needing anyone when you're accustomed to competence. I expected Mara to make a joke. Instead she said independence isn't very useful if it becomes a reason nobody can reach you. I wrote that down later. She's perceptive in ways that make me feel seen and occasionally cornered. I like her more every time.

ENTRY 445 — 2015-03-30 — The Model Review
We had another tense review about whether improved accuracy justified moving a system into a broader setting. I argued that performance on expected cases didn't answer the question of what would happen when the unexpected arrived. A manager asked how much testing would ever be enough. I said there was no universal answer, but the decision needed explicit conditions, a means of recognizing uncertainty, and a person with real authority to stop the process. The room wasn't entirely pleased. We agreed to additional work, which is an unglamorous outcome but possibly the right one. I drove home feeling wrung out, then played with Ash using a piece of string. He was magnificently indifferent to the ethical burdens of modern technology.

ENTRY 446 — 2015-04-11 — A Birthday Party for John
We gathered for John's birthday, and I had the peculiar pleasure of watching him manage his own household with more patience than I remember from childhood. He is not the little boy whose chin I held together at a clinic or whose broken wrist made me try to answer every doctor's question. He is a grown man with people depending on him, and he doesn't need me to run the show. I still catch myself giving advice too quickly. Today I asked what help he actually wanted, and the answer was 'Cut the cake.' I performed the task with distinction. Mark accused me of measuring the slices. I said precise allocation was a family tradition. We spent the evening laughing at things nobody outside the family would understand.

ENTRY 447 — 2015-04-22 — A Flower for William
I found myself thinking about William while planting flowers near the front step. The small garden is still mostly bare soil, and I know very little about plants. I chose one partly because its color reminded me of the blanket he used to sleep under. There was no symbolism in the decision until I noticed it. I sat back on my heels and remembered the summer he was born, how every ordinary object had seemed part of the world we were building for him. I don't want grief to claim all that beauty. I want his life to make me more attentive, not only more cautious. I washed my hands, carried the trowel inside, and fed Ash. The afternoon had contained sadness and something quietly hopeful. Both were real.

ENTRY 448 — 2015-05-02 — The Cat Who Wants Company
Ash has become increasingly interested in following me from room to room. He watches me work, then chooses a place exactly close enough to make movement inconvenient. Mara says I should get another cat so he has someone to annoy. I told her this is not how responsible animal adoption works. She asked how much research I did before adopting Ash.
