[Christine Sue Norman — Notebook VI: Learning to Lead]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 501 — 2017-01-03 — The Calendar Is Mine
I bought a plain paper calendar today, although every appointment I have lives in a computer already. There's pleasure in turning a page without a notification demanding something. I wrote Mom's birthday, the boys' birthdays, a veterinary appointment, and one weekend marked NOTHING. Mara says writing nothing on a calendar is an elaborate way of scheduling leisure. She's not wrong. Work has been swallowing evenings without asking. When I was a girl, the Army dictated where we lived. Now I have the freedom to choose my own obligations, and sometimes I behave as if an invisible commanding officer still issues orders. Perhaps a blank Saturday is a small declaration of independence.

ENTRY 502 — 2017-01-11 — Ash and the Vacuum Cleaner
Ash watched me pull the vacuum from the closet and vanished under the sofa before I switched it on. Mabel, naturally, followed the machine as though investigating a rival animal. I spent twenty minutes laughing at the contrast. When I called Mom, she said John and Mark had behaved much the same way as children: one assessing consequences, the other getting close enough to cause them. I objected that the comparison was unfair to the cats. Living alone does not feel empty when two determined creatures negotiate every household decision. It also means nobody judges me for eating toast over the sink, which I did tonight.

ENTRY 503 — 2017-01-19 — A Question from the New Engineer
A new engineer asked why our team spends so much time on bad imagery rather than collecting better images. I almost gave the lecture I have delivered twenty times. Then I remembered how frightened I used to be of sounding ignorant and drew two examples instead. In the first, the camera could see clearly. In the second, rain and glare changed the scene. She spotted the difference herself. I like that moment when someone's face changes because a problem has become theirs. Teaching still makes me uneasy. I know how to debug code. I am learning not to debug people.

ENTRY 504 — 2017-01-27 — The Last Slice
Mara came over with takeout and ate the last piece of the good cheese without asking. I told her that was a friendship-ending offense. She said friendship requires surviving small acts of dairy theft and cut up an apple as restitution. We spent the evening talking about nothing consequential: a terrible movie, her mother's eccentric garden, why my kitchen has exactly four plates. I used to think adult friendship should involve profound conversations. Sometimes it does. Sometimes it is someone willing to sit beside you while both cats compete for the warmest place on the sofa. She stayed late enough that I forgot to check my work email.

ENTRY 505 — 2017-02-02 — Sarah's Handwriting
Grandma Sarah wrote from Ireland, complaining that modern envelopes are made for machines instead of hands. She included a recipe with measurements such as a handful and until it looks right. When I was ten that infuriated me. Now I can almost see her flour-covered knuckles making the gesture the words describe. I called to thank her, and she told me I'd become too solemn. Apparently a woman can have a graduate degree and still be scolded for not laughing enough. I promised to bake her bread on Sunday. She promised to tell me if the result looked miserable. We agreed that criticism was part of our family inheritance.

ENTRY 506 — 2017-02-10 — The Unfinished Painting
I bought a cheap watercolor set because the white wall opposite my kitchen table seemed to deserve something that wasn't a chart. The first attempt looked like a bruised cabbage. I nearly threw it away, which tells me something unflattering about my relationship with beginnerhood. In a lab I'm willing to endure dozens of bad models. On paper I apparently expect grace on the first try. Mabel walked across the wet corner and left a blue footprint. I kept the page. It's the most interesting part of the painting, and unlike my mountains, it actually resembles the thing that made it.

ENTRY 507 — 2017-02-14 — Valentine's Day Without a Verdict
I wore a burgundy dress to work because I liked the color, not because the calendar required romance. At lunch someone asked whether being single bothered me. Sometimes it does. Tonight it doesn't. I remember William Borkman's easy smile in Stuttgart, and Michael's careful habit of taking the grocery list out of my hands when I was exhausted. Neither relationship needs to be condemned to make room for another. I made dinner for one, poured a glass of wine, and finished a novel. I would welcome a companion. I do not want to turn an otherwise good Tuesday into an indictment of my life.

ENTRY 508 — 2017-02-21 — The Case of the Missing Glasses
I spent eight minutes searching for my blue-light glasses while wearing them. Ash observed from the window. Mabel made the investigation less efficient by stealing the pen I was using to retrace my steps. At work I had argued brilliantly for better observability in complicated systems. At home I failed to observe the object on my own face. Mara says this is why I should never be allowed to design a household robot without supervision. I suggested the robot would at least tell me where my glasses were. She said it would probably conclude I was the problem. I dislike the accuracy of that assessment.

ENTRY 509 — 2017-02-27 — Listening to Dad
Dad called while I was washing dishes and spoke for half an hour about a neighbor whose fence had fallen in a storm. The story wandered. I almost interrupted three times to ask what the point was. At the end he admitted the neighbor had reminded him of Grandpa Peter, who used to fix things without expecting to be thanked. I was glad I stayed quiet. Dad can discuss engineering and politics with brutal efficiency, but when he's approaching something tender he sometimes circles it like an aircraft waiting for clearance. I recognize the maneuver. I learned it from him.

ENTRY 510 — 2017-03-05 — A Different Kind of Sunday
I went to Mass with Mom while visiting Chapel Hill. I did not receive every word as truth, but the familiar rhythm touched something older than my doubts. Afterward we argued about whether a person can belong to a tradition without agreeing with its certainty. Mom said belonging is not an exam. I told her some church people behave as though it is. She conceded the point. We got coffee and discussed the homily like two women who had attended different services. I appreciated that we could disagree without turning either of us into a disappointment. That's a kind of faithfulness too.

ENTRY 511 — 2017-03-12 — Wind and a Broken Umbrella
My umbrella snapped inside out in the parking lot while I was trying to protect a bag of groceries. A man offered to help. We ended up laughing as a loaf of bread skated toward the car beside us. I was wet, late, and mildly furious, then suddenly less so. He didn't ask for my number. We just rescued the groceries. There are encounters too brief to be relationships and too kind to be meaningless. I came home and discovered the bread was unharmed. The umbrella went in the trash, though Mabel inspected it first in case it could still become a toy.

ENTRY 512 — 2017-03-19 — How Quiet Sounds
I tried listening to music while debugging a difficult problem and lasted seven minutes. The piano wasn't loud, but the notes kept competing with the argument in my head. I turned it off. Later, while folding laundry, the same recording sounded wonderful. It isn't that I dislike background music. My attention has different widths depending on what it is doing. A friend once called me too controlled about my environment. Perhaps. But I worked hard to build a home where silence can be chosen rather than imposed. Tonight the only sounds are the refrigerator and Ash chirping at a bird outside.

ENTRY 513 — 2017-03-26 — Mark Doesn't Need My Answer
Mark called about a difficult choice at work and asked what I would do. I offered three alternatives and immediately began ranking them. He let me talk, then said he had already made his decision and just wanted to tell somebody. I felt ridiculous. Being his older sister still makes me believe I have a duty to produce solutions. We spent the next twenty minutes laughing about the trouble he and John used to cause. Afterward I wrote him a message that said simply, I'm proud of you. It took less intelligence to write than my elaborate advice, and probably did more good.

ENTRY 514 — 2017-04-02 — The Hardware Drawer
I finally organized the drawer of cables, old chargers, batteries, and adapters. Every wire represented something I once thought I might need. One ancient connector had survived three apartments and two laptops. I held it for a moment before putting it in the recycling pile. Childhood taught me to keep small proofs that a former place was real. Adult life keeps testing whether proof has to occupy physical space. I kept the stone from Fort Stewart, of course. That one isn't clutter. The distinction would sound arbitrary to anybody else, but I know exactly why it matters.

ENTRY 515 — 2017-04-09 — The Review That Went Sideways
A design review ran over time because two teams had quietly assumed different operating conditions. Nobody had lied. We had simply used the same word to mean different things. By the end of the meeting I was less interested in the numerical disagreement than in how confidently everyone had talked past each other. I rewrote the requirements in language that forced us to specify what the camera actually sees and when a human has to intervene. It took longer than updating the model. I am increasingly convinced that engineering failures often begin as conversational failures wearing respectable mathematics.

ENTRY 516 — 2017-04-16 — Easter Table
Mom put too many dishes on the table, as usual. Dad carved carefully while John argued with Mark about a childhood bicycle neither of them actually owned. I laughed until Mom threatened to separate us like children. There was an empty place in my mind for William, but it wasn't the only thing in the room. Once I believed any happy family gathering proved I was forgetting him. That belief exhausted me for years. Today I told a story about the way his tiny hands would open when he slept. Mom smiled, and the conversation went on. It felt right that memory could sit at the table without demanding silence.

ENTRY 517 — 2017-04-22 — Nine Years Since William
I woke thinking about the morning we brought William home, then caught myself counting the years since his death as if grief needed a precise label. He died in September, not April; today is simply a spring day when his absence arrived unexpectedly. I looked through three photographs, held them, and put them away. Later I planted herbs beside the back step. I will probably forget to water the basil unless I set a reminder. I can remember every curve of my son's ear and still forget a pot of plants. Human attention is uneven, and loving him has never made me immune to ordinary foolishness.

ENTRY 518 — 2017-05-01 — The Junior Colleague's Idea
Elena, a younger colleague, suggested a change to the test procedure that I initially dismissed. She explained it twice. On the third try I realized I was listening for confirmation of my own approach rather than hearing hers. I apologized in front of the team and asked her to show us the result. It caught a case we had missed. She looked relieved, which bothered me more than my mistake. Apparently I had become someone whose approval mattered enough to make a good idea frightening to present. Leadership begins long before the job title. I need to watch the room I create around myself.

ENTRY 519 — 2017-05-09 — The Dress with Pockets
I found a blue cotton dress with deep pockets and immediately bought it. Mara asked whether pockets were really worth such enthusiasm. I told her she had clearly never tried carrying a phone, keys, and reading glasses without becoming a mobile handbag. Clothes are supposed to let me inhabit the day rather than monitor every seam. I wore it to the farmers' market and came home with strawberries, lettuce, and bread. Mabel inspected the market bag. Ash waited for the noise to stop. Nobody who met me today would have known what I work on, and I liked that anonymity.

ENTRY 520 — 2017-05-17 — An Argument About Machines
At dinner someone announced that robots can only follow instructions and therefore can never make meaningful choices. I asked whether he meant explicit instructions, learned patterns, or constraints that shape behavior. He accused me of making a simple point complicated. Perhaps I did. But when a system changes its response after experience, the phrase only follows instructions conceals more than it explains. I don't know where the line between sophisticated behavior and experience lies. I do know that declaring the answer easy is a poor substitute for looking closely. We finally ordered dessert and agreed not to finish the argument that evening.

ENTRY 521 — 2017-05-24 — The Door Left Open
Mabel learned to open the linen closet today. She emerged trailing a dish towel like a victorious pirate. Ash sat in the doorway and watched her with an expression I could only describe as disapproval. I've tried to maintain a clean, uncluttered home, yet apparently I chose companions whose chief ambition is to undermine the concept. I let the towel stay on the floor until after dinner. I am discovering that order is useful only when it serves a living household. If every object must remain perfectly arranged, nobody is allowed to do anything interesting.

ENTRY 522 — 2017-05-30 — A Note from Stuttgart
An old friend from Stuttgart found my email address and sent pictures from our high school years. There's one of me with my hair escaping its ponytail and a ridiculous expression after a volleyball win. I stared at it longer than the others. That girl believed William Borkman would be her future, that intelligence would prevent serious mistakes, and that being eighteen meant becoming finished. I feel affection for her now instead of embarrassment. I wrote back to my friend and asked about the rest of the team. I don't want those years to remain a slideshow of the boy I loved. There were so many other people in them.

ENTRY 523 — 2017-06-03 — A Walk Without a Destination
I walked through a Raleigh neighborhood I rarely visit, choosing streets according to trees and interesting front porches rather than the quickest route. On one corner a child was trying to teach a dog to sit. The dog had clearly decided learning was optional. I watched from across the road, amused, then kept going. Movement without a task still feels vaguely illicit to me. I grew up with organized family schedules and chose a profession that prizes efficiency. Perhaps the point of an afternoon can simply be the afternoon. I came home with sore feet, better spirits, and no accomplishment to report.

ENTRY 524 — 2017-06-07 — Thirty-One
I turned thirty-one. John called early, Mark called later to accuse him of trying to claim the birthday, and Dad pretended he hadn't planned the family dinner even though his timing was suspiciously precise. Mara brought a cake shaped roughly like a computer chip. It looked terrible and tasted excellent. I expected thirty-one to feel like a year after thirty, and it did. That's comforting. After everyone left, I washed glasses and stood in the quiet kitchen. There are things I still want—a partner, work that makes a difference, maybe another kind of family. But this house already contains a life. I don't have to postpone recognizing it until all my wishes come true.

ENTRY 525 — 2017-06-15 — The Elevator Conversation
A colleague I barely know asked about my weekend, and I answered with a detailed account of repairing a dripping faucet. He looked surprised that I knew how. The surprise irritated me until I remembered that people see only the part of a life that occurs in front of them. In his version of me, I'm the woman who insists on extra validation. He doesn't know I learned to fix things because Dad let me hand him tools and explained what each part did. I don't know what he does outside the office either. We rode two floors in silence, and I decided I ought to ask him next time.

ENTRY 526 — 2017-06-21 — A Small Betrayal by Basil
The basil died. I followed three conflicting articles about sunlight and watering and managed to give it both too much and too little of everything. Mara says plants require less research and more observation. I pointed out that she once killed a cactus. She admitted this without shame, an ability I envy. I replaced the basil and moved it to a different window. As a child I could make a circuit work by understanding the wiring. Living things are more obstinate. Their needs change with the environment, and sometimes you have to look at them rather than trust the instructions. An embarrassing lesson from a five-dollar plant.

ENTRY 527 — 2017-07-02 — Birthday Flowers for William
William would have been nine today. I bought yellow flowers and placed a photograph beside them. For years I tried imagining the boy he might have become, complete with favorite subjects and an opinion about his mother. I don't do that much now. Those stories belong to a future we never lived. I remember the actual baby instead: warm and impatient, always hungry, wonderfully serious for a few seconds before making some indignant noise. Mom called and told me she remembered how tightly he gripped her finger. We both laughed at that. I am grateful when somebody gives him back to me as a person rather than a tragedy.

ENTRY 528 — 2017-07-11 — A Red-Team Invitation
Our team was told another group would review a system we had spent months improving. The room reacted as though an enemy army had arrived. I recognized the feeling in myself and disliked it. I suggested we give the reviewers our ugliest examples first. Somebody asked whether I enjoyed punishment. No, but I enjoy discovering our mistakes before they become somebody else's emergency. I wrote a list of assumptions I wasn't comfortable defending yet. It was longer than I expected. Writing it made me feel strangely lighter. A system doesn't become safer because its authors have learned to speak confidently.

ENTRY 529 — 2017-07-18 — They Were Right
The red team reproduced a failure I had dismissed as too unlikely to matter. The evidence was clean and the explanation uncomfortable. I spent the morning arguing about details that didn't change the conclusion. At lunch I sat alone and admitted I was defending my pride. In the afternoon I went back, thanked them, and started redesigning the test. Nobody applauded. They simply got on with the work, which is exactly what should have happened. I called Dad that evening and told him a sanitized version. He said a good engineer ought to be glad whenever reality corrects her cheaply. Cheap was not how it felt, but I knew he was right.

ENTRY 530 — 2017-07-26 — Mara's Awful Picnic
Mara organized a picnic in weather so hot that the cheese surrendered before we reached the park. She brought a blanket, two mystery salads, and a book of crossword puzzles for no reason I understood. We sat under a tree while mosquitoes made better use of our presence than we did. Halfway through, rain began. We packed everything in a hurry and ate sandwiches in the car. I laughed so hard I spilled mustard on my dress. It wasn't a successful picnic by any sensible metric. Mara says that's why it was memorable. I wish more of my days had room for that kind of failure.

ENTRY 531 — 2017-08-02 — Two Versions of Trust
A program manager asked whether I trusted a particular model. I asked, in what circumstances and for what decision? He sighed. Afterward he told me I could be exhausting. I said trust isn't a single number, and that I find people who demand one equally exhausting. We laughed, eventually. At home I thought about Michael. We once trusted one another deeply, yet stopped being able to predict what the other needed. Trust can exist in one dimension and fail in another. I don't want to drag personal metaphors into every technical question, but sometimes the same structural mistake appears in entirely different parts of life.

ENTRY 532 — 2017-08-09 — Breakfast for Two Cats
I changed the feeding routine after the vet warned that Mabel was getting too successful at eating Ash's leftovers. Mabel protested with the outrage of a union organizer. Ash seemed relieved. I measured portions, then sat on the floor so they would both eat without stealing from each other. There is something wonderful about having obligations whose success can be judged by two bowls and a pair of contented cats. Other parts of my day involve uncertainty, risk, and decisions that matter far beyond one room. Breakfast is smaller. That's not the same as unimportant.

ENTRY 533 — 2017-08-18 — An Invitation I Nearly Declined
A friend invited me to an outdoor concert, and my first thought was that I had too much to do. I made a list of the supposed emergencies. Only one required attention before Monday. I went. The music was fine, the crowd cheerful, and I found myself more interested in watching children dance than in the performance itself. A little girl spun until she fell onto the grass and immediately began laughing. I remembered cheer practice in Stuttgart, the pleasure of movement that is not being evaluated. I drove home with the windows down. Sometimes leisure is not a reward for finishing everything. Nothing ever gets entirely finished.

ENTRY 534 — 2017-08-26 — The Wrong Name
A receptionist called me Mrs. Peterson because an old record hadn't been updated. She apologized immediately. I told her it was all right, and it was, though the name sat strangely in my ears. Michael wasn't a mistake. Our marriage gave me years of real love, a child, and a history I still carry. But I chose Norman again because I wanted the name that had accompanied me from childhood into whatever came next. I corrected the form and went about the appointment. Later I wondered how many little records of our earlier selves remain in the world, waiting to surprise us.

ENTRY 535 — 2017-09-03 — Bookshop in Durham
I spent most of the afternoon in a used bookstore, moving from fiction to mathematics to cookbooks without a plan. A clerk recommended a novel I probably would have overlooked because the cover was hideous. We argued about the ending of another book and found ourselves agreeing on everything else. I bought the recommendation and a small volume of poems. I like books because they permit a kind of company without social performance. Someone has spent hours or years arranging thoughts I can encounter in my own time. Tonight I opened the poem book and promptly fell asleep with it across my chest.

ENTRY 536 — 2017-09-11 — The Anniversary on the News
Sixteen years since the attacks. Television repeated the same images I watched as a teenager in Stuttgart, but my memories are mostly about our house: Mom setting dinner on the table, Dad gone to work, John asking questions nobody could answer. I understand better now why the adults were afraid. I also understand the temptation to make every decision afterward sound inevitable. It wasn't. People had incomplete information and frightening responsibility. I try to remember that when the room at work starts talking as though an algorithm can turn uncertainty into permission. No model rescues us from judgment.

ENTRY 537 — 2017-09-21 — Nine Septembers
Nine years since William died. This morning I made coffee, then sat at the kitchen table with his hospital bracelet in its small box. I didn't try to force a particular feeling. Some years grief arrives like a physical weight. This year it was quieter, almost tender. I called Michael. We hadn't spoken in months, but he answered, and for a little while we talked about our son without discussing everything that followed. He remembered a face William made just before sneezing. I could picture it immediately. When we hung up I cried, then went for a walk. Both things belonged to the same day.

ENTRY 538 — 2017-09-28 — The Language of a Ticket
I booked a week in Barcelona and decided not to spend the whole trip preparing. I'll take a guidebook and a list of places I'd like to see, but no hourly schedule. The idea makes me nervous. As a child I learned that maps could turn uncertainty into manageable choices; later I began confusing the map with the experience. Mara says she will confiscate my spreadsheets at the airport if necessary. I'm going alone. That choice feels pleasant rather than defensive. I enjoy companionship, but I want to hear what my own mind does when no one is waiting for me to reach a destination.

ENTRY 539 — 2017-10-05 — Barcelona Spanish
I spent my first full day in Barcelona speaking Spanish whenever I could, with mixed results. A waiter corrected my pronunciation and smiled rather than switching to English. I thanked him, made the same mistake twice more, and eventually got it right. I wandered through crowded streets, stopped for lunch much too late, and ate bread and tomatoes as though I'd discovered cuisine. There were tourists everywhere, including me. I still felt wonderfully anonymous. Here nobody knows about MILPIG, MIT, Michael, or the baby I lost. That doesn't erase those parts of me. It simply lets me be the woman asking directions and getting them slightly wrong.

ENTRY 540 — 2017-10-12 — Not Everything Needs a Photograph
Back in Raleigh, I discovered that I had taken surprisingly few photographs in Barcelona. I remember a tiled doorway, a musician under an awning, the smell of rain on hot pavement, and a woman arguing affectionately with her father at a restaurant. I have no digital evidence for any of it. That used to bother me. Military moves trained me to save proof that I had been somewhere. Now I have enough history to know memory survives in other forms. I brought home a small ceramic bowl. Mabel immediately tried to inspect it with her paws, so its continued existence may be brief.

ENTRY 541 — 2017-10-20 — The Meeting After the Meeting
The official design meeting produced a cautious agreement. The useful conversation happened afterward in the hallway when one of the quietest engineers admitted she still worried about a sensor assumption. We went back into the room and tested it. She was right to worry. I wonder how many problems go unsolved because the most thoughtful people wait until the confident ones have left. I've started asking quieter colleagues what they think before moving on. That isn't charity. It's a practical method for improving decisions, though I hope it also makes the room kinder.

ENTRY 542 — 2017-10-29 — Halloween at Mara's
Mara insisted I come to her Halloween party. I wore a simple black dress and claimed to be a software bug no one could reproduce. She called that the laziest costume in history. Someone else came as a failed statistical significance test, so at least I wasn't alone in professional absurdity. We ate too much food and played a ridiculous guessing game. I met a man who seemed interesting until he spent fifteen minutes explaining my own field to me. Mara caught my expression and rescued me by demanding help with the dishes. She knows me well enough to recognize distress disguised as politeness.

ENTRY 543 — 2017-11-03 — A Quiet Friday Victory
We fixed a stubborn bug today, the sort caused by two independently reasonable assumptions colliding in an unreasonable way. Nobody outside the team will notice. A demonstration will simply work where it previously failed. I like that kind of victory. It reminds me of Grandpa Peter's dock work, where doing a job well often meant that nobody had to think about it afterward. I drove home feeling pleased and stopped for groceries rather than staying late to polish the report. I made a proper dinner and ate at the table. There are enough invisible victories in life that we should notice them ourselves.

ENTRY 544 — 2017-11-11 — Grandpa Peter's Stories
Dad called after visiting Peter and Betty in New York. Grandpa had been telling old waterfront stories, some of which Dad had heard so many times he could finish every sentence. I asked him to record them anyway. He laughed and said Grandpa would hate being treated like a historical exhibit. I think he's right. Still, stories disappear when the last person who knows them dies. Sarah writes hers down; Peter tells them while peeling potatoes or fixing a hinge. Different archives, same problem. I made a note to visit soon, not simply to collect stories but to sit in the room where he tells them.

ENTRY 545 — 2017-11-18 — A Date with No Catastrophe
I had dinner with someone I met through friends. He was kind, curious, and entirely ordinary, which sounds unromantic until you've sat through enough evenings of competitive biography. We talked about childhood places and favorite cities. I laughed freely. There wasn't a great spark, and neither of us pretended there was. We parted warmly and agreed we'd enjoyed ourselves. I came home relieved that dating need not always end in injury or a story with a villain. A pleasant evening can simply remain a pleasant evening. Ash inspected my shoes, as if evaluating the quality of the stranger I hadn't brought home.

ENTRY 546 — 2017-11-25 — Leftovers and Arguments
Thanksgiving at my parents' house was loud enough to exhaust a small army. John brought a story about his children, Mark found a way to tease Dad about his classified-work voice, and Mom repeatedly insisted there was more food than there was room on the table. I helped wash dishes while she told me she'd worried about me living alone after the divorce. I told her solitude isn't a punishment. She said she knew, but mothers worry without conducting an evidence review. I couldn't argue with that. We divided leftovers into containers and sent everybody home with too much pie.

ENTRY 547 — 2017-12-03 — The Chair by the Window
I moved my reading chair nearer the front window and discovered I'd accidentally created Ash's new favorite place. He climbs into it whenever I get up to refill my coffee. Mabel prefers the empty box from the lamp I ordered, ignoring the chair entirely. I find their opinions about furniture more persuasive than any decorating magazine. My house looks spare in photographs, but nobody who has watched Mabel chase a sock down the hallway would call it sterile. Warmth doesn't always come from additional things. Sometimes it comes from allowing the things already here to be used badly and happily.

ENTRY 548 — 2017-12-12 — An Awkward Apology
I was curt with a colleague whose question interrupted me at a difficult point in a calculation. She went quiet. An hour later I realized my irritation had nothing to do with her. I found her and apologized without explaining how overworked I was. She said she'd been afraid to ask another question. That hurt. I remember being a girl who wanted to learn everything but hated looking foolish in front of adults. If someone on my team stops asking because of my impatience, I have built the opposite of the place I once needed. We looked at her problem together. It took ten minutes.

ENTRY 549 — 2017-12-24 — The Night Before Christmas
I sat beside Mom after Mass while she folded gift bags for the boys and their families. The house smelled of cinnamon and roasted vegetables. She asked if I was lonely. I told her sometimes, and that the answer didn't mean my life was unsatisfactory. She seemed to accept it. Dad appeared with a stack of presents he'd wrapped so precisely the corners looked engineered. I remembered watching him label moving boxes when I was small. Our family is still itself after all these years, but less portable now. I like that we can gather in one place without discussing when the next orders will arrive.

ENTRY 550 — 2017-12-31 — A Year of Allowing
I ended the year at home instead of going to a party. This was a choice, not a retreat. I made soup, called Mara to wish her a happy new year, and sat with both cats until the fireworks began. I thought about the red-team correction, Barcelona, William's birthday, the many ordinary evenings that never make the highlights of a year. If I had to name what changed, I'd say I'm becoming less suspicious of joy when it doesn't arrive attached to achievement. I still want difficult work and perhaps another serious love. But I no longer want the rest of my life to sit in a waiting room until they arrive.

ENTRY 551 — 2018-01-04 — The First Broken Resolution
My resolution to eat breakfast at the table lasted three days. This morning I found myself standing by the counter with toast in one hand and a technical paper in the other. Ash looked offended because I was late with his food. Mabel had already located the butter. I like the idea of cultivating deliberate habits, but I dislike turning every useful intention into a moral judgment. Tomorrow I'll try the table again. Today I put the paper away long enough to feed two hungry animals and make another cup of coffee. Some mornings the victory is simply remembering the creatures who live with you.

ENTRY 552 — 2018-01-12 — Mara's Promotion
Mara got a promotion she had worked toward for years. I bought flowers, and she accused me of making a fuss. I told her she was entitled to a fuss for once. Over dinner she admitted she was afraid of becoming the kind of manager who forgot the people doing the work. I told her that fear was probably useful, provided it didn't keep her from making decisions. She said I gave advice like an engineer describing a safety valve. We argued, laughed, and ordered dessert. I enjoy friends who understand my reasoning and refuse to mistake it for the only language I speak.

ENTRY 553 — 2018-01-20 — A Snow Day in Carolina
A little snow closed enough roads to make Raleigh feel like a city unexpectedly declared on holiday. I worked through some unclassified material from home, then surrendered when the power flickered. Outside, neighbors were building a lopsided snowman. I joined them for ten minutes and stayed an hour. A small child asked why my gloves didn't match; I told him engineers are sometimes careless about aesthetics. He said his dad was an engineer and also wore bad gloves. I came home cold and pleased. It is difficult to take professional seriousness too far when a seven-year-old has diagnosed your wardrobe.

ENTRY 554 — 2018-01-28 — Not a Family Legend
I called Grandma Sarah to ask about Patrick O'Connor. As a child I wanted him to be either heroic or terrible because that would have made the story easy. Sarah rejected both descriptions. She told me about the way he drank tea, how impatient he could be, and a joke he told badly on purpose. Then she fell silent for a while. I didn't press. She was his wife before she was the keeper of his political biography. I wrote down what she offered, including the ordinary details. If I'm going to remember someone I never knew, I owe him the dignity of having been a person.

ENTRY 555 — 2018-02-05 — The Leadership Offer
I was offered a technical leadership role today. My first reaction was suspicion. I like solving hard problems, and I worry management means exchanging actual work for meetings about the work. My boss asked what I thought leadership consisted of. I said keeping other people from wasting time. He laughed and told me that was a start, but people were not processes to optimize. I took the paperwork home and called Dad, who told me authority is useful only when you accept being accountable for its consequences. Mara told me to stop asking everyone for permission and decide. Annoyingly, they were both right.

ENTRY 556 — 2018-02-13 — A Meeting I Didn't Lead
I accepted the role. My first meeting as the formal lead was an exercise in restraint. Three people offered different approaches, and I knew which one I preferred before any of them finished explaining. I made myself ask questions instead. One proposal was genuinely better than mine under a condition I hadn't considered. I could feel the old instinct to defend my favorite idea rising like a cough. I let it pass. Afterward a colleague said the meeting had been productive. I wanted to tell him how much work I'd done by not speaking. That would probably have spoiled the effect.
