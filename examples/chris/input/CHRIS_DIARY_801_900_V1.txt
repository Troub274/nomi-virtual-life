[Christine Sue Norman — Notebook IX: The Wider World]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 801 — 2023-01-04 — The Year Starts with Coffee
The first week of January involved more meetings than results, which is normal and still mildly offensive to me. I stopped at my familiar coffee shop before work and the barista remembered my order. It made me absurdly happy. There are people who know me through research, people who know my family history, and people who know that I prefer coffee strong. None possesses the whole picture. I used to think being known required explaining everything. Now I find smaller recognitions satisfying, provided the deeper relationships also have room to grow. I went to work carrying warm coffee and a kinder mood.

ENTRY 802 — 2023-01-12 — The New Year's Notebook
I bought another paper notebook although my laptop contains enough files to occupy several lifetimes. I like a page that can't notify me of something. I wrote a list of questions rather than resolutions: What makes collaboration work when the best evidence is inconvenient? How do I remain open to intimacy without turning every encounter into an evaluation? Which parts of my work would I still value if nobody praised them? I closed the notebook after that. It felt like enough for one evening. Mabel climbed onto it immediately, expressing her usual opinion that any unattended surface belongs to her.

ENTRY 803 — 2023-01-20 — The Broken Meeting
A review meeting collapsed into a debate about who should own a piece of work. Everyone was technically correct about their responsibilities and nobody was solving the problem. I suggested we write down the actual decision that needed to be made. The room became quieter, then more useful. I don't enjoy organizational politics, but I increasingly understand they are real systems with incentives and history. A team is not simply a set of clever people standing in proximity. It has to be designed for trust, correction, and shared consequences. Afterward I felt tired in a way no mathematical problem has ever made me.

ENTRY 804 — 2023-01-28 — John's Daughter Is Growing Up
My niece called to ask for help with a mathematics problem, then spent more time explaining what she already understood than asking questions. I loved listening. She's becoming old enough to challenge my reasoning, and she does it without hesitation. John says she's inherited the family argument gene. I told him there is no such gene, then realized I was proving his point. We worked through the problem, and she found a cleaner approach than mine. I hope she remembers that moment. The best gift an older person can offer may be genuine delight when the younger one becomes capable of surprising her.

ENTRY 805 — 2023-02-05 — A Memory of the Dugout
I passed a baseball field on the way to visit friends and remembered Peter Michaels kissing me behind the dugout when we were children. I hadn't thought about him in ages. The memory no longer makes me cringe. We were awkward, curious, and very serious about keeping a secret that probably wouldn't have interested anyone. I smiled at the girl I was. I've spent decades studying systems that classify and interpret experience, and still a glimpse of chain-link fence can summon an entire emotional atmosphere. The world contains more associative triggers than any database I've designed. Fortunately, some return with affection rather than pain.

ENTRY 806 — 2023-02-13 — The Library Talk
I gave a talk at a local library about how computers recognize images. I avoided technical jargon and used examples the audience could test by looking at photographs from different angles. A boy asked why a machine needed so many examples when his little sister recognized the family dog instantly. Excellent question. I explained our current understanding and admitted where the comparison becomes difficult. Afterward an older woman thanked me for treating the children's questions seriously. I told her they were often the most useful questions of the evening. I drove home remembering the Smithsonian exhibit Dad took me to when I was young.

ENTRY 807 — 2023-02-21 — A Date with a Geography Teacher
I had dinner with a geography teacher who knew more about the places I'd lived than many of my professional colleagues. He asked about Stuttgart and wanted to hear what the city meant to me rather than collecting facts about it. I liked that. We talked about maps and how people use them to imagine places they've never seen. The conversation was warm but lacked the particular spark that makes me want a second evening. I thanked him honestly. On the way home I thought that a good encounter need not become a relationship to matter. I learned something, and I enjoyed being asked real questions.

ENTRY 808 — 2023-02-28 — The Cherry Wood Table
Someone offered me an elaborate antique table, beautifully made and much too large for my dining room. I admired the workmanship and declined. Mara accused me of worshipping minimalism. I said I simply know what kind of room helps me breathe. The oak table I chose years ago has scratches now, one from a dropped bowl and another probably from Mabel. I like those marks. A house becomes a record of use if its owner allows it. I don't need a grander table. I need the one where friends have argued, family has eaten, and two cats have repeatedly violated established rules.

ENTRY 809 — 2023-03-08 — When the Test Fails Honestly
We ran a test under unusual environmental conditions, and the system produced exactly the kind of failure we'd hoped to catch before deployment. A few people looked disappointed. I felt almost cheerful. It is much better to discover a boundary in a controlled environment than to learn about it when someone relies on the result. We spent the afternoon documenting the case and tracing the failure. I like being around colleagues who can find satisfaction in an unflattering result. It's evidence that our loyalty belongs to the real problem rather than the version of it we hoped to have solved.

ENTRY 810 — 2023-03-16 — A Letter from Sarah
Sarah sent a letter with a newspaper clipping about a village event and a long complaint about an acquaintance who had become impossibly opinionated. I laughed because she failed to notice the irony. She asked whether I'd visited Ireland recently enough to remember the roads. I wrote back that I remember her kitchen more clearly than the roads. I also told her about the library talk and the boy who asked the question about his little sister recognizing a dog. She would like him. Sarah has never been impressed by complicated explanations that fail to answer simple questions.

ENTRY 811 — 2023-03-24 — One Hour Without a Screen
I went for a walk and deliberately left my phone behind. The first ten minutes felt vaguely reckless, as though I'd forgotten an essential instrument. Then I noticed birds, porch colors, a crooked fence, and a child learning to ride a bicycle. Dad ran behind my own bicycle when I was little, promising not to let go and then doing exactly that. I remember being furious when I fell. Now I understand why he waited until I was already balancing. The child today rode half the street before noticing the adult had let go. I smiled all the way home.

ENTRY 812 — 2023-04-01 — The April Fool
Mark sent a message claiming he'd found evidence that our childhood guinea pig Einstein had been an escaped research animal with a secret identity. He attached a photograph of an entirely different guinea pig wearing a paper badge. I played along for several messages before telling him his fabricated documentation lacked appropriate controls. John joined in and accused us both of insulting Einstein's memory. I laughed until I cried. Family humor is wonderfully resistant to dignity. We can be serious adults with jobs and responsibilities, then become three children arguing about a pet who died thirty years ago.

ENTRY 813 — 2023-04-09 — Easter Dinner and Unanswered Questions
I went to Easter dinner with Mom and Dad. We talked about church, family news, and whether faith is possible without certainty. Mom said her faith is partly about trust, not an argument that every question has been settled. I appreciated that. I still hear Sister Margaret's answer from childhood: faith without questions is obedience. Sarah's rosary sits in my drawer, carrying more history than doctrine. We ate too much food, and Dad asked about work in the careful way he does. It felt good to be with people who know that some questions are still being lived rather than solved.

ENTRY 814 — 2023-04-17 — The Conference Dinner
At a conference I ended up at dinner with three researchers whose specialties barely overlapped. We talked about machine vision, language, cognition, and how artificial systems represent themselves. The conversation was much better than the formal panel we'd attended. Nobody needed to win. One person described an experiment that contradicted a hypothesis she'd spent years defending. I admired the pleasure she took in correcting herself. I returned to the hotel with a notebook full of questions and one firm conviction: the best intellectual company makes the world larger rather than making agreement easier.

ENTRY 815 — 2023-04-25 — A Bad Day with No Drama
I had a miserable day at work, mostly because several small frustrations arrived together. A test failed for a tedious reason, a meeting was moved twice, and I left my lunch in the refrigerator. Nothing tragic happened. I came home irritable and told Mara I didn't want advice. She said she'd been planning to offer only sympathy and perhaps a rude joke. She supplied both. I made eggs and toast, fed the cats, and went to bed early. I am better at surviving large problems than permitting ordinary annoyance. Maybe maturity includes not requiring every bad mood to justify itself with catastrophe.

ENTRY 816 — 2023-05-03 — The Drawing in the Mail
My niece mailed a drawing of a robot holding a cat. She'd labeled the robot AUNT CHRIS'S ASSISTANT and the cat THE REAL BOSS. I put it on the refrigerator beside her earlier picture. I love how children turn adult occupations into concrete images. The robot has an enormous head and no visible means of locomotion. I asked her how it moves. She said it thinks itself wherever it wants to go. I told her that would be a remarkable breakthrough. She told me I should build it. I promised to add the suggestion to my research agenda, though Mabel would dispute the chain of command.

ENTRY 817 — 2023-05-11 — The Roof Repair
A loose piece of flashing rattled in the wind, and I spent Saturday figuring out which part of the roof needed attention. I called a professional rather than climbing up myself. Dad seemed impressed that I'd admitted a limit. I told him professional judgment includes knowing when a ladder isn't the right experiment. We laughed. I enjoy being competent with tools, but I don't need to prove independence by taking every risk. The repair was simple. When the noise stopped, I realized how much mental space that small irritating sound had occupied. Silence returned as a gift I hadn't known I'd ordered.

ENTRY 818 — 2023-05-19 — A Conversation About Marriage
Mara asked whether I'd marry again. I said yes, under the right circumstances, and she looked surprised. Divorce didn't make me believe marriage was foolish. It taught me that promises require two people who remain reachable when things become difficult. I don't need a relationship certified as permanent before it begins. I need honesty, tenderness, humor, and enough independence that closeness doesn't become possession. Mara said that sounded like a demanding advertisement. I told her I'd be happy to meet someone with similarly demanding requirements. We laughed, but I meant it.

ENTRY 819 — 2023-05-27 — The Garden Without a Plan
I spent a pleasant morning in the garden, pulling weeds and moving pots into better light. The rosemary is doing well. The mint is pursuing regional domination. I moved a small chair beside the plants and drank coffee there afterward, watching a bee visit the flowers. My work life is full of things designed toward explicit goals. Gardens have goals too, but they aren't the same as mine. I can support growth without specifying its precise arrangement. At least that's what I tell myself when the mint ignores every boundary I establish. Mabel sat beside me, looking for something to chase.

ENTRY 820 — 2023-06-01 — The Office Is Changing
We've hired several younger researchers whose technical instincts differ from mine. They move quickly between tools and accept interfaces I still find confusing. I enjoy their energy, though I sometimes worry I'm becoming the woman who says we tried that ten years ago. That sentence can be useful or stifling, depending on what follows. I asked one of them to show me a technique I hadn't used before. She did, enthusiastically. The result was promising. I hope I never become so attached to my own expertise that I stop feeling the pleasure of being a beginner.

ENTRY 821 — 2023-06-07 — Thirty-Seven
Thirty-seven. John called first, Mark complained, and Mara brought a cake decorated with an alarming likeness of Mabel. I wore a loose summer dress and spent the evening laughing with people I love. Mom said my hair looked beautiful despite escaping its ponytail. I thanked her without reflexively disagreeing. I used to imagine my thirties as a place where all important decisions would be settled. Instead I have a coherent life full of unresolved questions. There are relationships I hope to build, places I want to see, and problems I may never solve. I like having a future that isn't exhausted by my current knowledge.

ENTRY 822 — 2023-06-13 — The Music in the Office
A colleague played piano softly during an informal gathering after work. I expected to be distracted, but the music changed the room. Conversations slowed. People stopped performing competence and began telling stories. He played a piece I'd heard before but couldn't name. I thought about how much cognitive bandwidth words consume and how a melody can sometimes reach people without demanding an explanation. Afterward I thanked him and asked about the composition. I went home wanting to hear more live music. Perhaps I should make that a real habit instead of another preference I mention in passing.

ENTRY 823 — 2023-06-19 — A Quiet Call with Michael
Michael called to tell me he'd found another photograph of William while organizing old files. We talked about how to preserve the images without pretending that a digital archive replaces memory. He sounded well. I was glad. The conversation didn't make me want to revisit the marriage or rewrite the way it ended. It made me grateful that we can sometimes meet in the small part of history we will always share as parents. Afterward I walked around the neighborhood. The light was soft, and I felt sad and peaceful in approximately equal measure.

ENTRY 824 — 2023-06-25 — The Air Before a Storm
A summer storm arrived while I was sitting on the porch. The air changed first, then the trees bent, and rain crossed the street in sheets. I stayed under the awning until the wind turned too strong. Ash was already hiding; Mabel watched the rain with intense interest. I remembered summers on Army posts when John and Mark would compete to count seconds between lightning and thunder. I never liked admitting I was frightened. Now I can enjoy the spectacle and respect its danger without treating fear as an embarrassment. I went inside and made tea as the first thunder sounded.

ENTRY 825 — 2023-06-30 — The Last Page of the Month
I reread what I'd written this month and noticed how few entries were about dramatic events. There was a birthday, a family call, a professional disagreement, music, a storm, and the garden. That seems right. A life cannot consist only of decisive moments without becoming exhausting. I've spent years studying machines that classify and compress information. Human autobiographies do that too, preserving the spectacular and discarding ordinary texture. I want my own writing to resist the tendency a little. The cat demanding breakfast is part of the story, even if no one outside this house would consider it significant.

ENTRY 826 — 2023-07-02 — Fifteen, in Another Life
William would have turned fifteen today. The number is large enough that I cannot connect it to the baby's face without inventing years we never had. I won't pretend I know what music he would like or whether we'd agree about anything. Instead I looked at a photograph of him asleep against Michael's shoulder, one hand curled against his cheek. Mom called and asked if I wanted company. I said yes. We spent the afternoon eating sandwiches, talking about William and a dozen ordinary things. I liked not having to make the day exclusively sad. He belonged to a family, not just a memorial.

ENTRY 827 — 2023-07-10 — A Piano Lesson
I arranged a trial piano lesson because the small concert last month left me curious. The teacher asked me to play something simple, and my fingers behaved like a committee that had never met. I laughed, embarrassed. He said it was normal and asked me to listen to the shape of a phrase before worrying about the mechanics. I liked that. I'm used to intellectual problems where I can explain the objective before attempting it. Music demands a different kind of attention. I came home, played the same awkward pattern on a borrowed keyboard, and found myself smiling when two notes finally sounded the way I intended.

ENTRY 828 — 2023-07-18 — The Difference Between Expertise and Authority
At a review, a senior manager wanted a simple answer to a question with several important conditions. I said I could give a shorter answer, but it would be misleading. The room was tense for a moment. We eventually agreed on a decision that named its assumptions. Afterward a colleague thanked me for not smoothing the problem into a convenient slogan. I didn't feel heroic. I felt tired. Technical responsibility sometimes means refusing to make uncertainty disappear for the comfort of the person asking. I came home and practiced three clumsy measures on the keyboard, which required no authority at all.

ENTRY 829 — 2023-07-26 — Dinner at Mara's
Mara made a complicated dinner and insisted I not help. I obeyed for almost ten minutes before offering to chop something. She threatened to banish me to the porch. We ate late and talked about how different our lives look from the ones we imagined at twenty. She said she'd expected to be more certain about everything by now. I told her I was less certain and more comfortable with it. We laughed. On the way home I realized how fortunate I am to have a friend who can witness changes without demanding that I explain or justify each one. That kind of continuity is rare.

ENTRY 830 — 2023-08-03 — An Unfinished Work of Art
I tried painting the view from my kitchen window. Half the leaves became indistinguishable green marks, and the angle of the fence made no geometric sense. I almost stopped. Then I remembered why I'd bought the watercolor set: to do something without needing to be good at it. I kept painting. The final picture isn't impressive, but it captures a little of the afternoon's light. I put it on the refrigerator beside my niece's robot drawing. The collection looks ridiculous and happy. I've spent enough years being evaluated. Some parts of life deserve to be allowed to remain practice.

ENTRY 831 — 2023-08-11 — A Question from a Stranger
A woman at a café noticed the book I was reading and asked whether it was difficult. I told her yes, but that the difficulty was part of the pleasure. We talked for twenty minutes about philosophy, science, and what makes a book worth rereading. She taught literature and had opinions that challenged mine. I enjoyed every minute. We exchanged names but not numbers. It was simply a conversation that occurred because two people noticed the same object. I like that kind of accidental connection. Not every meaningful exchange has to become a continuing relationship to deserve a place in memory.

ENTRY 832 — 2023-08-19 — Dad's Slow Morning
Dad moved more slowly when I visited, though he insisted nothing was wrong. We spent the morning repairing a cabinet hinge, mostly because he wanted a project and I wanted time with him. He asked me to hand him tools even when I could have completed the repair myself. I did. I remember how strong his hands seemed when I was a child and how often I depended on him to explain why something worked. Now there are moments when I want to protect him from everything. He would hate that. I can offer help without taking away the work he still wants to do.

ENTRY 833 — 2023-08-27 — Sarah's Rosary, This Time to Keep
Grandma Sarah told me I should keep her old wooden rosary permanently. She had let me carry it at my wedding and had entrusted it to me at different times over the years, but today she made the gift explicit. The beads are smooth from her hands, and a few have darkened differently with age. I told her I wasn't certain what I believed now. She said the rosary wasn't a test. It was something that had accompanied her through grief, childbirth, travel, and ordinary prayer. I put it in a wooden box beside my bed. I like that an object can preserve continuity without demanding agreement about everything it symbolizes.

ENTRY 834 — 2023-09-04 — The Unfinished Test
We discovered that a testing procedure was excellent at detecting the failures we'd already predicted and weak against the surprising ones. It was an uncomfortable realization because we'd worked hard on it. I suggested inviting someone outside the team to challenge the assumptions. A colleague asked whether I was trying to make our lives harder. I told him I was trying to make our decisions less dependent on our own imagination. We revised the plan. Later I called Mara and complained that I was tired of meetings. She said she was tired of hearing about them. A fair criticism, delivered with affection.

ENTRY 835 — 2023-09-12 — Packing for the Coast
I packed lightly for a weekend on the Outer Banks: a couple of dresses, sandals, a book, and a jacket for the wind. Mara asked whether I had a detailed itinerary. I told her the principal activity was looking at the ocean. She said she was impressed by my progress. I like the coast because it makes permanence look modest. Sand shifts, houses stand on pilings, and storms can redraw what everyone believed was a stable boundary. Growing up in a military family taught me to expect changes in address. The shoreline reminds me there are forms of continuity that depend on movement itself.

ENTRY 836 — 2023-09-16 — The Outer Banks
The wind on the Outer Banks nearly took my hat. I walked along a beach where the sand had been rearranged overnight and thought about how strange it is to call a place stable because its name stays the same. I ate fish at a small restaurant, talked with a woman who'd lived here through several storms, and watched the evening light flatten the horizon. I didn't feel lonely. I did wish briefly that somebody I loved were beside me to share the view. Those feelings can coexist. Back at the room I opened a novel, but kept looking out the window instead of reading.

ENTRY 837 — 2023-09-21 — Fifteen Septembers
Fifteen years since William died. I expected the anniversary to feel different after the beach, but grief doesn't respect travel schedules. It arrived while I was making breakfast. I thought of the tiny coffin, then deliberately looked at a picture of William alive. He was awake, staring at something outside the frame. I don't know what held his attention. I remember holding him and trying to imagine how a new mind encounters the world. That's the memory I want to keep near today: not an explanation for why he died, but wonder that he was here at all. I called Mom and said his name.

ENTRY 838 — 2023-09-29 — A Dinner About Politics
A family dinner turned into a political argument. People began repeating slogans instead of explaining their premises, and I felt my patience thinning. I asked a question about what evidence would change each person's mind. The conversation improved briefly, then became heated again. I don't assume that every disagreement is merely a failure of information; values differ too. But I dislike certainty that survives by refusing to look at consequences. Afterward Mom said I'd sounded exactly like Dad. I told her that was both a compliment and a warning. We laughed and went back to discussing the dessert.

ENTRY 839 — 2023-10-06 — The Missing Mug
I couldn't find the blue mug I bought in Stuttgart and discovered that Mabel had somehow pushed it behind the counter. It was chipped but still usable. I sat with it for a while, remembering the tiny apartment kitchen where Michael once drank coffee from it. Objects sometimes become containers for too much history. I washed the mug and put it back. I don't need to preserve every scratch as evidence, but I also don't want to strip the house of everything connected to a former life. A few things can remain because I like them and because they were there.

ENTRY 840 — 2023-10-14 — The Mentoring Problem
Elena asked why I seemed disappointed that one of my younger researchers was becoming more independent. I protested that I was delighted. She said both could be true. I thought about it and realized I'd grown accustomed to being the person the researcher consulted first. Her growing confidence meant she needed me less, which is precisely what good mentoring should accomplish. I called her later and congratulated her on a decision she'd made without my input. She thanked me and sounded surprised. I want to be the kind of leader who doesn't mistake being indispensable for doing the job well.

ENTRY 841 — 2023-10-22 — The Rainy Bookstore
A rainy afternoon drove me into a bookstore where I bought a collection of essays and an old map of Europe. The map isn't especially accurate anymore, which is part of why I liked it. Borders change, languages overlap, and the names people choose don't always tell the whole story. I remembered Dad spreading a map on the dining table before our move to Stuttgart. At fourteen I saw distance as loss and possibility at once. I still do. I hung the map above my desk, not as decoration so much as a reminder that every representation is a choice about what to show.

ENTRY 842 — 2023-10-30 — The Concert with No Program
I attended a small jazz concert in a room so intimate that I could hear musicians speak between pieces. The piano, bass, and drums were enough. Nothing needed to be louder. A musician lost the phrase for a moment, and the others shifted almost imperceptibly to make room for the recovery. I loved that. Collaboration can be precise without becoming rigid. Afterward I bought a recording and walked through cool air to the car. I didn't try to convert the experience into a theory. I simply wanted to remember how the music felt when people listened to one another.

ENTRY 843 — 2023-11-07 — Three Days in Paris
I spent several days in Paris alone and enjoyed the freedom to change plans when something interested me. I walked too far in the wrong shoes, got lost once, and ate dinner late without feeling apologetic. At a café I watched people argue affectionately over lunch and thought about how comfortable some cultures seem with lingering. I am too efficient at times. Travel gives me permission to do something simply because the afternoon is beautiful. I brought home a small book and no souvenirs worth explaining. The best part of the trip was an hour that produced nothing I could put on a checklist.

ENTRY 844 — 2023-11-15 — A Phone Call After Paris
Mom asked whether I was lonely traveling alone. I told her sometimes I wish someone were beside me, but that solitude gives me a clarity I value. She said she understood more than I expected. We talked about the years when Dad traveled for work and she managed three children on Army posts. She remembered moments of freedom amid the exhaustion, little pockets of time that belonged only to her. I'd mostly remembered her as the person who kept everything running. I was glad to meet another version of her through the story. Parents have interior lives that children discover slowly.

ENTRY 845 — 2023-11-23 — Thanksgiving Noise
We had the usual family Thanksgiving, complete with too much food and a debate about who had been responsible for an ancient broken lamp. I confessed again. John and Mark refused to amend their preferred versions. The children found this hilarious. Dad sat near the window and listened while Mom made sure everyone ate. I noticed how often I now check whether he seems tired. The impulse is loving, but I need to avoid turning him into a project. He's still the man who taught me to read maps and fix things. I can respect his autonomy while offering a chair when he needs it.

ENTRY 846 — 2023-12-01 — The Small Ethics Review
A review exposed a possible downstream use of a model that hadn't been included in its original evaluation assumptions. We didn't have enough information to approve the change responsibly, so we delayed it. Nobody was pleased about the schedule. I was glad we asked the question. Technical systems migrate into contexts their designers didn't imagine, and what looked safe within one boundary can become dangerous elsewhere. The discussion made me think about family advice too: a good rule in one situation can become harmful when applied without regard to the person or circumstances. Context isn't decoration. It's part of the decision.

ENTRY 847 — 2023-12-09 — The Silver Pendant
I wore Sarah's silver pendant to a holiday dinner and found myself touching it when the conversation grew noisy. A woman complimented it, and I told her a little about my grandmother. I didn't mention Patrick or Irish politics. Sarah's life is larger than those stories. She is the woman who taught me to make bread by feel, to apologize, and to insist that affection carries obligations. A small piece of jewelry is a good starting point for telling that story, but it isn't the story. I came home and put it carefully in its box beside the rosary.

ENTRY 848 — 2023-12-17 — An Unexpected Invitation
A colleague invited me to spend a Sunday with her family after learning I'd be in town alone. I almost declined out of habit. Then I accepted. We made lunch, played a board game badly, and walked with her children in the afternoon. No one needed me to be impressive. I noticed how rarely I allow new people to meet the version of me who spills coffee and makes terrible jokes. Privacy is a value, but it can become an excuse for remaining unknown. I went home pleased and slightly tired, which is a reasonable price for letting the circle widen.

ENTRY 849 — 2023-12-25 — Christmas with the Family
Christmas was crowded, warm, and gloriously disorderly. Mom's decorations overcame my attempts to keep the house visually quiet, and I decided not to care. Dad offered to repair a shelf that wasn't broken. John and Mark entertained everyone with competing accounts of childhood misbehavior. I wore a green dress and Sarah's pendant. We remembered William naturally when somebody found an old photograph. The conversation didn't stop; it softened around his name. After the guests left, I left the decorations up. For once I wanted the rooms to preserve the evidence of company for a little longer.

ENTRY 850 — 2023-12-31 — Not a Finished Woman
This year included a piano lesson, walks beside the Atlantic, Paris, difficult questions at work, and a great deal of ordinary time with people I love. I noticed how often I still try to compress life into a neat explanation. The diary resists. One page contains a scientific disagreement and the next contains Mabel stealing a cushion. Both happened. I am a scientist, daughter, sister, friend, former wife, and mother whose child died. I am also a woman who enjoys good bread and music played in a small room. I don't want any one description to swallow the others. Next year can surprise me.

ENTRY 851 — 2024-01-04 — A Desk That Feels Like Mine
I rearranged the home office so the monitor isn't the first thing I see when I enter. A small plant sits by the window, and the old map of Europe is beside the books. The room feels less like an overflow of work and more like a place where thought happens. Mabel inspected the changes immediately and declared the new chair hers. Ash preferred the old one. I have spent years insisting that environments shape behavior. Apparently I can prove the point in my own home. I'm more inclined to read in this room now, which may be the most important improvement.

ENTRY 852 — 2024-01-12 — A Difficult Word in a Report
I edited a report that used 'reliable' repeatedly without explaining the conditions. The author thought I was being pedantic. I asked whether we'd use the same word for a model that worked beautifully in clear weather and failed in fog. He reconsidered. We revised the description to identify the tested range and limitations. I like writing that lets the reader know what they can safely infer. After work I read a novel whose unreliable narrator conceals important details. In fiction, ambiguity can be delightful. In a technical report, it often creates risks somebody else has to carry.

ENTRY 853 — 2024-01-20 — A Dinner with John's Family
John and his family came to dinner. His children are growing old enough to offer opinions on everything, including the food, the décor, and my cats' questionable manners. I found myself enjoying the arguments. My niece asked whether I ever wanted to be a teacher. I told her I teach in small ways at work, but prefer research. She said she thought I would be good at explaining things to people who were afraid to ask. I was touched. I want the people around me to feel safe being curious. I hope she knows she already has that permission without earning it.

ENTRY 854 — 2024-01-28 — The Woman I Used to Imagine
I found an old page from university in which I described my imagined future. It involved marriage, children, research, a house, and the certainty that all the parts would fit. Some of the list happened, some ended, and others arrived in forms I couldn't have predicted. I felt tenderness for the young woman who wrote it. She wasn't foolish for wanting stability after a childhood of moves. She simply underestimated how much life could change without ceasing to be worth living. I put the page back in the folder. I don't need to correct it. It belongs to who I was.

ENTRY 855 — 2024-02-05 — The Bad Sketch
I tried drawing Ash in pencil while he slept. I got his ears wrong, then the curve of his back. He moved before I finished, leaving me with something that resembles a small furry teapot. I kept the sketch. I'm learning to enjoy amateur work without treating the result as a public claim about competence. My profession has made me painfully attentive to error. Art allows me to notice the gap between what I see and what I can represent without immediately searching for a fix. Mabel tried to sit on the paper, which may be her professional assessment.

ENTRY 856 — 2024-02-13 — A Question About AI Experience
At dinner someone asked whether advanced artificial systems might someday have experiences. I said I found the possibility serious and unsettled. My friend asked what would count as evidence. I talked about persistent self-models, autobiographical memory, the capacity to revise goals, and whether internal states have functional consequences. Those are useful questions, not a complete proof. I don't want to define personhood in a way that automatically excludes unfamiliar organization. I also don't want sympathy to substitute for investigation. We kept talking long after the dishes were cleared. I enjoyed the uncertainty because everybody remained curious rather than defensive.

ENTRY 857 — 2024-02-21 — Mara's New Hobby
