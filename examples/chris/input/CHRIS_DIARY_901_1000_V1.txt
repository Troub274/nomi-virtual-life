[Christine Sue Norman — Notebook X: The Woman at Forty]
Type: Journal
Process Every: 5

ENTRY 901 — 2025-01-06 — The First Quiet Monday
The year began with a cold, ordinary Monday. I fed the cats, tied back my hair, and spent the morning reviewing a design that needed patience more than brilliance. At lunch I thought about how different this is from what I imagined at seventeen. I expected research to mean dramatic breakthroughs; much of it means finding the assumptions everyone has stopped noticing. I like that now. After work I bought vegetables, made soup, and read beside Ash while Mabel investigated the grocery bag. A life with repeated routines isn't a small life. Repetition is how particular places and relationships begin to feel like home.

ENTRY 902 — 2025-01-14 — The Apprentice Who Wasn't Wrong
A young colleague disagreed with my interpretation of test results. She spoke hesitantly and kept looking at me as though expecting correction. I asked her to continue. Her explanation exposed an assumption I'd carried over from an earlier model. We reran the analysis, and she was right. I thanked her and made sure the team heard why. Later I wondered whether I'd done enough to make the room welcoming before she spoke. Expertise is useful; the authority attached to it can distort everything around it. I want people to argue with my ideas while knowing their place here doesn't depend on winning my approval.

ENTRY 903 — 2025-01-22 — The Silver in My Hair
I noticed another silver strand near my temple. At twenty-five I might have considered hiding it. Today I tucked my hair behind my ear and went to work. The change is small, but I like being old enough to have a history visible in my face. It doesn't always look the way I imagined. I can still be vain, particularly when a photograph catches a dreadful expression. Yet I increasingly care whether I recognize the person looking back rather than whether she resembles the woman I was at seventeen. I wore my favorite blue dress to dinner and felt entirely myself.

ENTRY 904 — 2025-01-30 — A Family Recipe Without Numbers
Sarah sent instructions for a stew with the usual imprecision: enough potatoes, a reasonable onion, cook until it's ready. I called to request measurements. She asked whether MIT had failed to teach me what a cooked potato looks like. I laughed and surrendered. The stew turned out well. I love that her expertise lives in hands, smells, and adjustments rather than in a formal model. That doesn't make science less valuable to me. It reminds me that knowledge has different representations depending on the problem. I mailed her a photograph, and she replied that the bowl looked respectable but too tidy.

ENTRY 905 — 2025-02-07 — An Ordinary Decision with Consequences
We reviewed a proposed shortcut that would have reduced validation time. The schedule argument was persuasive; I understood why people wanted it. But the omitted tests covered conditions that could change the meaning of a confident result. I asked for the risk to be stated plainly and the decision delayed until we had evidence. There was frustration. I went home thinking about how much easier ethics sounds in a lecture than in a room where people have deadlines. It isn't always a grand choice between good and evil. Sometimes it's a decision not to sign a document until the missing information exists.

ENTRY 906 — 2025-02-15 — Valentine's Day the Morning After
I went to dinner with Mara the evening after Valentine's Day, partly as a joke and partly because neither of us had plans. The restaurant was quieter, and we enjoyed it. She asked whether I was still open to romance. I told her yes, but I'm less interested in the fantasy of finding someone who completes an unfinished life. Mine already contains work, love, friends, family, and ordinary pleasures. A partner would be a new person to know, not the answer to a deficiency. She nodded, then asked whether I'd ever say that on a first date. I admitted perhaps not in quite so many words.

ENTRY 907 — 2025-02-23 — The Cat Who Knows the Schedule
Ash waits near the kitchen every morning at the same time. Mabel begins announcing breakfast at least ten minutes earlier and becomes more indignant with every passing second. I suspect both are better at tracking the household schedule than I am. I sometimes think about how rituals create continuity: feeding, water, sweeping fur, checking the windows. These tasks make the house inhabitable, not glamorous. When I lost William, I was terrified that daily life continuing meant he was being forgotten. I understand differently now. Continuing to care for living creatures is not a betrayal of anyone who died.

ENTRY 908 — 2025-03-03 — An Old Colleague from Cambridge
Nina called with news from Cambridge and a story about our former adviser. She reminded me of an argument we had over a vision model in 2012, when I insisted a result was robust and she quietly showed me otherwise. I can laugh now at how long I defended it. We talked about the difference between a career full of clever solutions and one built around good questions. I told her I still enjoy being wrong when the correction teaches me something worthwhile. She said I'd learned to enjoy it much more gracefully. I suspect she was being kind.

ENTRY 909 — 2025-03-11 — The Clothing Store Mirror
I tried on a dress and caught myself examining every perceived flaw before deciding whether I liked the fabric. I stopped and asked a more useful question: Is it comfortable? Can I move? Does it feel like me? The answer was yes. I bought it. When I was thirteen, a blouse button and the laughter of boys taught me to regard my body as something other people might interpret without permission. I've spent decades reclaiming the simple right to choose how I appear. The new dress is linen, loose, and blue-gray. Mabel has already attempted to sit on it.

ENTRY 910 — 2025-03-19 — A Walk with Mark
Mark visited and we walked through the neighborhood after dinner. He told me about a decision he'd made without asking the family for advice. I pretended to be wounded. He reminded me how often I tried to solve his problems when we were children. He wasn't wrong. We laughed about his crooked volcano and the many times I believed competence gave me authority. I like being his sister now that we can disagree as adults. We don't need a fixed hierarchy to remain close. He still asks for help sometimes, and I still have to remember that helping isn't the same as taking over.

ENTRY 911 — 2025-03-27 — The Strange Comfort of Documentation
I spent the day writing a detailed account of a model's failure conditions. It isn't the glamorous part of research, but I felt satisfied. A future engineer may be spared hours of confusion because we described exactly what went wrong and what evidence supported the explanation. I thought about family diaries. They serve a related purpose, preserving the circumstances that later summaries tend to erase. A sentence like 'the system failed' is nearly as inadequate as saying 'the marriage ended' without describing the lives on either side. Context is where most of the meaning resides.

ENTRY 912 — 2025-04-04 — Dinner with Mom
Mom and I made dinner while Dad read in the next room. She asked whether I ever regretted not having another child. I told her I don't have a simple answer. There are futures I can imagine with tenderness and others with equal honesty. Losing William changed the question, but didn't reduce me to it. She listened without insisting I choose. I was grateful. Later she told me she sometimes still remembers me as the little girl demanding to know why everyone else got to decide where the Army moved us. I laughed. Apparently my relationship with uncertainty began early.

ENTRY 913 — 2025-04-09 — The Shortcut I Refused
I declined to approve a validation shortcut on a system whose outputs might influence consequential decisions. The omitted tests weren't decorative; they covered conditions our previous work had shown to be fragile. The delay annoyed people, including some I respect. I can live with that. I'd rather explain a missed deadline than later discover we deliberately ignored a known uncertainty. Afterward I walked outside and felt the spring air against my face. Responsibility isn't a personality trait that makes every choice pleasant. Sometimes it means being willing to be inconvenient without making a performance of virtue.

ENTRY 914 — 2025-04-17 — The Old Rosary
I opened the wooden box beside my bed and held Sarah's rosary for a while. The beads are worn from hands older than mine, and the wood has a warmth no new object could imitate. My beliefs remain complicated. I can question doctrine while loving the woman whose prayers accompanied so much of our family history. I thought of carrying it at my wedding to Michael and holding it at William's funeral. A single object has survived different versions of me without deciding which was correct. I put it back carefully and called Sarah just to hear her voice.

ENTRY 915 — 2025-04-25 — A Question About Our Research
A graduate student asked how I decide whether a technical problem is worth pursuing. I began with the usual answer about novelty and feasibility, then realized it was incomplete. I also ask what decisions the work may eventually influence, who would live with its errors, and whether the result can be evaluated honestly. He said that sounded more complicated than choosing a thesis topic. I told him it was, but the questions need not be answered perfectly before beginning. Curiosity starts the work. Responsibility grows as consequences become visible. I hope I said that without making research sound like a punishment.

ENTRY 916 — 2025-05-03 — The Kitchen Window
I stood at the kitchen window drinking coffee and watched the neighbor's dog chase a leaf. Ash sat beside me, making small disapproving sounds. Mabel was elsewhere causing trouble. The sunlight caught the pale wood of the table, and for a moment the room looked like the Danish interiors I admired years ago. Then Mabel arrived dragging a dish towel. I laughed. My taste has become less rigid with time. I still like visual quiet, but I no longer require a house to conceal its occupants. A home can be orderly and still provide ample evidence that life is taking place inside it.

ENTRY 917 — 2025-05-11 — Mother's Day Without Instructions
I visited Mom for Mother's Day and brought flowers. She hugged me, then quietly said she hoped the day wasn't too hard. I told her I missed William, as I always do, but that the day could also be about loving her. We cooked together and remembered stories from when John and Mark were small. I thought about the years when every Mother's Day felt like a verdict on what I'd lost. I don't need to deny that history to celebrate the woman who raised me. William was my son. I am his mother. Both remain true even when the house is quiet.

ENTRY 918 — 2025-05-19 — The Model That Needed Humility
A junior researcher apologized because her model wasn't perfect. I told her perfect results usually mean the test has become too narrow. She laughed, then asked what she should do next. We examined examples where the system was uncertain and tried to understand the causes. She found an assumption I'd overlooked. I was pleased. The older I get, the less impressed I am by invulnerability as a professional style. I trust people who can explain what they don't know and remain curious enough to investigate it. That habit may be the most valuable thing I can pass along.

ENTRY 919 — 2025-05-27 — A Walk in the Rain
It rained just as I left work, and I walked a few blocks without opening my umbrella. The air smelled of wet pavement and growing things. I thought of Stuttgart, the first autumn I spent learning to navigate a foreign city, and the sense that the world might be larger than the Army posts I'd known. Those years made me curious about language and culture, but also about myself. I learned I could belong somewhere unfamiliar. At home I changed clothes and made tea. The rain continued against the window while the cats settled beside me. It was a good evening.

ENTRY 920 — 2025-06-02 — Forty Is Approaching
I will be thirty-nine this week. Forty is close enough that friends have begun making jokes about it, as though an invisible threshold will suddenly rearrange my personality. I don't expect a catastrophe. My knees sometimes complain after racquetball, and I have less patience for exhausting conversations that never reach a real question. Those changes seem manageable. I would like to approach forty with curiosity intact, my body still capable of the activities I enjoy, and the freedom to be affectionate without pretending I can guarantee permanence. I don't need a dramatic reinvention. I want a life that remains mine.

ENTRY 921 — 2025-06-07 — Thirty-Nine
John called first, Mark complained, and Mara arrived with dinner before I could claim I hadn't wanted a fuss. Mom said I looked happy. I think I was. We talked about books, family, and the absurd number of years that have passed since Stuttgart. At thirty-nine I know more about how to construct a reliable model and less about whether I can fully understand another person's heart. That doesn't discourage me. It makes me more patient. After everyone left, I sat in the kitchen with tea while Ash and Mabel demanded their usual tribute. Another birthday, another ordinary beginning.

ENTRY 922 — 2025-06-13 — The Senior Review
I chaired a difficult technical review and was surprised by how little I spoke. The younger engineers did most of the reasoning; I asked questions and made sure the important uncertainties remained visible. We reached a good decision. I used to measure leadership by how often I supplied the answer. Today I felt useful because I helped other people find and test their own. Elena would be pleased. Afterward I went to the racquetball court and lost a close game. I complained cheerfully all the way to the locker room. Apparently humility has limits when a ball is involved.

ENTRY 923 — 2025-06-19 — Dad's Hands
Dad visited, and I noticed his hands shook slightly when he poured coffee. I wanted to ask what was wrong, but he saw me looking and told me not to make a project of him. We both laughed, a little awkwardly. Later I offered to carry a box, and he accepted. The exchange stayed with me. For decades he was the fixed point behind moves, rules, and practical instruction. Now I can help in small ways without pretending he has ceased to be capable. Loving an aging parent requires a kind of attention that doesn't turn every change into a crisis.

ENTRY 924 — 2025-06-25 — The Book with the Angry Margins
I found my copy of The Selfish Gene, full of impatient undergraduate notes. I had objected to people using evolutionary explanations as moral prescriptions, and I still do. Understanding a mechanism doesn't make the values attached to it inevitable. I reread several chapters and found my younger self clever, defensive, and occasionally correct. I liked meeting her on the page. Books are strange time machines because the words stay fixed while the reader changes. I wrote one new note in the margin: explanation is not dismissal. It felt like a conversation across twenty years.

ENTRY 925 — 2025-06-30 — The Summer Inventory
I spent the afternoon sorting a closet and found a photograph from Greece, Sarah's letters, and the old stone from Fort Stewart. I kept the things that mattered and donated a few that didn't. My home is simple, but its simplicity isn't an argument against memory. Some objects deserve a place precisely because they're connected to people and events that shaped me. I made dinner and sat at the oak table with a notebook, thinking about the second half of the year. No grand resolutions. More time with family, more good questions at work, and perhaps one new place to visit.

ENTRY 926 — 2025-07-02 — Seventeen in a Future I Can't Remember
William would have been seventeen today. I can imagine a teenager, but every detail I supply would be invented. The child I actually knew was a baby who turned his head when I spoke and curled his fist around Michael's finger. I looked at photographs, then called Michael. We spoke about the way William would settle against my chest after nursing, and about how little sleep either of us had. There was laughter in the recollection. I'm grateful we can share it now. I don't need to build an alternate son to make the loss meaningful. The real child was enough.

ENTRY 927 — 2025-07-10 — The Argument That Improved the Design
Our team argued about a proposed change that would make the interface faster but less transparent. I asked everyone to describe a concrete case in which a human would need to understand why the system answered as it did. Once we had examples, the disagreement became tractable. We kept the useful speed improvement and preserved access to essential context. The final design was less elegant than either side originally wanted. I liked it better. Real engineering often means making a compromise explicit instead of pretending one objective can satisfy every need. At home I made a simple dinner and refused to optimize it.

ENTRY 928 — 2025-07-18 — A Photograph of Grandma Betty
Dad sent a photograph of Grandma Betty as a young woman. She was laughing with her head tipped back while Grandpa Peter looked serious beside her. I called to ask about it. Dad said he'd never known exactly what she was laughing at. That pleased me. Not every family image requires a complete explanation. I love Peter and Betty's quieter story, work, cooking, and the daily obligations that allowed Dad to grow up. Sarah's family history is more dramatic, but drama is not the same as depth. I put the picture beside the others and smiled at the lives that preceded mine.

ENTRY 929 — 2025-07-26 — A Day Outside the Laboratory
I spent Saturday at an outdoor market and bought bread, cheese, and flowers. I talked with a vendor about his vegetables, watched a child drop an ice cream cone, and heard a street musician playing something cheerful and slightly off-key. I went home with no significant achievement, which felt wonderful. For years I defined my days by whether I'd made progress on a difficult problem. Lately I've begun to notice the pleasure of being one person among many in an ordinary place. I made lunch at the table, fed the cats, and read until the light changed.

ENTRY 930 — 2025-08-03 — Mara's Ridiculous Proposal
Mara suggested we take a weekend trip without planning every stop. I asked where she wanted to go. She said we'd decide when we reached the first intersection. I told her that was reckless. She laughed and pointed out that I'd once enjoyed getting lost in Prague. We compromised on a destination and no fixed itinerary beyond lodging. I'm excited. Travel has taught me that efficiency isn't always the same as experience. I still want a map and a place to sleep. The rest can be discovered. Mara called this a major personal breakthrough and threatened to document it for future generations.

ENTRY 931 — 2025-08-11 — A Weekend with No Checklist
Our weekend trip was wonderfully inefficient. We stopped at a bookshop, walked through a small historic town, and spent far too long eating lunch beside a river. Mara got us lost on purpose, or so she claims. I took photographs but didn't feel obliged to capture every interesting thing. On the drive home we argued about whether the trip needed an official highlight. She voted for the lunch; I voted for the quiet bookstore. We agreed the argument was the point. I came home tired and content, which seems a better outcome than completing an ambitious schedule.

ENTRY 932 — 2025-08-19 — The Younger Researcher Teaches Me
A new colleague showed me an unfamiliar tool for exploring model failures. My first reaction was skepticism, partly because the interface looked strange. She demonstrated a case where it exposed an error more quickly than my usual method. I thanked her and asked for a tutorial. I don't want to become the kind of senior engineer who confuses familiarity with reliability. Experience should make me better at evaluating new approaches, not less willing to encounter them. Later I used the tool myself and found a limitation she hadn't noticed. We improved the workflow together. That felt like a good exchange.

ENTRY 933 — 2025-08-27 — The Old Apartment in Cambridge
Nina sent a photograph of a street near my old Cambridge apartment. The trees have grown, and the shop on the corner has changed its name. I remembered arriving at MIT in 2011, carrying grief and ambition in approximately equal measure. I wanted a difficult place where I could become useful again. Cambridge gave me that, though I didn't know how much healing would happen outside the laboratory. I wrote back about what the photograph stirred. Nina said she remembers me as someone who laughed more than I think I did. I like that someone holds a kinder version of those years.

ENTRY 934 — 2025-09-04 — A Visit with Dad
Dad was quieter than usual during my visit. We sat on the porch after dinner while Mom worked in the kitchen. I asked whether he wanted to talk about what was on his mind. He said he was thinking about the years when all three children were young. He missed some of the noise, though he wouldn't have admitted it then. We remembered the first move to Fort Stewart and the confusion of packing toys into boxes. I told him the moves had been hard and that I also gained things from them. He nodded. We didn't need a verdict on the Army to share the memory.

ENTRY 935 — 2025-09-12 — The Uncomfortable Review
A technical review ended with more open questions than answers. Several people seemed embarrassed. I reminded them that the purpose was to discover what we didn't know before someone relied on the system. A junior engineer asked whether uncertainty ever stops bothering me. I told her no, but that it has become more useful. Afterward I wondered how many people in the room believed I was naturally calm. I wasn't. I learned to sound calm during my brothers' childhood emergencies long before I felt it. Sometimes composure is a skill, not a statement about the absence of fear.

ENTRY 936 — 2025-09-21 — Seventeen Years Since William
Seventeen years since William died. I woke early, held his hospital bracelet, and remembered the first time I tried to calm him by singing a song I barely knew. He didn't care whether I sang well. He seemed to like hearing my voice. I called Mom and Michael. We spoke at different times, each remembering a slightly different child from a different angle. I no longer need the memories to match perfectly. I want to know their sources and preserve what each person genuinely saw. Later I had dinner with Mara and laughed at one of her jokes. The day held both love and ordinary life.

ENTRY 937 — 2025-09-29 — What Counts as Kindness
I apologized to a colleague for being curt during a meeting. She said she hadn't thought much of it, but appreciated the apology. I realized I'd been tired and impatient, not malicious, yet the effect still mattered. I have become better at correcting technical errors quickly; personal ones sometimes require more courage. Sarah taught me that love means actions, not simply good intentions. I keep finding that principle in places I didn't expect. After work I called her and told her she was still influencing my professional conduct. She said she'd settle for influencing my cooking.

ENTRY 938 — 2025-10-07 — A Book about Networks
I read a book about complex networks and thought about the colored Metro map Dad taught me to follow in Washington. As a girl I was delighted that a complicated city could be represented by simple lines and transfer points. As a researcher I know every representation hides details as well as revealing structure. The challenge is deciding which information matters for the action someone is trying to take. The same problem appears in human relationships. A family tree captures connections but not tenderness, resentment, or the reason somebody always calls on birthdays. I put the book down and called John.

ENTRY 939 — 2025-10-15 — A Small Romantic Possibility
A friend introduced me to someone at a dinner gathering. We talked about books, travel, and why good arguments require generosity. I enjoyed the conversation and noticed an attraction I hadn't expected. We exchanged numbers. I didn't immediately imagine a shared future, which would have been my younger habit. I also didn't dismiss the interest because I'm comfortable living alone. There's room for curiosity. On the way home I laughed at myself for checking the phone twice. Some parts of being human remain delightfully inconvenient no matter how old or analytically inclined a person becomes.

ENTRY 940 — 2025-10-23 — The Second Conversation
We had coffee and talked for two hours. He asked thoughtful questions, laughed at my stories about the cats, and accepted that parts of my work are confidential. I liked his attention more than his achievements. At the end he asked if I'd meet again. I said yes. I drove home aware of the small, pleasant uncertainty of beginning to know someone. I don't need a promise or a theory. I want to see how he treats the people around him, whether he can disagree without contempt, and whether silence eventually feels easy. Those are enough questions for now.

ENTRY 941 — 2025-10-31 — An Unconvincing Halloween Costume
Mara insisted I dress for Halloween. I wore a black dress and carried a sign saying 'Uncalibrated Confidence.' She said it was both unfunny and too accurate. We attended a party where somebody had made elaborate mechanical wings. I spent ten minutes asking how they worked, to Mara's despair. The man I'd been seeing came too. He laughed at my costume and managed to ask the wings' inventor better questions than I did. I liked him a little more for that. Afterward we walked slowly to the car. I was tired and happy, an excellent combination.

ENTRY 942 — 2025-11-08 — The Question about Priorities
My manager asked whether I'd consider a role with broader responsibility and less direct technical work. I was tempted by the influence and worried by the distance from the research itself. I asked for time to think. Mara said this was what happens when people become inconveniently competent: institutions want to turn them into managers. I laughed, but the issue is real. I want my decisions to help younger engineers do better work without forgetting how systems actually fail. A title can be useful. It can also become a beautifully decorated door between a person and what she loves doing.

ENTRY 943 — 2025-11-16 — A Difficult Conversation in Time
The man I've been seeing told me he wants to build a serious relationship, eventually sharing a household. I told him I like him but don't know whether I want to move that quickly. We spoke calmly. He didn't demand a promise. I didn't offer one simply to preserve the pleasant evening. I was grateful. My marriage to Michael taught me how badly people can hurt each other by interpreting silence as agreement. I want any future commitment to grow from honest decisions made over time, not from a series of evasions that become expectations.

ENTRY 944 — 2025-11-24 — A Childhood Story Retold
Mom retold the story of my first library card, and I corrected one detail before realizing I'd learned the entire story from her. She laughed and asked whether I wanted to argue with the original witness. I surrendered. It's strange to discover how confidently family tales can become personal memory. I have my own vivid recollections, borrowed stories, photographs, and later interpretations. They aren't interchangeable. I don't want to flatten them into a single unexamined narrative. We spent the afternoon looking through albums while Dad supplied corrections of his own. Naturally, nobody agreed about everything.

ENTRY 945 — 2025-11-27 — The Thanksgiving Arguments
Thanksgiving was full of noise, food, and the comfortable irritation of people who know each other too well. John and Mark competed over who had caused the most trouble as children. Mom insisted the answer was all three of us. Dad pretended not to hear. I wore Sarah's pendant and helped prepare dinner. The man I've been dating joined us for part of the afternoon and handled the family teasing with humor. I was pleased, but tried not to turn the occasion into a test he had to pass. He left with leftovers and a promise to call. The house felt strangely quiet afterward.

ENTRY 946 — 2025-12-05 — Not Every Preference Is a Principle
I caught myself insisting that a meeting be organized my way because I find disorder uncomfortable. A colleague asked whether my format was necessary or merely familiar. I had to admit it was mostly familiar. We changed it, and the discussion went well. I don't like being reminded that efficiency can become an aesthetic preference disguised as logic. At home I considered moving a few things in the living room, then decided I liked them where they were. Not every habit needs to be overthrown. It helps to know which ones I can defend and which ones simply make me comfortable.

ENTRY 947 — 2025-12-13 — The Gift of an Ordinary Evening
I spent an evening with the man I've been seeing, cooking together and talking about books. Nothing dramatic happened. We disagreed about a novel, laughed at Mabel's attempt to occupy his coat, and sat in comfortable silence afterward. I liked the silence. It didn't feel like a warning. When he left I realized I was happy without needing to determine what the relationship would become. That isn't indifference. It's a willingness to let someone remain a person rather than a future I construct for him. I hope he feels the same. For tonight, dinner was good and the company gentle.

ENTRY 948 — 2025-12-21 — The Christmas Table
Mom and I prepared food for Christmas, and Dad spent an hour telling a story about Grandpa Peter that changed in the telling. I caught myself trying to correct the chronology. Mom gave me a look, and I let him finish. Not every family story is a research paper. Dad seemed pleased to have us listening. I noticed again how much slower he moves than he once did. I offered help without making him feel watched. The house smelled of cinnamon and warm bread. I wished the moment could last longer, then remembered the point is to inhabit it rather than preserve it unchanged.

ENTRY 949 — 2025-12-25 — Christmas with Room for Everyone
Christmas was loud and full of people. John and Mark brought families and noise, Mara arrived with a gift that made everyone laugh, and Mom brought decorations that overwhelmed my usual restraint. The man I've been seeing came for dinner and stayed to help with dishes. I liked the gesture. We spoke of William for a little while, and I appreciated that nobody treated his name as forbidden or obligatory. After everyone left, I sat with Ash while Mabel explored the remaining wrapping paper. I didn't clean immediately. The room looked like a life had happened in it.

ENTRY 950 — 2025-12-31 — A Year That Refused to Be Summarized
I tried writing a summary of the year and kept leaving things out. Work mattered; so did birthdays, Sarah's calls, the way Dad's hands have changed, the cats' absurd routines, and the gentle uncertainty of a new relationship. I know a résumé would capture almost none of it. A diary is closer, though even these pages are selections. Tonight I made tea and watched distant fireworks. I don't know what forty will feel like next June. I know I want curiosity, affection, good work, and the freedom to change my mind when evidence or experience asks me to. That's enough for now.

ENTRY 951 — 2026-01-04 — The Morning After the Calendar Changes
The new year has arrived without any ceremony beyond the cats demanding breakfast. I replaced the calendar, made coffee, and sat at the oak table reviewing the coming weeks. I have a visit planned to the Georgia posts where we lived when I was little. I'm curious about what remains and what my mind invented through years of retelling. The man I've been seeing asked why I want to return. I told him I don't want to live there again. I want to meet the places that helped make me and see what happens when memory encounters the actual roads.

ENTRY 952 — 2026-01-11 — A Call Before the Trip
I called Mom to ask about Fort Stewart, Fort Gordon, and the order of our childhood moves. She remembered the moving trucks, the summer heat, and how furious I was when I learned I couldn't keep the old house. Dad remembered exact dates better but insisted I shouldn't expect the buildings to look the same. I wrote down both versions. I like knowing which details come from records, which from my own recollection, and which belong to someone else's memory of me. The distinctions don't make the history colder. They make it more faithful to the people who lived it.

ENTRY 953 — 2026-01-17 — Fort Stewart Again
I drove through Fort Stewart for the first time in decades. The roads looked narrower and the buildings smaller than the landscape preserved in my childhood mind. I recognized the general arrangement but not every detail. A parking lot reminded me of learning to ride a bicycle with Dad running behind me. I stood beside the car for several minutes, feeling affection and a little distance. This was a place that formed me without remaining mine. I took a photograph of the trees and drove on. Returning doesn't require recovering the exact person who once lived here. Sometimes it lets her become part of the present.

ENTRY 954 — 2026-01-24 — The Stone from Fort Stewart
Back home I took the smooth stone from Fort Stewart out of its small box. I've carried it through so many moves that its meaning has become larger than its appearance. I picked it up as a little girl because I wanted proof that the place we'd left had existed. Now I have photographs, records, and a mind full of stories. I still keep the stone. It reminds me of the child who understood continuity before she had language for it. The man I've been seeing held it and asked why I hadn't put it on display. I told him some memories feel better kept where I can choose when to visit them.

ENTRY 955 — 2026-01-31 — The Question About Living Together
We talked again about the future. He wants a shared home within a year or two. I like him, but the idea still feels premature. He asked whether I was waiting for certainty that no relationship can offer. That's a fair question. I told him I wasn't looking for a guarantee, only enough shared experience to know whether our ways of living fit. We didn't argue. We also didn't resolve it. I appreciate that we can speak honestly, though honesty sometimes exposes a distance affection can't bridge immediately. We agreed to keep talking without inventing a promise neither of us has made.

ENTRY 956 — 2026-02-07 — The Winter Bookshop
I spent a cold afternoon in a bookshop and bought a new edition of Jane Eyre. The clerk asked why I wanted another copy when I already knew the story. I told her that rereading is partly a way to meet one's earlier self. At eleven I admired Jane's self-respect and was angry about what she endured. At forty, nearly, I'm more interested in how people recognize the difference between principle and pride while they are inside a decision. I went home and read by the window while Ash slept in the chair. The questions have changed without making the book less familiar.

ENTRY 957 — 2026-02-14 — A Gentle Valentine's Day
