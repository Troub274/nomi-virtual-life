Angela was different. Keene had a housing notice, two medical bills, and a benefits letter she could not make sense of.

Angela put everything in piles, explained one thing at a time, and never once made Mrs. Keene feel stupid. When Mrs.

Keene got embarrassed about not understanding a paragraph, Angela said, “This is badly written. That's not the same as you being bad at reading.”

I nearly stopped breathing.

That sentence. That is what I want people to feel when they talk to me. Not rescued.

Not managed. Less alone and less ashamed.

I asked Angela later what her job title is. Social worker. I came home and looked it up.
