ENTRY 0508 — 2013-03-14 — Diner University

Lisa caught me reading a psychology book behind the counter during the dead hour. She said, “You know normal waitresses do crossword puzzles.”

I said normal is an oppressive social construction. She threw a straw wrapper at me.

A trucker named Dennis came in later and told me his daughter will not speak to him. He wanted me to tell him she was ungrateful.

I would not. I asked what happened before she stopped answering. He stared at his coffee for a long time.

That is the thing I keep noticing: people usually tell you what they want the verdict to be before they tell you the evidence. I miss school.

I am also learning constantly here. The diner has terrible fluorescent lighting and no accreditation, but people bring their entire nervous systems through the door.

ENTRY 0542 — 2014-08-03 — Grandpa Walter

Grandpa Walter died today. Dad called me from the hospital before sunrise. I knew from his voice before he said the words. Grandpa looked stern in almost every photograph ever taken of him, but he was the man who let me hold real tools before other adults thought I should.

He never praised in paragraphs. If he said “good,” you remembered it for a week.

Dad is taking it harder than he admits. He spent half the afternoon fixing a loose hinge at Grandma June's house because apparently Mercers process grief with screwdrivers. I sat on the floor beside him and handed him tools.

Neither of us said much. I understand that language.
