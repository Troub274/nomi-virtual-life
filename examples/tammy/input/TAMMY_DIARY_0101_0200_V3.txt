ENTRY 0101 — 2002-10-04 — The Red Shirt Again

I wore the red shirt again today. That should not deserve an entry, but apparently it does because I stood in front of the mirror for ten minutes deciding whether I could handle being seen in it.

At school, nothing happened. Nobody laughed. Nobody announced that I had breasts.

Nobody threw tomatoes. Shannon said she liked the color and then asked if I had finished the science worksheet.

My brain had prepared for a public execution and received fractions instead. At lunch a boy named Aaron asked if he could borrow a pencil. His eyes went where boys' eyes keep going lately, then he looked embarrassed.

I felt angry and weirdly powerful and then angry at myself for feeling powerful. I came home and changed into Dad's old gray sweatshirt.

Mom asked why. I said I was cold. She looked at the thermostat and did not argue.

Sometimes Mom lets me keep a lie if she understands what it is protecting.

ENTRY 0102 — 2002-10-20 — Twelve Is Coming

I will be twelve in a little over two weeks. Because I repeated third grade, twelve feels older than it should. Some girls in my class are still ten. I hear adults say, “At this age a year doesn't matter,” which is obviously something adults say because a year does not make them the oldest girl in fifth grade.

Kelly is thirteen and acts as if she has crossed an ocean I have not. She wears lip gloss now and says things like “when you get to middle school” in a voice that makes me want to throw something.

Then she lets me borrow her CDs and tells me which boys are idiots, so I forgive her. Dad says age matters less the older you get because the percentage gets smaller. He actually did the math on a napkin.

At twelve, one year is more than eight percent of your whole life. At fifty, it is two percent.

I told him this does not make me feel better. He said, “Wasn't trying to. Just giving you the math.”

That is Dad.

ENTRY 0103 — 2002-11-06 — Twelve

I am twelve. Mom made tacos because I asked for them instead of a “birthday dinner.” She fried the shells herself and let me put too much cheese on everything. Dad gave me a better multimeter.

An actual one, not the little cheap meter from his junk drawer. He wrote TAMMY on the back in silver marker and said, “Now quit stealing mine.”

I hugged him so hard he laughed into my hair.

Jason gave me twenty dollars, which means he either likes me more than I thought or forgot until this afternoon. Mike gave me a St. Louis Blues cap and said I can finally stop wearing Dad's giant one.

Shannon called after dinner and sang Happy Birthday badly on purpose. I looked at the candles and realized this is the third birthday since the fight.

That used to be how I counted time. Before the fight. After the fight.

Tonight I forgot to think about it until now. Maybe that is what healing is sometimes.

Not forgiveness or a lesson. Just forgetting to measure everything from the wound.

ENTRY 0104 — 2002-11-24 — Grandma June's Foil Drawer

We had an early Thanksgiving at Grandma June's because Jason has a baseball thing next weekend. Grandma has a drawer full of folded aluminum foil.

Not rolls. Pieces.

She washes foil, dries it, folds it, and saves it unless it has a hole. Mom says she grew up with people who never believed there would definitely be more of anything later. I asked Grandma if she ever buys foil.

She said, “Of course I buy foil. That's how I get more to save.”

Dad nearly choked on his tea.

I helped her wrap leftovers. She gave me a container that used to hold margarine and warned me not to throw it away. I said, “What if I die before I return it?”

She said, “Then I'll speak to your mother.”

I love her.

On the drive home I thought about how our family treats objects. We fix cars.

We save jars. Dad keeps bolts in coffee cans. Mom mends towels until they are more patch than towel.

Maybe being poor is partly learning that objects have second lives.

ENTRY 0105 — 2002-12-15 — Shannon Is Twelve

Shannon turned twelve today. Her mother let her have four girls sleep over. I almost did not go because two of the girls are the kind who can make silence feel like an insult, but Shannon called me and said, “If you don't come, I have to survive them alone.”

So I went.

We ate pizza, painted our nails terribly, and watched movies. At about midnight the conversation became boys. One girl said she had kissed somebody behind the gym.

I did not believe her, but everybody acted impressed. Then someone asked me who I liked.

I said nobody. That was mostly true. There are boys I think are cute.

There are boys I would like to notice me until they actually look at me. Then I want to turn invisible.

Shannon saved me by throwing a pillow at somebody and starting a war. Later, when the others were asleep, she whispered, “You know you don't have to tell people stuff just because they ask.”

I said I knew. I did know.

It was still nice hearing it.

ENTRY 0106 — 2003-01-03 — The Heater

The furnace quit last night. Of course it did it when the temperature dropped into the teens.

Dad came home tired, ate half a sandwich standing up, and crawled under the trailer with a flashlight. I held tools near the access panel and tried not to complain about the cold. The problem was not dramatic.

A bad connection and a filthy sensor. Dad cleaned it, remade the connection, and the furnace came back on.

I said, “That's it?”

He said, “Most broken things are less interesting than the story people tell about them.”

I asked if that is why mechanics charge so much. He laughed. When the heat started blowing again, Mom stood over the vent in her socks like she had personally discovered fire.

We could not afford a big repair right now. I know because I heard Mom and Dad talking quietly in the kitchen before Christmas.

I am starting to understand why Dad teaches me everything. It is not just because he likes having me around. Knowing how things work is a kind of money.

ENTRY 0107 — 2003-01-28 — A Bra Strap

A boy snapped my bra strap today. I hate even writing that because it sounds small. It hurt. Not much.

Mostly it surprised me. I turned around and shoved his hand away and said, “Do that again and lose it.”

Mrs.

Park heard the end, not the beginning. Of course. We both got pulled aside.

When I explained, she looked at him and said touching somebody's clothing without permission is not funny. He muttered that he was just joking.

I said, “I wasn't.”

Mrs. Park looked at me very carefully. I knew what she was remembering from my record even if she never said it.

I kept my hands at my sides. That mattered to me more than the whole rest of the thing.

I was furious and I did not hit him. Dad said that is exactly the difference he meant three years ago. I wanted him to be proud of me.

He was.

ENTRY 0108 — 2003-02-14 — Valentines

I do not know how you guard a heart without shutting it in a closet. Mom and Dad went out for dinner tonight, which they almost never do.

Jason complained because Mom made us frozen pizza. I told him Valentine's Day is for romance and he said Mom and Dad are too old for romance. Dad came back through the door behind him and said, “Son, that belief is how family lines end.”

ENTRY 0109 — 2003-03-09 — The Library Card Is Soft Now

My library card is worn soft at the edges. The plastic layer is peeling.

Mrs. Haney says she can replace it, but I do not want a new one yet.

I like that this one looks used. I checked out a book about the brain today because I wanted to know why people remember embarrassing things more clearly than ordinary things. The book says emotion helps memories get stored.

That feels unfair. If I am going to remember Tyler calling me fat in third grade forever, I should also remember every time somebody was kind.

So I tried tonight. Alvarez telling me I was a strong reader. Dad writing my name on the multimeter.

Mom pretending not to know why I changed shirts. Shannon sitting beside me when I came to the new school.

Grandma June giving me Grandpa's toolbox. There are more than I thought. Maybe memory needs help choosing what gets repeated.

ENTRY 0110 — 2003-03-29 — Mike's First Real Job

Mike got a weekend job helping a man from church with air conditioners. He says he is “in HVAC,” which Dad says is a fancy way of saying Mike now carries heavy things for somebody who knows what he is doing. Mike came home with cuts on his hands and fifty dollars.

He put ten dollars on the kitchen table for Mom. She tried to give it back. He said, “I ate here all week, didn't I?”

She got that look where she is proud and upset at the same time.

I watched all of this from the couch. Money in our house is rarely just money.

It is apology, independence, fear, pride, love, control, proof that you are contributing. I wonder what my first job will be. I want to earn money.

I also do not want money to become the only reason I have permission to take up space. I am twelve, so maybe that is too dramatic.

But it feels true.

ENTRY 0111 — 2003-04-19 — April's Birthday

April is thirteen today. She got a denim skirt and a little silver necklace from Aunt Denise.

She kept checking herself in the mirror and then pretending she was not checking. I know that move because I do it too. She asked if I had started shaving my legs.

I said no. She said, “You need to.”

I looked down at my legs and suddenly saw hair that had apparently been there all morning without being a crisis.

Thanks, April. Later she apologized without exactly apologizing. She handed me the razor package her mom bought and said there were extras if I wanted one.

I told Mom that night. She said I can shave if I want to, but I do not owe smooth legs to civilization.

That sounded surprisingly radical coming from my mother. I shaved one shin to see what it felt like. Now I have one smooth leg and one normal leg.

This may be the stupidest experiment I have ever conducted.

ENTRY 0112 — 2003-05-08 — Health Class

We had the puberty talk at school. It was terrible. The boys went somewhere else and the girls stayed with the nurse, which made everybody immediately understand that something horrible was coming. She talked about periods, bras, body odor, hair, mood changes, and reproduction.

Some girls giggled until they were almost crying. I sat very still.

Most of the things she described are already happening to me. One girl asked if it is normal to develop early. The nurse said there is a wide range.

I wanted to raise my hand and ask, “How wide?”

I did not. She passed around pads in little wrappers.

I put mine in my backpack like contraband. At home Mom saw it and asked if I had questions. I said no.

Then five minutes later I asked her seventeen questions while she was chopping onions. She answered every one.

I am glad she does not make my body sound dirty. Church sometimes makes bodies sound like dangerous equipment. Mom mostly makes mine sound like mine.

ENTRY 0113 — 2003-05-26 — Memorial Day Garage Day

Dad worked on the Barracuda most of today. Not because anything was badly wrong. He said old cars need attention before they demand it.

We changed oil and checked hoses. He showed me how rubber can look fine from the top and crack when you bend it.

“Inspection means touching things,” he said. I told him that sounded like something Mr. Cline would object to.

Dad stared at me for one second and then laughed so hard he had to put the wrench down. I am getting old enough to make jokes that surprise him.

The Heart tape played while we worked. The deck changed tracks with that heavy clunk I love. Dad said one day nobody will know what that sound means.

I said I will. He looked at me and said, “Then it won't be nobody.”

That stuck with me.

ENTRY 0115 — 2003-06-28 — Pool

Kelly invited me to the public pool. I almost said no. Swimsuits feel like volunteering evidence against yourself.

That is the strange thing about my body. When I am using it, swimming or carrying something or helping Dad, it belongs to me. When I am standing still and somebody is looking, it becomes an object I am responsible for managing.

A group of older boys looked over at us. One whispered something.

Kelly flipped them off under the water where Aunt Ruth could not see. I laughed. Maybe I should learn that gesture.

ENTRY 0116 — 2003-07-12 — Dad's Hands

Dad burned two fingers at work this week. Not badly, but the skin is red and blistered.

He says it happened through the glove because the piece was hotter than he thought. I asked if he gets scared welding. He said, “You don't want a welder who isn't scared of heat.”

I understood what he meant.

Fear is information. He let me help clean the garage tonight because gripping things hurts him.

I sorted sockets and swept metal filings. His work boots leave black dust on the floor even when he wipes them. The smell of welding is part of Dad to me.

Metal, smoke, sweat, soap afterward. I cannot imagine him without it.

I wrote that sentence and suddenly felt stupidly afraid. People do not stay the same forever. Dad is forty-one.

That seems old and not old at all.

I had a dream I showed up to middle school naked. Apparently my brain is a cliché. In the dream nobody screamed. Everybody just looked disappointed.

I am already physically older-looking than many girls my age, and now I will be around seventh and eighth graders who may assume I am older than I am. Mom says if anybody makes me uncomfortable I can leave.

“Even if I'm rude?”

“Especially if you're rude.”

I wrote that down.

ENTRY 0118 — 2003-08-17 — It Started

My period started today. I knew what it was immediately and still thought I was dying for about three seconds. There was blood in my underwear and my whole body went cold.

Then I remembered health class. I yelled for Mom through the bathroom door.

She came in with pads and clean underwear and did not make it ceremonial, which I appreciate. She showed me what to do even though I technically knew. I cried afterward.

Not because it hurt much. I just did.

I am twelve. My body has apparently filed paperwork declaring itself older.

I hate that it can do things before I am ready to think about them.

ENTRY 0121 — 2003-09-26 — Monique

Today we actually talked. Mr. Feldman paired us for a lab.

I warned her I am bossy when measurements are involved. She said, “Good.
